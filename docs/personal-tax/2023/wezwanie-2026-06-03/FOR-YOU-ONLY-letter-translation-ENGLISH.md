### II. Change of tax residency — context

1. Until 11 April 2023 my place of residence and tax residency were in the

2. On **12 April 2023** I moved my centre of vital interests to Poland and

   **12 April 2023**. That agreement also records my then-current permanent

   residency (Annex 3).
3. Consequently, for the years 2020–2022 and for the period from 1 January to
   11 April 2023 I was not subject to Polish taxation on my worldwide income
   and did not file PIT-38 returns for those periods. Income from that period,
   including from the disposal of virtual currencies, was settled in Sweden.

### III. Circumstances of the original return and the correction

1. The original PIT-38 return for 2023 was filed on 7 June 2024, together
   with a voluntary disclosure (czynny żal). I entrusted the preparation of
   my 2023–2024 tax filings to a professional tax firm, because as a person
   who had only just moved to Poland I knew neither the Polish tax system nor
   the Polish language.
2. The tax firm did not ask me any questions about purchases or holdings of
   virtual currencies from the period preceding the transfer of my residency
   to Poland. As a result, the original return did not include the costs of
   acquiring virtual currencies incurred before 12 April 2023 (field 36 =
   PLN 0), and also omitted part of the revenue from the paid disposal of
   virtual currencies.
3. It was only in 2026, after my own detailed analysis of my filings and of
   the rules for taxing virtual currencies in Poland, that I realized that
   documented acquisition costs incurred before the residency change,
   relating to virtual currencies held on the date of the residency change,
   are relevant to the PIT-38 settlement, and that the original return was
   also incomplete on the revenue side.
4. For these reasons, on 27 April 2026 I filed a correction of the return in
   which I reported both **higher revenue** (PLN 264,113.82 versus
   PLN 226,115.71 in the original return) and costs incurred in previous
   years (field 36). The purpose of the correction was to bring the
   settlement into line with the actual facts and the law.

### IV. Nature of the amount reported in field 36

The amount of PLN 588,848.16 **is not a carry-over of costs from field 38 of
an earlier PIT-38 return** (no such return for 2022 exists, for the reasons
described in section II). The amount is the sum of **documented expenses
directly incurred to acquire virtual currencies** (Art. 22(14) of the PIT
Act), which:

1. were incurred before 12 April 2023 (in 2021–2022 and in the period from
   1 January to 11 April 2023),
2. relate to virtual currencies that **remained in my possession on the date
   my Polish tax residency began** (12 April 2023) — a detailed inventory of
   holdings as of that date, prepared from blockchain data, remains at the
   Authority's disposal,
3. **were not deducted as costs** either in Poland or — to the best of my
   knowledge — in Swedish settlements (costs relating to virtual currencies
   disposed of before 12 April 2023 were settled in Sweden and are **not
   included** in the amount in field 36),
4. are directly connected with revenue from the paid disposal of virtual
   currencies earned by me after 12 April 2023 and reported in Polish PIT-38
   returns (field 34 of the 2023 return).

The legal basis for deducting these costs is Art. 22(14)–(16) in conjunction
with Art. 30b(1a) and 30b(6)(2) of the PIT Act. The PIT Act conditions the
deductibility of virtual-currency acquisition costs on their documentation and
direct connection with the acquisition; it contains no condition that such
expenses must have been incurred during a period of Polish tax residency. At
the same time, excluding these costs would lead to taxation of gross revenue
instead of income, contrary to the construction of Art. 30b(1a) of the PIT
Act, and to double economic burden given the earlier taxation in Sweden.

The position that expenses incurred to acquire virtual currencies before
obtaining Polish tax residency may constitute tax-deductible costs in the
PIT-38 settlement, provided they were not previously deducted as costs in
another country, was confirmed by the Provincial Administrative Court (WSA)
in Warsaw in its judgment of 29 August 2024, case no. III SA/Wa 1290/24.

Expenses incurred in foreign currencies were converted into PLN using the
National Bank of Poland mid-rate from the last business day preceding the day
the cost was incurred (Art. 11a(2) of the PIT Act); the applied rates are
shown for each transaction in Annex 2.

For full transparency, I note that part of the above expenses was incurred in
the period from 1 January to 11 April 2023, i.e. in the 2023 tax year but
before the start of Polish residency. Reporting them in field 36 rather than
field 35 has no effect whatsoever on the amount of income (field 37), costs
carried forward (field 38) or tax due — both fields are deducted in the same
return.
