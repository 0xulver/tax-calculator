## 2. JDG liability: can you pursue Mateusz Szklarski personally?

Yes, if the seller is indeed operating as a **jednoosobowa działalność gospodarcza**. A JDG is not a separate legal person like a sp. z o.o.; the business is the natural person acting under a business name. Polish official business guidance states that in JDG the entrepreneur bears liability with all assets, and that the JDG is not separate from the natural person for liability purposes. ([Business.gov.pl][1])

A public CEIDG search result also identifies the business as **“mateusz szklarski gpucomputer”**, which is consistent with the debtor being the individual entrepreneur rather than a company vehicle. ([CEIDG][2])

Practical consequences:

* The debtor is not “GPUcomputer” as a separate pocket of assets; the debtor is **Mateusz Szklarski trading as GPUcomputer**.
* If you obtain an enforceable title, enforcement should be possible against his **personal bank accounts, personal movable assets, business inventory, receivables, vehicles, and real estate**, subject to ordinary enforcement limits.
* Suspension, closure, or deletion of the JDG should not erase the debt.
* If he enters bankruptcy as an entrepreneur, it is essentially the bankruptcy of the **individual debtor’s estate**, not a limited company shield.

This is significantly better than suing a thinly capitalized sp. z o.o. However, it does **not** guarantee recovery. Personal liability helps only if there are reachable assets.

EPU has no simple fixed statutory deadline requiring a payment order within a specific number of days. Practitioner sources give broad practical ranges such as roughly **two weeks to two months** or **two to three months**, depending on workload and case issues; these are not legal guarantees. Your claim type fits the EPU framework: EPU is used for monetary claims, and the Code of Civil Procedure provides that EPU applies to claims that became due within three years before filing. ([Isap][4])

## 4. What happens next in EPU?

### If the payment order is issued

The defendant will be served. In payment-order procedure, the defendant generally has **two weeks** from service to pay or object; if no effective objection is filed, the payment order has the effect of a final judgment. ([Isap][4])

Once final and enforceable, you should move immediately to enforcement with a bailiff.

### If the court refuses to issue an EPU order

The EPU court may discontinue if there is no basis to issue a payment order. In EPU, the plaintiff indicates evidence but does not attach it, which means a documentary-heavy dispute can sometimes be pushed out of EPU into ordinary proceedings. ([Isap][4])

If EPU is discontinued, you should file in ordinary court within **three months** to preserve the legal effects of the EPU filing and account for EPU costs. ([Isap][4])

Because your claim is **155,000 PLN**, an ordinary civil/commercial claim would likely go to the **Sąd Okręgowy**, since district courts generally handle property claims up to 100,000 PLN except for special categories such as EPU. ([Isap][4])

### If the defendant objects

In EPU, the defendant’s objection does not need to include detailed evidence. If an objection is filed, the payment order loses force in the challenged part and the EPU proceeding is discontinued to that extent. ([Isap][4])

Then you should file the ordinary-court case within the three-month window, with all evidence attached and organized.

## 8. What to do immediately if bankruptcy appears in KRZ

If you see a Polish bankruptcy announcement for Mateusz Szklarski / GPUcomputer, do **not** rely only on the EPU case. You must file your creditor claim in the bankruptcy system.

Bankruptcy Law requires a creditor who wants to participate in bankruptcy proceedings to file the claim to the trustee through the teleinformatic system within the deadline stated in the bankruptcy order. ([Isap][9])

Your claim filing should include:

* 155,000 PLN principal;
* statutory delay interest up to the bankruptcy declaration date;
* court costs, if recoverable/awarded;
* evidence of the transaction and refund promise;
* evidence of payment;
* evidence of non-payment;
* your bank details;
* your NIP/business details;
* EPU case number Nc-e 552126/26.

Late filing can create additional costs and practical disadvantages. Bankruptcy Law provides that late claim filing can require the creditor to bear flat-rate costs, even if the delay was not the creditor’s fault. ([Isap][9])

Once bankruptcy is declared, ordinary court and enforcement dynamics change sharply. Proceedings concerning the bankruptcy estate are conducted by or against the trustee, and enforcement against the bankruptcy estate is suspended by law on the bankruptcy date and later discontinued after the bankruptcy order becomes final. ([Isap][9])

Interest is also affected: in bankruptcy, interest is generally satisfied from the estate only up to the bankruptcy declaration date, subject to special secured-creditor rules. ([Isap][9])

As an unsecured trade creditor, your principal claim would likely be an ordinary unsecured claim. Bankruptcy Law categories place ordinary claims in category two, while interest often falls lower, and lower categories are paid only after higher categories; same-category creditors share pro rata if funds are insufficient. ([Isap][9])

## 9. What if restructuring appears instead of bankruptcy?

Restructuring is different from bankruptcy. Its purpose is to avoid bankruptcy through an arrangement with creditors. Polish restructuring law includes four main types: arrangement approval proceeding, accelerated arrangement proceeding, arrangement proceeding, and sanation. ([Isap][10])

A **postępowanie o zatwierdzenie układu** can begin with an arrangement day and notice by an arrangement supervisor. After notice, protective effects may apply, and the effects can expire if no arrangement-approval application is filed within four months. ([Isap][10])

In restructuring, court proceedings may sometimes continue to establish the claim, but enforcement is often suspended or blocked for claims covered by the arrangement. For example, in accelerated arrangement proceedings, enforcement concerning claims covered by the arrangement is suspended by law, and new enforcement or security proceedings for such claims are generally inadmissible after opening. ([Isap][10])

Sanation has even stronger protective effects: enforcement against assets forming the sanation estate is suspended, and new enforcement or security against that estate is generally inadmissible. ([Isap][10])

If restructuring appears, you should immediately have a Polish restructuring lawyer check:

* whether your claim is on the list of claims;
* whether the amount is correct;
* whether interest and costs are included;
* whether you can object;
* voting deadlines;
* whether enforcement/security is blocked;
* whether the arrangement proposal treats you fairly.

Under Polish civil procedure, security can be requested before or during proceedings. The claimant must make the claim probable and show a legal interest in security; legal interest exists when lack of security would prevent or seriously hinder enforcement or otherwise prevent achievement of the proceeding’s purpose. ([Isap][4])

Possible security measures for money claims include seizure of movables, bank-account receivables and other rights, compulsory mortgage over real estate, bans on disposal or encumbrance, and compulsory administration over an enterprise in some cases. ([Isap][4])

My recommendation: **speak to a Polish commercial-litigation/restructuring lawyer within the next 7 days specifically about a security motion.** EPU itself is not ideal for this because EPU does not operate as a full evidence-attached proceeding. A separate or ordinary-court security strategy may be more effective.

## 12. Criminal angle: fraud complaint

A criminal complaint is possible, but it should be framed carefully.

Polish Criminal Code art. 286 §1 covers fraud where, for financial gain, someone causes another person to disadvantageously dispose of property through misleading them, exploiting a mistake, or exploiting inability to understand the action. ([Isap][11])

Facts that would matter:

* whether the seller already knew they could not deliver when taking your money;

* whether the “customs cleared” and delivery promises were false;
* whether there are other victims;

A criminal complaint can create pressure and may help uncover information, but it is not a debt-collection substitute. The prosecutor/police do not act as your bailiff. I would not lead with accusations of fraud in ordinary correspondence. If you file, use the neutral formula:

> zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa

and present documents chronologically, without defamatory language.

Given the facts, I would consider a criminal complaint if:

* KRZ remains empty but the seller keeps claiming “bankruptcy protection”;
* there is evidence that delivery promises were knowingly untrue;
* assets appear to be disappearing.

## 13. Settlement and installment strategy

Do not withdraw or pause the EPU case based on vague promises.

If the seller proposes installments, the safest structure is:

* written acknowledgment of debt;
* exact principal, interest, and costs;
* payment schedule;
* acceleration clause: one missed installment makes the whole debt immediately due;
* no waiver of claims until full cleared payment;
* no withdrawal of EPU until payment is fully received, unless your lawyer approves a stronger enforceable instrument;
* preferably a **notarial deed with submission to enforcement under art. 777 k.p.c.**

A notarial deed under art. 777 can be an enforcement title if the debtor submits to enforcement and the payment obligation/deadline or conditions are properly specified. ([Isap][4])

This is especially useful if they ask for installments. Without it, an installment agreement can simply become another broken promise.
