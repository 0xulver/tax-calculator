## Section A — Statutory map of joint-liability theories

### A0. Civil proof frame

In a Polish civil case, **you carry the burden of proving the facts from which you derive legal consequences** under KC art. 6, and parties are expected to identify supporting evidence under KPC art. 232. The court then evaluates credibility and evidentiary force under KPC art. 233 § 1. In practical terms, the court does not need “beyond reasonable doubt,” but it will need a coherent, document-backed narrative strong enough to find the key facts proven after both sides’ evidence. ([Sip Lex][1])

Your evidence should be divided into three buckets:

| Evidence category                    | What belongs there                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Already proveable                    | Wayback pages showing 3dkrakow/Waldek and GPUcomputer/Mateusz under one domain; live 2026 contact page; CEIDG records; Yelp/other business listings; invoice; bank transfer; demand/refund emails.                                                                                           |
| Civil-litigation evidence to develop | Sworn translations; email headers; full thread chronology; screenshots with timestamps; website archive captures; witness statement from you; proof of payment; proof of non-delivery; written refund promise; proof Waldemar used the same mobile/email/domain in the relevant transaction. |
| State-power evidence                 | Bank records, JPK/VAT records, supplier invoices, phone metadata, server logs, customer pattern evidence, seized devices. These usually require prosecutor activity, not ordinary Polish civil “discovery.”                                                                                  |

### A1. Implied / de facto **spółka cywilna** — KC arts. 860 and 864

**Polish text, key parts**

KC art. 860 § 1: “Przez umowę spółki wspólnicy zobowiązują się dążyć do osiągnięcia wspólnego celu gospodarczego przez działanie w sposób oznaczony…”
**Working translation:** “By a civil partnership agreement, the partners undertake to pursue a common economic purpose by acting in a specified manner…”

KC art. 860 § 2: “Umowa spółki powinna być stwierdzona pismem.”
**Working translation:** “The partnership agreement should be evidenced in writing.”

KC art. 864: “Za zobowiązania spółki wspólnicy odpowiedzialni są solidarnie.”
**Working translation:** “The partners are jointly and severally liable for the obligations of the partnership.” ([Sip Lex][2])

**Elements to prove**

You would need to prove: an agreement, express or implied, between Mateusz and Waldemar; a common economic purpose; specified contributions/actions by each; and that the 155,000 PLN obligation arose from that joint activity, not solely from Mateusz’s separate JDG.

**Fit to your facts: Moderate, but not strongest.**

The supporting facts are real: shared GPUcomputer domain, Waldek’s GPUcomputer email, shared or overlapping phone/contact infrastructure, same vertical, decade-long co-presentation, and Waldek’s role in configurations/customer-facing work.  The problem is that Polish courts will likely treat separate CEIDG entries, separate NIPs, separate brand names, separate historical addresses, and the fact that Mateusz was the invoice/bank-transfer party as strong contrary evidence. The public-facing evidence can support “one operation,” but a spółka cywilna theory normally becomes much stronger with proof of shared revenue, shared expenses, shared suppliers, joint inventory, profit-sharing, joint customer acquisition, or internal communications showing they treated GPUcomputer/3d Kraków as one undertaking.

**Case-law / doctrine signals**

A civil partnership agreement is not invalid merely because it is not written; the writing requirement is evidentiary. Courts have accepted oral or conduct-based arrangements where entrepreneurs actually implemented a common economic project, and the lack of writing did not bar proof between entrepreneurs in the same way it may in other settings. ([orzeczenia.piotrkow-tryb.so.gov.pl][3]) SN III CZP 130/10 is important because it confirms the core enforcement consequence: under art. 864 KC, a creditor need not sue all civil partners; partners are solidarily liable for partnership obligations. ([Supreme Court][4])

**Evidence needed beyond what you have**

You need more than “they looked joint.” Best evidence would be: shared invoices or bank accounts; supplier invoices involving both; accounting overlap; “we/our company” language in the emails; statements on the website presenting the two as one team; proof the same phone handled both businesses; proof 3d Kraków inventory was used for GPUcomputer orders; or proof Waldemar accepted/managed your specific order as part of a joint enterprise.

**Probability estimate**

On public co-marketing alone: **20–30%**. With the full email thread showing “we,” joint control, and a single integrated GPUcomputer/3d Kraków commercial operation: **35–50%**. This is worth pleading as a fallback, but it should not be the lead theory unless the email/accounting evidence is stronger than currently described.

### A2. Joint tortfeasor liability — KC arts. 415, 422, 441, with KC art. 443 allowing tort overlap

**Polish text, key parts**

KC art. 415: “Kto z winy swej wyrządził drugiemu szkodę, obowiązany jest do jej naprawienia.”
**Working translation:** “Whoever, through his own fault, causes damage to another is obliged to repair it.”

KC art. 422: liability extends not only to the direct wrongdoer, but also to the person who induced another to cause damage, helped cause it, or knowingly benefited from damage caused to another.

KC art. 441 § 1: if several persons are liable for damage caused by a tort, their liability is solidary.

KC art. 443 preserves the possibility of tort liability even where the facts also constitute non-performance of a contract. ([Sip Lex][5])

**Elements to prove**

You need: a wrongful/fault-based act or omission; damage; causation; and Waldemar’s own role. For art. 422, it is not enough that Waldemar was nearby or useful to Mateusz. You need to show that he consciously helped cause the loss, induced the harmful transaction/reliance, or knowingly benefited from the harm.

**Fit to your facts: Strong, conditionally.**

This is the best theory if the emails show that Waldemar was the person who negotiated the order, gave delivery assurances, managed the refund promise, and knew facts showing non-performance/refund was materially unlikely. Your brief states that every substantive negotiation was with “Waldek” from `waldek@gpucomputer.pl`, that he handled delivery/refund communications, and that Mateusz was mainly the invoice/bank recipient.  That maps better to tort participation than to formal partnership: Waldemar’s own conduct may have induced the payment, prolonged your reliance, or delayed protective action.

**Case-law / doctrine signals**

SN II CNP 50/12 is the caution: **mere breach of contract is not automatically a tort**; non-performance becomes delictual only if the conduct also violates a general legal duty, such as deception or bad-faith concealment. ([Supreme Court][6]) SN III CSK 338/06 is the caution on “helper” liability: art. 422 requires causation between the helper’s conduct and the damage; helping hide a loss after it already occurred is usually not enough unless the assistance was part of the earlier harmful scheme. ([Supreme Court][7]) SN III CZP 89/17 is the caution on “knowing benefit”: conscious benefit means actual knowledge that one is benefiting from another’s tort, not mere negligence or suspicion. ([Supreme Court][8])

The criminal-law analogy also helps but does not decide the civil case. SN V KK 240/17 recognized that fraud by silence/concealment can occur where a party consciously withholds an objectively existing situation affecting the ability to perform transaction terms. That supports the factual theory, but the civil court still needs its own KC elements unless there is a final criminal conviction. ([Supreme Court][9])

**Evidence needed beyond what you have**

The email thread must be organized into a chronology showing:

1. What Waldek said before payment.
2. What he knew or should have known then.
3. What he said after payment.
4. Whether his delay/refund statements caused you not to cancel, sue, charge back, report, or secure assets earlier.
5. Whether he personally benefited: payment routed to Mateusz but inventory/revenue/customer relationship transferred to or used by Waldemar/3d Kraków.

**Probability estimate**

If emails show only ordinary customer-service optimism: **20–35%**. If emails show Waldek knowingly made false or reckless assurances, concealed material non-performance risk, or made a refund promise he knew could not be honored: **45–65%**. If state evidence later shows shared proceeds, repeated failed-order pattern, or asset routing: **65%+**.

### A3. Criminal co-perpetration and civil consequences — KK arts. 18, 286, 46; KPC art. 11; KPK art. 315

**Polish text, key parts**

KK art. 18 § 1 treats as a perpetrator not only one who commits the act alone, but also one who commits it jointly and in agreement with another. KK art. 286 § 1 is the fraud provision: obtaining a material benefit by inducing another to make an unfavorable disposition of property through deception or exploitation of error. ([Sip Lex][10])

KK art. 46 provides that, upon conviction, the criminal court may—and on the injured party’s request must—order full or partial repair of damage or compensation applying civil-law rules. KPC art. 11 provides that findings of a final criminal conviction as to commission of an offence bind the civil court. ([Sip Lex][11])

KPK art. 315 § 1 lets the injured party and counsel file motions for investigative acts. That is the procedural hook for asking the prosecutor to add Waldemar-focused evidence steps. ([Sip Lex][12])

**Fit to your facts: Moderate as leverage/evidence route; slow as recovery route.**

You can submit a **supplemental criminal filing** naming Waldemar and attaching the email chronology. You can request: interrogation of Waldemar; preservation/seizure of email/server/phone records; inquiry into bank/JPK/supplier records; and consideration of criminal asset security. You cannot force the prosecutor to treat Waldemar as a suspect rather than witness; that decision depends on evidence establishing sufficient suspicion.

**Important correction:** a criminal conviction is not a quick substitute for civil enforcement. It may create a restitution order and bind the civil court as to the offence, but waiting for it could take longer than a civil action.

**Probability estimate**

As a route to fast repayment: **low to moderate**. As a route to evidence and asset freeze pressure: **moderate to high**, especially if there are other victims or documents showing repeated concealed inability to perform.

### A4. Apparent authority / agency / contractual co-debtor — KC arts. 97, 103, 105

**Polish text, key parts**

KC art. 97: a person active in premises intended for public service is presumed, in case of doubt, authorized to perform legal acts usually performed with customers of that enterprise. ([Sip Lex][13])

KC art. 103: where someone concludes a contract as an agent without authority or exceeding authority, validity depends on confirmation by the person in whose name the contract was concluded. KC art. 105 protects third parties in some cases of expired authority where the other side did not know and could not easily know the authority had expired. ([Gofin Przepisy][14])

**Fit to your facts: Weak for making Waldemar liable; strong for binding Mateusz.**

Agency law mainly helps prove that Waldek could bind **Mateusz/GPUcomputer**, not that Waldek is personally liable. If Waldek acted as Mateusz’s apparent representative and Mateusz issued the invoice/accepted the bank transfer, the contract route points to Mateusz. To make Waldemar a contractual co-debtor, you need language showing he contracted as a principal, partner, guarantor, or personal refund obligor, not merely “the company will refund.”

**Evidence needed**

The key is exact wording. Compare:

| Email wording                               | Effect                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “GPUcomputer will refund”                   | Mostly binds Mateusz/GPUcomputer.                                      |
| “We will refund”                            | Supports joint-operation / partnership / apparent co-principal theory. |
| “I will refund you” or “I guarantee refund” | Possible direct Waldemar personal undertaking.                         |
| “3d Kraków / Waldemar Łopata accepts…”      | Much stronger contractual route against Waldemar.                      |

**Probability estimate**

As a standalone 155,000 PLN contract claim against Waldemar: **15–30%** unless the emails contain personal undertaking language. With personal guarantee/refund undertaking: **35–55%**.

### A5. Bad-faith negotiations / deceit / consumer unfair practice — KC arts. 72, 84, 86, 88; unfair market practices act

KC art. 72 § 2 provides damages where a party starts or conducts negotiations contrary to good morals, especially without intending to conclude the contract, causing reliance loss. KC art. 84 covers mistake induced by or known to the other party; art. 86 covers deceit. ([Sip Lex][15])

If you were acting as a consumer, the unfair-market-practices statute may help. A market practice is unfair if contrary to good morals and materially distorts or may distort the average consumer’s transactional decision, and consumers can seek remedies including damages on general principles. ([Sip Lex][16])

**Fit to your facts: useful support, but not the cleanest joint-liability route.**

These theories help frame deception and reliance. They are especially useful if the emails or website misrepresented capacity, supplier control, refund ability, or who operated the business. But they do not automatically solve the Waldemar problem unless Waldemar was the actor applying the practice or part of the “entrepreneur” operation. UOKiK materials also recognize that, where a civil partnership is involved, consumer claims can be directed against the partners, which loops back to the spółka cywilna theory. ([Urząd Ochrony Konkurencji i Konsumentów][17])

**Probability estimate**

As supporting legal framing: **moderate**. As the sole basis for holding Waldemar liable: **weak to moderate**, depending on whether you qualify as a consumer and whether Waldek’s communications were materially misleading.

### A6. Actio pauliana — KC arts. 527–534

**Polish text, key part**

KC art. 527 allows a creditor to seek relative ineffectiveness of a debtor’s legal act made with prejudice to creditors if a third party obtained a financial benefit and knew or could have known of the debtor’s awareness. Where the third party is in a close relationship with the debtor, knowledge is presumed. ([Sip Lex][18])

**Fit to your facts: legally strong if facts emerge; currently speculative.**

This is not currently your best lead claim because you have no identified transfer. It also does **not** make Waldemar a normal co-debtor for the whole 155,000 PLN. It lets you reach assets or rights transferred to him through a prejudicial transaction. It becomes powerful if Mateusz moved inventory, receivables, customer lists, cash, domain assets, equipment, or supplier relationships to Waldemar/3d Kraków after or while creditors were forming.

**Case-law / doctrine signals**

Actio pauliana protects creditors where the debtor’s transaction creates or deepens insolvency; recent SN materials emphasize that prejudice can include making satisfaction harder or delayed, and the assessment can be tied to the status by close of trial. ([Supreme Court][19]) Court-fee law also now makes some pauliańska suits much cheaper if the creditor’s claim is already established by a qualifying final title: art. 13f provides a special fixed-fee route in those circumstances. ([Sip Lex][20])

**Evidence needed**

Specific transfer, date, asset/value, Mateusz’s insolvency before/after, benefit to Waldemar, and close relation/knowledge. A surname inference is useful but not enough; prove kinship or close business relationship.

**Probability estimate**

Now, without transfer evidence: **under 15% as an immediate lawsuit**. With a concrete post-debt transfer to a close relative/business collaborator and evidence Mateusz was insolvent or worsened insolvency: **60–80%**.

### A7. Art. 5 KC / “piercing the veil” / one economic enterprise

KC art. 5 bars exercising a right contrary to its socio-economic purpose or principles of social coexistence. ([Isap][21]) It is not a standalone damages claim. It can help rebut a formalistic defense—“I was just a separate JDG”—if the evidence shows Waldemar deliberately used formal separation to mislead customers. But Polish law does not need “piercing the veil” for JDGs because a JDG has no separate legal personality from the individual. The hard problem is not piercing Waldemar’s JDG; it is proving Waldemar personally owed you a duty or participated in Mateusz’s obligation/tort.

**Fit:** supporting argument only. **Probability as standalone basis:** near zero.

## Section B — The EPU question

### B1. Can you add Waldemar to the pending EPU?

**Practical answer: do not rely on it.**

Ordinary civil procedure has mechanisms for adding or replacing defendants under KPC art. 194, but they require a court order and are designed for ordinary litigation, not the document-light EPU workflow. Art. 194 § 3 allows the court, on the plaintiff’s motion, to call additional persons into the case where the same claim could be brought against them, but joining is not automatic. ([Sip Lex][22])

EPU is a special electronic payment-order procedure for monetary claims, and it has a hard discontinuation architecture: if there are no grounds to issue an order, if the order cannot be effectively served in Poland, or if a defendant files an objection, the EPU proceeding is discontinued in the relevant scope. ([Sip Lex][23])

Your claim against Waldemar is evidence-heavy, legally contested, and likely to require translated emails, website evidence, and witness testimony. That is exactly the opposite of what EPU is good at.

### B2. Filing-date priority

KPC art. 505^37 § 2 gives a preservation rule: if, within three months after EPU discontinuation, the plaintiff files the **same claim against the same defendant** in non-EPU proceedings, the legal effects tied to bringing the action relate back to the EPU filing date. The wording is “against the defendant” and “the same claim,” so it is not a safe route to create a retroactive filing date against Waldemar, who was not an EPU defendant on 2026-04-08. ([Sip Lex][24])

**Result:** the 2026-04-08 EPU date should be preserved only for Mateusz if the statutory conditions are later met. A new Waldemar suit gets its own filing date.

### B3. Fee implications

EPU uses one quarter of the normal court fee. Since the normal fee for a 155,000 PLN property claim is 5%, the ordinary fee is **7,750 PLN** and the EPU fee is about **1,937.50 PLN** before any rounding/portal mechanics. Art. 19 of the court-costs statute states that a quarter fee is charged for EPU, and the EPU fee can be credited toward a new non-EPU claim only in the art. 505^37 § 2 scenario. ([Sip Lex][25])

A separate ordinary claim against Waldemar will normally require its own **7,750 PLN** court fee.

| Keep EPU against Mateusz + file separate Waldemar suit | Best balance. EPU may still generate a fast title; Waldemar case proceeds on evidence-heavy theory without contaminating EPU.                                |

If Waldemar is held liable, the enforcement surface changes materially: Polish-resident individual, active long-running JDG, Kraków address, potential bank accounts, receivables, inventory, equipment, and any personally owned real estate. Your brief identifies exactly this as the strategic reason to add him. 

**Post-Waldemar recovery estimate:** moderate to high **if** liability is established and assets are not moved before security/enforcement. This is the core upside of the strategy.

### D3. Recovery multiplier

A practical recovery multiplier of **2x–4x** is plausible, not because the legal claim is guaranteed, but because a Polish-resident business operator with potentially attachable assets is much easier to enforce against than a debtor whose real life and wages are in the Netherlands.

### D4. Asset-flight risk

The asset-flight risk is real. Waldemar receiving service may trigger movement of bank balances, inventory, or receivables. That is why the Waldemar filing should ideally be paired with a **zabezpieczenie** motion, and the criminal supplement should request consideration of asset security if suspicion rises to the appropriate level.

## Section F — Risks and downsides

### F1. Adverse costs

If you lose against Waldemar, the baseline adverse lawyer-cost exposure is likely **5,400 PLN** plus court costs and possible expert/translation costs, because the claim value is 155,000 PLN. The actual adverse-cost order may vary, but 5,400 PLN is the key tariff number for the 50,000–200,000 PLN bracket. ([Sip Lex][39])

Your sunk costs if you file and lose could be: 7,750 PLN filing fee + your lawyer + translations + possible adverse costs. This is why the full Waldek email review is the gate.

### F2. Defamation / personal-rights / RODO risk

Court filings and criminal reports should be factual, evidence-based, and limited to what is necessary. KC arts. 23–24 protect personal rights such as reputation, and art. 24 allows claims where personal rights are threatened or violated unless the action is lawful. ([Sip Lex][40])

Good-faith court/prosecutor filings are much lower risk than public accusations. Case law indicates that a criminal notification becomes a personal-rights problem mainly where it is knowingly false or clearly baseless. ([Orzeczenia Katowice][41]) For RODO/GDPR, processing personal data for legal claims will usually be framed under legitimate interest / legal-claim necessity, but the data shared should be minimized and kept to lawyer/court/prosecutor channels. ([Sip Lex][42])

### F3. Slowing the EPU

A **separate** Waldemar suit should not slow the Mateusz EPU. Trying to amend the EPU to include Waldemar is the risky move because it may introduce doubts and procedural complexity into a simplified electronic track.

### F4. Counterclaim risk

A counterclaim or separate personal-rights claim by Waldemar is possible but not likely to be strong if your filings are private, factual, sourced, and proportionate. The risk rises if you publish accusations online, contact his customers/employer, or label him a “fraudster” outside court/prosecutor/lawyer channels before judgment.

## Section G — Strategic synthesis

### G1. Clear recommendation

**Recommended path:**

1. **Leave the Mateusz EPU alone.** Do not attempt to add Waldemar there.
2. **Immediately assemble the Waldemar evidence pack**: full email thread, headers, invoice, bank transfer, Wayback/live website captures, CEIDG/Biała Lista outputs, refund promise.
3. **Have a Polish litigator review the emails first.** The go/no-go question is whether Waldek’s language shows personal control, knowledge, inducement, or personal undertaking.
4. **If the email review is favorable, file a separate ordinary suit in Sąd Okręgowy w Krakowie against Waldemar**, led by KC arts. 415/422/441 and supported by alternative implied-spółka/joint-operation/consumer-deception theories.
5. **File a civil security motion if the evidence is strong enough.**
6. **Submit a supplemental criminal filing naming Waldemar** and request investigative acts under KPK art. 315, including email/domain/phone/business-record preservation and consideration of asset security.
7. **Preserve actio pauliana for later** unless you find a concrete transfer now. It becomes much more attractive after a title against Mateusz or once transfer evidence appears.

### G2. Decision matrix

| Scenario             | Recommended move                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mateusz cooperative  | Do not abandon the Waldemar angle until cleared funds are received. Use it as leverage, but prioritize a formal settlement with enforceability—ideally drafted by a Polish lawyer. If Mateusz offers real repayment, escalation against Waldemar can be paused.                                                                                                                       |
| Mateusz silent       | Current state favors escalation. Keep EPU running, prepare/file the Waldemar ordinary suit if emails support tort participation, and seek security. Waiting only helps defendants move assets.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Mateusz contests EPU | Ordinary litigation becomes inevitable. File the same claim against Mateusz in non-EPU within the statutory 3-month window if EPU is discontinued, preserving the EPU-date effects against Mateusz where available. At that point, consider a combined ordinary suit against both or coordinating the Mateusz refile with the Waldemar action. Waldemar still gets a new filing date. |

### G3. Honest litigator-style bottom line

A Polish windykacja litigator would probably **not** tell you to bet the case on an implied spółka cywilna from website co-marketing alone. They would likely say: keep the EPU against Mateusz, translate and analyze the Waldek emails immediately, and—if those emails show Waldek personally negotiated, assured delivery/refund, and had reason to know performance/refund was not realistic—file a separate Kraków suit against Waldemar on tort/joint-tortfeasor grounds with a security motion. If the emails are weaker than your summary suggests, Waldemar should remain a criminal-investigation target and future actio pauliana target rather than a civil co-defendant today.
