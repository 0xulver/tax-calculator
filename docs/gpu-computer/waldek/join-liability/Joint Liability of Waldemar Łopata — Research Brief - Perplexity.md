# Joint Liability of Waldemar Łopata — Research Brief
### Claim: 155,000 PLN | EPU Case Nc-e 552126/26 | May 2026

***

## Executive Summary

This brief analyses whether a Polish court would hold Waldemar Łopata (NIP 7752083995, JDG "WALDEMAR ŁOPATA 3d Kraków") jointly and severally liable for the 155,000 PLN debt arising from a failed GPUcomputer workstation order. It maps six liability theories, the procedural route to add him to (or alongside) the existing EPU proceeding against Mateusz Szklarski-Łopata (Nc-e 552126/26, Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód), and the strategic effect on recovery.

**Bottom line:** Two theories are strong enough to proceed on today's evidence — **art. 422 KC joint tortfeasor liability** (Waldek as participant in the civil fraud) and **de facto spółka cywilna under KC art. 860–864**. A third theory — **actio pauliana (art. 527 KC)** — is not yet evidenced but should be held in reserve. **Subjective amendment of the EPU case is procedurally impossible**; the correct route is a **separate ordinary civil suit (pozew) against Waldemar in Sąd Okręgowy Kraków**, filed concurrently with keeping the EPU running against Mateusz, combined with an immediate **zabezpieczenie roszczenia (art. 730 KPC)** application against Waldemar's JDG assets.

## Section A — Statutory Map of Joint Liability Theories

### A.1 — Hypothesis 1: De Facto / Implied Civil-Law Partnership (KC art. 860–864)

**Statute (Polish text + translation)**

> *Art. 860 § 1 KC:* „Przez umowę spółki wspólnicy zobowiązują się dążyć do osiągnięcia wspólnego celu gospodarczego przez działanie w sposób oznaczony, w szczególności przez wniesienie wkładów."
> ("By a partnership agreement the partners undertake to pursue a common economic goal by acting in a specified manner, in particular by making contributions.")

> *Art. 860 § 2 KC:* „Umowa spółki powinna być stwierdzona pismem." — **ad probationem only** (not ad solemnitatem); an oral or implied agreement is legally valid; the writing requirement affects only evidence.[^1]

> *Art. 864 KC:* „Za zobowiązania spółki wspólnicy odpowiedzialni są solidarnie."
> ("Partners are jointly and severally liable for the obligations of the partnership.")

**Elements to prove**

1. An agreement — express, implied, or inferred from conduct — to pursue a common economic goal.
2. Both parties acted in furtherance of that goal (contributions, shared infrastructure, divided labour).
3. The obligation was incurred in the course of that common pursuit.

**Fit to the facts**

*Strength: Moderate-to-Strong, conditional.*

The case for implied spółka cywilna is built on: (a) the 2016–2026 gpucomputer.pl About-page co-marketing of "3dkrakow" (Waldek) and "GPUCOMPUTER" (Mateusz) as the two arms of one operation; (b) the shared domain, shared mobile number (+48 883 109 779 on both the Konfiguracje label and the main GPUCOMPUTER line), and shared email domain (@gpucomputer.pl); (c) Waldek's decade-long operational role (customer-facing, configuration, refund promises) alongside Mateusz's legal-entity and banking role; and (d) the identical PKD 26.20.Z (computer production) filed by both JDGs.[^2][^1]

The counter-argument is real: the Supreme Court and commentary treat the separate CEIDG registrations as evidence of intent to keep the businesses formally distinct, and Polish doctrine requires a genuine agreement to pursue a *common* goal — not merely an informal collaboration or division of labour between related persons. The 2016/2018/2019 About-pages listing *separate* addresses for 3dkrakow and GPUCOMPUTER is double-edged evidence: it shows public co-marketing, but it also shows the parties distinguished their units.[^2]

**The decisive question:** did Mateusz and Waldemar share the commercial proceeds of GPUcomputer orders, or were the two JDGs merely complementary service providers? If Waldek's 3dkrakow only received payment for its own separate hardware work (3D printing, etc.) and GPUcomputer received payment for the CPU/GPU builds, a court could find functional complementarity rather than a common enterprise. If the gpucomputer.pl storefront pooled revenue and they divided profits, the spółka cywilna theory becomes very strong.

**Evidence needed beyond current holdings**

- Bank records (requires prosecutor's access to JPK files or subpoena — not available in civil pre-trial) showing revenue routing.
- Invoice flows between 3dkrakow and GPUCOMPUTER, or to the same end customers.
- Any written communication between Waldek and Mateusz referring to "our" business, common accounts, or shared profits.

**Key SN rulings**

- SN IV CSK 469/18 (18 April 2019): addressed proper denomination of spółka cywilna in civil proceedings; partnership can exist even without formal registration.[^3]
- SN I CSK 428/10 (28 April 2011): partners of spółka cywilna are solidarily liable for debts; liability is not subsidiary, meaning creditor can proceed directly against personal assets of any partner.[^4]
- SA Szczecin I ACa 106/13 (24 April 2013): confirmed art. 860 § 2 as ad probationem, not ad solemnitatem; an implied partnership can be proven by conduct.[^5]

**Probability of acceptance by Kraków court:** 35–50%. The theory is legally sound but fact-intensive, and courts apply it cautiously where parties maintained separate CEIDG entries. It is significantly stronger if evidence of shared revenue can be developed; without it, the tort theory below is more reliable.

### A.2 — Hypothesis 2: Joint Tortfeasor / Participation Liability (KC art. 422 + art. 441)

**Statute**

> *Art. 422 KC:* „Za szkodę odpowiedzialny jest nie tylko ten, kto ją bezpośrednio wyrządził, lecz także ten, kto inną osobę do wyrządzenia szkody nakłonił albo był jej pomocny, jak również ten, kto świadomie skorzystał z wyrządzonej drugiemu szkody."
> ("Liability for damage rests not only on the party who caused it directly, but also on a person who induced another to cause it, aided in it, or who knowingly derived benefit from the damage caused to another.")

> *Art. 441 § 1 KC:* „Jeżeli kilka osób ponosi odpowiedzialność za szkodę wyrządzoną czynem niedozwolonym, ich odpowiedzialność jest solidarna."
> ("Where several persons bear liability for damage caused by a tort, their liability is joint and several.")

> *Art. 415 KC:* „Kto z winy swej wyrządził drugiemu szkodę, obowiązany jest do jej naprawienia."
> ("A party who causes damage to another through their own fault is obliged to repair it.")

**Elements to prove**

1. A civil tort (delikt) by the primary wrongdoer (Mateusz) — here: fraudulent or grossly negligent failure to deliver, combined with the contractual relationship.
2. Waldemar's participation: (a) aiding (pomocnictwo) — conscious facilitation; or (b) inducement (nakłonienie); or (c) knowing beneficiary (świadome skorzystanie).
3. **For aiding specifically:** Waldemar must have acted with at least *dolus eventualis* — he was aware his conduct could help bring about the harmful event and accepted that risk.[^6][^7]
4. Causal link between Waldek's participation and the damage (does not require a *necessary* (sine qua non) causal link — it is sufficient that his conduct *facilitated* the harm).[^8]
5. The damage and its quantum (155,000 PLN prepayment).

**Fit to the facts**

*Strength: Strong, on current evidence.*

This is the most powerful available theory given the email correspondence. The chain runs as follows:

- Waldek negotiated the order specifications, set and extended delivery deadlines, and made the explicit written refund promise — all using the waldek@gpucomputer.pl address while the formal contract was in Mateusz's name.
- Waldek had full operational knowledge of inventory capacity and supplier status. If he continued to promise delivery and a refund while knowing delivery was not going to happen, that is conscious facilitation of the fraud-by-omission theory developed in the criminal track.
- The tort under art. 415 KC maps precisely onto the civil-fraud theory (concealment of an inability to perform, SN V KK 240/17 framing applies as analogical civil framing).
- A helpers' conscious understanding of the consequences does not require *intent* to harm the plaintiff — only awareness that the conduct facilitated the harmful act.[^6]

**Critical Supreme Court ruling (2025):** SN II CSKP 607/23 (3 July 2025) — the Supreme Court confirmed that art. 422 KC imposes liability on helpers (*pomocnicy*) who act with either direct intent (*dolus directus*) or conditional intent (*dolus eventualis*). It further held that between the helper's conduct and the harm, a strict "but-for" causal chain is not required; it is sufficient that the helper's action *actually facilitated* the commission of the tort. This directly supports the theory here.[^8]

**SA Gdańsk I ACa 39/15 (21 May 2015):** confirmed that showing the helper's conduct "created the conditions for the harm" is sufficient — the plaintiff need not prove that without the help the harm would definitely not have occurred.[^6]

**SN I CSK 559/10 (21 June 2011):** a helper must have *consciously aided* the primary wrongdoer; mere facilitation without awareness is insufficient. The email thread showing Waldek *wrote* the refund promise satisfies this threshold.[^7][^6]

**Evidence strength assessment**

| Evidence element | Currently available | Needs development |
|---|---|---|
| Waldek negotiated order | Yes (emails) | — |
| Waldek set delivery deadlines | Yes (emails) | — |
| Waldek made refund promise | Yes (emails — decisive) | — |
| Waldek had operational knowledge | Strongly inferrable (17yr JDG, PKD 26.20.Z, "Konfiguracje" role) | Bank/supplier records would strengthen |
| Waldek knew delivery was impossible | Inferrable from sequence of failed promises | Email chain timeline; any WhatsApp/SMS |
| Waldek benefited financially | Unknown | Bank records, shared accounting |

**Probability of acceptance by Kraków court: 55–70%** if the email thread is as described. The key risk is whether the court characterises Waldek's conduct as negligent delay-management (not tortious, only Mateusz's contractual breach) rather than conscious participation in a fraudulent scheme. The criminal-track framing (art. 286 KK *współsprawstwo*) is relevant because SN II CSKP 607/23 reaffirmed that a criminal conviction for co-perpetration, once binding under art. 11 KPC, eliminates the need to re-litigate the civil helper's role. However, without a criminal conviction, the civil court makes its own factual determination.

**Note on the "knowing beneficiary" sub-theory:** Under art. 422 KC, a person who *knowingly derived benefit* from the harm is also liable. If Waldek received any commission, inventory, or customer redirects from the failed GPUcomputer orders (e.g., customers sold 3dkrakow products instead), this sub-theory applies independently of whether he "aided" in the classic sense.[^9]

### A.3 — Hypothesis 3: Civil Consequence of Criminal Co-Perpetration

**Statute**

> *Art. 18 § 1 KK:* „Odpowiada za sprawstwo nie tylko ten, kto wykonuje czyn zabroniony sam albo wspólnie i w porozumieniu z inną osobą..."
> (Co-perpetration of a criminal offence.)

> *Art. 46 § 1 KK:* „W razie skazania sąd może orzec [...] obowiązek naprawienia, w całości albo w części, wyrządzonej przestępstwem szkody..."
> (Criminal restitution measure.)

> *Art. 11 KPC:* Findings of a binding criminal conviction bind the civil court as to commission of the offence.

**Fit to the facts**

*Strength: Moderate, contingent on criminal investigation progress.*

If the criminal complaint currently naming Mateusz under art. 286 KK is extended to include Waldemar as a co-perpetrator or accessory, and if the prosecutor proceeds to indictment, then: (a) the court can issue an art. 46 KK restitution order against Waldemar in the criminal proceeding, providing a fast enforcement title without separate civil proceedings; and (b) any criminal conviction binds the civil court under art. 11 KPC, eliminating need to re-prove the core factual elements in the civil case.[^8]

**Procedural route for expanding the criminal complaint**

A *pokrzywdzony* (victim with standing in the criminal proceeding) can file a supplementary complaint (*uzupełnienie zawiadomienia o przestępstwie*) naming Waldemar as a suspected co-perpetrator at any point before the prosecutor closes the investigation phase. The threshold for the prosecutor to interrogate Waldemar as a *suspect* (rather than a *witness*) under art. 71–74 KPK is "reasonable suspicion" (uzasadnione podejrzenie), which is a low evidential bar. The email evidence (Waldek negotiated, promised refund, had operational knowledge) likely meets this threshold.

**Risk:** If Waldemar is interrogated as a suspect, he is protected by the right to silence (art. 175 KPK) and may retain counsel. This is not a downside — it prevents him from testifying as a mere witness (under oath), which could otherwise be strategically useful to the defence.

**Probability of criminal extension leading to conviction of Waldemar:** Low-to-Moderate (30–40%) without the prosecutor accessing his bank records, because the operational evidence is circumstantial rather than direct. However, even without conviction, the supplementary complaint creates investigative pressure and may prompt settlement.

### A.4 — Hypothesis 4: Apparent Authority / Ostensible Principalship

**Statute**

> *Art. 97 KC:* „Osobę czynną w lokalu przedsiębiorstwa przeznaczonym do obsługiwania publiczności poczytuje się w razie wątpliwości za umocowaną do dokonywania czynności prawnych, które zazwyczaj bywają dokonywane z osobami korzystającymi z usług tego przedsiębiorstwa."
> ("A person acting in the business premises of an enterprise serving the public is presumed, in case of doubt, to be authorised to perform legal acts customarily performed with persons using services of that enterprise.")

**Fit to the facts**

*Strength: Weak as a standalone theory; moderate as a supplementary argument.*

Art. 97 KC creates a presumption in favour of the customer, binding the *enterprise* (Mateusz's JDG) for acts done by its "active person" (Waldek). This theory makes **Mateusz** additionally bound by Waldek's acts (delivery promises, refund commitments), but does not itself impose liability *on Waldek* as a co-debtor.[^10][^11][^12]

However, the reverse question — whether Waldek can be held as a *contractual co-principal* because the About-page listed him as co-head of the operation — is possible under a theory of **apparent joint enterprise** (*pozorny współprzedsiębiorca*). Polish doctrine does not have a formal "piercing the veil" doctrine for JDGs (since a JDG has no veil — the owner is personally liable for all JDG debts), but if a court accepts that Waldek held himself out as a co-operator of gpucomputer.pl, it can hold that the 155,000 PLN order was transacted with both Waldek and Mateusz, and that Waldek is a contractual co-debtor. This is essentially the spółka cywilna argument restated at the contractual level.

**Practical value:** Art. 97 KC strengthens the argument that Mateusz cannot later claim Waldek acted without authority. It supports the art. 422 theory rather than creating independent liability.

### A.5 — Hypothesis 5: Actio Pauliana (KC art. 527 ff.)

**Statute**

> *Art. 527 § 1 KC:* „Gdy wskutek czynności prawnej dłużnika dokonanej z pokrzywdzeniem wierzycieli osoba trzecia uzyskała korzyść majątkową, każdy z wierzycieli może żądać uznania tej czynności za bezskuteczną w stosunku do niego..."
> ("Where a legal act performed by the debtor to the detriment of creditors results in a third party obtaining a financial benefit, any creditor may demand that the act be declared ineffective against them...")

> *Art. 527 § 3 KC (presumption for close persons):* „Jeżeli wskutek czynności prawnej dłużnika dokonanej z pokrzywdzeniem wierzycieli uzyskała korzyść majątkową osoba będąca w bliskim z nim stosunku, domniemywa się, że osoba ta wiedziała, iż dłużnik działał ze świadomością pokrzywdzenia wierzycieli."
> ("If a person in a close relationship with the debtor obtains the benefit, it is presumed that person knew the debtor was acting with awareness of harming creditors.")[^13][^14]

> *Art. 527 § 4 KC (presumption for regular business partners):* Extended presumption of knowledge also applies to entrepreneurs in ongoing commercial relations with the debtor.[^13]

> *Art. 534 KC:* Five-year limitation period from the date of the act.

**Fit to the facts**

*Strength: Speculative now; potentially very strong if asset transfers can be evidenced.*

The actio pauliana theory requires a specific *legal act* (czynność prawna) by Mateusz transferring an asset to Waldemar that caused Mateusz's insolvency or worsened it. Without that specific act, there is no pauliana claim. The current asset-mapping has not identified such a transfer.[^15][^13]

However, three factors make this theory worth holding in reserve:

1. **Presumption of knowledge:** Waldemar is almost certainly in a "close relationship" (bliski stosunek) with Mateusz as a Łopata-side family member (father/uncle/elder brother). The art. 527 § 3 KC presumption would automatically apply — the creditor need not prove Waldemar knew about the fraudulent intent. Additionally, as a regular business partner, art. 527 § 4 KC offers a parallel presumption.[^14][^16][^13]
2. **Five-year window:** Any asset transfer by Mateusz to Waldemar since 2021 is within the limitation period (art. 534 KC). Given that GPUcomputer became increasingly thin as a business while 3dkrakow remained active, transfers of customers, inventory, or revenue are plausible.
3. **Low procedural barrier:** An actio pauliana claim can be filed at any time — before or after the EPU nakaz, before or after komornik enforcement. The plaintiff in pauliana proceedings is the creditor, not the debtor. The defendant is Waldemar (the third-party beneficiary), not Mateusz.

**SN rulings on actio pauliana**

- SN I CSK 323/12 (15 February 2013): the protected claim must exist at the time of the fraudulent act, the time of filing, and the time of the court's final decision.[^15]
- SN V CSK 454/14 (29 May 2015): the debtor's "awareness of harming creditors" does not require intent — awareness of the *possibility* of harm is sufficient.[^15]

**Probability:** Conditional — 70% if evidence of a specific asset transfer can be obtained; 0% without it.

**Timing recommendation:** Do not file pauliana now. Commission Phase 9 (the historical Kazimierza Wielkiego 36/3 address mapping) and, after obtaining the EPU nakaz, instruct the komornik to pursue *zlecenie poszukiwania majątku* (art. 801² KPC) against Mateusz. If the komornik finds that assets moved to 3dkrakow, file pauliana against Waldemar within the five-year window from those transfers.[^17][^18]

### A.6 — Hypothesis 6: Supplementary Theories

**Art. 415 KC + Art. 5 KC (abuse of right)**

Waldemar relying on CEIDG separation to disclaim liability while publicly co-marketing the joint operation and personally making refund promises could constitute abuse of a right (*nadużycie prawa podmiotowego*) under art. 5 KC. In practice, art. 5 is a defensive tool (it prevents a party from exercising a right that conflicts with zasady współżycia społecznego), not an independent cause of action. It would be raised in the context of, e.g., Waldemar arguing formal non-privity as a defence to the tort claim. The court could use art. 5 to reject that formalistic defence — but it does not replace the need for a substantive theory (art. 422 or spółka cywilna).

**Art. 97 KC re "active person in commercial premises"**

Waldek's role as the customer-facing operator of gpucomputer.pl satisfies the "osoba czynna" criterion. This binds Mateusz for Waldek's acts under the contract (strengthening the contractual claim and any art. 472 KC negligent performance claim against Mateusz) but, as noted above, does not independently bind Waldek.[^11][^19]

**Art. 84 KC (deceit / error induced by fraud)**

Art. 84 KC and art. 88 KC allow annulment of a contract made under fraudulent inducement. The remedy is against the contracting party (Mateusz), not against a third-party operator. There is no viable route under these articles to impose liability *on Waldek* as a contractual co-defendant. They are relevant only for the alternative theory of rescinding the contract and claiming restitution from Mateusz.

**UOKiK / Consumer protection (Ustawa o ochronie praw konsumenta)**

UOKiK enforcement is administrative and aims at injunctions and fines against the "przedsiębiorca" responsible for the unfair practice. It does not generate a private civil judgment directly awarding 155,000 PLN to the individual consumer. A UOKiK complaint is useful as a parallel pressure track but is not an enforcement vehicle for the specific claim.

**"Piercing the veil" between two JDGs**

There is no statutory "przebicie zasłony" doctrine in Polish law for JDG entities. A JDG is not a separate legal person — the owner is the enterprise (they are the same legal subject), so there is nothing to pierce. The question is not whether assets of the JDG can be reached (they can, automatically), but whether Waldemar's personal assets can be reached for Mateusz's debts. That is precisely what art. 864 KC (spółka cywilna) or art. 422 KC (tort) achieves.

## Section B — The EPU Question

### B.1 — Can a Co-Defendant be Added to the Pending EPU?

**Short answer: No. Subjective amendment (dopozwanie) is incompatible with EPU procedure.**

The EPU regime operates through Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód (the e-court) exclusively on electronic filings. Art. 505²⁸ KPC incorporates the general provisions of the civil procedure code, *with explicit exclusions*. The 2015 and 2020 KPC amendments established that EPU **cannot be combined with any other separate procedure (postępowanie odrębne) at any stage**, and that all pleadings must pass through the e-court's IT system.[^20][^21]

Art. 194 § 3 KPC (adding a permissive co-defendant to the same claim) requires a court that can hear evidence, hold a hearing, and issue a reasoned order — none of which are possible within the e-court's paperless, no-evidence format. Adding Waldemar — a new party with his own domicile (Kraków), his own legal identity, and a distinct legal basis for liability — would require the e-court to: (i) serve him physically, (ii) determine whether he has a ground to be joined, and (iii) potentially change the competent court (Kraków, not Lublin). The electronic system cannot do any of these.[^22][^23][^24]

**Confirmed by doctrine:** Commentary on art. 50²⁸ KPC confirms that the legislator "explicitly excluded EPU from any combination with other separate procedures or with provisions requiring hearings, physical documents, or multi-party dynamics not reducible to the electronic format."[^20]

### B.2 — What Happens if You Try?

If you were to submit a subjective amendment motion through the e-court portal, the e-court would either:
- Reject it as formally impermissible and return it; or
- Transfer the entire case (art. 50²⁸ § 2 in conjunction with art. 50²⁶ KPC) to the court of general jurisdiction — which is Mateusz's general jurisdiction court (his registered address in Kraków would have been the transfer destination under old rules; post-2020 the e-court should dismiss rather than transfer in this scenario).

**Neither outcome is desirable.**

### B.3 — Filing-Date Priority and Lis Pendens

The EPU filing date **2026-04-08** creates lis pendens (zawisłość sprawy) and starts the limitation period running for the specific claim against Mateusz. That date priority is preserved for the Mateusz claim under art. 192 KPC. It does **not** create lis pendens against Waldemar because Waldemar is not a party to the EPU proceeding.[^23]

**Critical 2020 rule (art. 50²⁸ KPC as amended):** If EPU is discontinued (*umorzone*) and within three months a new pozew is filed against the same defendant in ordinary procedure, the new filing date relates back to the EPU filing date. This **does not** help with adding Waldemar — it only preserves priority for the Mateusz claim.[^21]

### B.4 — Court Fee on Transfer

The EPU fee is 1.25% of 155,000 PLN = **1,937.50 PLN** (already paid). If the EPU is converted to ordinary procedure (e.g., because Mateusz files a sprzeciw), the additional fee of 3.75% (from 1.25% to 5%) — approximately **5,812.50 PLN** — becomes payable. That top-up is owed *after* the transfer, not on filing.[^24][^25][^26]

For the *separate* Waldemar action, the full 5% court fee applies from the outset: 5% × 155,000 PLN = **7,750 PLN**.

## Section C — Parallel Suit Against Waldemar

### C.1 — Jurisdiction and Court

- Value of claim: 155,000 PLN > 75,000 PLN threshold under art. 17 pkt 4 KPC.[^25]
- **Competent court: Sąd Okręgowy w Krakowie, Wydział Cywilny (or Wydział Gospodarczy if Waldemar opposes the case as a business matter).**
- Territorial jurisdiction: Waldemar's registered address Św. Filipa 23/4, 31-150 Kraków (art. 27 KPC — defendant's domicile/principal place of business).[^27]

### C.2 — Pozew Requirements and Court Fee

- Full court fee: **7,750 PLN** (5% × 155,000 PLN), payable on filing.[^26][^25]
- Attachments: printed copies of email correspondence (in original Polish; if mixed-language, sworn Polish translation of the English portions at 44–130 PLN/page), screenshots of gpucomputer.pl/kontakt and /o-nas pages (authenticated), CEIDG extracts for both JDGs, bank transfer receipt confirming the 155,000 PLN payment to Mateusz.
- The pozew must be signed by either the plaintiff personally (filing pro se is permitted in Polish civil procedure) or by a Polish lawyer (adwokat or radca prawny) holding a written pełnomocnictwo — you cannot appear pro se in Sąd Okręgowy without a lawyer for trial-stage hearings, though you can file the initial pozew yourself.
- **Language:** The court operates only in Polish. All exhibits in other languages require sworn (przysięgłe) translations.[^28]

### C.3 — Service

Waldemar is a Polish resident registered at Św. Filipa 23/4 Kraków. Service will be domestic postal service (art. 131–135 KPC), which is generally reliable and fast (1–2 weeks). No special cross-border service regime applies. If Waldemar does not accept delivery, double-*awizacja* (two failed attempts) constitutes effective service under current rules.

### C.4 — Connection of Cases (Art. 219 KPC)

Art. 219 KPC allows a court to join two related cases for joint trial if economy of procedure justifies it. Once the EPU against Mateusz is converted to ordinary procedure (either by sprzeciw or by voluntary withdrawal and re-filing), the Mateusz case would transfer to the competent ordinary court (almost certainly Sąd Okręgowy Kraków, given his registered address). At that point, a motion under art. 219 KPC to join the Waldemar Kraków case with the converted Mateusz case before the same division is procedurally clean and strategically advantageous: one trial, one set of witnesses, one set of legal fees for Waldemar's appearance, and a single judgment establishing joint and several liability.[^29]

**Pros of joinder:** Single comprehensive judgment; cost efficiency; avoids inconsistent findings on the same factual matrix; the email evidence goes in once.

**Cons of joinder:** The more complex multi-party case will take longer; Mateusz's potential non-appearance (Netherlands resident) could slow the joint proceeding; risks that Waldemar's stronger Polish-resident defence complicates the Mateusz claim.

### C.5 — Criminal Zabezpieczenie Majątkowe and Waldemar

If the criminal complaint is extended to include Waldemar as a co-suspect (art. 286 KK), the prosecutor can apply for *zabezpieczenie majątkowe* under art. 291–295 KPK, freezing Waldemar's JDG bank accounts, inventory, and identified real property. This is the most powerful early-stage protective measure available, because it requires no hearing and operates ex parte. However, it depends on the prosecutor accepting Waldemar's co-suspect status — which requires filing the supplementary complaint first.

## Section D — Effect on Realistic Recovery

### D.1 — Pre-Waldemar Outcome (Mateusz Only)

| Enforcement route | Asset targeted | Estimated reach |
|---|---|---|
| Polish bank accounts | Mateusz's Biała Lista entries | Unknown; likely thin |
| EAPO (Reg. 655/2014) | Wages at Sims Lifecycle Services, Son en Breugel | Legally strong; Dutch bank must freeze |
| European Enforcement Order (EEO) | Any future Dutch/EU assets | Medium-term option |
| Komornik zlecenie poszukiwania majątku | All domestic registries | 100 PLN fee; standard tool[^30][^31] |

EAPO against Mateusz's Dutch wages is the most realistic immediate cross-border enforcement route. ### D.3 — Risk: Waldemar Transfers His Own Assets

If Waldemar receives service of the pozew and his asset position is good, he may transfer assets in anticipation of enforcement. This is the mirror risk to the Mateusz situation. Two countermeasures:

1. **Art. 730 KPC zabezpieczenie roszczenia:** File the application simultaneously with or *before* the pozew (art. 730 § 2 KPC allows pre-action applications). The application requires only *uprawdopodobnienie* (prima facie showing) of the claim — not proof. The email evidence and public co-marketing materials are sufficient. 2. **File the zabezpieczenie application immediately, before the pozew** — this is the recommended sequence.

**Key note:** A zabezpieczenie before the pozew must be followed by the pozew within two weeks (art. 733 KPC), otherwise the court will lift the freeze automatically. Co-ordinate lawyer engagement to file both documents in quick succession.

## Section F — Risks and Downsides

### F.1 — Adverse Cost Rule

If the Waldemar claim fails entirely (e.g., court finds no spółka cywilna and insufficient evidence of art. 422 participation), he recovers legal costs from you. The statutory tariff for a claim of 155,000 PLN is approximately **5,400–7,200 PLN** in advocate's fees (pursuant to the current scale under *Rozporządzenie Ministra Sprawiedliwości w sprawie opłat za czynności adwokackie*). His actual lawyer costs may be higher but courts typically award tariff rates unless exceptional circumstances apply.[^27][^28]

**Net maximum adverse cost (all-in, if you lose): ~25,000–35,000 PLN** (Waldemar's court fee reimbursement + lawyer fees + your own lawyer fees for the Waldemar case). This is material but is significantly less than 155,000 PLN.

### F.2 — RODO / Defamation Exposure

Naming Waldemar in a court filing as a co-fraudster is fully privileged within the proceedings under Polish doctrine of procedural immunity. There is no art. 24 KC *naruszenie dóbr osobistych* exposure for statements made in a pleading before a Polish court.[^39][^40]

**Pre-filing communications are different:** Any written pre-litigation demand letter to Waldemar (a wezwanie do zapłaty) that accuses him of fraud — especially if sent to third parties — could theoretically support a *naruszenie dóbr osobistych* claim. The safe protocol is to send a brief factual demand to Waldemar (referencing the email correspondence, the refund promise, and the unpaid 155,000 PLN) without characterising his conduct as "fraud" in writing. Keep fraud characterisation to the pozew and any criminal complaint.

### F.3 — Effect on EPU from Waldemar Filing

Filing a separate ordinary suit against Waldemar has **no procedural side-effect** on the EPU case against Mateusz. The two proceedings are independent. Lis pendens (art. 192 KPC) prevents the same claim against the same defendant from running in two courts simultaneously — but Waldemar and Mateusz are different defendants, so there is no lis pendens conflict.

There is a theoretical risk that, if the EPU e-court is made aware of the Kraków ordinary proceeding (through Mateusz's sprzeciw mentioning the existence of the Waldemar case), the e-court could decide the EPU claim is "connected" and transfer the case to Kraków for joinder. In practice this is uncommon, and you can mitigate it by not referencing the Waldemar case in EPU communications.

### F.4 — Counter-Claim Risk

A Waldemar counter-claim for *naruszenie dóbr osobistych* (art. 24 KC) is possible in theory — being named as a co-fraudster is harmful to reputation. In practice:
- The claim would need to allege that the pozew's factual assertions are *false* and filed in bad faith. If the email evidence is as described (Waldek made the refund promise), the assertion is factually grounded, and a good-faith litigation position protects against this.
- The litigation privilege doctrine (*immunitariusz procesowy*) is not formally codified in Polish law but is recognised in case law — statements made in judicial proceedings in pursuit of a legitimate claim are not defamatory.[^39]
- **Risk level: Low,** provided the pozew accurately reflects the documented evidence and does not include inflammatory language beyond what is legally material.
