## Hypotheses to test

I am not a Polish lawyer and may be mis-naming offences and remedies. Test
each against the actual statutes and current case law, not the labels.

### Hypothesis 1 — Implied / de facto spółka cywilna (KC art. 860–875)

Mateusz and Waldemar may have formed a **civil-law partnership** by conduct,
even without a written partnership agreement. They share a domain, share a
mobile number, jointly market the same workstations and servers, and divide
labour publicly (Mateusz = owner / banking party; Waldek = configurations /
customer-facing). Under art. 864 KC the partners of a spółka cywilna are
**solidarily liable** for partnership obligations — the creditor can sue
either one and obtain full satisfaction from either's personal assets.

**Counter:** They each maintained a separate JDG with its own NIP and own
PKD filings. The 2016/2018/2019 About pages list two **separate addresses**
for the two brands. Polish courts have been cautious about inferring spółka
cywilna where parties carefully maintained separate CEIDG entries — the
separate registrations are themselves evidence of intent NOT to form a
partnership. But "spółka cywilna" does not require registration; it requires
agreement (art. 860 KC) and that agreement can be implied from conduct.

**Test:** what is the Polish case law on inferring a spółka cywilna from
public co-marketing + shared infrastructure + divided labour where both
participants kept separate CEIDG registrations? Sąd Najwyższy rulings? Glosy?
Commentary by Pietrzykowski, Gniewek, or Osajda?

### Hypothesis 2 — Joint tortfeasor liability (KC art. 422 + art. 441)

If the underlying conduct that caused my damage was a **tort (delikt)** —
i.e., civil fraud, deceit, breach of the duty of good faith in contracting
(art. 72 § 2 KC) — and Waldemar **participated, aided, induced, or knowingly
took advantage** of that tort, then under art. 422 KC he is co-liable, and
under art. 441 KC their liability is **solidary**.

This is the strongest theory on the facts I have:

- Waldek was the one negotiating the order specs
- Waldek issued the delivery promises
- Waldek wrote the explicit refund promise
- Waldek had operational knowledge of supplier capacity and inventory
- The fraud-by-omission theory developed in the criminal-law track (concealing
  the uninsured-foreign-supplier risk, concealing pre-existing inability to
  perform — SN V KK 240/17 framing) maps onto a civil tort under art. 415
  KC; art. 422 KC then makes any participant a joint tortfeasor

**Test:** is "I knew about the failed orders / lack of stock and told the
customer to wait anyway" enough conduct for art. 422 KC participation
liability? What does the case law require: actual aid in the deception?
Inducing? Knowing benefit? What is the evidentiary threshold compared to
criminal *współsprawstwo* under art. 18 § 1 KK?

### Hypothesis 3 — Civil consequence of criminal co-perpetration

If a Polish prosecutor were to extend the art. 286 KK fraud investigation
to include Waldemar as a co-perpetrator (*współsprawca*, art. 18 § 1 KK) on
the same facts, an eventual conviction against Waldemar would carry an art.
46 KK *środek kompensacyjny* (restitution measure) joint with Mateusz, and a
civil judgment against Waldemar under res judicata of the criminal verdict.

**Test:** procedurally, can a *pokrzywdzony* expand the scope of an existing
criminal complaint to add a second suspect mid-investigation? What is the
threshold for the prosecutor to interrogate Waldemar as a suspect rather
than a witness on these facts? Does that change if the email evidence
shows Waldemar made the refund promise?

### Hypothesis 4 — Apparent authority / agency by estoppel

If Polish law recognises **apparent authority** (*pełnomocnictwo dorozumiane*
or *odpowiedzialność za rzekomego pełnomocnika*, KC art. 97 / art. 103 / art.
105), then customers who reasonably believed Waldek was acting on behalf of
GPUcomputer could bind Mateusz (the principal). But that route makes **only
Mateusz** liable, not both. The interesting inverse is: if Waldek represented
himself as a principal of the joint operation (which the gpucomputer.pl About
pages effectively did, listing him as one of the two business heads with
3dkrakow), can the customer hold him personally on the contract under a
theory of joint principalship or *przedsiębiorca de facto*?

**Test:** is there any route by which Waldek becomes a contractual co-debtor
rather than only a tortious co-defendant?

### Hypothesis 5 — Skarga pauliańska / actio pauliana (KC art. 527 ff.)

If Mateusz, foreseeing the failed-delivery wave, routed inventory, revenue,
customer relationships, or any other asset of value to Waldemar's "3d Kraków"
JDG to put it out of creditor reach, **actio pauliana** allows me to attack
that transfer and force satisfaction of my claim out of the transferred
asset, even though Waldemar is a third party. The standard requires:

1. Existence of my claim (yes — pre-court demand, EPU)
2. A transfer (legal act) by the debtor that benefits a third party
3. The transfer caused insolvency or worsened it
4. Debtor's awareness it harmed creditors
5. Third party's knowledge of the harm — *presumed* if the third party is a
   *bliska osoba* (close person), which a Łopata-side relative would be

**Counter:** I have no concrete evidence of an asset transfer between the
two JDGs yet. Phase 8 closed at "sufficient evidence" for identification but
did not run the bank-trail or invoice analysis that would surface specific
transfers. This theory is currently speculative on facts but very strong on
law if facts emerge.

**Test:** what would a Polish lawyer recommend as the right time to commission
the actio pauliana track? After EPU nakaz? After komornik bezskutecznosć
(unsuccessful enforcement) is documented? Or in parallel from the start?

### Hypothesis 6 — Anything else

Specifically I want flagged:
