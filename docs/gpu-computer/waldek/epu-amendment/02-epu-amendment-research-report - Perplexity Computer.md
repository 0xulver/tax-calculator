## 1. Subjective Amendment in EPU — Is It Even Allowed?

**Short answer: No. EPU does not permit subjective amendment (zmiana podmiotowa) — adding a second defendant via the e-sad.gov.pl portal is not procedurally available. Any attempt functions as a trigger for dismissal (umorzenie) and transfer to ordinary procedure.**

### Controlling statutory framework

EPU is governed by art. 505²⁸–505³⁹ KPC. The key provisions are:

- **Art. 505²⁸ § 1 KPC** applies the general payment-order proceedings rules (postępowanie upominawcze) with EPU-specific derogations.
- **Art. 505²⁸ § 2 KPC** provides that a nakaz zapłaty *cannot* be issued if: (1) the claim is non-monetary, or (2) service on the defendant would have to be effected outside Poland ("doręczenie pozwanemu nakazu miałoby nastąpić poza granicami kraju").
- **Art. 505²⁹ § 1 KPC** states that the provisions on separate proceedings other than those listed in art. 505²⁸ § 1 do *not* apply in EPU. This exclusion is broadly interpreted to exclude provisions on amendment of claims.
- **Art. 505³¹ § 1 KPC** mandates that *all* plaintiff filings in EPU be made exclusively via the teleinformatic system ("Powód wnosi pisma wyłącznie za pośrednictwem systemu teleinformatycznego").
- **Art. 505³³ KPC**: if there is no basis for issuing a nakaz, the court dismisses (umarza) the proceeding.

### The doctrinal position on zmiana podmiotowa in EPU

The overwhelming doctrinal consensus — confirmed in legal commentary (Góra-Błaszczykowska, Flaga-Gieruszyńska) and in lower court practice — is that **EPU does not permit subjective amendment of the pozew**. The reasoning is threefold:

1. **Structural incompatibility:** EPU is a closed, highly automated procedure with no hearing, no evidentiary stage, and no room for post-filing positional changes. Art. 505²⁹ § 1 KPC excludes "przepisy o postępowaniach odrębnych" and is interpreted to exclude art. 193 KPC (zmiana powództwa) and art. 194–198 KPC (joinder mechanics) entirely. The e-sad.gov.pl portal has no UI function to add a co-defendant to an open case; the portal only supports filing the initial pozew, accessing case files, and (for defendants) filing a sprzeciw.

2. **Technical impossibility:** Art. 505³¹ § 1 KPC mandates electronic filing for all plaintiff communications. There is no portal form for "amendment adding defendant." Attempting to submit a paper amendment to the Lublin court would be procedurally defective under art. 505³¹.

3. **Transfer as the systemic alternative:** Art. 505³³ KPC provides that when there is no basis (brak podstaw) to issue a nakaz zapłaty, the court *dismisses* (umarza) the proceeding. Any attempt at subjective amendment would be processed, if at all, as a disclosure that the proceeding is unsuitable for EPU — which triggers dismissal under art. 505³³ KPC rather than a formal amendment.

### The three available paths

If subjective amendment in EPU is not possible, the options are:

**(a) Cofnięcie pozwu in EPU (voluntary withdrawal) + refile as ordinary suit naming both defendants.** The plaintiff withdraws the EPU pozew (via the portal), receives a partial fee credit under art. 79 UKSC (see Section 4), then files a new ordinary suit at Sąd Rejonowy or Sąd Okręgowy Kraków naming both Mateusz and Waldemar.

**(b) Wait for the EPU to collapse under its own procedural weight** (foreign defendant issue, see Sections 2 and 9), after which the proceeding is dismissed under art. 505³³ or art. 505³⁴ § 2 KPC, and the plaintiff re-files in ordinary procedure within the 3-month window of art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC naming both defendants.

**(c) Wait for nakaz against Mateusz → let sprzeciw trigger dismissal (art. 505³⁶ KPC) → then re-file in ordinary procedure naming both.** Slowest, and dependent on Mateusz actually filing a sprzeciw.

**Path (b) is very likely to occur automatically** given Mateusz's Netherlands address — see Section 2.

## 2. What Triggers Transfer Out of EPU to Ordinary Procedure?

EPU does not "transfer" to ordinary court in the classical art. 200 KPC sense. Instead, EPU **dismisses** (umarza) the proceeding and the plaintiff re-files. The mechanisms are:

### A. Art. 505³³ KPC — Court finds no basis for nakaz

"W przypadku braku podstaw do wydania nakazu zapłaty sąd umarza postępowanie."

If the EPU court determines that any of the art. 505²⁸ § 2 conditions are met — including that the defendant cannot be served in Poland — *it must dismiss*, not transfer. This is the most likely outcome given Mateusz's Netherlands address.

### B. Art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC — Defendant abroad (the cleanest exit ramp)

**This provision is the decisive mechanism for case Nc-e 552126/26.**

The KPC bars issuance of a nakaz zapłaty if "doręczenie pozwanemu nakazu miałoby nastąpić poza granicami kraju." Mateusz Szklarski-Łopata has resided in Eindhoven, Netherlands since October 2022. His CEIDG registration at a virtual office in Kraków does not create a valid domestic service address for court documents — Polish courts applying this provision look to the defendant's actual residential address, not a registered business address, when the defendant is a natural person.

Once the EPU court ascertains (or is informed) that Mateusz cannot be served in Poland, the court must act:
- **Pre-nakaz:** The court dismisses under art. 505³³ KPC ("brak podstaw do wydania nakazu" because art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 is met).
- **Post-nakaz:** Under art. 505³⁴ § 2 KPC, if it becomes apparent *after* the nakaz is issued that it cannot be served in Poland, the court *ex officio* quashes the nakaz and dismisses, unless the plaintiff removes the obstacle within one month (maximum). That call is not repeated.

### C. Art. 505³⁴ § 1 KPC — Failed service on domestic defendant

If the nakaz is issued and Mateusz's Kraków address is tried but fails, the court evaluates whether the address matches PESEL. If not, § 2 applies (see above).

### D. Art. 505³⁶ KPC — Sprzeciw by defendant

"W przypadku wniesienia sprzeciwu sąd umarza postępowanie w zakresie, w którym nakaz zapłaty utracił moc." Sprzeciw triggers dismissal. The plaintiff then re-files in ordinary procedure.

### E. Plaintiff's own wniosek for przekazanie?

There is **no express statutory vehicle** for a plaintiff to request "przekazanie sprawy do sądu właściwości ogólnej" in EPU. The plaintiff can cofnąć pozew (withdraw) under art. 203 KPC (applicable via art. 505²⁹ § 1 cross-reference to general rules), which triggers automatic umorzenie. That is effectively the plaintiff's "exit ramp."

### F. Does filing an amendment that adds a defendant itself trigger transfer?

As discussed in Section 1, there is no valid mechanism to file such an amendment in EPU. Attempting to do so would likely be treated as a defective filing and returned or ignored; it would not itself cause transfer. The transfer-triggering mechanism remains the art. 505²⁸ § 2 bar once the foreign address is verified.

### G. The NL-address problem: automatic exit ramp analysis

The plaintiff filed the EPU pozew on 2026-04-08. It has been 47 days with no nakaz issued. The most likely explanation is that the EPU court has identified the service problem with Mateusz's address. Under art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC, the court should either (i) have already dismissed, or (ii) be preparing to dismiss. **The plaintiff should consider proactively notifying the EPU court of Mateusz's Netherlands address to accelerate the dismissal**, which starts the 3-month clock under art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC. Alternatively — since more than 45 days have passed with no activity — the plaintiff may simply withdraw the pozew under art. 203 KPC, triggering dismissal, and re-file in ordinary proceedings immediately.

## 3. Does the EPU Filing Date Survive Transfer?

**Yes, but conditionally and with strict time limits.**

### The governing rule: art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC

"Jeżeli w terminie trzech miesięcy od dnia wydania postanowienia o umorzeniu elektronicznego postępowania upominawczego powód wniesie pozew przeciwko pozwanemu o to samo roszczenie w postępowaniu innym niż elektroniczne postępowanie upominawcze, **skutki prawne, które ustawa wiąże z wytoczeniem powództwa, następują z dniem wniesienia pozwu w elektronicznym postępowaniu upominawczym**."

Translation: If the plaintiff files a new claim against the same defendant for the same claim in non-EPU proceedings **within 3 months of the dismissal order**, all legal effects of filing (including limitation interruption) relate back to the EPU filing date (2026-04-08).

### What "skutki prawne związane z wytoczeniem powództwa" encompasses

- **Art. 123 § 1 pkt 1 KC (interruption of limitation period):** Filing a court claim interrupts the running of the limitation period (przerwanie biegu przedawnienia). Under art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC, if the ordinary suit is filed within 3 months of EPU dismissal, the interruption is deemed to have occurred on 2026-04-08.
- **Art. 118 KC (applicable limitation period):** Claims arising from business activity have a 3-year limitation period. For claims against Mateusz and Waldemar (commercial activity via JDG), the 3-year period runs from when the claim became due. The EPU filing date of 2026-04-08 freezes the limitation clock.
- **Art. 192 KPC (lis pendens):** Lis pendens attaches from service of the pozew. In the ordinary suit, this attaches when the pozew is served on the defendants in that proceeding; the EPU filing date is not automatically carried forward for lis pendens purposes against the new defendant Waldemar (who was not a party in EPU).
- **Statutory interest accrual:** Interest accrual is a matter of substantive law (art. 481 KC), not affected by the procedural filing date.
- **Zabezpieczenie priority:** See Section 7.

### Critical point on the 3-month window

The 3-month period runs from the **date of the dismissal order** (postanowienie o umorzeniu), not from when the plaintiff receives it. If the EPU court has not yet dismissed, the clock has not started. Once it issues the dismissal, the plaintiff must file the ordinary pozew **within 3 calendar months**. Missing this window means losing the date-preservation benefit; the ordinary suit's filing date becomes the new interruption date.

### SN doctrinal support for continuation principle

The SN resolution of **18 October 2013 (III CZP 63/13, OSNC 2014/7-8, poz. 69)** confirms the "continuation principle" (zasada kontynuacji): proceedings initiated in EPU are *continued* (not started anew) before the court of general jurisdiction when the nakaz cannot be issued or is quashed. The research institute study (IWS, Rylski, 2025) confirms that "za datę wszczęcia należy uznać wniesienie pozwu w formie elektronicznej" — the electronic filing date is the commencement date for all purposes.

### Caveat for the second defendant (Waldemar)

The date-preservation rule in art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC expressly says "pozew przeciwko pozwanemu o **to samo roszczenie**" — the same defendant (singular). For Waldemar, who was not a party in EPU, the EPU filing date does **not** automatically carry over as a limitation-interruption date. His addition as defendant in the ordinary suit is governed by art. 194 § 3 KPC (see Section 6), and limitation interruption against him runs from the date the ordinary court formally summons him (art. 198 § 1 KPC — "wezwanie zastępuje pozwanie"). This is not problematic if the limitation period against Waldemar has not yet expired.

## 4. Court Fee Differential

### Current fee rates

| Proceeding | UKSC basis | Rate | Fee on 155,000 PLN claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPU | Art. 19 ust. 2 pkt 2 UKSC | **1/4 of ordinary fee** = 25% of 5% | **1,937.50 PLN** (already paid) |
| Ordinary (ponad 20,000 PLN) | Art. 13 ust. 2 UKSC | **5%** of claim value, max 200,000 PLN | **7,750 PLN** |
| Fee differential | — | — | **5,812.50 PLN** |

*Correction to question wording: EPU fee = 1/4 of the standard fee, not 1.25% directly. For a 155,000 PLN claim: 5% × 155,000 = 7,750 PLN ordinary fee; EPU fee = 7,750 × 25% = 1,937.50 PLN.*

### On transfer/re-filing: who pays what and when?

EPU does not "transfer" with a fee top-up obligation. Instead:

- **If the plaintiff re-files within 3 months of dismissal (art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC):** The EPU fee paid (1,937.50 PLN) is **credited toward** ("zalicza się na poczet") the ordinary suit filing fee of 7,750 PLN (art. 19 ust. 2 pkt 2 in fine UKSC). The plaintiff owes **5,812.50 PLN** at time of filing the ordinary pozew.
- **If the plaintiff re-files after 3 months, or for a different claim/defendant:** The EPU fee is not credited; the plaintiff pays the full 7,750 PLN.
- **If the plaintiff withdraws the EPU pozew before any nakaz or service:** Art. 79 ust. 1 pkt 1 lit. b UKSC — the plaintiff *may* get a **full refund** if withdrawal occurs before the copy of the pozew has been sent to the defendant. In EPU, since the court has issued no nakaz and served nothing on Mateusz, this condition is likely met. However, art. 79 ust. 3 UKSC reduces the refund by one minimum fee amount.

### Non-payment consequences

If the ordinary suit is filed without paying the required fee differential:
- The court issues a summons to pay under **art. 130 KPC** (7-day deadline as a rule).
- Failure to pay within deadline: **art. 130² KPC** — zwrot pozwu (return/rejection of the pozew). This does not constitute dismissal; the plaintiff can re-file but loses the benefit of the original filing date.

### Does the fee scale by defendant count?

**No.** The filing fee scales by the *value of the claim*, not by the number of defendants. For a joint suit against both Mateusz and Waldemar claiming 155,000 PLN (solidary liability, so one undivided sum), the ordinary fee remains **7,750 PLN** total.

### Fee refund analysis on withdrawal

| Event | UKSC provision | Refund |
|---|---|---|
| Withdrawal before pozew copy sent to defendant | Art. 79 ust. 1 pkt 1 lit. b | 100% (minus minimum fee per art. 79 ust. 3) |
| Withdrawal before hearing starts | Art. 79 ust. 1 pkt 3 lit. a | 50% |
| EPU dismissed (umorzenie) — no withdrawal by plaintiff | Art. 505³⁷ + art. 19 ust. 2 pkt 2 UKSC | Fee credited against new pozew within 3 months |

In case Nc-e 552126/26, since no nakaz has been issued and no copy of the pozew has been served on Mateusz, the plaintiff almost certainly qualifies for a **full fee refund** under art. 79 ust. 1 pkt 1 lit. b UKSC on voluntary withdrawal. This makes Path C (voluntary withdrawal + new ordinary suit) financially attractive: the full 1,937.50 PLN is refunded, and the ordinary suit fee of 7,750 PLN is paid fresh.

## 5. Path Comparison — A, B, and C

### Path A: Amend EPU to Add Waldemar

**Procedurally blocked.** EPU does not permit subjective amendment (Section 1). This path does not exist.

### Path B: Keep EPU for Mateusz + Separate Ordinary Suit Against Waldemar

**Procedural sequence:**
1. EPU case Nc-e 552126/26 continues vs. Mateusz (or collapses — likely given Netherlands address).
2. Plaintiff simultaneously or subsequently files a separate ordinary suit vs. Waldemar at Sąd Rejonowy Kraków or Sąd Okręgowy Kraków (depending on value: 155,000 PLN is within SR jurisdiction as monetary — SR handles up to 100,000 PLN; for 155,000 PLN, Sąd Okręgowy is competent under art. 17 pkt 4 KPC if it is a regional-court case, but the standard rule for commercial claims puts SO at > 75,000 PLN, so Sąd Okręgowy Kraków would hear this).
3. Two parallel proceedings at different courts.

**Art. 219 KPC — connection of cases:**
Art. 219 KPC reads: "Sąd może zarządzić połączenie kilku oddzielnych spraw toczących się przed nim w celu ich łącznego rozpoznania lub także rozstrzygnięcia, jeżeli są one ze sobą w związku lub mogły być objęte jednym pozwem." The statute expressly says "toczących się **przed nim**" — cases pending **before the same court**. Connection under art. 219 KPC is **only available within one court**. An EPU case pending in Lublin and an ordinary case in Kraków **cannot be connected** under art. 219 KPC. This is a significant procedural disadvantage of Path B.

**Cost analysis (Path B):**
- EPU fee already paid: 1,937.50 PLN (likely irrecoverable unless withdrawn)
- Separate ordinary suit fee for 155,000 PLN vs. Waldemar: 7,750 PLN
- Lawyer fees (estimate): Kraków windykacja pozew flat fee typically 3,000–6,000 PLN net for initial pozew; if both cases litigated to judgment, estimate 8,000–15,000 PLN total legal fees across both proceedings
- **Total out-of-pocket (fees only): ~9,687.50 PLN + legal fees**

**Calendar time:** EPU likely collapses within 1–3 months. Separate Kraków ordinary case: 12–24 months to first-instance judgment (Sąd Okręgowy Kraków is known for backlog).

**Limitation/filing date risk:** The EPU filing date is preserved for Mateusz within the 3-month window. For Waldemar, limitation runs from the date the Kraków court summons him. No major risk if action is taken promptly.

**Litigation risk:** Two parallel proceedings → inconsistency risk if different judges reach different conclusions on joint liability → possible different damage awards. Enforcement against solidary obligors becomes more complex with two separate judgments.

**Procedural sequence:**

2. EPU court dismisses (umarza).
3. Within the 3-month window under art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC, plaintiff files a new ordinary pozew at Sąd Okręgowy Kraków naming both Mateusz Szklarski-Łopata and Waldemar Łopata as co-defendants (współuczestnictwo materialne under art. 72 § 1 pkt 1 KPC — see Section 6).

The 3-month clock starts from that postanowienie.

**Cost analysis (Path C):**

**Litigation risk:** Lowest. Single proceeding, single judge, single judgment. If liability is established, one enforcement title covers both defendants solidarily.

## 6. Joinder Rules (Art. 195–198 KPC) and Współuczestnictwo Classification

### Classification of the joinder

Under art. 72 § 1 KPC, there are two types of non-necessary joinder:

- **Art. 72 § 1 pkt 1 KPC — Współuczestnictwo materialne:** Parties whose rights or obligations derive from the *same legal relationship* or from legal relationships of the *same kind*. This applies when the co-defendants are jointly and severally liable under the *same* legal basis.
- **Art. 72 § 1 pkt 2 KPC — Współuczestnictwo formalne:** Parties whose claims are of the same type and based on similar factual and legal grounds.
- **Art. 72 § 2 KPC — Współuczestnictwo konieczne:** Necessary joinder — parties whose participation is *jointly necessary* for adjudication. This applies where a legal relationship cannot be adjudicated without all parties being present (e.g., certain property disputes, dissolution of companies).

### For Mateusz + Waldemar

**Under art. 441 KC (solidary tortfeasors) + art. 422 KC (inducer/helper):** If both are joint tortfeasors, each bears solidary liability to the plaintiff. The legal relationship is the same wrongful act and resulting damage. This is **współuczestnictwo materialne (art. 72 § 1 pkt 1 KPC)**.

**Under art. 860 + art. 864 KC (spółka cywilna):** Partners of a spółka cywilna are solidarily, personally, and jointly liable for the partnership's obligations. This is also **współuczestnictwo materialne (art. 72 § 1 pkt 1 KPC)** — the legal relationship is the partnership agreement and the obligation incurred through it.

**In both scenarios:** This is **NOT współuczestnictwo konieczne (art. 72 § 2 KPC)**. The settled doctrine and practice is that solidary debtors — whether under tort (art. 441 KC) or partnership obligation (art. 864 KC) — can be sued separately or jointly. The creditor's right to sue any one of them, all of them, or any combination flows from art. 366 § 1 KC ("wierzyciel może żądać spełnienia świadczenia od wszystkich dłużników łącznie, od kilku z nich lub od każdego z osobna"). Therefore, joinder of both defendants is **permissible but not mandatory**.

### Practical consequence: art. 195 KPC does not apply

Art. 195 KPC ("wezwanie lub zawiadomienie współuczestników koniecznych") empowers the court to require the plaintiff to designate missing necessary co-parties. Since the joinder is *materialne, not konieczne*, **art. 195 KPC does not apply**. The court has no duty or power to compel the plaintiff to add Waldemar. The plaintiff adds him voluntarily under art. 194 § 3 KPC ("sąd na wniosek powoda może wezwać te osoby do wzięcia udziału w sprawie").

### How dopozwanie of Waldemar works in ordinary proceedings

1. Plaintiff files ordinary pozew naming both defendants simultaneously — no separate dopozwanie motion needed.
2. Alternatively, after filing ordinary suit vs. Mateusz alone, plaintiff files a written wniosek under art. 194 § 3 KPC asking the court to summon Waldemar.
3. The court issues an order (postanowienie) summoning Waldemar. This summons "zastępuje pozwanie" (substitutes for filing a separate claim against him) per art. 198 § 1 KPC.
4. Waldemar is then a full party and may request "powtórzenie dotychczasowego postępowania" (repetition of prior proceedings) at his first procedural act under art. 198 § 3 KPC.

**Most efficient approach:** Name both defendants in the initial ordinary pozew simultaneously. This avoids the two-step motion process and eliminates any argument about the date limitation interruption against Waldemar.

## 7. Zabezpieczenie Roszczenia (Art. 730 et seq. KPC) Against Waldemar

### Standard under art. 730¹ KPC

A motion for zabezpieczenie requires the applicant to *uprawdopodobnić* (make plausible, not prove):
1. **The claim** (uprawdopodobnienie roszczenia) — a lower evidentiary threshold than full proof; the court performs an initial assessment based on asserted facts and presented evidentiary material, requiring a "high probability" that the claim is justified.
2. **Legal interest in security** (interes prawny w udzieleniu zabezpieczenia) — typically that without security, enforcement would be impossible or seriously impeded.

### Available measures (art. 747 KPC)

- **Zajęcie rachunku bankowego** (attachment of bank account) — most effective for a defendant with active business accounts
- **Hipoteka przymusowa** (compulsory mortgage) on real property owned by Waldemar
- **Zajęcie ruchomości** (attachment of movables — equipment, inventory)
- **Zakaz zbywania nieruchomości** (prohibition on alienating real property)

### Kaucja (security deposit) under art. 739 KPC

When applying for zabezpieczenie before judgment, the court *may* require the applicant to post a security deposit (kaucja) against potential damage to the defendant if the case is ultimately lost. For a 155,000 PLN claim, a typical kaucja might range from 5,000–20,000 PLN at the court's discretion (there is no fixed formula). Kaucja is more likely when:
- The applicant resides abroad (increasing perceived risk of non-recovery by defendant)
- The security measure is particularly burdensome (e.g., account freeze halting business operations)

The plaintiff here resides abroad (Poland is not their residence country), which increases the likelihood the court will require kaucja.

### Is the available evidence sufficient?

**For art. 441 KC + art. 422 KC (tortfeasor theory):**

The evidentiary material described (domain co-marketing since 2016 per Wayback Machine, Waldemar personally negotiating the order and delivery/refund promises via waldek@gpucomputer.pl) addresses the key elements: (1) there was a tort (fraudulent non-delivery/refund), (2) Waldemar actively participated (negotiated, made promises via his email, co-marketed). This is **sufficient for uprawdopodobnienie** at the preliminary stage — the court needs only high probability, not certainty. The email correspondence is especially strong evidence of personal involvement.

**For art. 860 + art. 864 KC (spółka cywilna theory):**

The threshold for uprawdopodobnienie of an implied spółka cywilna is somewhat higher because it requires showing a joint business purpose, common assets, and profit-sharing intent — which may not be directly evident from marketing materials alone. The 17+ years in the same vertical, shared gpucomputer.pl domain, and Waldemar personally managing customer relationships *is* probative but may need supplementation. This theory's uprawdopodobnienie is achievable but stronger evidence of joint operation (shared invoicing, joint bank account, or CEIDG cross-references) would be needed to give the court confidence at the preliminary stage.

**Recommendation:** File zabezpieczenie motion on both theories in the alternative. Structure it so that even if the court is not convinced on the spółka cywilna theory, it grants security on the tortfeasor theory.

### Mandatory vs. discretionary refusal grounds

The court **must** refuse zabezpieczenie if:
- Neither uprawdopodobnienie prong is satisfied
- The measure would cause Waldemar damage disproportionate to the plaintiff's interest (art. 730¹ § 3 KPC)

The court **has discretion** to require kaucja (art. 739 KPC) or to limit the scope of measures.

- **Krajowy Węzeł Identyfikacji Elektronicznej (login.gov.pl)** — the national identity gateway, which includes Profil Zaufany as one sub-method but also supports banking authentication (e.g., Moje ING, PKO Bank Polski, etc.) and other certified identity providers.
- **Kwalifikowany podpis elektroniczny** (qualified electronic signature).

The system does **not** exclusively require Profil Zaufany. The plaintiff (Magnus) who does not yet have Profil Zaufany but is a foreign national can set up authentication via **login.gov.pl** using another recognized identity provider, or alternatively use a qualified electronic signature (obtainable from EU-certified providers like Asseco, EuroCert, etc.).

**However:** Since the recommendation in Section 10 is to *withdraw* the EPU and file in ordinary court, the plaintiff's lack of Profil Zaufany is not a blocking issue. The withdrawal can be done through the e-sad portal using the above alternatives; the subsequent ordinary suit is paper-based (or filed via the ordinary court's information portal, which also accepts paper filing for parties not represented by counsel).

### Can amendments be submitted by paper post?

**No.** Art. 505³¹ § 1 KPC is clear: "Powód wnosi pisma wyłącznie za pośrednictwem systemu teleinformatycznego." All plaintiff submissions in EPU **must** go through the teleinformatic system. Paper submissions by the plaintiff to the Lublin court are not valid in EPU proceedings. This means the plaintiff cannot submit a paper amendment, paper withdrawal, or paper motion in the EPU case — all must go through e-sad.gov.pl.

### Portal UI capabilities for adding a defendant

Based on current research of the e-sad.gov.pl system and the EPU 3.0 project status, the portal **does not have a UI function to add a co-defendant to an existing case**. The available plaintiff actions are: (1) view case files, (2) file new motions/applications relating to the existing case. There is no "amend pozew to add defendant" form. Adding a defendant would require a new pozew in a new case — but as established in Section 1, this is procedurally incorrect in EPU.

**Uncertainty flag:** The EPU 3.0 project (launched per Ministry of Justice as a 2023–2027 project) is still in transition. The portal as of May 2026 may have updated features compared to the 2024 documentation. The browser agent prompt below should be used to verify current UI capabilities.

### Can a Polish pełnomocnik take over the existing EPU account?

Art. 505³¹ § 2² KPC provides: "Pełnomocnik powołuje się na pełnomocnictwo, wskazując jego zakres oraz okoliczności wymienione w art. 87, wnosząc pierwsze pismo procesowe w sprawie." An attorney (adwokat or radca prawny) can file under their own professional account on e-sad.gov.pl and reference the pełnomocnictwo in the first filing. They do not need to use or take over the plaintiff's personal account. Since March 2024, adwokaci and radcy prawni are required to maintain accounts in the court information portal (art. 37d of the Bar Act; art. 22¹¹ of the Legal Advisers Act), which integrates with e-sad. **The plaintiff does not need to share their e-sad account credentials.** The attorney files independently.

## 9. Sprzeciw Deadline Path — Does It Pay to Wait?

### Scenario: Nakaz issues against Mateusz alone

For a nakaz to issue against Mateusz, the EPU court would need to serve it on him in Poland. Given his Netherlands residence, this is unlikely to succeed:

- **Art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC** bars issuance of a nakaz if service would have to occur outside Poland.
- His CEIDG-registered address (virtual office in Kraków) *might* be used for a service attempt if the court accepts it as a valid domestic address for a sole trader. If the court tries and the service fails or is rejected, art. 505³⁴ § 2 KPC applies: the court quashes the nakaz and dismisses unless the plaintiff cures within one month.

**Most probable outcome:** The nakaz will *not* issue, and the case will be dismissed under art. 505³³ or art. 505³⁴ § 2 KPC. The "wait for sprzeciw" scenario is therefore largely hypothetical.

### Service to Eindhoven under Regulation (EU) 2020/1784

EU Regulation 2020/1784 on service of documents governs service between Polish and Dutch courts. Key practical points:
- The regulation requires transmission through designated transmitting and receiving agencies.
- In the Netherlands, service is effected through the deurwaarder (court bailiff) system.
- **Realistic timeline:** 3–6 months from transmission to confirmed service, depending on cooperation and address accuracy. Service of a Polish nakaz zapłaty in the Netherlands under Regulation 2020/1784 is resource-intensive, requires a translated document, and the 2-week sprzeciw deadline under art. 505³⁵ KPC begins to run only upon confirmed service on Mateusz in the Netherlands.
- Even if the Polish court were to attempt cross-border service of the nakaz (which EPU bars under art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC), the process would take many months.

**Conclusion:** The wait-for-nakaz-and-sprzeciw path is blocked by art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC itself. The court should not issue the nakaz at all.

### The "wait for transfer" strategy: analysis

If the plaintiff simply waits, the EPU court will eventually realize (if it hasn't already — the 47-day inactivity suggests it already has) that Mateusz's address is foreign and dismiss the proceeding under art. 505³³ KPC. This dismissal then starts the 3-month clock of art. 505³⁷ § 2 KPC.

**Is this the cheapest way to add Waldemar?**

In strict fee terms, waiting for the court-initiated dismissal then re-filing in ordinary proceedings is equivalent to voluntary withdrawal — in both cases the plaintiff files within 3 months and gets the EPU fee credited. **However:**
- Waiting for court-initiated dismissal adds weeks or months of delay.
- The plaintiff retains no control over the timing of the dismissal, which means the 3-month window could start running while the plaintiff is unaware.
- The court is not required to notify the plaintiff promptly; the dismissal order is served on the plaintiff under art. 505²⁹ § 4 KPC, but delays can occur.

**Verdict:** The wait-for-transfer strategy works procedurally but adds unnecessary uncertainty and delay. The recommended approach is for the plaintiff (or their attorney) to proactively withdraw the EPU pozew and immediately re-file the ordinary suit, giving maximum control over timing.

### Post-dismissal: can plaintiff freely amend under art. 193 KPC to add Waldemar?

**Yes.** Once the case is in ordinary proceedings, art. 193 KPC applies in full. The plaintiff can include Waldemar from the outset of the new ordinary pozew (most efficient), or — if the ordinary suit is filed against Mateusz alone first — request joinder of Waldemar under art. 194 § 3 KPC at any point before the court closes the evidentiary stage. There is no restriction on adding defendants in ordinary civil proceedings prior to closing arguments, provided the amendment does not affect jurisdiction (art. 193 § 1 KPC).
