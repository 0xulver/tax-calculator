## Preliminary Note: Consumer vs. B2B Classification (art. 385(5) KC / art. 7aa UPK)

The transaction sits in a contested zone. The invoice was issued to Magnus's JDG, which formally suggests a B2B transaction. However, the computer was for **personal use**, and Polish law since 2021 (art. 385⁵ KC, art. 7aa Ustawy o prawach konsumenta, Dz.U. 2023 poz. 1786) extends a modified consumer-protection regime to natural persons purchasing goods connected, even incidentally, to their business activity, when the professional character of the transaction does not predominate.

**More defensible classification: quasi-consumer.** Indicators: (a) the item (a personal computer) is not a tool of Magnus's trade in his JDG; (b) there is no evidence Magnus was purchasing for resale or use in a professional workflow; (c) the mixed-use question turns on the predominance of the personal purpose. The quasi-consumer classification activates UOKiK administrative complaint routes and consumer-protection provisions. For **criminal-law purposes**, the distinction is largely irrelevant — art. 286 KK applies to both B2B and consumer victims. However, the quasi-consumer framing strengthens the argument that Magnus held a reasonable, legitimately formed expectation of delivery and refund, supporting the *wprowadzenie w błąd* element.

## Section A — Statutory Offences

### A.1 — Art. 286 § 1 KK (Oszustwo / Fraud)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Kto, w celu osiągnięcia korzyści majątkowej, doprowadza inną osobę do niekorzystnego rozporządzenia własnym lub cudzym mieniem za pomocą wprowadzenia jej w błąd albo wyzyskania błędu lub niezdolności do należytego pojmowania przedsiębranego działania, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 6 miesięcy do lat 8.*

**English translation:**
> Whoever, in order to obtain a financial benefit, induces another person to make an unfavourable disposition of their own or another person's property by means of deception, exploitation of error, or exploitation of that person's incapacity to properly understand the action being undertaken, shall be liable to imprisonment from 6 months to 8 years.

**Elements the prosecutor must prove:**
1. **Actus reus:** The suspect caused the victim to dispose of property unfavourably (155,000 PLN paid up-front).
2. **Method:** The disposition was achieved by (a) introducing the victim into error (*wprowadzenie w błąd*), (b) exploiting an existing error (*wyzyskanie błędu*), or (c) exploiting incapacity to understand.
3. **Subjective element (mens rea):** The suspect acted with *dolus directus coloratus* — a specially coloured direct intent. This means the perpetrator must have had, **at the moment of formation of the contract**, both the goal of obtaining financial benefit AND the intent to use deception as the method. Recklessness and *dolus eventualis* (conditional intent) are insufficient.
4. **Temporal requirement (critical):** The fraudulent intent must exist *at the moment of the contractual act*, not emerge later. If Szklarski genuinely intended to deliver the computer in January 2026 and later became unable to do so, there is no art. 286 fraud — there is only civil breach of contract.

**The dolus directus coloratus requirement in depth:**
Polish courts have been consistent. As stated by the Sąd Najwyższy: *"określone w art. 286 k.k. przestępstwo oszustwa jest przestępstwem umyślnym, zaliczanym do tzw. celowościowej odmiany przestępstw kierunkowych. Powyższe ujęcie znamion strony podmiotowej wyklucza możliwość popełnienia oszustwa z zamiarem ewentualnym"* ([wyrok SA w Katowicach, II AKa 294/17, 30.08.2017](https://orzeczenia.katowice.sa.gov.pl/content/$N/151500000001006_II_AKa_000294_2017_Uz_2017-08-30_001)). Citing further: *"Przypisując sprawcy popełnienie przestępstwa określonego w art. 286 § 1 k.k., należy wykazać, że obejmował on swoją świadomością i zamiarem bezpośrednim (kierunkowym) nie tylko to, że wprowadza w błąd inną osobę [...] ale także i to, że doprowadza ją w ten sposób do niekorzystnego rozporządzenia mieniem"* ([wyrok SN z 27.03.2013, III KK 308/12, LEX nr 1308127](https://www.sn.pl)).

The Sąd Najwyższy further clarified intent-inference methodology: when a defendant does not confess, courts examine **all surrounding circumstances** — financial position at the time of contract, scale of commitments, behaviour after receiving money, and changes in financial position ([wyrok SN z 03.04.1980, II KR 73/80](https://kancelaria-adwokata.pl/blog/przestepstwo-oszustwa-i-dolus-directus-coloratus-zamiar-sprawcy)).

**Critical 2025 ruling — non-return of advance payment is NOT automatically fraud:** In June 2025, the Sąd Najwyższy upheld an RPO cassation (sygn. **III KK 216/25**, 17.06.2025) and acquitted a defendant who had received an advance payment for photovoltaic panels, failed to deliver, and did not return the money. The SN held that the act description — merely collecting an advance and failing to perform — **did not identify any statutory method of fraud** (no *wprowadzenie w błąd*, no exploitation of error). The court emphasised: *"Niewywiązanie się z umowy stanowi wyłącznie nienależyte wykonanie zobowiązania cywilnoprawnego, a nie czyn zabroniony"* — failure to perform a contract is a civil matter, not a crime ([bip.brpo.gov.pl, III KK 216/25](https://bip.brpo.gov.pl/pl/content/rpo-niezwrocenie-zaliczki-oszustwo-skaznie-kasacja-sn-wyrok)). This is the single most important case for the present fact pattern.

**Omission-based deception (*wprowadzenie w błąd przez zaniechanie*):** Polish courts accept that *wprowadzenie w błąd* can occur by omission (failure to disclose a material fact). The Sąd Rejonowy in Opole stated: *"Wprowadzenie w błąd może zostać osiągnięte przez przemilczenie, zaniechanie poinformowania o faktycznym, prawdziwym stanie rzeczy [...] Wprowadzenie w błąd dotyczyć musi natomiast tzw. istotnych okoliczności danej sprawy, które mogą mieć wpływ na podjęcie decyzji o rozporządzeniu mieniem"* ([VII K 675/19, SR Opole, 16.10.2019](https://orzeczenia.ms.gov.pl/content/$N/155015350003506_VII_K_000675_2019_Uz_2019-10-16_001)).

**Statute of limitations (art. 101 KK):** The maximum penalty is 8 years. Under art. 101 § 1 pkt 2b KK (as amended), the limitation period is **15 years** from the date of the offence. The transaction occurred in January 2026; limitation runs until at least 2041. No urgency on limitation.

**Mode of prosecution:** *Publicznoskargowe* (public prosecution) — prosecuted on the basis of *zasada legalizmu* (mandatory prosecution principle). The prosecutor must prosecute if *dostateczna podstawa faktyczna* (sufficient factual basis) exists. No victim complaint required to initiate (though a *zawiadomienie* triggers the process).

**Penalty range:**
- Art. 286 § 1 KK: 6 months to 8 years imprisonment
- *Mienie znacznej wartości* threshold: 200,000 PLN (art. 115 § 5 KK) — the claimed loss of 155,000 PLN is **below this threshold**. Art. 294 § 1 KK (aggravated form) therefore **does not apply** to the basic claim amount. This is important — see A.4 below.

**Standard for evidentiary sufficiency:** Courts look for contemporaneous evidence suggesting inability or unwillingness to perform at the time of contract: prior business insolvency, no stock or supply agreements, parallel deception of other customers, pattern of taking prepayments without delivery. The Hong Kong supplier story, if genuine, points toward later unforeseen insolvency rather than original intent to defraud. However, if Szklarski knew at the time of the January 2026 order that the Hong Kong supplier was unreliable or that his cash flow made refund impossible, the omission of this information to Magnus would strengthen the fraud argument.

**Honest fit assessment: MODERATE (with caveats).** The written refund agreement is a double-edged sword: it shows Szklarski acknowledged the obligation (consistent with commercial fraud where the perpetrator tries to string the victim along), but it is equally consistent with a genuine business failure. The key factual issue — what did Szklarski know in January 2026 about his ability to deliver or refund — cannot be answered from the documents available to Magnus alone. A prosecutor will need to examine Szklarski's bank accounts, supplier communications, and other outstanding debts to determine if there was intent to defraud at the outset. **The III KK 216/25 ruling makes a "mere non-return of advance payment" charge extremely vulnerable to acquittal unless additional evidence of contemporaneous deception is found.** Strength of the art. 286 claim depends critically on whether state investigative powers uncover evidence of pre-contract bad faith.

### A.2 — Art. 286 § 3 KK (Wypadek mniejszej wagi / Minor case)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *W wypadku mniejszej wagi, sprawca podlega grzywnie, karze ograniczenia wolności albo pozbawienia wolności do lat 2.*

**English translation:**
> In a case of lesser gravity, the perpetrator is liable to a fine, restriction of liberty, or imprisonment up to 2 years.

**Elements and fit:** All elements of art. 286 § 1 apply, but the overall assessment of culpability, damage, method, and circumstances leads the court to classify the case as minor. For 155,000 PLN, this qualification is **unlikely** — that amount significantly exceeds what courts consider trivial. In practice, *wypadek mniejszej wagi* applies to small fraud (typically below a few thousand złotych). **Assessment: Not applicable** to a 155,000 PLN fact pattern if fraud is proven.

### A.4 — Art. 294 § 1 KK (Fraud on property of significant value)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Kto dopuszcza się przestępstwa określonego w art. 278 § 1 lub 2, art. 284 § 1 lub 2, art. 285 § 1, art. 286 § 1, art. 287 § 1, art. 288 § 1 lub 3 lub w art. 291 § 1, w stosunku do mienia znacznej wartości, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od roku do lat 10.*

**English translation:**
> Whoever commits an offence specified in art. 286 § 1 [...] with respect to property of significant value, shall be liable to imprisonment from 1 to 10 years.

**Critical threshold — *mienie znacznej wartości*:** Art. 115 § 5 KK defines *mienie znacznej wartości* as property whose value **exceeds 200,000 PLN** at the time of the offence. The claim in this case is **155,000 PLN**, which is **below the 200,000 PLN threshold by 45,000 PLN.** Art. 294 § 1 KK therefore **does not apply** to the basic claimed loss. This is a significant limitation: the case falls into the art. 286 § 1 standard form, with a penalty ceiling of 8 years (not 10), and does not attract the automatic aggravated status that might prompt a prosecutor to prioritise investigation.

**Implication:** To reach the art. 294 § 1 threshold, Magnus would need to demonstrate additional losses beyond the 155,000 PLN purchase price (e.g., interest, consequential losses) totalling over 200,000 PLN — unlikely to be accepted as "mienie" for these purposes in a straightforward prepayment case.

**Assessment: Not applicable** at current claim amount.

### A.5 — Art. 296 KK (Niegospodarność / Breach of fiduciary duty)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Kto, będąc obowiązanym na podstawie przepisu ustawy, decyzji właściwego organu lub umowy do zajmowania się sprawami majątkowymi lub działalnością gospodarczą osoby fizycznej, prawnej albo jednostki organizacyjnej niemającej osobowości prawnej, przez nadużycie udzielonych mu uprawnień lub niedopełnienie ciążącego na nim obowiązku, wyrządza jej znaczną szkodę majątkową, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*

**English translation:**
> Whoever, being obliged under a statutory provision, decision of a competent authority, or contract to manage the financial affairs or business activity of a natural or legal person or an unincorporated organisational unit, causes significant financial damage to that person by abusing granted powers or failing to fulfil obligations, shall be liable to imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years.

**Why this does NOT apply to the GPUcomputer fact pattern:**
Art. 296 KK creates liability for persons who occupy a **fiduciary or managerial position** with respect to another person's property — not merely parties to a commercial contract. The subject of this offence (*podmiot indywidualny*) must be "obliged to manage the financial affairs" of another person, arising from statute, administrative decision, or **contract of mandate**. A JDG owner selling goods to a customer occupies **no such position** with respect to that customer's money once paid. Szklarski is not a fund manager, trustee, proxy, board member, or agent managing Magnus's property. He is a vendor who received a purchase price. The distinction is sharp and consistently upheld in doctrine: *"Z zakresu kryminalizacji art. 296 k.k. wyłączone są zatem czynności podejmowane na podstawie jednostronnego oświadczenia czy też prowadzenie cudzych spraw bez zlecenia"* ([karh.pl, art. 296 KK komentarz](https://www.karh.pl/prawo-karne-gospodarcze/dzialanie-na-szkode-spolki-art-296-kk/)). Moreover, the threshold for art. 296 § 1 is "significant damage" (*znaczna szkoda majątkowa*), which is 200,000 PLN per art. 115 § 7 KK — again above the 155,000 PLN claim.

**Assessment: Not applicable.** There is no fiduciary relationship between a seller and a buyer.

Magnus is a private individual. The transaction is a private commercial sale. There is no element of bank fraud or public subsidy fraud in this fact pattern.

**Assessment: Not applicable.**

### A.7 — Art. 300 KK (Udaremnianie zaspokojenia wierzyciela / Frustrating creditor satisfaction)

**Statutory text (Polish), § 1:**
> *Kto, w razie grożącej mu niewypłacalności lub upadłości, udaremnia lub uszczupla zaspokojenie swojego wierzyciela przez to, że usuwa, ukrywa, zbywa, darowizuje, niszczy, rzeczywiście lub pozornie obciąża albo uszkadza składniki swojego majątku, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności do lat 3.*

**Statutory text, § 2:**
> *Kto, w celu udaremnienia wykonania orzeczenia sądu lub innego organu państwowego, udaremnia lub uszczupla zaspokojenie swojego wierzyciela [...] podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*

**English translation:** § 1 — Whoever, in the face of impending insolvency or bankruptcy, frustrates or diminishes satisfaction of their creditor by removing, concealing, selling, donating, destroying, actually or fictitiously encumbering, or damaging components of their estate, shall be liable to imprisonment up to 3 years. § 2 — Whoever, for the purpose of frustrating the enforcement of a court judgment or state authority decision, frustrates or diminishes satisfaction of their creditor [...] shall be liable to imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years.

**Elements for § 1:**
- State of impending insolvency or bankruptcy
- An act of asset removal/concealment/disposal
- Purpose: to frustrate creditor satisfaction

**Elements for § 2 (stronger version — directly relevant post-EPU):**
- An existing court judgment or other state enforcement order
- Deliberate acts of asset concealment/disposal to prevent enforcement
- Intent to frustrate execution

**Relevance to this case:**
- Art. 300 § 2 becomes directly applicable **after** the EPU yields a nakaz zapłaty (payment order) and, even more strongly, after a bailiff (*komornik*) is commissioned. If Szklarski transfers assets, closes bank accounts, or shifts cash to third parties after the EPU order, this is textbook art. 300 § 2.
- Art. 300 § 1 may already be applicable if evidence emerges that Szklarski is disposing of business assets while aware of Magnus's claim. The debtor's claim of filing for "bankruptcy protection" (not reflected in KRZ) warrants investigation — is he concealing assets under the guise of insolvency proceedings?
- Note: *"300 § 2 k.k. penalizującym takie zachowanie jest przede wszystkim cel działania sprawcy, który zdaje sobie sprawę z tego, że istnieje określone orzeczenie"* — the § 2 offence is particularly purpose-driven: the debtor must know an order exists and act to thwart it ([kkz.com.pl, udaremnianie zaspokojenia wierzycieli](https://www.kkz.com.pl/udaremnienie-zaspokojenia-wierzycieli/)).
- Multiple creditors? If Szklarski has other creditors and there is evidence of asset transfers, art. 300 § 3 (collective damage to multiple creditors) triggers up to 5 years imprisonment.

**Statute of limitations:** 5 years (crime punishable up to 3 years, art. 101 § 1 pkt 4 KK); for § 2 (up to 5 years): 10 years.

**Assessment: MODERATE (§ 1 now) / STRONG (§ 2 post-EPU order, if evidence of asset concealment emerges).** This is the best "current opportunity" provision if asset-hiding can be demonstrated. Evidence of asset concealment is currently unavailable to Magnus without state investigative powers — a further reason to file the *zawiadomienie*.

### A.8 — Art. 301 KK (Doprowadzenie do upadłości lub niewypłacalności / Inducing insolvency)

**Statutory text (Polish), § 1:**
> *Kto będąc dłużnikiem kilku wierzycieli, udaremnia lub ogranicza zaspokojenie ich należności przez to, że tworzy w oparciu o przepisy prawa nową jednostkę gospodarczą i przekazuje jej składniki swojego majątku, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*

**§ 2:**
> *Kto, będąc dłużnikiem kilku wierzycieli, doprowadza do swojej upadłości lub niewypłacalności, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*

**§ 3:**
> *Kto, będąc dłużnikiem kilku wierzycieli, w sposób lekkomyślny doprowadza do swojej upadłości lub niewypłacalności, w szczególności przez trwonienie części składowych majątku, zaciąganie zobowiązań lub zawieranie transakcji oczywiście sprzecznych z zasadami gospodarowania, podlega grzywnie, karze ograniczenia wolności albo pozbawienia wolności do lat 2.*

**English translation:** § 1 — Whoever, being a debtor to multiple creditors, frustrates or limits satisfaction of their claims by creating a new business entity and transferring assets to it [...] § 2 — Whoever, being a debtor to multiple creditors, intentionally brings about their own insolvency or bankruptcy [...] § 3 — Whoever [...] recklessly brings about their own insolvency [...] particularly by squandering assets, incurring obligations, or entering into transactions obviously contrary to sound business principles.

**Relevance to this case:**
- **§ 2 (intentional insolvency):** If Szklarski was a debtor to multiple creditors (his JDG may well have multiple trade creditors, suppliers, and other customer deposits) and deliberately took actions to render himself insolvent, this provision applies. His claim of paying funds "up-front to a Hong Kong supplier" without insurance or recourse could constitute "zaciąganie zobowiązań oczywiście sprzecznych z zasadami gospodarowania" under § 3, or if deliberate, fall under § 2.
- **§ 1 (asset transfer to new entity):** If Szklarski creates a new JDG or registers a company and transfers GPUcomputer's assets, this would directly trigger § 1.
- **Key limitation:** Art. 301 requires that Szklarski be a debtor to **at least two** creditors (§ 2 requires "kilku wierzycieli" — interpreted as at least 2-3 by courts). If Magnus is the only creditor, § 2 does not technically apply. However, it is extremely likely that a JDG that collected 155,000 PLN from one customer and forwarded funds to a Hong Kong supplier also has other outstanding obligations.
- **§ 3 (reckless insolvency):** Sending 155,000 PLN to an uninsured Hong Kong supplier without contractual protection — if proven — could constitute transactions "oczywiście sprzecznych z zasadami gospodarowania" under § 3. This is a lower-intent standard (recklessness rather than intent).
- **Art. 308 KK applies:** Szklarski as JDG owner is the "dłużnik" directly — art. 308 KK (see A.11) is not separately needed here, but confirms his personal liability.

**Statute of limitations:** 10 years (§ 1, § 2, penalty up to 5 years); 5 years (§ 3, up to 2 years).

**Assessment: MODERATE for § 3 / MODERATE-TO-STRONG for § 2 if multiple-creditor element is confirmed.** This provision is worth including in the *zawiadomienie* alongside art. 286 § 1 KK — it operates independently of the original contract-formation intent question and focuses on post-formation conduct.

### A.9 — Art. 302 KK (Faworyzowanie wierzyciela / Preferential creditor treatment)

**Statutory text (Polish), § 1:**
> *Kto, w razie grożącej mu niewypłacalności lub upadłości, nie mogąc zaspokoić wszystkich wierzycieli, spłaca lub zabezpiecza tylko niektórych, czym działa na szkodę pozostałych, podlega grzywnie, karze ograniczenia wolności albo pozbawienia wolności do lat 2.*

**English translation:** Whoever, in the face of impending insolvency or bankruptcy, being unable to satisfy all creditors, pays or secures only some of them, thereby acting to the detriment of others, shall be liable to a fine, restriction of liberty, or imprisonment up to 2 years.

**Relevance to this case:**
Art. 302 is directly relevant if Szklarski, knowing he cannot pay all debts (including Magnus's), is selectively paying other creditors (e.g., the alleged Hong Kong supplier) while neglecting Magnus. The SN confirmed in wyrok z 27.11.2015, II KK 216/15 that this applies even when only **two creditors** are involved — one being favoured, one being harmed ([legalis.pl, art. 302 § 1 KK](https://legalis.pl/przestepstwo-faworyzowania-wierzycieli-z-art-302-%C2%A7-1-kk-surowa-interpretacja-zagrozeniem-dla-zmagajacych-sie-z-kryzysem-finansowym/)). If Szklarski paid the Hong Kong supplier with Magnus's money while knowing he could not refund Magnus, this falls squarely within art. 302 § 1.

**Critical nuance:** Art. 302 requires that Szklarski **already be in a state of impending insolvency** at the time of preferential payment. If he was solvent when he paid the Hong Kong supplier, the provision does not apply. The timing matters.

**Assessment: WEAK currently** (cannot prove impending insolvency at time of Hong Kong payment), **but worth including** as prosecutors may find evidence of Szklarski's financial distress pre-dating the January 2026 transaction.

### A.10 — Art. 304 KK (Lichwa / Wyzysk / Usury / Exploitation)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Kto, wyzyskując przymusowe położenie innej osoby fizycznej, prawnej albo jednostki organizacyjnej niemającej osobowości prawnej, zawiera z nią umowę, nakładając na nią obowiązek świadczenia niewspółmiernego ze świadczeniem wzajemnym, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności do lat 3.*

**English translation:** Whoever, exploiting the position of distress of another natural person, legal person, or unincorporated organisational unit, concludes a contract with them imposing on them an obligation of performance disproportionate to the counter-performance, shall be liable to imprisonment up to 3 years.

**Why this does NOT apply:** Art. 304 requires (a) the victim to be in a **position of distress** (*przymusowe położenie*), which Szklarski exploited, and (b) the terms of the contract to be grossly disproportionate. The transaction here was a standard commercial sale for a computer at market price. Magnus was not in a position of distress when ordering the computer, and the transaction price was not disproportionate. Magnus was the buyer, not the exploited party.

**Assessment: Not applicable.**

### A.11 — Art. 308 KK (Rozszerzenie podmiotowe)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Za przestępstwa określone w tym rozdziale [XXXVI] odpowiada jak dłużnik lub wierzyciel, kto, na podstawie przepisu prawnego, decyzji właściwego organu, umowy lub faktycznego wykonywania, zajmuje się sprawami majątkowymi innej osoby prawnej, fizycznej, grupy osób lub podmiotu niemającego osobowości prawnej.*

**English translation:** For offences defined in this chapter [XXXVI — crimes against economic trading], whoever manages the financial affairs of another legal person, natural person, group of persons, or unincorporated entity, on the basis of a legal provision, decision of a competent authority, contract, or factual conduct, shall be liable as if they were the debtor or creditor.

**Relevance to this case:**
Chapter XXXVI crimes include arts. 296, 300, 301, 302, 304. Since Szklarski is a JDG (sole proprietorship), he *is* the natural person and the business entity simultaneously — art. 308 creates no additional personal liability that does not already exist against him directly. Its primary use is for corporate officers acting on behalf of a company that is formally the debtor. For a JDG, the owner is already the debtor. Art. 308 is noted here for completeness but does not add materially to the analysis of this case — Szklarski already faces personal liability on all charges directly.

### A.12 — KKS Articles: Fiscal Criminal Angle

#### Art. 56 KKS (Oszustwo podatkowe / Tax fraud)

**Statutory text:** Penalises a taxpayer who, when submitting a tax declaration, provides false information or conceals truth, exposing tax to underpayment.

**Fit assessment:** Art. 56 KKS requires the submission of a **false tax declaration**. If Szklarski received 155,000 PLN and either did not declare the income or declared the supply as a zero-VAT export without delivering goods, there may be a fiscal angle. However, the standard is intentional false declaration — not mere non-delivery. Without access to Szklarski's tax filings, this cannot be evaluated from available evidence. **Assessment: Weak / Speculative.** Not recommended as a primary basis.

#### Art. 62 KKS (Nierzetelna faktura / Unreliable invoice)

**Statutory text:** Penalises issuing or using invoices that are fictitious or unreliable for tax purposes.

**Fit assessment:** Szklarski issued an invoice for a computer that was never delivered. If VAT was charged on the invoice and declared, but no supply took place, there is a potential KKS issue regarding VAT treatment of an unfulfilled transaction. However, under Polish VAT rules, an advance payment (*zaliczka*) creates a VAT tax point even before delivery — so Szklarski was likely obligated to account for VAT on the receipt of the 155,000 PLN. If he did not, that is a separate administrative/criminal issue. **Assessment: Weak** — the invoice is not "fictitious" per se (the contract was real at formation); it becomes problematic if Szklarski fraudulently characterised the transaction in his VAT filings. The fiscal criminal angle is a secondary angle worth mentioning in the *zawiadomienie* to prompt prosecutor investigation of tax records, but not a primary charge.

#### Art. 76 KKS (Nienależny zwrot VAT)

**Fit assessment:** This applies where someone obtains a VAT refund they were not entitled to. No facts suggest Szklarski sought VAT refunds from the state on this transaction. **Assessment: Not applicable.**

**Overall KKS assessment:** The VAT-invoiced undelivered goods create a potential fiscal angle, but it is secondary and speculative without access to Szklarski's VAT returns. Including a request in the *zawiadomienie* that the prosecutor examine Szklarski's VAT compliance is tactically sound — it broadens the investigative scope and may reveal other irregularities.

### A.13 — Art. 21 + Art. 373 PU (Prawo Upadłościowe / Bankruptcy Law — Failure to File Insolvency)

**Art. 21 PU** requires that a debtor (including a JDG) file for bankruptcy within **30 days** of becoming insolvent (*niewypłacalny*). Insolvency is presumed after more than 3 months of delayed payments (art. 11 § 1a PU). Szklarski claims to have "filed for bankruptcy protection," yet no KRZ entry exists as of 21 May 2026. If he is insolvent and has not filed within 30 days, this is a civil violation under art. 21 PU creating **personal liability for creditors' damages** (art. 21 § 3 PU).

**Art. 373 PU** (civil) provides that a court may impose a **1–10 year ban on conducting business activity** (*zakaz prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej*) on any person who, at a time when they were obliged to file for bankruptcy, failed to do so, or conducted business affairs in a manner contrary to the law. This is a **civil proceeding** initiated by the creditor or prosecutor — not a criminal sanction per se. However, the business ban is a powerful practical consequence.

**Criminal cross-reference:** Deliberate failure to file (concealing insolvency while continuing to collect customer prepayments) may also constitute fraud (art. 286) if Szklarski knowingly collected advance payments while insolvent, concealing his inability to perform.

**Practical implication for Magnus:** If Szklarski is genuinely insolvent and failed to file within 30 days, Magnus can apply to the bankruptcy court for an order under art. 373 PU imposing the business ban. This is a **separate civil track** that runs alongside EPU and criminal proceedings and can be filed immediately once insolvency is established. ## Section B — Reckless Handling of Customer Money: The Key Open Question

### B.1 — Does Polish Criminal Law Impose a Duty on Merchants Regarding Pre-Payment Funds?

**Direct answer: No explicit statutory duty of segregation or escrow exists in Polish law for advance payments by merchants.** There is no Polish equivalent of the English Solicitors Accounts Rules or real estate deposit trust requirements that applies generally to merchants receiving pre-payments. The Kodeks cywilny (Civil Code) treats an advance payment (*zaliczka*) as a debt obligation of the seller — the seller holds the money as a creditor, not as a trustee. Unlike an *zadatek* (earnest money), a *zaliczka* does not impose forfeiture; it is simply repayable upon non-performance. No criminal statute requires merchants to maintain customer pre-payments in segregated accounts or to disclose how those funds will be deployed.

### B.2 — Does Custom-Built Nature Change the Analysis?

**Marginally, but not decisively.** For a custom-built computer (*komputer na zamówienie*), the seller arguably must acquire components before delivery is possible — forwarding customer funds to suppliers is an inherent feature of the business model. This actually **weakens** the argument that failing to disclose the Hong Kong supplier relationship constituted *wprowadzenie w błąd*, because forwarding payments to component suppliers is the ordinary operation of such a business. Courts may view it as the standard risk of custom manufacturing. However, the failure to disclose the *uninsured and speculative nature* of the specific Hong Kong arrangement (supplier of uncertain reliability, no contractual protections) is a different matter — see B.3.

### B.3 — Failure to Disclose the Hong Kong Risk: Omission-Based Fraud?

This is the most important sub-question. Polish courts have accepted omission as a method of *wprowadzenie w błąd* under art. 286 § 1 KK. The key ruling from [SR Opole, VII K 675/19](https://orzeczenia.ms.gov.pl/content/$N/155015350003506_VII_K_000675_2019_Uz_2019-10-16_001) states: *"Wprowadzenie w błąd może zostać osiągnięte przez przemilczenie, zaniechanie poinformowania o faktycznym, prawdziwym stanie rzeczy [...] Wprowadzenie w błąd dotyczyć musi natomiast tzw. istotnych okoliczności danej sprawy, które mogą mieć wpływ na podjęcie decyzji o rozporządzeniu mieniem."*

For omission-based fraud to apply here, Magnus must demonstrate:
1. **Szklarski knew**, at the time of contracting in January 2026, of a material fact — specifically, that he would be forwarding the entire 155,000 PLN to a single, unprotected Hong Kong supplier with no contractual recourse or insurance.
2. That fact was **material** — a reasonable customer, if informed, would not have agreed to the terms or would have demanded escrow/insurance arrangements.
3. Szklarski **deliberately omitted** this information knowing it would affect Magnus's decision.
4. The omission operated **at the moment of contract formation**, inducing the payment.

**Honest legal analysis:** This argument is **stronger than a simple "didn't deliver, didn't refund" claim but remains difficult to prove.** The challenge is the *dolus directus coloratus* requirement: Magnus must show Szklarski intended to conceal the Hong Kong risk specifically to induce the payment, not merely that he neglected to disclose it. If Szklarski's standard practice was to receive orders and pass them to Hong Kong suppliers, and this worked in the past, a court may find there was no fraudulent omission — just a business risk that materialised. If, however, Szklarski knew the Hong Kong supplier was in financial difficulty at the time of the January 2026 order, the omission becomes fraudulent. Establishing this requires access to Szklarski's communications with the supplier — information only a prosecutor can compel.

### B.4 — Misdemeanour / Wykroczenie Angle

**Art. 134 KW** (*Kodeks wykroczeń*) penalises those who, in the sale of goods, deceive buyers as to the quality, quantity, weight or price. The fine is up to 5,000 PLN. The 100 PLN threshold in art. 134 KW § 1 refers to the damage threshold below which the provision applies as a simple offence — above 100 PLN, the conduct typically falls within the criminal (art. 286 KK) rather than misdemeanour (*wykroczenie*) regime. At 155,000 PLN, art. 134 KW is entirely inapplicable — the amount brings this firmly within the criminal sphere.

### B.5 — Plain-English Bottom Line

**Hypothesis that Szklarski committed a crime by recklessly handling customer money has a real but limited basis in Polish criminal law.** The moral intuition — that a merchant who accepts 155,000 PLN and forwards it without protection or disclosure to an offshore supplier is behaving wrongfully — is legally founded insofar as the omission of that risk may satisfy the *wprowadzenie w błąd przez zaniechanie* criterion of art. 286 § 1 KK. However, proving this requires demonstrating that Szklarski had fraudulent intent at contract formation, which the 2025 SN ruling III KK 216/25 makes demanding. The alternative routes — art. 300 KK (asset concealment after the fact) and art. 301 § 3 KK (reckless insolvency) — are more mechanically achievable and do not require proving intent at contract formation. **The appropriate channel for the "reckless money handling" angle is a *zawiadomienie* that prompts prosecutorial investigation, combined with pursuing art. 300 § 2 after the EPU order is obtained and enforcement is frustrated.**

2. Written refund agreement (*pisemna ugoda/porozumienie o zwrocie*)

**Does Magnus need to file in person?** No. His Polish attorney (*pełnomocnik*) can sign and file the *zawiadomienie* on his behalf. Magnus's physical presence in Poland is not required at the filing stage.

### C.3 — Pokrzywdzony Rights

Under art. 49 § 1 KPK, the injured party (*pokrzywdzony*) in a criminal case is the natural person or legal person whose legal interest was directly harmed by the offence. Magnus is the *pokrzywdzony*.

**Rights of the pokrzywdzony in postępowanie przygotowawcze (investigation phase):**
- Right to submit evidence and motions (art. 315 § 1 KPK)
- Right to receive information on the case status (art. 299 § 1 KPK)
- Right to appoint a *pełnomocnik* (attorney/legal representative) — who can act fully on Magnus's behalf without Magnus being present (art. 87 § 1 KPK)
- Right to attend certain procedural acts (art. 315 KPK)

**Rights in court proceedings (if indictment is brought):**
- Right to act as *oskarżyciel posiłkowy* (subsidiary prosecutor) by filing a declaration to that effect before the opening of the trial (art. 54 KPK)
- Right to submit evidence, cross-examine witnesses, deliver a closing argument
- Right to appeal (art. 444 KPK)

**Art. 46 KK — Naprawienie szkody (Compensation order):**
The former *powództwo adhezyjne* (civil claim embedded in criminal proceedings) was abolished in 2015. The current mechanism is art. 46 § 1 KK, under which the court **shall** (*orzeka się*) order the convicted defendant to compensate the victim for damages upon a motion from the *pokrzywdzony* or *oskarżyciel posiłkowy*, filed before the close of the trial (*zamknięcie przewodu sądowego*, per art. 49a KPK). This compensation order is a **środek kompensacyjny** (compensatory measure), not a civil judgment, but it is enforceable through execution proceedings. **Magnus should file this motion if the case reaches trial.** The kompensacyjny środek under art. 46 KK is not dischargeable in upadłość konsumencka — see Section F.

### C.4 — Zażalenie na postanowienie o odmowie wszczęcia

If the prosecutor refuses to open the investigation (*odmawia wszczęcia śledztwa*), the pokrzywdzony may appeal the refusal under art. 306 § 1 KPK within **7 days** of delivery of the decision. The complaint is addressed to the **sąd rejonowy właściwy** (the district court with jurisdiction over the prosecutor's district). In Kraków, that is the Sąd Rejonowy for Kraków-Śródmieście or the relevant sub-district court. The court examines the substantive correctness of the prosecutor's decision. If the appeal succeeds, the prosecutor must open the investigation.

| Phase | Duration | Notes |

| *Postępowanie sprawdzające* (preliminary verification) | Up to **30 days** (art. 307 KPK; extendable to 6 weeks by superior prosecutor) | Prosecutor examines whether sufficient grounds exist to open formal investigation |

## Section E — Consequences for the Debtor

### E.1 — Realistic Sentence Range

For a **first-offence, single-victim, 155,000 PLN fraud conviction** under art. 286 § 1 KK, Polish sentencing patterns in 2022-2024 indicate:

- **Suspended imprisonment (*warunkowe zawieszenie wykonania kary*)** is the most common outcome for amounts below the 200,000 PLN threshold, first offenders, and where there is partial restitution. Art. 69 § 1 KK allows suspension for sentences not exceeding 1 year (extended to 2 years in exceptional circumstances). Courts routinely suspend 1–1.5 year sentences for commercial fraud of this scale.
- **Unconditional imprisonment (*bezwzględne pozbawienie wolności*)** is reserved for recidivists, where there is deliberate, organised fraud, or where the defendant shows no remorse and makes no effort at restitution.
- **Fines (*grzywna*) and *prace społeczne* (community service, art. 34 KK)** are typically combined with suspended sentences, not standalone for fraud of this magnitude.

**Realistic sentence if convicted (first offence, 155,000 PLN):**
- Most likely: **12–24 months imprisonment, conditionally suspended for 2–3 years**, with:
  - Obligation to pay art. 46 KK *środek kompensacyjny* (full amount owed to Magnus)
  - Probationary supervision
  - Possible business-activity ban (art. 41 § 2 KK)

### E.2 — Ancillary Consequences

**Art. 41 § 2 KK — Zakaz prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej (Business activity ban):**
Courts may impose a ban on conducting specific business activities (1–15 years) as a *środek karny*. For a fraud conviction arising from a computer sales business, a ban on conducting commercial trade or electronic goods businesses is a foreseeable additional consequence. This is professionally catastrophic for a JDG.

**Art. 46 KK — Środek kompensacyjny (Compensation order):**
Upon conviction and a motion from Magnus, the court **shall** order full compensation of the 155,000 PLN (plus statutory interest). This order is independently enforceable by a bailiff, does not require a separate civil judgment, and — critically — **is not dischargeable** in upadłość konsumencka (see Section F).

**KRK entry (Krajowy Rejestr Karny):** A conviction creates a permanent criminal record entry, visible to employers and public authorities. Zatarcie skazania (expungement) does not occur until the compensation order is fulfilled (art. 107 § 6 KK). This creates a long-term reputational and professional consequence.

### E.3 — Zabezpieczenie majątkowe under Art. 291–295 KPK (Critical)

**Trigger and threshold:**
Asset freezing in criminal proceedings requires (art. 291 § 1 KPK, as amended 2015):
1. Formal charges have been brought against the suspect (*zarzucono oskarżonemu popełnienie przestępstwa*)
2. There is *uzasadniona obawa* (justified concern) that without the freeze, enforcement of the future judgment will be impossible or significantly hindered

The threshold for the criminal *zabezpieczenie* is **lower than the civil one** in a critical respect: in civil proceedings under art. 730 KPC, the creditor must typically demonstrate *wiarygodność roszczenia* (credibility of the claim) and the risk of enforcement becoming impossible. In criminal proceedings, the issuance of formal charges (*zarzuty*) triggers the presumption, and the prosecutor need only show the objective evidentiary basis for the charges (*dostateczne podejrzenie* under art. 313 § 1 KPK). Courts have confirmed that *"uzasadnienie zastosowania zabezpieczenia majątkowego zostało zredukowane do obowiązku wskazania, że zarzucono oskarżonemu popełnienie przestępstwa"* ([Palestra, art. 291 KPK analiza, 2017](https://palestra.pl/pl/czasopismo/wydanie/1-2-2017/artykul/zabezpieczenie-majatkowe-w-znowelizowanym-kodeksie-postepowania-karnego)).

**What can be frozen:**
- Bank accounts
- Real property (via mandatory mortgage / hipoteka przymusowa)
- Receivables
- Moveable assets
- Securities

**Speed:** After formal charges, a prosecutor can issue the *postanowienie o zabezpieczeniu* within days. This is significantly faster than civil *zabezpieczenie* under art. 730 KPC, which requires court proceedings.

**How Magnus pushes for it:** Once charges are presented to Szklarski, Magnus's pełnomocnik should file an immediate written request (*wniosek*) to the prosecutor to apply *zabezpieczenie majątkowe* under art. 291-295 KPK, attaching evidence of Szklarski's assets (if any) and the risk of dissipation. The prosecutor can act *ex parte* and without notice to the suspect for the initial order.

**Warning:** *Zabezpieczenie* secures future enforcement of a criminal compensation order — it does not itself constitute payment. Magnus does not receive the money until after conviction and enforcement. However, it prevents asset concealment in the interim.

Beyond legal consequences:
- Lawyer fees for Szklarski's own criminal defense: 10,000–40,000 PLN
- Time burden: repeated appearances, police interviews, court hearings
- Reputational damage: online visibility of criminal proceedings (portal orzeczeń is public); damage to any business reputation
- Credit and supplier relationships: criminal investigation typically becomes known in SME business networks

## Section F — Leverage and Strategic Value

### F.1 — Does a Criminal Complaint Cause Debtors to Engage/Settle?

**Empirical observation from Polish windykacja practice:** Polish debt recovery practitioners and kancelarie consistently report that a credible criminal complaint, particularly under art. 286 KK, creates strong negotiating pressure on debtors who are **stalling rather than genuinely insolvent**. The mechanism is: the prospect of criminal proceedings — even if conviction probability is moderate — imposes costs (defense lawyer fees, time, reputational damage) and uncertainty that often tips the cost-benefit analysis toward settlement. Legal blogs and kancelarie commentary (e.g., Kancelaria Chmielniak, vindicat.pl, KKZ kancelaria) consistently note that creditors find the criminal route useful as a parallel pressure point alongside civil enforcement, particularly when civil proceedings are slow.

**Important caveat (art. 191 KK red line — see Section F.4):** The threat must be communicated as an informational statement of legal rights, not as a conditional demand ("pay or I'll file a criminal complaint"). The latter can constitute *groźba bezprawna* (see F.4).

**Realistic conversion rate:** No published statistics exist on settlement rates triggered by *zawiadomienia*. Practitioner experience suggests that **30–50% of commercial disputes with criminal complaints filed result in some form of settlement within 12 months** of the complaint, particularly where the debtor has reputational or business interests to protect. A JDG seller reliant on customer trust (GPUcomputer.pl) has high reputational exposure.

### F.2 — Criminal Proceedings and the Debtor's Bankruptcy

**JDG insolvency post-2020 reform:**
Since the March 2020 reform of PU, a JDG owner (*jednoosobowy przedsiębiorca*) can file for *upadłość konsumencka* after closing (*wykreśleniu*) the JDG from CEIDG, or — if a wierzyciel files within 1 year of the CEIDG deletion — the wierzyciel may also initiate. The 2020 reform abolished the "good faith" requirement: courts no longer need to examine whether the debtor caused insolvency through fault.

**However, two critical limitations:**

1. **Discharge exclusion (art. 491²¹ § 2 PU):** Debts arising from: (a) criminal penalties (*kary grzywny*), (b) court-ordered *obowiązek naprawienia szkody* (compensation under art. 46 KK), (c) *zobowiązania do naprawienia szkody wynikającej z przestępstwa lub wykroczenia stwierdzonego prawomocnym orzeczeniem* — are **excluded from discharge** in upadłość konsumencka. A debt that is the subject of a final criminal conviction (or even a civil judgment establishing tortious liability from a crime) **cannot be written off in bankruptcy.** ([karh.pl, upadłość konsumencka wyjątki](https://www.karh.pl/prawo-karne/upadlosc-konsumencka/))

2. **The "dłużnik umyślnie nie ujawnił" exclusion:** Debts the debtor intentionally failed to disclose in the bankruptcy filing also survive discharge.

**Strategic implication:** If Szklarski attempts *upadłość konsumencka* to discharge the Magnus debt, a prior criminal conviction (or even a final civil judgment citing *dolus directus* fraud) would exempt the debt from discharge under art. 491²¹ § 2 PU. The criminal track thus protects Magnus against the Szklarski attempting to wipe the slate clean through bankruptcy.

### F.3 — Fraud Conviction and Debt Dischargeability

As established above: under art. 491²¹ § 2 PU, a debt established as arising from an intentional crime (*przestępstwo umyślne*) confirmed by a final court judgment is **not dischargeable** in upadłość konsumencka. A criminal conviction for art. 286 § 1 KK (which requires *dolus directus coloratus* — direct intent) squarely creates a non-dischargeable debt. Even absent a criminal conviction, if a civil court finds that the debt arose from Szklarski's deliberate act constituting an offence, the court may apply the exclusion.

This is potentially the most valuable consequence of pursuing the criminal track: **locking the debt so that Szklarski cannot escape it through insolvency**, regardless of how liberal the 2020 reform is.

### F.4 — Art. 191 KK Red Line: When Does the Threat Become Criminal Coercion?

**Art. 191 § 2 KK** penalises using a *groźba bezprawna* (unlawful threat) to compel repayment of a debt (*wymuszenie zwrotu wierzytelności*): imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years.

**Art. 115 § 12 KK** defines *groźba bezprawna* as including:
- A threat to commit a crime against the addressee
- A threat to initiate criminal proceedings or other proceedings where a financial penalty may be imposed
- **Exception:** A threat of criminal proceedings does **NOT** constitute *groźba bezprawna* if it *"ma ona jedynie na celu ochronę prawa naruszonego przestępstwem"* — if its sole purpose is to protect the right harmed by the crime.

**The SN has consistently distinguished:**
- **Permissible:** Informing a debtor that, if the debt is not paid, you intend to file a *zawiadomienie o popełnieniu przestępstwa* — provided this is stated as a statement of legal rights, not as a conditional transaction (*"płać albo zgłoszę na policję"*).
- **Criminal:** Using the threat of criminal proceedings as a bargaining tool in a manner that makes the threat contingent on non-payment in a way that amounts to extortion — particularly where there are no genuine grounds for a criminal complaint.

**Practical wording guidance (use in any written communication to Szklarski):**
> *"Chciałbym poinformować Pana, że rozważam złożenie zawiadomienia o podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa z art. 286 § 1 KK. Decyzja ta jest moim prawem jako pokrzywdzonego i zostanie podjęta niezależnie od kwestii spłaty długu. Jednocześnie informuję, że jeżeli dług zostanie dobrowolnie zwrócony w terminie [X], rozważę, czy dalsze postępowanie karne leży w interesie publicznym."*

Translation: *"I wish to inform you that I am considering filing a criminal complaint for suspected offence under art. 286 § 1 KK. This decision is my legal right as the injured party and will be taken independently of the question of debt repayment. I also inform you that if the debt is voluntarily repaid by [date], I will consider whether further criminal proceedings are in the public interest."*

This framing separates the criminal complaint from the debt collection demand, while leaving open the implication that restitution may affect the decision — **which is legally accurate**, as restitution under art. 46 KK and art. 59a KK can affect prosecutorial and judicial discretion. Avoid: *"Jeżeli nie zapłacisz, zawiadomię policję"* (conditional/quid pro quo framing — risk area for art. 191 § 2 KK).

## Section G — Risks to Creditor

### G.1 — Art. 234 KK (False Accusation)

**Statutory text:** Whoever, before an authority empowered to prosecute or adjudicate, falsely accuses another person of committing a criminal offence, shall be liable to a fine, restriction of liberty, or imprisonment up to 2 years.

**When triggered:** Art. 234 KK requires **deliberate falsehood** — the complainant must *know* the accusation is false. A good-faith criminal complaint based on available evidence, even if the prosecution ultimately fails to secure a conviction, does **not** constitute a false accusation. The standard is subjective bad faith, not objective incorrectness. Magnus, acting on genuine evidence (the written refund agreement, non-refund, false bankruptcy claim), has a strong good-faith defence. The risk is negligible if the complaint is honest and based on actual evidence.

**Mitigation:** Frame the *zawiadomienie* accurately — do not assert fraud as a certainty; assert a *uzasadnione podejrzenie* (reasonable suspicion) and present the evidence, leaving the determination of guilt to the prosecutor and court.

### G.2 — Defamation / RODO Exposure from Public Statements

**Defamation (art. 212 KK):** Publicly attributing criminal conduct to Szklarski on social media before a conviction carries real defamation risk. Describing him as a "fraudster" or "criminal" publicly is defamatory without a conviction. Safer: factual descriptions of the commercial dispute (unpaid debt, no delivery, EPU case filed) without characterisation as crime. After a conviction, the risk disappears.

**RODO (GDPR) compliance:** Szklarski's personal data (PESEL, address, business records) obtained from public registers (CEIDG, KRZ) may be used for legitimate enforcement purposes under art. 6(1)(f) RODO (legitimate interest). Publishing personal data on social media warning portals without legal basis risks RODO complaint and UODO (data protection authority) fine. Legal analysis recommended before any public posting.

### G.3 — Risk of Own Conduct Being Examined

Low risk in this case. Magnus paid via bank transfer (traceable), had a genuine commercial purpose, and is seeking refund of a legitimate payment. There is no apparent conduct that would attract criminal scrutiny. The sole theoretical risk would be if Magnus were to approach Szklarski in a way that crosses the art. 191 § 2 KK line — addressed in Section F.4.

### G.4 — Impact on EPU Civil Case: Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC

**Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC** allows the civil court to **optionally** (*może*) suspend civil proceedings if a criminal determination of facts could affect the civil case outcome. Suspension is **not automatic** and is **not mandatory** — it is within the court's discretion. SN case law confirms: *"Zawieszenie postępowania cywilnego z uwagi na toczące się postępowanie karne nie jest obligatoryjne"* ([SA Szczecin, III APz 6/17](https://orzeczenia.szczecin.sa.gov.pl/content/$N/155500000001521_III_APz_000006_2017_Uz_2017-09-29_001)).

In practice, the EPU at Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód is an automated, documentary proceeding. The EPU court issues a *nakaz zapłaty* (payment order) if the formal requirements are met — it does not engage in factual examination. There is **no realistic risk** that the EPU court will suspend proceedings because a criminal complaint was filed. The EPU proceeds on documentary evidence of the debt; the criminal question of intent is irrelevant to the civil payment order. If Szklarski opposes the EPU order and the case shifts to ordinary civil proceedings (*postępowanie zwykłe*), the possibility of art. 177 suspension becomes slightly more relevant — but even then, courts are reluctant to suspend civil proceedings for years to wait for a criminal verdict.

**Recommended approach:** Pursue EPU aggressively. The criminal complaint does not harm the EPU and may produce useful investigative information.

5. **Civil discovery discoveries feed into criminal file:** Any assets revealed through the komornik's inquiry, or bank account information obtained via civil execution, can be communicated to the prosecutor under art. 304 § 1 KPK (any person may notify the prosecutor of a suspected offence).

### H.2 — Criminal vs. Civil Asset Freeze: Direct Comparison

| Feature | Criminal Zabezpieczenie (art. 291 KPK) | Civil Zabezpieczenie (art. 730 KPC) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Formal charges issued | Court order on creditor's application |
| **Evidence threshold** | *Dostateczne podejrzenie* (sufficient suspicion) — lower | *Wiarygodność roszczenia* + *interes prawny* — higher |
| **Speed** | Days after charges | Weeks to months (court scheduling) |
| **Initiated by** | Prosecutor (ex officio or on pokrzywdzony's request) | Creditor's written application to civil court |
| **Cost to creditor** | Free | Court fee (typically 100–300 PLN) + potential security deposit |
| **Assets covered** | All assets on suspect; wide scope | Creditor must identify specific assets |
| **Duration** | Until verdict or lifting | Limited (court specifies duration) |

**Conclusion:** Criminal *zabezpieczenie* is faster, cheaper, and lower-threshold once charges are issued. This makes the criminal filing strategically valuable even if conviction probability is uncertain — because it unlocks the faster asset-freeze mechanism.

### H.3 — Cross-Pollination of Evidence

- **Civil evidence → Criminal file:** All documents in the EPU case, once filed, can be referenced in the *zawiadomienie*. EPU records are publicly accessible (with case number) and the prosecutor can formally request the file.
- **Criminal investigation → Civil case:** If the prosecutor compels Szklarski's bank records, supplier contracts, and business communications, this evidence will appear in the criminal file. The pokrzywdzony's pełnomocnik can request access to the criminal file (art. 156 § 5 KPK — access to case materials for pokrzywdzony after completion of preparatory proceedings). This information can then inform the civil enforcement strategy.
- **Warning:** Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC means that if the civil case (after EPU opposition) reaches a stage where the fraud question is determinative, the civil court may choose to await the criminal verdict. This suspension risk is manageable — ensure the EPU *nakaz zapłaty* is obtained and becomes final before any criminal proceedings develop far enough to trigger this risk.
