brief at `criminal-leverage-research-brief.md`. Goal: a single, opinionated
map of what each researcher converged on, where they diverged, and what is
actually actionable.

GPUcomputer for a custom PC; no delivery; written refund promise; no refund;
EPU Nc-e 552126/26 filed 8 April 2026; debtor claims "bankruptcy protection"

## 1. Strong consensus across all sources

### 1.1 The primary charge is art. 286 § 1 KK (oszustwo / fraud)

All four agree. Statutory text uniformly cited; penalty 6 months–8 years;
limitation 15 years (offence well under the limitation horizon — runs to
~2041). Public prosecution under *zasada legalizmu* (art. 10 KPK), so the
prosecutor is obligated to examine a credible complaint.

### 1.2 Art. 294 § 1 KK (aggravated form) does NOT apply

The threshold for *mienie znacznej wartości* is **over 200,000 PLN** (art.
115 § 5 KK). 155,000 PLN is below the line by 45,000 PLN. This matters in
two ways: (a) the base 6 mo–8 yr range applies, not 1–10 years; (b) prosecutors
generally treat sub-200k single-victim cases with lower urgency.

### 1.3 The dominant doctrinal obstacle is the *dolus directus coloratus* requirement at contract formation

All four cite the same problem: art. 286 requires direct, purpose-coloured
intent **at the moment of the contractual act**, not later. Recklessness and
conditional intent are insufficient.

Two sources (Perplexity Computer, Perplexity Tactical) flag the **2025
Supreme Court ruling SN III KK 216/25 (17.06.2025)** as the single most
important authority against us: SN held that taking an advance, failing to
deliver, and failing to refund — without more — is civil breach, not fraud.
The act description must identify a *specific method* of *wprowadzenie w
błąd*. Plain "didn't deliver, didn't refund" complaints get dismissed under
art. 17 § 1 pkt 2 KPK.

### 1.4 The winning framing is omission-based deception (przemilczenie)

All four converge on: the strongest theory is not "he didn't deliver" but
"he obtained payment by concealing material facts" — specifically (a) the
real supplier risk (uninsured Hong Kong transfer), and/or (b) pre-existing
insolvency or inability to perform, and/or (c) any pattern of taking
prepayments from other customers without delivering.

Key authority: **SN V KK 240/17** (and SN IV KK 631/21, SN II KK 465/23) —
knowingly concealing an objectively existing circumstance that materially
affects ability to perform is fraud, even when the perpetrator otherwise
"intended" to perform.

Practical complaint wording (all sources agree): plead *uzasadnione
podejrzenie popełnienia przestępstwa* ("reasonable suspicion"), not "he
definitely committed fraud."

### 1.5 Articles that all sources rule OUT

- **Art. 296 KK (nadużycie zaufania)** — requires a fiduciary/management
  duty over the victim's property. A seller is not a fund manager. Hard
  no.
- **Art. 297 KK (credit fraud)** — only applies to banks/public bodies.
- **Art. 304 KK (wyzysk/usury)** — requires the victim to be in
  *przymusowe położenie*. Not us.
- **Art. 286 § 3 KK (wypadek mniejszej wagi)** — for trivial sums; 155k
  is too high.

### 1.6 "Reckless handling of customer money" is NOT a standalone Polish criminal offence

All four explicitly answer Hypothesis 2 with: **no.** No segregation duty,
no escrow mandate, no general criminal duty of disclosure for an ordinary
merchant. Once payment hits the seller's account it is the seller's
working capital. The only criminal hook for that intuition is omission-based
art. 286 KK (see 1.4) plus arts. 301 § 3 / 302 § 1 KK if a multi-creditor
insolvency pattern exists.

There is also no clean *wykroczenie* (misdemeanour) fallback — art. 134 /
121 KW are limited to small-value commercial cheats. 155k is firmly in the
*przestępstwo* zone.

### 1.7 Secondary criminal angles worth pleading in the alternative

| Article | When it bites | Strength now | Strength later |
|---|---|---|---|
| **art. 300 § 1 KK** — frustrating creditor satisfaction (asset removal under threatened insolvency) | Pre-EPU; *wnioskowe* (motion required); up to 3 yrs | Weak — no evidence of asset movement yet | Moderate if asset transfers emerge |
| **art. 300 § 2 KK** — same conduct in order to frustrate court/state order; *ex officio*; 3 mo–5 yrs | Triggers **after EPU nakaz zapłaty** issues and assets are *zagrożone zajęciem* | Not yet applicable | **Strong** post-nakaz if any asset movement |
| **art. 300 § 3 KK** — multi-creditor aggravation; 6 mo–8 yrs | If pattern of damaged creditors emerges | Unknown | Moderate–strong if other victims surface |
| **art. 301 § 2 KK** — intentionally inducing own insolvency, multiple creditors | Requires *kilku wierzycieli* (≥ 2–3) | Conditional | Conditional |
| **art. 301 § 3 KK** — *reckless* induction of insolvency (squandering assets, obviously imprudent transactions) | Closest match to the "uninsured Hong Kong transfer" intuition | Conditional — needs multi-creditor evidence | Moderate |
| **art. 302 § 1 KK** — preferential payment when unable to satisfy all creditors | If Szklarski paid HK supplier *while already insolvent and knowing he couldn't refund us* | Weak — needs proof of pre-existing insolvency | Moderate if investigation reveals it |
| **art. 303 KK** — unreliable business documentation | Investigative lead (force prosecutor to secure books/invoices) | Weak | Investigative value |

### 1.8 Bankruptcy-Law lever: art. 21 + art. 373 PU

All four highlight this as a high-leverage *non-criminal* parallel track:

- A JDG is obligated to file for bankruptcy within **30 days** of becoming
  insolvent (3-month payment delay = presumption of insolvency, art. 11 § 1a
  PU).
- The KRZ shows no entry as of 2026-05-21, contradicting Szklarski's
  "bankruptcy protection" email. Either (a) he's lying, (b) he means he
  *intends* to file, or (c) very recent unindexed filing.
- Failure to file in time = personal civil liability under art. 21 § 3 PU +
  potential **1–10 year business ban** under art. 373 PU.
- A creditor can apply directly to the bankruptcy court for the art. 373 PU
  ban independently of any criminal track. This is **professionally
  catastrophic for a JDG** and is the single highest-pressure lever short of
  criminal asset freeze.

### 1.9 KKS (fiscal) angle is collateral, not primary

If VAT was charged on the 155k advance, Szklarski had a tax point on receipt.
A *donos* to KAS / Urząd Skarbowy may force him to defend his VAT/JPK
position. Relevant: art. 56 KKS (false declarations), art. 62 KKS (unreliable
/ empty invoices), art. 76 KKS (undue refund). The creditor has **no
standing** as *pokrzywdzony* in a KKS case (the State Treasury is the
victim), but the *donos* itself imposes audit pressure on the debtor — a
real leverage cost.

### 1.10 Zabezpieczenie majątkowe (art. 291–295 KPK) is the killer feature

This is unanimous across all four sources, and it's why the criminal track
is worth pursuing despite the dolus-directus obstacle:

- **Threshold:** lower than civil *zabezpieczenie roszczenia* (KPC art. 730).
  Once Szklarski becomes formally *podejrzany* (charged), the prosecutor
  needs only justified concern that future enforcement will be impossible/
  hindered.
- **Scope:** bank accounts, real property (hipoteka przymusowa), receivables,
  vehicles, securities — all the debtor's assets, not just identified ones.
- **Speed:** *postanowienie* by prosecutor, no court hearing required for
  the initial order; effective immediately.
- **Cost:** zero to the creditor.
- **Catch:** **only available after the debtor formally becomes
  *podejrzany*.** That takes months (typically 6–9 months of investigation
  before charges).

Civil *zabezpieczenie* under art. 730 KPC remains available in parallel and
should not be abandoned — it requires *wiarygodność roszczenia* + *interes
prawny* + sometimes a security deposit, but doesn't depend on criminal-track
timing.

### 1.11 Art. 46 KK restitution motion replaces the abolished powództwo adhezyjne

The classic *powództwo adhezyjne* (civil claim embedded in criminal trial)
was repealed in July 2015. Current mechanism is **art. 46 § 1 KK**: on
conviction and on the *pokrzywdzony*'s motion (filed before close of
trial, art. 49a KPK), the criminal court **must** order *obowiązek
naprawienia szkody*. This is a *środek kompensacyjny* (compensatory
measure), independently enforceable as an execution title.

### 1.12 Art. 491(21) PU — the bankruptcy-shield

Three of four sources (Gemini called this "The Nuclear Option") emphasize:
a final criminal conviction for an *intentional crime* (art. 286 KK is
intentional by definition) renders the resulting debt **non-dischargeable**
in *upadłość konsumencka* under art. 491(21) PU. This permanently bars
Szklarski from wiping the 155k via personal bankruptcy. Even an art. 46 KK
*środek kompensacyjny* order — without a separate civil judgment — survives
discharge.

This is the strategic prize that justifies the criminal track even if cash
recovery via criminal channels never materialises.

### 1.13 Art. 191 § 2 KK — the leverage red line

Polish law explicitly excludes from *groźba bezprawna* a threat to initiate
criminal proceedings **when the threat is aimed at protecting a right harmed
by the crime** (art. 115 § 12 KK). So announcing the complaint is legally
fine; conditional-transaction wording is not.

| Safe | Dangerous |
|---|---|
| "I am considering filing a complaint under art. 286 KK. This is my legal right as pokrzywdzony." | "Pay me or I report you to the police." |
| "If the debt is voluntarily repaid, I will consider whether further criminal proceedings are in the public interest." | "Pay 155k by Friday or I destroy your reputation." |
| "Given discrepancies in the Hong Kong supplier story, I suspect financial irregularities and will refer the matter to the Prosecutor's Office." | Any quid-pro-quo formulation tied to a payment deadline as the sole condition. |

All communications to Szklarski should be drafted by a Polish lawyer to stay
on the safe side. This protects against art. 191 § 2 KK (3 mo–5 yr) and
keeps a clean leverage posture.

### 1.14 Other risks to creditor — all low

- **Art. 234 KK (false accusation):** requires deliberate falsehood; phrasing
  the complaint as suspicion (not assertion) with documented evidence
  neutralises this.
- **Defamation (art. 212 KK):** real only if Magnus publicises accusations
  online or in customer warnings. A complaint to authorities is privileged.
- **RODO / GDPR:** processing debtor's data under art. 6(1)(f) (legitimate
  interest) for enforcement is fine. Don't publish.
- **Own conduct exposure:** none identified — straightforward private
  purchase paid by traceable bank transfer.
- **Civil case suspension under art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC:** discretionary,
  not automatic. EPU is documentary and is **unlikely** to be stayed. If
  Szklarski files *sprzeciw* and case moves to ordinary civil track, mild
  risk arises but courts are reluctant to suspend for years to await
  criminal verdict.

### 1.16 Procedural skeleton

1. **Filing target:** Prokuratura Rejonowa in Kraków covering Szklarski's
   CEIDG address. Likely *Śródmieście Wschód* or *Śródmieście Zachód*
   (ul. Mosiężnicza 2, 30-965) — depends on the exact virtual-office
   street. Browser-agent prompts in source #2 and #3 will resolve this.
2. **Form:** *Zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa wraz z
   wnioskiem o ściganie, wnioskami dowodowymi, wnioskiem o zabezpieczenie
   majątkowe oraz wnioskiem o naprawienie szkody*.
3. **Language:** Polish only. English exhibits attached with sworn Polish
   translations of key documents.
4. **Filing channel:** Registered paper post or lawyer in person at *biuro
   podawcze*. ePUAP/ESP requires Polish Profil Zaufany — practical only
   via the Polish lawyer. **Avoid ordinary email** for the main filing;
   it is procedurally fragile.
5. **Six-week rule (KPK):** if no notice of opening/refusal arrives within
   6 weeks, complain to the superior prosecutor.
6. **Refusal appeal:** *zażalenie* under art. 306 KPK, **7 days** from
   service of the decision, to the competent sąd rejonowy. Plan for this
   — it's likely to be needed.
7. **Subsidiary indictment:** after a second refusal upheld by the superior
   prosecutor, the *pokrzywdzony* may file a *subsidiary indictment* within
   1 month. Backstop.
8. **Remote testimony:** request videoconference under KPK art. 177 § 1a.
   Pełnomocnik handles everything except personal witness testimony.

## 3. Decision matrix — synthesized

| Scenario | Indicator | Move |
|---|---|---|
| **Debtor truly insolvent** (HK collapse is real, no assets, multiple creditors) | KRZ eventually shows filing; komornik finds nothing; multi-creditor evidence | File criminal complaint primarily for (a) **bankruptcy-shield via art. 491(21) PU**, (b) art. 21/373 PU business-ban application to the insolvency court, (c) investigation of whether collapse was caused by art. 301 § 3 KK reckless conduct. Limit lawyer spend; recovery via criminal track unlikely. |
| **Debtor stalling but solvent** (assets exist, business operating, ignoring civil) | CEIDG active, VAT active, no KRZ, no engagement with EPU | File criminal complaint immediately for maximum pressure. Request art. 291 KPK *zabezpieczenie* once charges issue. | **Debtor actively concealing assets** (transfers to third parties, new business entity) | CEIDG changes, real-property transfers, new JDG/sp. z o.o. registered, family-member entries | Strongest package: art. 286 + art. 300 § 1/§ 2 + art. 301 § 1 KK. ## 5. Single-paragraph honest bottom line

A Polish lawyer experienced in *windykacja* and *prawo karne gospodarcze*
would advise filing a parallel criminal complaint under art. 286 § 1 KK
with alternative directions under arts. 300–302 KK and art. 303 KK,
**not** as a primary recovery mechanism but as a strategic leverage
multiplier — the filing is free, *zasada legalizmu* obliges the prosecutor
to examine it, and the realistic prize is not a conviction (3–5 years out,
prosecutors dismiss 40–60% of sub-200k single-victim cases at the first
stage, and SN III KK 216/25 makes "mere non-return of advance" an automatic
dismissal trigger) but four collateral effects: (1) the *zabezpieczenie
majątkowe* under art. 291 KPK that becomes available once Szklarski is
formally *podejrzany* and freezes his assets at a lower threshold and at
zero cost compared to civil *zabezpieczenie*; (2) the bankruptcy-shield
under art. 491(21) PU that, on conviction, permanently bars discharge of
the 155k in any future *upadłość konsumencka*; (3) the existential pressure
on a JDG operator of a police summons, criminal record entry, and potential
business ban (art. 41 KK + art. 373 PU), which empirically tips stalling
debtors into settlement; and (4) the state's ability to compel evidence
(bank records, supplier verification, parallel-victim search) that is
otherwise inaccessible. The total realistic cost is 15,000–50,000 PLN over
3–5 years (10–32% of the claim). The "reckless handling of customer money"
intuition is not a freestanding Polish criminal offence, but the same
underlying facts — concealment of supplier risk and pre-existing insolvency
— support the omission-based fraud theory under SN V KK 240/17. File now,
keep the EPU running, file the art. 373 PU bankruptcy-ban application as
soon as the 30-day insolvency window has elapsed without a KRZ filing, and
stay strictly on the safe side of art. 191 § 2 KK in all communications
with Szklarski.
