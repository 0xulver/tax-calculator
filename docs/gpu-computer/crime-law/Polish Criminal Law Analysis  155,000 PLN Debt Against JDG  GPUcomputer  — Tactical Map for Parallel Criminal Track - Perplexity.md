# Polish Criminal Law Analysis: 155,000 PLN Debt Against JDG "GPUcomputer"
## Tactical Map for a Parallel Criminal Track

**Prepared:** May 2026 | **Debtor:** Mateusz Szklarski / GPUcomputer (JDG, NIP active, Kraków) | **Amount at issue:** 155,000 PLN

***

## Executive Summary

This brief maps every plausible Polish criminal-law angle arising from a 155,000 PLN pre-payment for a custom computer, non-delivery, a written refund promise, and subsequent silence. The core finding: **art. 286 § 1 KK (fraud/oszustwo) is the primary and most credible charge**, carrying a realistic threat of asset freeze (*zabezpieczenie majątkowe*) once the debtor is formally suspected. Art. 300 § 1 KK (creditor harm by asset removal) and art. 302 § 1 KK (preferential repayment) are secondary charges that become available if evidence of asset movement or selective payment to the Hong Kong supplier emerges. The criminal complaint is essentially **free to file**, takes hours to draft, and — critically — the *zabezpieczenie majątkowe* it can unlock operates faster and on a lower evidentiary standard than civil *zabezpieczenie roszczenia*. The realistic leverage value is **moderate to high** for a stalling-but-not-genuinely-insolvent debtor; **low** if the debtor is truly asset-stripped.

## A. Statutory Offences

### A.1 Art. 286 § 1 KK — Oszustwo (Fraud)

**Statutory text (Polish):**
> *Kto, w celu osiągnięcia korzyści majątkowej, doprowadza inną osobę do niekorzystnego rozporządzenia własnym lub cudzym mieniem za pomocą wprowadzenia jej w błąd albo wyzyskania błędu lub niezdolności do należytego pojmowania przedsiębranego działania, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 6 miesięcy do lat 8.*

**English translation:**
> Whosoever, in order to obtain financial gain, leads another person into an unfavourable disposition of their own or another's property by deceiving them, exploiting their error, or exploiting their incapacity to properly comprehend the action being taken, is liable to imprisonment from 6 months to 8 years.

**Elements the prosecutor must prove:**
1. The perpetrator acted with the *purpose* of obtaining financial gain (dolus directus — direct intent required; recklessness or negligence does not suffice).[^1][^2]
2. The perpetrator *introduced the victim into error*, exploited an existing error, or exploited the victim's incapacity to understand the transaction.[^1]
3. As a result of that error, the victim made an **unfavourable disposition of property** (*niekorzystne rozporządzenie mieniem*).[^2]
4. A causal link exists between the deception and the property disposition.[^1]

The key difficulty is element (1): **the deceptive intent must exist at the moment the contract was formed or the money was received** — not at some later point when commercial difficulties arose. However, Polish courts and the Supreme Court have accepted that intent can be inferred circumstantially; the SN ruled in case III KK 220/12 that it is sufficient to establish that "the victim would not have entered the contract had they known the true facts".[^3][^4]

**Aggravated form — Art. 294 § 1 KK (mienie znacznej wartości):** Applies when the property exceeds **200,000 PLN**. Penalty: 1–10 years. At **155,000 PLN, this case falls below that threshold**. The basic form (6 months–8 years) applies. Note: the Oct. 2023 KK amendment introduced an even higher threshold (mienie wielkiej wartości above 1,000,000 PLN) carrying 3–20 years.[^5]

**Penalty range under 2023-amended law:** 6 months–8 years imprisonment. Conditional suspension of the sentence (*warunkowe zawieszenie wykonania kary*) is only available for sentences up to **1 year** post-2023 reform (down from 2 years), sharply reducing the realistic prospect of walk-away suspended sentences for mid-range fraud.[^5]

**Statute of limitations:** Art. 286 § 1 KK carries a maximum of 8 years — it therefore predates under the 15-year rule (occurrence zagrożony powyżej 5 lat). If charges are presented before that 15 years expires, a further 10-year extension applies.[^6][^7]

**Prosecution mode:** *Ściganie z urzędu* (public prosecution, obligatory under the *zasada legalizmu*). Art. 286 § 1 KK is prosecuted ex officio — the State must investigate a credible complaint regardless of the victim's wishes. The exception is § 4 (offence against a close family member), which does not apply here.[^3]

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: MODERATE TO STRONG**

*Arguments in favour:*
- Seller accepted 155,000 PLN upfront for a custom item never in stock.
- Seller's own account (email) reveals the money was immediately forwarded to an offshore supplier with no escrow, no guarantee, no risk disclosure to the buyer — implying the buyer's money was never treated as held for the buyer's account.
- Seller agreed in writing to refund, then did nothing — a pattern courts treat as corroborating pre-existing intent (see SA Warszawa II AKa 505/18: intent must be inferred from the totality of conduct).[^2]
- The debtor's claimed "bankruptcy protection" is unverifiable and absent from KRZ — suggesting the insolvency narrative may be constructed post-hoc.
- Internet-retailer fraud involving pre-payments and non-delivery is precisely the paradigm Polish courts most frequently prosecute under art. 286 KK.[^8][^3]

*Arguments against (weaknesses):*
- **Single most difficult hurdle:** the debtor will argue he genuinely intended to fulfil the order and the supplier's collapse is a *force majeure* — this is the classic "failed businessman, not fraudster" defence.[^3]
- The defence works in civil court (it is simply non-performance) and may persuade a prosecutor to classify the case as a civil dispute.
- There is no direct evidence (bank records, communications with the Hong Kong supplier, or prior similar complaints from other customers) proving pre-existing fraud intent. Such evidence would require prosecutorial investigative powers.

*What would strengthen the case:* Evidence that (a) the Hong Kong supplier was already known to be unreliable before January 2026; (b) other customers of GPUcomputer paid and received nothing around the same time (pattern evidence); (c) the debtor had personal financial difficulties or other debts pre-dating the transaction; (d) the debtor continued taking new orders after the supplier collapsed.

### A.2 Art. 300 § 1 and § 2 KK — Udaremnienie zaspokojenia wierzycieli (Frustrating Creditor Satisfaction)

**Statutory text:**
> *§ 1. Kto, w razie grożącej mu niewypłacalności lub upadłości, udaremnia lub uszczupla zaspokojenie swojego wierzyciela przez to, że usuwa, ukrywa, zbywa, darowuje, niszczy, rzeczywiście lub pozornie obciąża albo uszkadza składniki swojego majątku, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności do lat 3.*
>
> *§ 2. Kto, w celu udaremnienia wykonania orzeczenia sądu lub innego organu państwowego, udaremnia lub uszczupla zaspokojenie swojego wierzyciela przez to, że [usuwa, ukrywa, zbywa…], podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*
>
> *§ 3. Jeżeli czyn określony w § 1 wyrządził szkodę wielu wierzycielom, sprawca podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 6 miesięcy do lat 8.*

**English translation:**
> § 1: Whosoever, facing threatened insolvency or bankruptcy, frustrates or diminishes a creditor's satisfaction by removing, concealing, selling, gifting, destroying, actually or fictitiously encumbering, or damaging their own property assets, is liable to imprisonment up to 3 years.
> § 2: Whosoever does the same in order to frustrate execution of a court or other state-authority order is liable to 3 months–5 years.

**Elements:**
1. A threatened insolvency or bankruptcy exists (§ 1) **or** a court order is pending execution (§ 2).[^9]
2. The debtor takes a specific act (removal, concealment, sale, fictitious encumbrance, etc.) against their own property.
3. This act frustrates or diminishes creditor satisfaction.[^10][^9]

**Prosecution mode:** § 1 — *wnioskowy* (requires victim's motion if the State Treasury is not the victim), art. 300 § 4 KK. § 2 — *ściganie z urzędu* (ex officio). Critical: once an EPU order is issued or even *zagrożone zajęciem* (threatened with seizure), § 2 applies with heavier penalties and ex-officio prosecution.[^11]

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: WEAK NOW, potentially MODERATE after EPU order is issued**

The EPU was filed 8 April 2026 and is pending. Once a payment order (*nakaz zapłaty*) issues and becomes *zagrożone zajęciem*, § 2 is engaged — any subsequent asset movement by the debtor could trigger it. There is currently no public evidence of asset removal, but if investigation reveals: sale of business equipment, real property transfers, or transfers to family members after January 2026, § 1 and/or § 2 come into play. The "multiple creditors" aggravation in § 3 is plausible if other GPUcomputer customers were similarly defrauded.

**Note:** You need the EPU nakaz zapłaty to become enforceable before § 2 fully applies. The civil track directly feeds the criminal track here.

### A.3 Art. 302 § 1 KK — Faworyzowanie wierzycieli (Preferential Creditor Treatment)

**Statutory text:**
> *Kto, w razie grożącej mu niewypłacalności lub upadłości, nie mogąc zaspokoić wszystkich wierzycieli, spłaca lub zabezpiecza tylko niektórych, czym działa na szkodę pozostałych, podlega karze grzywny, ograniczenia wolności albo pozbawienia wolności do lat 2.*

**English translation:**
> Whosoever, in the face of threatened insolvency or bankruptcy, being unable to satisfy all creditors, repays or secures only some of them, thereby acting to the detriment of the rest, is liable to a fine, restriction of liberty, or imprisonment up to 2 years.

**Elements:**
1. Threatened insolvency or bankruptcy.
2. The debtor cannot satisfy all creditors.
3. The debtor consciously pays or secures *some* creditors while leaving others unsatisfied.
4. This harms at least one other creditor.[^12][^13]

**Prosecution mode:** *Ściganie z urzędu* (ex officio).[^14]

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: WEAK NOW, MODERATE if Hong Kong supplier payment established**

The debtor's email states he forwarded your payment to the Hong Kong supplier (another "creditor" in the sense that the debtor owed a purchase price to that supplier). If the debtor was already insolvent when he did so, paying that supplier with your money while knowing he could not satisfy you may constitute art. 302 § 1. However, the case law is split on whether a supplier in a simple purchase chain constitutes a "creditor" for purposes of art. 302, and the SA Wrocław II AKa 261/11 emphasised the article primarily covers commercial/financial debt relationships. This theory requires prosecutorial access to bank records and the debtor's financial state in January 2026 to prove. Treat as supplementary, not primary.[^14]

### A.4 Art. 296 KK — Nadużycie zaufania (Breach of Fiduciary Duty)

**Statutory text (abbreviated):**
> *Kto, będąc obowiązany na podstawie przepisu ustawy, decyzji właściwego organu lub umowy do zajmowania się sprawami majątkowymi lub działalnością gospodarczą osoby fizycznej, prawnej albo jednostki organizacyjnej niemającej osobowości prawnej, przez nadużycie udzielonych mu uprawnień lub niedopełnienie ciążącego na nim obowiązku, wyrządza jej znaczną szkodę majątkową, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności od 3 miesięcy do lat 5.*

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: NOT APPLICABLE**

Art. 296 KK requires the perpetrator to be **obligated to manage the victim's property affairs** by law, administrative decision, or contract. This is a classic managerial/fiduciary liability statute covering directors, trustees, and asset managers. A simple vendor selling goods to a buyer is **not** obligated to manage the buyer's property affairs. The relationship is one of contract/purchase, not fiduciary management. Academic commentary and case law consistently exclude ordinary commercial counterparties from the scope of art. 296. This theory has **no basis in your fact pattern**.[^15][^16][^17][^18]

The seller did not obtain anything from a bank or public body using false documents. This article does not apply to a private consumer paying a commercial vendor for goods.[^19][^20]

### A.6 Art. 304 KK — Wyzysk (Usury/Exploitation)

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: NOT APPLICABLE**

Your case involves a standard commercial sale, not a loan, and you were not in a "forced position" (przymusowe położenie) in the legal sense. This article does not apply.[^21][^22]

### A.7 Art. 301 KK — Doprowadzenie do upadłości lub niewypłacalności

**Statutory text (abbreviated):**
> *§ 2. Tej samej karze podlega, kto będąc dłużnikiem kilku wierzycieli doprowadza do swojej upadłości lub niewypłacalności.*
> *§ 3. Kto będąc dłużnikiem kilku wierzycieli w sposób lekkomyślny doprowadza do swojej upadłości lub niewypłacalności, w szczególności przez trwonienie części składowych majątku, zaciąganie zobowiązań lub zawieranie transakcji oczywiście sprzecznych z zasadami gospodarowania, podlega karze pozbawienia wolności do lat 2.*

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: WEAK / SUPPLEMENTARY**

Art. 301 § 2 requires intentional self-induction of insolvency to the detriment of multiple creditors. § 3 covers reckless self-insolvency. Both require evidence that: (a) the debtor has multiple creditors, and (b) the insolvency was intentionally or recklessly brought about. If other GPUcomputer customers have been defrauded similarly (pattern visible on consumer complaint forums or Ceneo), § 3 becomes plausible as a supplementary charge but remains secondary to art. 286. File as a supplementary charge alongside art. 286.[^9]

### A.8 Art. 373 Prawo Upadłościowe — Zakaz prowadzenia działalności / Failure to timely file for bankruptcy

**Statutory text:**
> *Art. 21 PU: Dłużnik będący osobą fizyczną prowadzącą działalność gospodarczą jest obowiązany nie później niż w terminie trzydziestu dni od dnia, w którym wystąpiła podstawa do ogłoszenia upadłości, złożyć we właściwym sądzie wniosek o ogłoszenie upadłości.*
>
> *Art. 373 PU: Sąd może orzec pozbawienie na okres od jednego do dziesięciu lat prawa prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej na własny rachunek.*

**Key mechanism:** A JDG debtor (sole proprietor with unlimited personal liability) who becomes insolvent must file for bankruptcy within **30 days**. Failure to do so:[^23][^24]
- Creates **personal civil liability** to creditors for losses attributable to the late filing;
- Can result in a court order prohibiting the debtor from operating a business for **1–10 years** under art. 373 PU;[^24][^23]
- Can disqualify the debtor from serving as a director, supervisory board member, or representative of any company, cooperative, foundation, or association.[^23]

**Fit to your facts — Assessment: MODERATE LEVERAGE TOOL (civil/insolvency, not strictly criminal)**

The debtor claims to have "filed for bankruptcy protection" but no KRZ entry exists as of 21 May 2026. This is either: (a) false — meaning a potential art. 286 aggravating factor (false statement); or (b) a very recent filing not yet indexed; or (c) incorrect terminology by the debtor (he may mean he *intends* to file). If the debtor was insolvent from approximately January 2026 and has not filed by end of February 2026, he is already in default of art. 21 PU. A creditor can file a **separate application to the insolvency court under art. 373 PU** for the business ban — independently of any criminal complaint. This is an underutilised but powerful tool: it does not require criminal conviction, only a judicial finding that the debtor failed to timely file and this harmed creditors.

**Assessment: VERY WEAK**

However, **this route does not directly benefit you as creditor** — it is a state-vs-taxpayer matter. You can mention the VAT in your zawiadomienie as a supplementary note, but it should not be the primary legal basis.[^25]

## B. The "Reckless Handling of Customer Money" Question (Hypothesis 2)

This is the most important analytical question, and the answer is nuanced.

### Does Polish criminal law impose a duty on a merchant to handle pre-payments in a specific way (segregation, escrow, risk disclosure)?

**Short answer: No such distinct statutory obligation exists in Polish criminal law as a freestanding criminal offence.** There is no Polish equivalent of, for example, the German *Untreue* applied to advance payments, nor a criminalised duty to segregate customer funds for an ordinary commercial vendor. Art. 296 KK (nadużycie zaufania) would be the closest candidate — but as explained in A.4, it requires a pre-existing legal or contractual obligation to manage the buyer's property affairs, which does not exist in a standard purchase contract.[^17][^15]

### However: "Reckless pre-payment forwarding" *can* constitute art. 286 KK through the omission/deception route

Polish Supreme Court jurisprudence accepts that **fraud by omission** (przemilczenie) is possible under art. 286 § 1 KK when the perpetrator: (a) has a duty to inform and wilfully withholds material information; or (b) withholds information that, if disclosed, would have prevented the victim from contracting. Specifically, the SN confirmed in III KK 220/12 that the relevant question is whether "the victim would not have entered the contract had they known the true facts."[^4]

**Applied to your case:** A seller who:
- Took 155,000 PLN upfront for a custom item not in stock,
- Immediately forwarded the funds to a foreign supplier under an uninsured, unescreened contract,
- Did not disclose that payment would be immediately remitted offshore,
- Did not disclose the commercial and counterparty risk the buyer was effectively taking on,

**may have** created a false impression of safety through omission — i.e., by not disclosing that the buyer's money was being placed at the risk of a third party, the seller allowed the buyer to labour under the (false) impression that the seller retained financial responsibility. If the seller knew at the time of contract that the Hong Kong supplier was risky, unreliable, or already in difficulty, this omission is stronger evidence of intent to deceive.[^8][^3]

**The theory has a basis in Polish criminal law — but it is not robust on current facts alone.** It requires the prosecutor to investigate what the seller knew about the Hong Kong supplier's reliability *before* January 2026. This is state-investigative-powers territory: bank records, emails with the supplier, and potentially customs/import records.

### Consumer law angle: Was there a "duty to inform" violation that is separately criminalised?

Under the Ustawa o prawach konsumenta (Consumer Rights Act, implementing EU Directive 2011/83), the seller had statutory duties to disclose material transaction information before contract. Violation of those duties is a civil matter and subject to UOKiK administrative enforcement — it is **not** directly criminalised. However, wilful and systematic violation of consumer information duties by a merchant targeting a broad class of consumers can support a UOKiK complaint (see Section A.10 below).[^26][^27]

### Is there a *wykroczenie* (misdemeanour) that applies where the criminal code does not?

The *Kodeks wykroczeń* does not contain a freestanding misdemeanour for "reckless pre-payment handling." Art. 135 Kodeksu wykroczeń covers certain commercial misrepresentation, and art. 138 covers refusal to provide contracted services — but neither reaches the full factual pattern here. The civil system (and the criminal fraud charge) is the intended mechanism for this fact pattern. **Conclusion: the "reckless handling" theory is not a freestanding criminal offence but is a supporting argument within the art. 286 KK fraud charge.**

The territorial competence is determined by where the offence occurred — which, for an online transaction, is typically either the seller's location (Kraków) or where the buyer suffered harm. Filing with a Kraków prokuratura is well-supported. If your residence or the bank where the transfer originated is elsewhere, that location's prokuratura rejonowa is also competent, and you could file there for convenience if you have a Polish address.*

### Form of the zawiadomienie

The *zawiadomienie o popełnieniu przestępstwa* (or more precisely: *zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa*) is **a free-text document — no statutory form is required**. It must include:[^33][^28]
- Your name, address, and contact details (pokrzywdzony/complainant)
- A clear description of the facts, the time and place, and the nature of the alleged offence
- The identity of the suspected perpetrator (name, NIP, REGON, CEIDG address)
- A list of exhibits (attachments)
- A statement under art. 304 § 2 KPK (social duty to report)
- Date and signature

**Language:** The zawiadomienie may be filed in English if accompanied by a certified (sworn) Polish translation. In practice, Polish authorities strongly prefer Polish-language submissions. A sworn translator (*tłumacz przysięgły*) is required for certified translations. However, the authorities are legally obligated to proceed even if you file in a foreign language — they may arrange a court interpreter at state expense for the formal procedural steps. Practically: have a Polish lawyer draft the zawiadomienie directly in Polish.[^28]

**Filing methods (in order of reliability):**
1. **Email to the biuro podawcze** (PDF scan, signed): biuro.podawcze.prkra-sza@prokuratura.gov.pl — practical for remote filers[^30]
2. **ePUAP / skrytka ESP**: Requires a *Profil Zaufany* (verified Polish electronic identity) — **you cannot use this remotely without one**. Route to Polish lawyer.
3. **Registered post** (list polecony): Sends the document with proof of delivery[^28]
4. **In person**: Not required[^34][^28]

**Required attachments:**
- Invoice / proof of payment (bank transfer confirmation)
- Order confirmation from gpucomputer.pl
- Written communications (the seller's written refund agreement, email explaining the Hong Kong supplier situation)
- Pre-court demand letter with proof of service
- EPU case filing confirmation (Nc-e 552126/26)
- CEIDG extract for the debtor
- KRZ search result (showing no bankruptcy entries as of 21 May 2026)
- *Biała lista* VAT confirmation

### Role of the pokrzywdzony (Injured Party)

Once a proceeding opens, you become the *pokrzywdzony* (injured party) with full standing as a party to the preparatory proceedings (*postępowanie przygotowawcze*). Rights include:[^35][^36]
- Access to the case file (at preparatory stage, subject to restrictions)[^37]
- Right to submit evidence requests (wnioski dowodowe)[^35]
- Right to be informed of all key decisions
- Right to file zażalenie (appeal) against refusal to open investigation or to stay[^38][^35]
- Right to declare yourself *oskarżyciel posiłkowy* (auxiliary prosecutor) once an indictment is filed — this preserves your standing throughout the trial phase[^39][^35]

**Duty to appear:** As pokrzywdzony you have a legal obligation to appear when summoned by police or prokuratura (art. 177 § 1 KPK). However:[^36]
- Non-resident witnesses/parties can request hearings via **rogatory letter** (*pomoc prawna*) through mutual legal assistance, or
- Can attend **via video-conference** in certain cases, or
- Can be represented by a **pełnomocnik** (attorney-at-law), who appears on your behalf for all procedural steps except for testimony that requires personal attendance.[^39]

In practice, for a remote complainant (non-resident), expect 1–3 summons over 2–3 years; the first is typically to clarify the complaint (*uzupełnienie zawiadomienia*), subsequent ones for witness interviews. A Polish pełnomocnik can handle most steps remotely on your behalf.

### The abolished powództwo adhezyjne — and the current mechanism

The *powództwo adhezyjne* (civil claim within criminal proceedings) was **abolished in July 2015**. The current mechanism is:[^40][^41]
- **Wniosek o obowiązek naprawienia szkody (art. 46 § 1 KK):** You may request — up to the moment the trial evidence phase closes — that the criminal court order the defendant to repair the damage (i.e., pay you 155,000 PLN) as a *środek kompensacyjny*. This is a penal measure, not a civil judgment, but it is enforceable as a title of execution.[^42][^40]
- If granted, this replaces the need for a separate civil enforcement action. However, the court's order is capped at the proven damage and depends on a conviction.

### Zażalenie na odmowę wszczęcia śledztwa

If the prokuratura refuses to open an investigation, you have the right to file a *zażalenie* (appeal) with the supervising court within **7 days** of receiving the refusal decision. The court may:[^38]
- Uphold the refusal (most common if facts genuinely suggest only civil dispute),
- Overturn it and order investigation (possible if your zawiadomienie is well-evidenced).

As *pokrzywdzony*, you also have the right to view the file before the appeal. If the court upholds the refusal, you may still file a new zawiadomienie with new evidence.[^38]

### Timeline expectations

| Stage | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| Receipt of zawiadomienie + initial review (czynności sprawdzające, art. 307 KPK) | 30 days (extendable to 6 weeks)[^43] |
| Decision: open investigation or refuse | Within 6 weeks of receipt |
| *Postępowanie przygotowawcze* (dochodzenie or śledztwo) | 3 months–3 years depending on complexity |
| Presentation of charges to suspect (*zarzuty*) | During investigation, once evidence threshold met |
| Indictment (*akt oskarżenia*) filed with court | After investigation closed |
| Trial (first instance) | 6 months–3 years after indictment |
| Final verdict (after appeals) | 3–7 years from filing in complex economic fraud |

*Economic fraud cases (art. 286 KK involving investigation of a JDG's books) regularly take 2–4 years from complaint to first-instance verdict.* The investigation alone frequently takes 1.5–2 years when bank records and cross-border supplier evidence is needed.

**Key cost advantage:** You do not pay court fees in criminal proceedings as pokrzywdzony. The cost cap for the complainant is typically the lawyer's fees. Successful conviction entitles you to claim legal costs reimbursement from the convicted defendant.[^39]

## E. Consequences for the Debtor

### Realistic sentencing under art. 286 § 1 KK for a 155,000 PLN, single-victim, first-offender

Post-October 2023 KK reform:
- **Suspended sentence** is only available for custodial sentences up to **1 year** (down from 2 years). For a single-victim fraud of 155,000 PLN by a first-time offender, courts typically issue 1–2 year custodial sentences, meaning partial or full suspension becomes limited but not impossible.[^46][^5]
- **Warunkowe zawieszenie wykonania kary** with probation period 2–5 years remains the most common outcome for first-time economic offenders, especially where restitution has been made or is ordered.[^47]
- **Warunkowe umorzenie postępowania** (conditional discontinuance with no conviction — available only where maximum penalty ≤ 5 years and offence not grave) is technically available under art. 66 § 1 KK but requires (1) uncontested facts, (2) first-time offender, and (3) low culpability. Given 155,000 PLN and the pattern of behaviour, courts are unlikely to apply it.[^48][^49]

### Ancillary measures available to the court

- **Obowiązek naprawienia szkody (art. 46 KK):** The court *must* order restitution if the pokrzywdzony requests it before the trial closes. This becomes a directly enforceable civil title.[^42]
- **Zakaz prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej (art. 41 § 2 KK):** The criminal court can order a business ban (separately from art. 373 PU proceedings) for a period up to 15 years.[^50]
- **Wpis do Krajowego Rejestru Karnego:** A criminal conviction creates a permanent criminal record entry visible to courts, employers, and licensing authorities.
- **Nawiązka (art. 46 § 2 KK):** If damage cannot be precisely established, the court can order an *equitable sum* up to 200,000 PLN as a substitute compensatory measure.

### Zabezpieczenie majątkowe — the most important practical mechanism

**Under art. 291–295 KPK**, once a person obtains the status of *podejrzany* (suspected person — after formal charges are presented), the prosecutor may issue a *postanowienie o zabezpieczeniu majątkowym* (asset freeze order) covering:
- Bank accounts (all balances and incoming funds up to the secured amount),
- Real property (hipoteka przymusowa — compulsory mortgage in land register),
- Vehicles, equipment, inventory,
- Shares in companies, cryptocurrency,
- Receivables.[^51][^52]

**This postanowienie is immediately effective as a title of execution** from the moment of issuance — it does not require a court hearing first. The realisation is handled by the prosecutor/komornik.[^51]

**Critical advantage over civil zabezpieczenie:**
- In civil proceedings, zabezpieczenie roszczenia requires: proof that the claim is credible (*prima facie* evidence) AND that enforcement without seizure would be difficult or impossible (*interes prawny*).
- In criminal proceedings, the standard is lower: the prosecutor needs only to believe that (a) a crime was committed, (b) the suspect may be ordered to pay damages or fines, and (c) execution may be jeopardised.
- The criminal freeze is triggered at the *postanowienie o zarzutach* stage, not at conviction.

**However:** The freeze is available only after the debtor formally becomes *podejrzany* (i.e., after charges are presented). This typically takes weeks to months after the investigation opens — not immediately upon filing the zawiadomienie.

## F. Leverage and Strategic Value

### How often does filing a criminal complaint cause a stalling debtor to settle in Poland?

There is no published empirical dataset on settlement rates following zawiadomienie filing in Poland. Based on published practitioner accounts:
- The **mere receipt of a police or prokuratura notification** that a criminal investigation has been opened against a JDG sole proprietor is a significant psychological and practical shock, particularly for a person operating under their own name.
- A **wezwanie na przesłuchanie** (police summons for interrogation) is often the point at which previously unresponsive debtors make contact to explore settlement.
- The **zabezpieczenie majątkowe** — freezing accounts — is the single most powerful coercive tool, as it can paralyse business operations within days of the order.[^51]

In commercial windykacja practice, experienced Polish attorneys report that a credibly evidenced art. 286 KK zawiadomienie has a meaningful deterrent effect on debtors who are stalling but possess assets. For a debtor who is genuinely insolvent, the leverage is lower (nothing to seize).

### Does a criminal proceeding affect the debtor's ability to file consumer/personal bankruptcy?

A JDG can file for *upadłość* (business bankruptcy) regardless of pending criminal proceedings. However:
- If assets are frozen under criminal *zabezpieczenie majątkowe*, the bankruptcy trustee cannot freely dispose of those assets — this creates a conflict between the criminal and insolvency regimes that can delay or complicate the insolvency, making the insolvency a less clean escape for the debtor.
- A criminal conviction for art. 286 KK **does not automatically block debt discharge** in bankruptcy — but it can be a basis for the insolvency court to **refuse** discharge under art. 369 § 1 PU (exceptional culpable conduct leading to insolvency).

### Does a criminal conviction affect dischargeability?

Art. 369 § 1 PU allows the insolvency court to refuse debt discharge where the debtor caused their insolvency or significantly increased its extent through *intentional culpable conduct*. A criminal conviction for art. 286 KK (fraud) provides very strong evidence of intentional culpable conduct, making it likely that the court would refuse discharge of your specific debt.

### Art. 191 KK — Is it permissible to use a criminal complaint as settlement leverage?

This is the critical ethical and legal boundary question. **Polish law draws a clear distinction:**

**Permissible:** "Pay me what you owe me, or I will file a criminal complaint." This is *not* a *groźba bezprawna* (unlawful threat) under art. 191 § 1 KK, because art. 191 KK expressly excludes from "unlawful threat" a genuine threatened exercise of a legitimate right (the right to report a crime) aimed at recovering a genuine legal interest (the debt). The SN confirmed in case III KKN 195/01 that threatening to report a crime in order to recover a genuine debt does not constitute the offence of *wymuszenie zwrotu wierzytelności* under art. 191 § 2 KK — that offence requires the use of *violence or unlawful threat*, and a lawful threat of criminal proceedings is not unlawful.[^53][^54]

**Impermissible:** "Pay me, or I will file a criminal complaint alleging a more serious offence than I have evidence for, in order to destroy your reputation." Fabricating, exaggerating, or weaponising criminal accusations beyond what the evidence supports crosses into art. 234 KK (*fałszywe oskarżenie* — false accusation) territory and potentially art. 212 KK (defamation).[^55]

**Practical rule:** You may lawfully communicate to the debtor (via counsel) that a zawiadomienie is being prepared and that you would prefer to resolve the matter civilly first. This is routine in Polish *windykacja* practice and does not constitute extortion.

## H. Coordination with the Civil Track

**Optimal sequencing: File both simultaneously.**

| Track | Status | Key next step |
|---|---|---|
| Civil (EPU Nc-e 552126/26) | Active — awaiting nakaz zapłaty | Press for nakaz; once issued, begin civil enforcement (komornik) and simultaneously apply for civil zabezpieczenie roszczenia |
| Criminal (art. 286 KK zawiadomienie) | Not yet filed | File immediately; the zawiadomienie is free and initiates state investigative powers |
| Art. 373 PU (business ban application) | Not filed | File once civil title exists, or now if insolvency is established — independent of criminal track |
| UOKiK complaint | Not filed | File now as supplementary pressure (see below) |

**Interaction between civil and criminal *zabezpieczenie*:**
- Civil *zabezpieczenie roszczenia* (art. 730+ KPC) requires a court application, prima facie proof, and evidence of enforcement difficulty. In electronic proceedings (EPU), the court may be reluctant to grant it without an in-person hearing.
- Criminal *zabezpieczenie majątkowe* (art. 291 KPK) is issued by the prosecutor **unilaterally** once the debtor is *podejrzany*, without a court hearing. It is immediately effective. However, it only becomes available after charges are presented — not immediately upon filing the zawiadomienie.
- **Strategy:** File the criminal zawiadomienie now. Once investigation opens and the debtor is called for questioning (typically 3–6 months), you can prompt your pełnomocnik to formally request (*wniosek*) that the prosecutor apply *zabezpieczenie majątkowe* covering 155,000 PLN plus costs. This request should specify the debtor's known assets (CEIDG business, any real property discoverable via land register search, known bank accounts).

## I. Realistic Outcomes — Base Rates

### Conviction and dismissal statistics for art. 286 KK

Quantitative national statistics from the Ministerstwo Sprawiedliwości on art. 286 KK specifically for the sub-200k PLN range are not publicly granular. General indicators from available data:

- **Prokuratura dismissal rate (umorzenie postępowania):** Polish prosecutors dismiss (umarzają) a very significant proportion of economic fraud complaints, especially where: (a) the suspect can argue "failed business" vs. fraud; (b) there is only one victim; (c) the sum is below the aggravated threshold. The most common dismissal reason is *brak dostatecznych danych uzasadniających podejrzenie przestępstwa* (insufficient grounds to suspect an offence) or *nieznaczna społeczna szkodliwość* (low social harm). For internet sales fraud cases: the dismissal rate is estimated at 40–60% at the first stage, with a significant number reopened on zażalenie.[^56]
- **Where charges are laid:** Conviction rate for art. 286 KK cases that reach trial is high — above 80% nationally — because prosecutors file indictments only in well-evidenced cases.
- **Sentencing pattern:** First-time offenders in sub-200k PLN fraud receive **1–2 year custodial sentences, typically suspended** for 2–5 year probation periods with a mandatory *obowiązek naprawienia szkody* (restitution order). Post-2023 reform, full suspended sentences require the base term to be ≤ 1 year, so some defendants receive partial-suspended outcomes or fines combined with shorter custodial terms.[^47][^46]

### Typical reasons for umorzenie in similar cases

1. The debtor provides a credible alternative narrative (supplier collapse, force majeure);
2. No evidence of pre-existing fraudulent intent at contract formation;
3. Single victim (lower priority for strained prokuratura resources);
4. Sum below aggravated threshold (less prosecution appetite);
5. Debtor is cooperating with civil enforcement.

## Single-Paragraph Honest Summary

A Polish lawyer experienced in *windykacja* and *prawo karne gospodarcze* would tell you the following: filing the zawiadomienie under art. 286 § 1 KK is worth doing as a tactical measure, not as a primary recovery mechanism, and you should do it **now and in parallel with the civil track** rather than choosing between them. The filing is free, the *zasada legalizmu* obligates the prosecutor to review it, and the realistic leverage comes not from a conviction (which will take 3–6 years if it happens at all and is far from certain — prosecutors dismiss a significant portion of single-victim commercial fraud cases below the 200k PLN aggravated threshold) but from the chain of events the investigation triggers: the debtor receives a police visit or summons, their name appears in a pending criminal case, their business accounts can be frozen once charges are formally presented, and the threat to a sole proprietor of a *zakaz prowadzenia działalności* (business ban) is existential in a way that a civil judgment — enforceable but slow — is not. The weakest link is proving intent at contract formation, which is genuinely difficult without investigative access to bank records and the debtor's supplier communications, so be prepared for a possible *umorzenie* (dismissal) at the first stage, and plan to file a *zażalenie* — but even a refusal by the prokuratura creates a paper trail that may usefully accompany any future insolvency proceedings. Put more simply: the criminal route is a pressure amplifier on a stalling debtor, not a substitute for the civil route, and at 155,000 PLN it sits in the zone where Polish practitioners routinely recommend filing.
