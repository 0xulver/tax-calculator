## The hypotheses I want tested

I am not a lawyer and may be mis-naming offences. The agent should evaluate each hypothesis against actual Polish criminal law, not adopt my labels.

**Hypothesis 1 — Outright fraud (oszustwo, art. 286 KK).** If, at the time of taking my money, the seller knew or accepted that delivery was implausible (e.g., funds already lost, supplier already unreliable, business already insolvent), the transaction was fraud. This requires showing direct intent at the moment of contract formation.

**Hypothesis 2 — Reckless / negligent endangerment of customer funds.** My intuition is that taking customer pre-payment for a 155k PLN custom item and forwarding it to a Hong Kong supplier with no escrow, no insurance, no LC, no guarantee, no segregation, no risk disclosure to the customer — and then blaming the customer when that supplier fails — should be a recognised wrong. I do not know if Polish criminal law actually criminalises this for a standard B2C/B2B merchant (it likely does for fiduciaries under art. 296 KK, but I am not a fiduciary's beneficiary — I am a customer). I want this thoroughly tested.

**Hypothesis 3 — Pre-bankruptcy creditor crimes (art. 300–302 KK).** If the seller, foreseeing insolvency, is moving assets, paying selected creditors preferentially, hiding property, or otherwise frustrating creditors, that is a separate set of offences.

**Hypothesis 4 — Failure to file bankruptcy in time (art. 21 + art. 373 PU).** A JDG debtor who is insolvent must file for bankruptcy within 30 days. Failure carries personal civil liability for resulting creditor losses, and the debtor can be banned from running a business for 1–10 years.

**Hypothesis 5 — Unfair commercial practices.** As a consumer (or quasi-consumer), I may have grounds to engage UOKiK (Office of Competition and Consumer Protection) — not criminal but administrative pressure.

**Hypothesis 6 — Tax / fiscal offences (KKS).** If VAT was charged on the invoice and the seller will not deliver or refund, there may be VAT fraud or KKS angles.

**Hypothesis 7 — Anything else** that fits this fact pattern that I have not thought of.

## Useful primitives the agent should be aware of

- **Zasada legalizmu (art. 10 KPK):** Polish prosecutors are *obliged* to investigate credible complaints. They cannot ignore a properly filed zawiadomienie.
- **Pokrzywdzony (art. 49 KPK):** The injured party has standing to participate, appeal dismissals (*zażalenie*), access the file, and become *oskarżyciel posiłkowy* (auxiliary prosecutor) if the prosecutor declines.
- **Zażalenie na postanowienie o odmowie wszczęcia śledztwa:** Appeal mechanism when prosecutor refuses to open an investigation — submitted to the court.
- **Zabezpieczenie majątkowe (art. 291–295 KPK):** Criminal-procedure asset freeze, available *during* investigation. Standard is lower than civil zabezpieczenie (does not require proof of *interes prawny* in the civil sense). If applicable to this fact pattern, this could be the most valuable single feature of the criminal route.
- **Powództwo adhezyjne / wniosek o naprawienie szkody (art. 46 KK):** Mechanisms to attach a civil claim to a criminal proceeding so the criminal court orders restitution directly.
- **Art. 191 § 1 KK** and **art. 304 KPK:** Threatening criminal prosecution to extract a civil payment can itself be an offence — flag where the line is.
- **Konsumencka vs. B2B status:** I purchased the computer for personal use; I run a JDG in software engineering but the computer was not for business use. Whether the transaction is classified as B2C or B2B affects available consumer-protection routes — the agent should address both possibilities and tell me which classification is more defensible.
