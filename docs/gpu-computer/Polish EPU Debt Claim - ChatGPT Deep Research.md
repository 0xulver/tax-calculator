## If insolvency appears

### What bankruptcy would mean

If a Polish bankruptcy is actually declared, the legal landscape changes sharply. The bankruptcy order is effective when issued and is publicly announced; creditors are called to submit claims to the trustee (**syndyk**) within **30 days** from the public announcement in the register; and a creditor who wants to participate and whose claim must be established should file the claim **through the court IT system**. In bankruptcy, only interest accrued **up to the bankruptcy date** can be satisfied from the estate for unsecured claims; post-bankruptcy interest is generally not paid from the estate. citeturn16view1turn13view1turn13view2

For pending litigation, bankruptcy is also disruptive. After bankruptcy, proceedings concerning the bankruptcy estate are brought **only by or against the trustee**; a pending suit that was already started against the debtor can be resumed against the trustee only if, after using the bankruptcy-list procedure, the claim is **not** placed on the list of claims. In other words, if bankruptcy appears, your EPU and any follow-on ordinary claim stop being the main collection vehicle; the claims-list procedure becomes central. citeturn13view0turn15view2

For enforcement, the effect is even harsher. Enforcement against property entering the bankruptcy estate is automatically stayed, then discontinued after the bankruptcy order becomes final; and after the bankruptcy date, no new enforcement can be directed to estate assets. So if you eventually get a payment order or judgment **after** bankruptcy opens, that title typically serves mainly to help establish the claim in the insolvency process, not to let you leapfrog other creditors through a bailiff. citeturn13view0

### What restructuring would mean

Polish restructuring is more complicated because there are several tracks. The key point is that a restructuring opening or the public announcement of an arrangement day can protect the debtor from enforcement **before** liquidation-style bankruptcy occurs. The Restructuring Law defines what claims the arrangement covers; as a rule it covers personal claims arising before the restructuring opening, and it also covers interest for the period from the opening date. Your unsecured refund claim would therefore ordinarily be a claim meant to be handled **inside** the restructuring framework. citeturn10view3turn10view4

In accelerated arrangement and ordinary arrangement proceedings, opening the proceeding does **not** fully block creditors from starting court proceedings to establish claims that should appear on the list, but enforcement of claims covered by the arrangement is stayed or barred. The judicial supervisor (**nadzorca sądowy**) enters ongoing proceedings by operation of law, and the debtor cannot validly acknowledge, waive, or settle claims in those proceedings without the supervisor’s consent. So you may still litigate to establish the claim if necessary, but you usually cannot bypass the collective process by running a bailiff against covered claims. citeturn10view1turn11view0turn9view2turn10view2

In **sanation**, the protective effect is still stronger. Proceedings concerning the sanation estate are brought only by or against the administrator (**zarządca**), and enforcement and execution of security against estate assets are barred after opening. That means that if the debtor lands in sanation, your realistic civil strategy becomes “establish the claim correctly inside the restructuring and react to the list/voting process,” not “race the bailiff.” citeturn11view1turn10view2

### What you must do as creditor if a proceeding appears

If **bankruptcy** appears in KRZ, your first move is not to argue with the debtor by email. It is to:

- note the **court, case number, date of announcement, and trustee**;
- submit an electronic **zgłoszenie wierzytelności** through KRZ / the court IT system within the **30-day** period stated in the bankruptcy announcement;
- include the principal, interest calculated to the bankruptcy date, the contractual and email basis, and attach the evidence set;
- watch whether the claim is recognized on the list and react if the trustee disputes or omits it. citeturn16view1turn13view1turn15view2

If **restructuring** appears, your tasks are different. In restructuring the list of claims is prepared from the debtor’s books and documents, and claims are separated into those covered by the arrangement by operation of law and those included only with consent. Practically, that means you should immediately identify the **supervisor/administrator**, confirm whether your claim is on the list and in what amount, and if it is omitted or disputed, file the appropriate objection (**sprzeciw**) in the restructuring system within the deadline stated in the proceeding. This is especially important because the statute sets a document-driven claims-list process and specific objection review rules. citeturn10view5turn11view5

If what appears is a **postępowanie o zatwierdzenie układu** with a public arrangement-day announcement, take it seriously. From the announcement date until the proceeding ends or is dismissed, the arrangement supervisor exercises court-supervisor powers, and the law applies the same kinds of anti-enforcement effects that operate in other restructuring tracks. But the statute also gives a creditor a remedy: the court may **lift the effects of the announcement** if they lead to creditor prejudice, and those effects automatically expire if the debtor does not file the approval application within **four months**. This is one of the reasons why close KRZ monitoring matters. citeturn8view4turn8view5

## Recovery outlook and risk

### Full recovery

**Full recovery is still possible, but mainly if there is no collective insolvency and the debtor still has reachable assets.** The best-case path is: EPU order issues, no objection is filed, enforceability is obtained, and a bailiff finds cash, receivables, bank funds, or other non-exempt assets before bankruptcy/restructuring blocks execution. Legally, that route exists. Practically, it depends almost entirely on asset reality, not on the legal merits of your claim. citeturn27view1turn31view0turn32view0turn32view1

On timing, the **fast** scenario is measured in **months**, not days: some weeks for EPU movement, then enforceability, then bailiff action. That is a practical estimate rather than a statutory deadline. If the debtor objects, the case likely leaves the fast lane immediately because EPU is then umorzone and you must proceed ordinarily. citeturn26view2turn26view3turn27view1

### Partial recovery

**Partial recovery becomes the center of gravity if formal restructuring or bankruptcy appears.** In restructuring, your claim is generally folded into the arrangement framework as an unsecured personal claim arising before opening; in bankruptcy, you are an unsecured personal creditor who must file into the estate process and whose estate-paid interest generally stops at the bankruptcy date. In both situations, you are no longer racing just one debtor; you are participating in a collective system that exists to distribute limited value among creditors. citeturn10view3turn13view1turn13view2

Timing in those scenarios is typically **longer and much less predictable**. Restructuring may involve voting, claim-list disputes, and staged payments under an arrangement. Bankruptcy may involve a trustee, claim review, liquidation, and later distributions. In plain English: if insolvency really materializes, the legal road gets longer and the recovery percentage gets less certain. That is an inference from the statutory architecture, not a guaranteed timetable. citeturn10view5turn11view5turn13view1turn13view0

### Non-recovery

The principal non-recovery risks are three.

The first is **asset poverty**: you can have a strong title and still recover little if there is nothing to seize. That is the classic unsecured-creditor problem, and it is why security and early enforcement matter. The second is **collective insolvency timing**: once bankruptcy or a protected restructuring stage opens, private enforcement is sharply restricted. The third is **procedural drift**: if you wait passively, miss a KRZ event, or fail to refile after EPU umorzenie within the preserved window, you lose leverage and time. citeturn13view0turn10view1turn10view2turn27view1turn44search0

So my qualitative risk assessment is:

- **Full recovery:** **moderate** if no insolvency exists and assets still exist; **low** if collective insolvency formally opens.  
- **Partial recovery:** **moderate to high** if restructuring/bankruptcy really happens but there is some real asset base.  
- **Non-recovery:** **material and rising** if the debtor is already asset-light, if collective insolvency appears, or if he is using delay to move assets beyond easy execution.  

That assessment is an informed litigation-risk inference from the procedural rules and the facts you described, not a statistical forecast.
