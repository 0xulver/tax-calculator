# Prompt 7 — The bankruptcy-shield: making the 155k non-dischargeable via a fraud conviction (art. 491²¹ PU)

## Role
You are a Polish insolvency-law specialist (prawo upadłościowe, including upadłość konsumencka). Earlier research flagged that a fraud conviction can make my debt permanently non-dischargeable — "the nuclear option." I want this explained rigorously, because it may be the single most important reason to pursue the criminal track even if I never get cash from it directly.

## Case facts
- Debtor: Mateusz Szklarski, sole proprietor (JDG GPUcomputer, NIP 8661681248). A JDG owner can deregister from CEIDG and then seek consumer bankruptcy (upadłość konsumencka).
- He has already invoked "bankruptcy protection" rhetorically (in an email), though KRZ shows **no** bankruptcy or restructuring proceeding for him as of 28.05.2026.
- My claim: 155,000 PLN + interest. I am pursuing an EPU civil title and considering an art. 286 § 1 KK fraud complaint.
- Real risk: that once I obtain a title, he files for consumer bankruptcy in Poland (or relocates the centre of main interests) and tries to have the 155k discharged (oddłużenie), leaving me with a worthless judgment.

## What I need

1. **How art. 491²¹ PU (and the związane przepisy on oddłużenie, e.g. art. 491¹⁶) actually work.** Which debts are excluded from discharge in upadłość konsumencka? Confirm and explain the exclusion for obligations arising from an **intentional tort / crime** (zobowiązania z czynów niedozwolonych popełnionych umyślnie, and obligations to repair damage from a crime). Quote the current statutory text.

2. **What exactly triggers the shield for my claim.**
   - Do I need a **final criminal conviction** (prawomocny wyrok skazujący) for art. 286 KK (an intentional crime by definition)? Or does a civil judgment that *finds* intentional tort suffice?
   - Does an **art. 46 KK** obowiązek naprawienia szkody order — without any separate civil judgment — by itself render the debt non-dischargeable? This matters because it could mean the criminal route alone produces a permanently bankruptcy-proof title.
   - Timing: must the conviction exist *before* his bankruptcy/discharge, or can a later conviction reopen/except an already-discharged debt?

3. **Practical effect.** If the shield applies: the 155k survives his consumer bankruptcy and remains enforceable against his future income/assets indefinitely (subject to limitation). Confirm this and explain the limitation/renewal mechanics for such a title. Does it also block the NL side (would a Dutch court or a Polish-recognised title respect the non-dischargeability)?

4. **Strategic value vs. cost.** Frame honestly: even with a 40–60% chance the prosecutor dismisses, is the non-dischargeability prize (permanent, follows him for life/until paid) worth pursuing the conviction? How does it change his incentive to settle *before* conviction?

5. **The mirror lever — forcing/penalising his (non-)bankruptcy.** Given KRZ is clean despite long non-payment: see prompt 9 on art. 21 / art. 373 PU. Here, just confirm whether his *failure to file* or a *future* filing changes the discharge analysis.

## Output format
Statute-grounded explanation first, then a crisp answer to "does art. 46 KK alone make it non-dischargeable," then the strategic verdict on whether the shield justifies the criminal track.
