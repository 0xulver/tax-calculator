# Prompt 6 — Collecting from the debtor in the Netherlands (cross-border enforcement)

## Role
You are a cross-border debt-enforcement specialist familiar with both Polish enforcement and Dutch (Netherlands) execution, and with EU enforcement regulations. The debtor's person and income are in the Netherlands; his Polish business assets appear minimal. I need to know whether and how I can collect against him in NL, and whether it is worth it for a 155,000 PLN claim.

## Case facts
- Creditor: me, Magnus Brantheim, resident in Warsaw, Poland (Swedish citizen).
- Debtor: Mateusz Szklarski, Polish sole proprietor (JDG GPUcomputer, NIP 8661681248), but **physically resident and employed full-time in the Netherlands** since at least 2022 — currently a production-line worker at **Sims Lifecycle Services, Son en Breugel (Eindhoven metro)**. So he has a Dutch employer, Dutch wages, and presumably a Dutch bank account.
- Claim: 155,000 PLN + statutory delay interest from 31.03.2026.
- Civil title in progress: EPU payment order Nc-e 552126/26 (Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód). I expect a Polish nakaz zapłaty.
- The debt arises from a clear documentary claim (proforma, payment proof, written refund promise).

## What I need

1. **Turning the Polish title into an enforceable instrument in NL.** Compare the routes and tell me which fits an EPU nakaz zapłaty:
   - **European Enforcement Order (Regulation (EC) 805/2004)** — certifying the Polish order as a EEO for an *uncontested* claim; the procedure to obtain the EEO certificate in Poland and then enforce directly in NL without exequatur. Does an EPU nakaz that became final by fiction-of-service qualify as "uncontested"?
   - **Brussels Ia (Regulation (EU) 1215/2012)** — automatic recognition/enforcement of a Polish judgment in NL without a declaration of enforceability; the Article 53 certificate; how it differs from the EEO in practice.
   - **European Order for Payment (Regulation (EC) 1896/2006)** — not my current route (I used domestic EPU), but note if re-filing via EOP would have been or could be cleaner for NL enforcement.

2. **Freezing his Dutch bank account: the European Account Preservation Order (Regulation (EU) 655/2014 — EAPO).**
   - Can I obtain an EAPO from a Polish court to freeze his Dutch account — before or after I have a title?
   - Evidence threshold, the bank-information request mechanism (finding his account via the Dutch authority), ex parte nature, security deposit, and cost.
   - Is this the cross-border equivalent of the asset freeze the lawyer says I can't get domestically within EPU?

3. **Enforcement on the ground in NL.**
   - Role of a Dutch **gerechtsdeurwaarder** (bailiff); how I instruct one; can it be done remotely.
   - **Wage garnishment (loonbeslag)** against his Sims Lifecycle Services salary — and the Dutch protected minimum, **beslagvrije voet**: realistically how much of a production-worker's wage can actually be attached per month, and how long 155k would take to recover at that rate.
   - Bank-account attachment (bankbeslag) and any other reachable assets.

4. **Cost, time, and feasibility for a 155k PLN claim.** Court/translation/bailiff fees on the NL side; sworn translations of the Polish title; realistic total cost and duration; recoverability of those costs from the debtor. Is pursuing NL enforcement economically rational here, or is it mainly useful as leverage?

5. **Interaction with the criminal track.** If a Polish criminal asset freeze (art. 291 KPK) or art. 46 KK restitution order exists, can those reach his NL assets too, and via what instrument?

## Output format
A comparison table of the EU instruments (when each applies, threshold, cost, exequatur or not), then a practical "here is the route for my EPU nakaz" recommendation, then an honest economic verdict on NL enforcement for this claim.
