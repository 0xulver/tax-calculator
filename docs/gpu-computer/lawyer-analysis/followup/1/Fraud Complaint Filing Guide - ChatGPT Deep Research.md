# DIY manual for filing a Polish fraud complaint from abroad

## Bottom line

For your fact pattern, the cleanest self-filed route is a signed written **zawiadomienie o uzasadnionym podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa z art. 286 § 1 k.k.** addressed **directly to Prokuratura Rejonowa Kraków–Śródmieście Wschód**, because the seller’s listed business address at **ul. Mogilska 16/7, Kraków** falls in **Dzielnica II Grzegórzki**, and that prosecutor’s office states that its territorial jurisdiction covers **Kraków districts II and III**. As a police alternative, **Komisariat Policji II w Krakowie**, **ul. Lubicz 21, 31-503 Kraków**, serves **Dzielnica II Grzegórzki**, and its districting page lists **Mogilska 11–99**, which captures Mogilska 16/7. citeturn24view0turn21search0turn21search1turn21search2

You **can** file at a prosecutor’s office or police station nearest to you in Warsaw and ask for transfer by territorial competence. Under **art. 118 § 3 KPK**, a process letter sent to the wrong authority is forwarded to the competent one. But because Polish fraud cases around unpaid prepayments are often screened against the “mere civil dispute” line, the safest DIY move is to file **directly in Kraków** with a carefully drafted fraud theory instead of a debt-recovery narrative. citeturn47view0turn0search4turn32view1

The three biggest DIY dismissal risks are these. First, filing a complaint that says only “I paid, they did not deliver, they did not refund,” because recent case law insists that non-performance alone does not prove fraud; the complaint must identify the **method of deception** and facts supporting **initial direct intent**. Second, filing remotely by ordinary email without a signed PDF, without a coherent annex index, and without Polish summaries of the key English-language exhibits. Third, expecting a non-lawyer friend to act as your full procedural representative on a private power of attorney; under the KPK, a criminal-case **pełnomocnik** is generally an advocate, legal adviser, or PG counsel, not an ordinary friend. citeturn0search4turn31view0turn41view0turn29view1

## How to file remotely or from abroad

### Postal filing

A written criminal complaint can be sent **by post**. The current government service page on reporting offences expressly says that a complaint may be sent **by email, fax, or post** to the police or prosecutor’s office, and warns that after a remote filing you may receive a **written summons for oral supplementation**. citeturn22view1turn22view2

For DIY purposes, the safest postal package is:

1. one **signed original** complaint in Polish;
2. a numbered annex index;
3. readable copies of annexes;
4. your email and mobile phone in the first page;
5. a brief note asking for contact by email if any defect needs curing quickly.

If you are abroad on the day you send it, use a tracked registered or courier method that gives you proof of dispatch and proof of delivery. For the initial complaint, there is no short statutory filing deadline, so proof of receipt matters more than any deadline-preservation rule.

### ePUAP and the real current electronic route

As of 2026, the practical online route for police/prosecutor filings is not ordinary e-Doręczenia but **ePUAP / “Wyślij pismo ogólne”**. The current gov.pl service page says that for **justice institutions such as the prosecutor’s office, police, courts, and prison service**, the exceptional route is **ePUAP**, not e-Doręczenia. That same page says that to send a general electronic filing you need an ePUAP account and either a **Profil Zaufany** or a **qualified certificate**, and that you receive an electronic **UPP**, which is as important as a postal return receipt. It also says that if you send the filing **without a signature**, you will not complete matters that require a signature. citeturn41view0turn38search1

So, in practice:

1. if you already have **Profil Zaufany** or a qualified signature, ePUAP is a very good remote route;
2. if you do **not** already have one, do **not** delay the fraud complaint just to build digital identity;
3. send the complaint by post instead.

A Kraków prosecutor page still contains an older-style ePUAP instruction referring to a qualified signature, but the current general gov.pl filing service is the better statement of present practice: for these institutions, use **ePUAP**, signed with **Profil Zaufany or a qualified certificate**. citeturn27view0turn41view0

### Can a foreign resident get Profil Zaufany

A foreigner can obtain **Profil Zaufany** if they have a Polish **PESEL**; the official PESEL service for foreigners confirms that a foreign resident can obtain a PESEL, and the official business-service guidance states that a foreigner may create a trusted profile if they have one. If you already have PESEL and domestic banking or access to a confirmation point, ePUAP is realistic. If not, use post and do not turn a 30-minute filing task into a two-week digital identity project. citeturn39search0turn39search8

### Is ordinary email acceptable

Yes, in the limited sense that the current government offence-reporting page says you may send the complaint **by email**. But it is **procedurally fragile** compared with post or ePUAP, for two reasons. First, email gives you no **UPP-equivalent** proof from the public filing system. Second, the same government page expressly anticipates a later summons for oral supplementation, so an email-only filing can easily become the least well-framed version of your case at the screening stage. citeturn22view1turn41view0

If you use email anyway, do not write the whole thing in the email body. Attach a **signed PDF complaint** and the annex index as PDFs.

### Can a Polish friend file it for you

A friend can **send or deliver** your complaint for you. The current gov.pl offence-reporting page explicitly says that if you cannot send the filing yourself, someone else may help and may send it in your name. But that is not the same thing as being your procedural **pełnomocnik** in the criminal case. Under **art. 88 § 1 KPK**, a criminal-case full representative is generally an **adwokat**, **radca prawny**, or PG counsel. An ordinary friend with a private power of attorney is therefore not a substitute for professional representation in the KPK sense. citeturn22view1turn29view1

The correct DIY distinction is this:

1. **Yes**: a friend can deliver or mail your already signed complaint, or even make an independent notification as a person who has information about an offence.
2. **No**: a friend should not be presented as your criminal procedural full representative on a normal POA.

### Ask for an interpreter if you need one

If you are not comfortable giving precise Polish evidence orally, say so in the complaint and ask for an **English interpreter** for any oral supplementation or witness examination. Under **art. 204 KPK**, an interpreter must be called when a person to be examined does not know Polish sufficiently, and also where a foreign-language document needs to be translated into Polish. citeturn48view0

**DIY risk here:** high if you rely on ordinary email only, or if you plan for a friend to “represent” you instead of merely delivering the papers.

## How to draft the complaint so it survives screening

### The legal minimum

Your complaint is a **pismo procesowe**, so under **art. 119 § 1 KPK** it should contain at least the authority addressed, the identity and address of the filer, the content/request, the date, and the signature. In a first filing, it should also include your telephone number and email address, or a statement that you do not have them. If a formal defect blocks further action, the authority should call on you to cure it within **7 days** under **art. 120 KPK**. Also helpful for your exact question: under **art. 118 KPK**, the legal effect of a filing is judged by its **content**, not its label, and a filing sent to the wrong authority is forwarded to the right one. citeturn47view0

### The threshold you are trying to meet

You are **not** trying to prove guilt in the complaint. You are trying to establish **uzasadnione podejrzenie popełnienia przestępstwa** sufficient to justify opening preparatory proceedings under **art. 303 KPK** and to survive the **art. 307 KPK** checking stage. That means your document should sound like this:

> “zachodzi uzasadnione podejrzenie, że …”

not like this:

> “sprawca na pewno popełnił oszustwo i ukradł moje pieniądze”.

That drafting discipline matters. It aligns with the statutory threshold for initiation, and it also keeps you away from the rhetorical danger zone associated with **art. 234 KK** on false accusation. citeturn47view0turn45search2

### The right structure

For your facts, the complaint should have these parts, in this order:

1. **Oznaczenie organu.**  
   Use PR Kraków–Śródmieście Wschód, or your Warsaw addressee plus an express transfer request.

2. **Your data as pokrzywdzony.**  
   Full name, Polish correspondence address, citizenship, email, phone. If you may be abroad, say so and ask that the authority first contact you electronically for scheduling.

3. **Persons whose roles require investigation.**  
   Name **Mateusz Szklarski** as the sole proprietor trading as GPUcomputer, with firm identifiers. Name **Waldemar Łopata** more cautiously, for example as the person using the addresses **waldek@gpucomputer.pl** and the root mailbox **gpucomputer@gpucomputer.pl**, whose exact role should be established. Do **not** plead “probable relative” as a fact unless you have evidence.

4. **A precise description of the act.**  
   Time window, amount, pro forma invoice number, the prepayment, non-delivery, refund assurance, contradictory explanations, and the damage amount.

5. **Preliminary legal qualification.**  
   Phrase it as **“czyn z art. 286 § 1 k.k.”** or **“co najmniej z art. 286 § 1 k.k.”**. Remember: the prosecutor is not bound by your label.

6. **Uzasadnienie.**  
   This is the heart of the case. It must explain **why the matter is not merely contractual non-performance**.

7. **Wnioski dowodowe.**  
   Numbered, specific, tied to the deception theory.

8. **Requests at the end.**  
   Request initiation of preparatory proceedings, your notification rights, and if needed an interpreter.

### Do you need a formal wniosek o ściganie

For **art. 286 § 1 k.k.**, a formal **wniosek o ściganie** is **not** ordinarily required, because this is an offence prosecuted **ex officio**; the special motion-based rule in **art. 286 § 4 k.k.** concerns only the case of a close relative as victim, which is not your situation. The legally cleaner ending is therefore:

> **wnoszę o wszczęcie i przeprowadzenie postępowania przygotowawczego**

rather than a formal motion-language formula. If you nevertheless write “wnoszę o ściganie sprawców,” art. 118 KPK means the filing should still be judged by its substance, not the caption. citeturn44view0turn47view0

### The current procedural mode in your amount band

Because your amount is **155,000 PLN**, and **art. 286 § 1 k.k.** is expressly listed in **art. 325b § 1 pkt 3 KPK** for cases where the amount does not exceed **200,000 PLN**, the matter will **usually** proceed as a **dochodzenie**, not a mandatory śledztwo. But **art. 309 pkt 5 KPK** allows the prosecutor to move it into śledztwo if the case’s weight or complexity justifies that. In other words, do not obsess about whether the office later calls it a dochodzenie or śledztwo; in your complaint, ask simply for **postępowanie przygotowawcze**. citeturn47view0

**DIY risk here:** very high if you omit the deception theory and write a debt-collection letter instead of a criminal complaint.

## How to plead the deception theory

### What recent case law now forces you to do

The 2025 Supreme Court line highlighted by **III KK 216/25** is exactly the danger you identified: **taking a prepayment, failing to deliver, and failing to refund** does **not by itself** establish fraud. The criminal file has to show that, when entering the civil-law relationship, the suspect already had the required fraudulent **direct intent** and used deception as the method for obtaining the money. The broader Supreme Court case law is consistent that fraud under **art. 286 § 1 k.k.** requires **zamiar bezpośredni i kierunkowy** and not mere eventual intent. citeturn0search4turn32view0turn32view1turn32view2

So your filing must not say only:

> “I paid, they breached the contract.”

It must say:

> “There is justified suspicion that they obtained the payment by creating or maintaining a false picture of their then-existing ability and intention to perform, and by concealing material facts that would have stopped me from paying.”

### Why omission and contradiction matter

Polish case law accepts that “wprowadzenie w błąd” under art. 286 can occur not only through an affirmative lie but also through **przemilczenie** or the concealment of facts that are material to the victim’s decision to part with money. SAOS case law, citing Supreme Court authority, states that deception may be achieved by **zaniechanie** and **przemilczenie** where silence about existing facts creates a false picture of reality relevant to the asset-disposal decision. citeturn31view0turn31view2turn31view3

That is the doctrinal opening your facts give you. Your complaint should not insist that you already know which explanation was false. It should instead say that the later mutually exclusive explanations are **circumstantial evidence** supporting the suspicion that, already when the prepayment was accepted, material facts about the actual supply situation, supplier risk, inventory position, or ability to refund were concealed or misrepresented. citeturn31view0turn0search4

### The concrete theory to plead on your facts

The strongest DIY theory is this:

1. the seller accepted a **full prepayment** for a custom workstation;
2. the transaction was carried through the firm and the firm’s mailboxes;
3. after non-delivery, you received **mutually inconsistent explanations** about the state of the order;
4. you then received a **written refund promise with interest and a 14-business-day deadline** from both relevant mailboxes, which was not honored;
5. that sequence supports a justified suspicion that the payment was obtained while **concealing** or **misstating** present facts crucial to your decision to pay: namely whether the components had actually been secured, whether the supply chain was under control, whether there was a real and timely capacity to perform, and whether there was a real ability to return the money if performance failed.

That is much better than framing the complaint as a quarrel about late delivery. It translates your facts into the legal architecture that Polish authorities look for after the 2025 Supreme Court warning line. citeturn0search4turn31view0turn32view2

### Concrete wording you can safely use

A good complaint paragraph in Polish would say something like this:

> **Nie opieram niniejszego zawiadomienia wyłącznie na samym niewykonaniu umowy i braku zwrotu ceny. Uzasadnione podejrzenie czynu z art. 286 § 1 k.k. wywodzę z tego, że już w chwili przyjmowania pełnej przedpłaty sprawcy co najmniej wytworzyli lub podtrzymywali fałszywy obraz istniejącej rzeczywistości co do realnej możliwości terminowego wykonania zamówienia, stanu zabezpieczenia komponentów, ryzyka po stronie dostawców oraz realnej zdolności niezwłocznego zwrotu środków. Istotne dla mojej decyzji informacje zostały przede mną przemilczane lub przedstawione w sposób niezgodny z rzeczywistym stanem rzeczy, na co wskazują późniejsze, wzajemnie sprzeczne wyjaśnienia dotyczące tej samej transakcji oraz niewykonana pisemna obietnica zwrotu całej kwoty z odsetkami.**

What makes this wording safe is its restraint. It alleges **reasonable suspicion**, identifies the **deception mechanism**, and leaves final fact-finding to the prosecutor. It does not overclaim that you have already proved guilt. That is exactly the right KPK posture. citeturn47view0turn31view0turn45search2

**DIY risk here:** this is the single most load-bearing part of the case. If this section is weak, the file is much more likely to be treated as a civil dispute.

## Annexes, copies, and translations

### What to attach

Attach only what advances the fraud theory. For your facts, the core annex set should be:

1. the **pro forma invoice 2/01-2026**;
2. proof of the **150,000 PLN SWIFT** and the **5,000 PLN payment** from **12.01.2026**;
3. the key correspondence showing the transaction was conducted through the firm and its mailboxes;
4. the **02.03.2026 refund offer**;
5. the **10.03.2026 written promise** to repay **155,000 PLN with interest within 14 business days**;
6. the later email with the **Hong Kong supplier bankruptcy** explanation;
7. if available, a short printout or screenshot confirming the seller’s firm data;
8. a one-page note identifying the **EPU case number Nc-e 552126/26** and saying that civil recovery is being pursued separately.

Do **not** attach a giant undifferentiated mailbox dump if the key theory can be shown with 10 to 20 carefully selected exhibits.

### Originals or copies

At the complaint stage, readable **copies/scans** are the practical norm. Keep the originals, electronic source files, and device metadata in reserve and say that you will produce originals on request. The government offence-reporting instructions speak of sending the written complaint and the needed documents; the KPK does not make survival of the initial complaint depend on enclosed originals. What matters most at this stage is readability, chronology, and relevance. citeturn22view1turn47view0

### Do English-language exhibits need sworn Polish translations now

Not as a general hard-condition for filing. **Art. 204 § 2 KPK** says an interpreter/translator shall also be called where there is a need to translate a document written in a foreign language into Polish or vice versa. That means the code itself supplies the mechanism for later official translation when the authority needs it. There is no rule in arts. 118–120 or 304–307 KPK making a sworn translation a formal prerequisite to lodging the complaint. citeturn48view0turn47view0

That said, the practical answer is more nuanced:

1. if the exhibits are in English, the complaint should still be filed now;
2. but for the **3 to 5 most important exhibits**, you materially reduce dismissal risk if you attach at least:
   - a short **Polish summary**, or
   - your own working translation, or
   - ideally a **sworn Polish translation** if the wording is central.

The most translation-worthy exhibits in your case are likely the documents that go directly to **deception**, not the entire thread: the pro forma invoice, the payment proof captions, the contradictory “customs cleared” vs “supplier bankrupt” messages, and the written refund promise.

### Rough pages and cost

A sensible DIY complaint in this case is usually:

- **5 to 8 pages** of complaint text,
- **1 page** of annex index,
- **10 to 20 pages** of selected evidence,
- and, if you de-risk with translations, **8 to 15 translation pages** for the core exhibits.

The current official translator tariff, as published in the 2025 consolidated regulation, is **44.07 PLN per page** for a certified translation **into Polish from English**, and **57.65 PLN per page** from Polish into English, with a translation page defined as **1125 characters**, and a possible **25% uplift** for specialist texts. Those are the official rates for work commissioned by courts, prosecutors, police, and public authorities; they are a useful benchmark, not a guaranteed private-market quote. On that benchmark, **8 to 15 pages** of English-to-Polish certified translation would be roughly **352.56 PLN to 661.05 PLN**, or around **440.70 PLN to 826.31 PLN** if a 25% specialist uplift were applied. citeturn34view1

**DIY risk here:** high if you file English annexes with no Polish roadmap at all. Medium if you attach English originals plus a careful Polish exhibit index and short Polish summaries. Low-to-medium if you certify only the core deception exhibits.

## What happens after filing

### The early path

After receipt, the authority assesses whether the materials justify opening preparatory proceedings. If the information gives **uzasadnione podejrzenie popełnienia przestępstwa**, proceedings are opened under **art. 303 KPK**. If the authority needs to supplement or verify the facts first, it may conduct **postępowanie sprawdzające** under **art. 307 KPK**. The current government guidance for remote offence reports expressly says that after a written remote filing, you may receive a **written summons for oral supplementation**. citeturn47view0turn22view1

Your file is therefore likely to follow this real-world order:

1. complaint enters registry;
2. authority reviews whether it is criminally framed or looks merely civil;
3. if needed, you are asked to supplement or are examined as a witness;
4. authority either opens the case or refuses initiation.

### Timing

Art. 307 KPK sets a short internal decision horizon for the screening stage, and **art. 306 § 3 KPK** gives you a specific remedy if you are left in the dark: if you are not notified within **6 weeks** of the complaint, you may complain to the **superior prosecutor** or the prosecutor entrusted with supervision over the authority to which you submitted the notification. That is the “silence remedy.” citeturn47view0turn36search2turn36search10

### How you learn the result

Under the KPK, the authority should notify both the notifier and the disclosed victim of the opening or refusal decision, and if the decision is issued by police in the dochodzenie track, the police refusal/termination posture is subject to the prosecutor’s approval. In practice, you learn the outcome through a formal letter, an ePUAP delivery, or a summons that reveals the case has been opened. Do not expect a consumer-style online status tracker. citeturn47view0turn41view0

### The refusal route and the seven-day deadline

If you receive a **postanowienie o odmowie wszczęcia**, you have **7 days** to appeal from the date of service. That follows from **art. 460 KPK** for complaints/appeals against decisions that are served. The complaint is heard by the **court competent to hear the case**, and for prosecutor decisions the relevant rule is **art. 465 § 2 KPK**. Current prosecutor guidance also states in plain terms that the appeal is filed **through the prosecutor who issued the decision**. citeturn35view0turn35view2turn36search0

So the appeal heading should look like this if PR Kraków–Śródmieście Wschód issued the refusal:

> **Do Sądu Rejonowego dla Krakowa–Śródmieścia w Krakowie**  
> **za pośrednictwem Prokuratury Rejonowej Kraków–Śródmieście Wschód**

If the case remained in the Mogilska / Grzegórzki territorial track, that is the most likely court, because **Mogilska** appears on the criminal street list of **Sąd Rejonowy dla Krakowa–Śródmieścia**. citeturn17view0turn18view0

### One more practical point after filing

Once you are being treated as a pokrzywdzony, the KPK requires written instruction on your rights, including rights connected to review mechanisms and interpreter support. Read that notice carefully when it arrives. For DIY filers, the single biggest post-filing mistake is missing the **7-day refusal appeal window** because the letter was read late or mentally treated as “just more correspondence.” citeturn48view0

**DIY risk here:** very high on the refusal-appeal stage. Missing the 7-day window is the classic false-economy mistake.
