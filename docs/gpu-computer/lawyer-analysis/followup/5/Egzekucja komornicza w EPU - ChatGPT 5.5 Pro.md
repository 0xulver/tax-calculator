## A. Starting point: what you actually need in hand

I’m assuming you have a **tytuł wykonawczy**: the final EPU **nakaz zapłaty** plus **klauzula wykonalności**. In EPU, the enforceability clause is added **ex officio immediately after the payment order becomes final**; only then does normal bailiff enforcement start. This is different from a classic **nakaz zapłaty w postępowaniu nakazowym**, which can operate as a security title on issuance under art. 492 KPC; EPU is a separate electronic writ-of-payment procedure. ([Isap][1])

One practical warning: because the debtor is actually in the Netherlands, check the EPU service/finality history. EPU has special service limitations where service would need to occur abroad, so a debtor who proves that the Polish address was not his real service address may try to attack finality or enforcement. That does not stop you from enforcing a valid title, but it is a foreseeable defence. ([Isap][1])

# B. Linear procedure once you have the tytuł wykonawczy

## 1. Choose the komornik

The default proper bailiff is tied to the debtor’s district / enforcement venue, but you generally have a **prawo wyboru komornika** under **art. 10 ustawy o komornikach sądowych**. This is not unlimited nationwide shopping: the creditor may choose a bailiff **within the appellate court area** in which the otherwise proper bailiff’s office is located. The choice does **not** apply to certain localised enforcement types, especially **real-estate enforcement**, delivery of property, taking possession, or eviction-type matters. ([Isap][2])

For a Kraków debtor address, the practical reason to choose a good, responsive office is speed: fast OGNIVO queries, fast bank attachments, fast ZUS/US/CEPiK requests, and clear communication before discontinuance for ineffectiveness. If you choose a bailiff outside his normal district, the application must include a written statement that you are exercising the art. 10 right; the selected bailiff may still refuse in backlog/low-effectiveness situations. ([Isap][2])

A key cost point: when a **chosen** bailiff acts outside his district, out-of-district travel, per diems, lodging, and special transport costs are ultimately borne by the creditor and are not shifted to the debtor as enforcement costs. So for this case, avoid ordering physical field visits unless you have a concrete asset location. ([Isap][3])

## 2. File the wniosek egzekucyjny

The enforcement application is filed with the bailiff, on paper or electronically where available. The application must identify the benefit to be enforced and attach the enforceable title; for an electronic title, the bailiff verifies it through the IT system. ([Isap][1])

Your application should not be limited to the known Alior-looking account. It should request enforcement against **all admissible assets**, because KPC now treats a general enforcement request as covering all permissible enforcement methods **except real estate**. Real-estate enforcement still requires a specific creditor request. ([Isap][1])

Include at least:

**Debtor identification**

Mateusz Szklarski, JDG “GPUcomputer”, NIP 8661681248, registered address ul. Mogilska 16/7, Kraków, and any Dutch address/employer details you have.

**Claim**

155,000 PLN principal, statutory/default interest as awarded, litigation costs, clause costs, and enforcement costs.

**Known Polish account**

PL84 2490 0005 0000 4530 9127 6540, but state that it may be empty and that you request a full bank search.

**Requested enforcement/search actions**

OGNIVO bank search and bank-account attachment; ZUS; tax office/KAS; CEPiK; land and mortgage register checks; receivables from third parties; tax refunds/overpayments; Polish wages if any; movables and cash only if there is a real physical location; and an asset search under art. 801² KPC if ordinary steps fail.

The bailiff should take necessary actions no later than 7 days after receiving the request, and where an advance is needed, no later than 7 days after the advance is paid. ([Isap][1])

## 3. First enforcement acts and notice to the debtor

The bailiff’s first act usually includes bank/account searches, attachments, and formal notice to the debtor. On the first enforcement act, the debtor receives notice of enforcement, the title content, enforcement methods, and remedies. For EPU/payment-order titles, the bailiff gives additional statutory notices about remedies and possible discontinuance grounds. ([Isap][1])

This notice does **not** mean the bailiff must wait before freezing Polish bank accounts. Bank attachment is designed to work quickly: the bailiff sends the bank a seizure notice up to the amount of the claim plus costs, the bank blocks withdrawals and transfers the seized amount or reports obstacles; the notice can be effective even if the exact account is not identified. ([Isap][1])

## 4. Upfront costs and fees

There are two separate categories:

**Advances / expenses.** You usually advance correspondence, information requests, register costs, travel if ordered, expert costs if needed, land-register fees, and similar expenses. The statute lists expenses such as correspondence, transport, information/document costs, experts, translators, advertisements, travel, and land-register costs. If enforcement is ineffective, the bailiff charges the creditor for unrecovered expenses not covered by advances or recovered funds. ([Isap][3])

**Bailiff enforcement fees.** In monetary enforcement, the basic enforcement fee charged to the debtor is generally **10% of the recovered/enforced amount**. If the debtor pays to the bailiff within one month of receiving enforcement notice, the reduced rate is **3%**. This is not a 10% retainer you pay upfront, but in a partial recovery the costs and fees are accounted for from enforcement funds, so your net recovery may be lower if the debtor has only limited assets. ([Isap][3])

If you order a formal **poszukiwanie majątku** under art. 801² KPC, the statutory fee is **100 PLN**. If the case is discontinued for reasons other than successful recovery, a **150 PLN** fee may apply, subject to statutory exceptions. If the enforcement was obviously pointless or directed against the wrong person, there is a separate creditor-risk fee, but that is not the normal scenario where you have a valid title against the correct JDG debtor. ([Isap][3])

## 5. Practical timeline

A realistic Polish timeline in this fact pattern:

**Day 0–7:** file the application, pay the initial advance, bailiff opens the case and sends first electronic requests/attachments.

**Week 1–3:** OGNIVO/bank results, known-account attachment, ZUS/tax/CEPiK requests, debtor notice, first responses from banks or institutions.

**Weeks 3–8:** if no accounts, no Polish employer, no vehicle, no tax refund and no obvious real estate, the bailiff either asks for more leads or asks whether you want formal asset search / further action before discontinuance.

**Month 2–4:** if purely electronic Polish searches are negative and no physical asset lead exists, expect movement toward discontinuance for ineffectiveness. Real estate, if found, is a different track and can take many months or more.

The statute gives the 7-day action rule; the longer ranges are practical estimates for a mostly asset-search case. ([Isap][1])

# C. What the komornik can search and seize in Poland

## 1. OGNIVO and bank accounts

The bailiff should run an OGNIVO search/communication, not merely attach the one account you know. OGNIVO is the electronic communication system between banks/SKOKs and authorised entities, including judicial enforcement bodies, used to replace slow paper correspondence and speed bank-information exchange. ([KIR][4])

If the known account is empty, the attachment still matters because it can catch later inflows while the seizure is active. The bailiff can also seize accounts found through the OGNIVO process. Bank attachment blocks payment out of the account up to the claim plus costs and requires the bank to transfer funds or report obstacles. ([Isap][1])

## 2. ZUS and tax office / KAS

The bailiff can request information from public administration, tax authorities, pension/rent bodies, banks, insurers, securities-account institutions, housing bodies, property managers, business-information bureaus and other entities. KPC expressly gives the enforcement organ this information-demand power. ([Isap][1])

In your case, ZUS may show no Polish employer because the debtor works in the Netherlands, but it is still worth checking. Tax-office/KAS routes may reveal Polish tax refunds, overpayments, declared activity, bank accounts used for tax purposes, or other receivables.

## 3. CEPiK vehicles

The bailiff can check vehicle data and, if a vehicle is registered in Poland, seize and sell it if physically reachable. The practical problem is location: a Polish registration entry is useful, but if the vehicle is abroad or not findable, sale may be hard.

Movables are seized by the bailiff through physical seizure. If the debtor has no real Polish premises and the Kraków address is only a virtual office, spending money on field visits there may produce nothing. KPC allows seizure of the debtor’s movables in the debtor’s or creditor’s possession, and third-party possession only under specific conditions such as consent or admission that the thing belongs to the debtor. ([Isap][1])

## 4. Land and mortgage registers / EKW and real estate

You should verify Polish real estate separately. If a property is found, remember that **real-estate enforcement is not covered automatically** by a general “all methods” request; you must expressly request enforcement from the specific real estate. ([Isap][1])

Real-estate enforcement is conducted by the bailiff at the court where the property is located. After a creditor requests enforcement against a specific property, the bailiff calls the debtor to pay within two weeks under threat of description and valuation, and requests entry of the enforcement in the land and mortgage register. ([Isap][1])

Separately, once you have a tytuł wykonawczy, you can obtain a **hipoteka przymusowa** on the debtor’s real estate. A creditor whose claim is confirmed by an enforceable title may obtain a compulsory mortgage on the debtor’s real estate; the secured sum is limited by the document and statutory rules. This is often faster as a protective registration step than waiting for a full auction path. ([Isap][5])

## 5. Wages and Polish income

The bailiff can attach Polish wages if there is a Polish employer. Wage attachment orders the employer not to pay the debtor beyond protected amounts and to provide wage information. The employer must then pay the attached amounts as instructed. ([Isap][1])

For this debtor, the expected problem is territorial: a Dutch employer is not a Polish employer subject to a Polish bailiff’s wage-attachment order. That is addressed below.

## 6. Receivables from third parties

If you know any Polish customers, contractors, marketplaces, payment processors, refund claims, or other parties owing money to GPUcomputer / Mateusz Szklarski, you can ask the bailiff to attach those receivables. The third-party debtor is then ordered not to pay Mateusz and must tell the bailiff whether and how much it owes. ([Isap][1])

This can matter more than the empty bank account. A dormant JDG may still have receivables, tax refunds, platform balances, card-settlement balances, deposits, or claims against suppliers.

## 7. Business and personal assets of a JDG

A JDG is not a separate company with a corporate veil. Enforcement is against **Mateusz Szklarski as the natural person conducting business**, not against a separate legal person called GPUcomputer. The enforceable title permits enforcement for the whole claim from all parts of the debtor’s assets unless the title itself limits enforcement. ([Isap][1])

So yes: the bailiff may pursue “business” assets and “personal” assets at the same time. There is no rule that business assets must be exhausted first. The real limits are different: third-party ownership, marital-property complications, statutory exemptions, territorial reach, and whether the asset can actually be found and seized.

# D. What if there is nothing in Poland

## 1. What “umorzenie wobec bezskuteczności egzekucji” means

If the bailiff’s searches and enforcement attempts show that it is obvious no sum higher than enforcement costs will be obtained, the bailiff discontinues the case ex officio under **art. 824 § 1 pkt 3 KPC**. This is the standard “bezskuteczność egzekucji” outcome. ([Isap][1])

Before you let that happen, make sure the bailiff has done the useful cheap searches: OGNIVO, known account attachment, ZUS, tax office/KAS, CEPiK, land-register/real-estate checks where possible, known third-party receivables, and the debtor’s asset inventory route. If ordinary ex officio asset checks fail, the bailiff may hear the creditor, and further asset search can require a creditor’s formal instruction. ([Isap][1])

What you receive is usually a **postanowienie o umorzeniu postępowania egzekucyjnego z powodu bezskuteczności egzekucji** or a document functioning as a **postanowienie o nieściągalności**. Ask for a final/stamped copy and a cost statement. The discontinuance does **not** cancel the debt and does **not** destroy the enforceable title.

## 2. Strategic value of the bezskuteczność document

**Tax write-off.** For Polish tax purposes, a bad debt can be documented by an enforcement authority’s decision confirming uncollectibility, provided other tax conditions are met, such as prior recognition as taxable revenue and the creditor recognising the decision as matching the factual state. The PIT and CIT statutes both contain this “postanowienie o nieściągalności” route. This is accounting-sensitive, so your accountant should check PIT/CIT/VAT treatment and limitation status. ([Isap][6])

**Criminal track.** Bezskuteczność is not automatic proof of art. 300 KK, but it is useful evidence. Art. 300 KK targets conduct such as removing, hiding, selling, donating, destroying, encumbering or damaging assets in a situation of threatened insolvency, or frustrating enforcement of a court/state-authority judgment. The enforcement failure document helps show that civil recovery was attempted and that the debtor’s assets were unavailable; the criminal case still needs evidence of dissipation, concealment, timing, intent, or transactions. ([Isap][7])

**Later Polish attempts.** Bezskuteczność is a snapshot, not a final verdict that the debtor will never have assets. It gives you a clean basis to close the unproductive run, preserve documentation, and re-open enforcement later if a Polish bank account, tax refund, vehicle, real estate, inheritance, or receivable appears.

**Cross-border enforcement.** Dutch enforcement does not require a Polish “bezskuteczność” certificate, but it is strategically useful. It shows that Polish enforcement was attempted and failed, supports proportionality/urgency narratives, and may help when seeking protective steps such as account preservation.

## 3. Wyjawienie majątku — asset disclosure under art. 913 KPC

After enforcement fails to satisfy the claim, or if seized assets clearly do not promise satisfaction, you may apply for **wyjawienie majątku**: the debtor is ordered to file a list of assets and swear an oath. The list covers things and their locations, receivables, other property rights, and certain transactions made within five years that caused or worsened insolvency. False statements are made under criminal-liability warning. ([Isap][1])

There is also a pre-enforcement route after you have an enforceable title: if you show that known assets/current payments over six months will not fully satisfy the debt, and you sent the debtor a registered 14-day demand to pay with no payment, you can seek disclosure before or alongside enforcement. ([Isap][1])

The application is filed with the court of the debtor’s general jurisdiction, and the court may fine, order forced production, and in some circumstances arrest for up to one month if the debtor fails to appear or refuses. ([Isap][1])

Because this debtor is actually in the Netherlands, wyjawienie is legally useful but practically weaker. A Polish court can summon and create procedural pressure, and a false asset list can matter later, but forced production and arrest are territorial tools. They become most useful if the debtor returns to Poland, maintains a Polish service address, or has Polish proceedings/assets. Expect service and venue issues if his real domicile is Dutch.

## 4. Re-running enforcement and limitation period

A final court-confirmed claim does not expire quickly. Under the Civil Code, claims confirmed by a final court decision generally prescribe after **six years**, while future periodic benefits, such as future interest instalments, prescribe after **three years**. Enforcement actions interrupt limitation, and after interruption the limitation period runs anew; while the enforcement proceeding is pending, the limitation period does not run until the proceeding ends. ([Isap][8])

Practical consequence: after a bezskuteczność discontinuance, calendar follow-up runs. Re-run Polish enforcement when you get a new lead, and consider periodic low-cost OGNIVO/ZUS/tax checks if the amount justifies it.

# E. Dutch wages: high-level only

A Polish komornik does **not** have direct coercive reach over a Dutch employer’s wage payroll. The Polish bailiff can attach Polish wages, Polish bank accounts, Polish receivables, Polish tax refunds and Polish assets; Dutch wages require enforcement in the Netherlands.

For an EU civil/commercial judgment, the Brussels I bis system provides that a judgment enforceable in one Member State is enforceable in another Member State without a declaration of enforceability. For Dutch enforcement you normally need the Polish judgment/title documents and the **art. 53 certificate**, and then enforcement proceeds under Dutch enforcement law through the Dutch enforcement route. ([EUR-Lex][9])

For Dutch bank accounts, the separate **European Account Preservation Order** can freeze funds in a bank account in another EU country in cross-border civil/commercial cases; it freezes bank funds, not wages at the employer level. ([online-forms.e-justice.europa.eu][10])

# F. Fastest anti-dissipation move after the nakaz

If the EPU nakaz is not final and does not yet have the clause, ordinary bailiff enforcement is not available. The EPU clause is added after finality, and the special art. 492 KPC immediate-security effect belongs to nakazowe, not EPU. ([Isap][1])

Once the title is enforceable, the fastest protective sequence is:

1. **Same-day wniosek egzekucyjny** to a responsive bailiff, with the enforceable EPU title attached or electronically verifiable.

2. **Immediate OGNIVO and bank-account attachment**, including but not limited to the known account. Bank seizure can be effective even without specifying the exact account and can catch later inflows while active. ([Isap][1])

3. **Immediate third-party receivable attachments** for any known counterparties, marketplaces, payment processors, customers, refunds, deposits, or tax overpayments.

4. **Immediate real-estate check.** If a Polish property is identified, file for **hipoteka przymusowa** and/or real-estate enforcement. The compulsory mortgage is a particularly important anti-dissipation step because it creates a land-register encumbrance based on the enforceable title. ([Isap][5])

5. **For Dutch assets**, move to the EU/Dutch track rather than waiting for Polish enforcement to fail. For wages, that means Dutch enforcement; for bank funds, consider the European Account Preservation Order route. ([EUR-Lex][9])
