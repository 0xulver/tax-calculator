# Prompt 10 — Fraud by misrepresentation: the "fake front" theory, built on what I can already prove

## Role
You are a Polish criminal lawyer specialising in *oszustwo* (art. 286 § 1 KK) and economic crime. I want a rigorous assessment of a specific fraud theory: that GPUcomputer obtained my 155,000 PLN by **affirmatively misrepresenting, before I paid, what the business actually was** — staging the appearance of a real, operating Kraków computer store with insured shipments, when in reality it is a virtual-office shell run remotely by an unregistered person while the registered owner works full-time on a Dutch factory line. Assess each misrepresentation against the elements of art. 286, **based on the evidence I can already produce**, and tell me how likely this leads to a real case. Distinguish sharply between actionable deception and non-actionable marketing "puffery."

## Why this theory may be stronger than "they didn't deliver"
Polish case law (incl. SN III KK 216/25, 17.06.2025) treats "took prepayment, didn't deliver, didn't refund" as a civil dispute unless deceptive **direct intent existed at the moment of contracting** (*dolus directus coloratus*) and a **specific method of *wprowadzenie w błąd*** is identified. The misrepresentations below were all present **before** payment and induced it — so they go directly to that hard element, rather than relying only on post-payment conduct.

## Case facts
- I am Magnus Brantheim, resident in Warsaw. In January 2026 I paid 155,000 PLN (150,000 SWIFT + 5,000, both 12.01.2026) to GPUcomputer for a custom ML workstation, on proforma 2/01-2026.
- Seller of record: Mateusz Szklarski, JDG GPUcomputer, NIP 8661681248, CEIDG address ul. Mogilska 16/7, Kraków.
- No delivery. Refund promised in writing 10.03.2026 (155k + interest, 14 business days); never paid.
- A second man, Waldek (Waldemar Łopata), ran the @gpucomputer mailboxes (incl. the root inbox) and the day-to-day dealings; he co-branded "3dkrakow" on the site since 2016 and is not VAT-registered.

## The specific misrepresentations and the evidence I already hold

**Misrepresentation 1 — that this is a genuine, operating Kraków computer business.**
- Reality: the registered owner, Mateusz, has lived in the Netherlands and worked **full-time on a factory production line** (Sims Lifecycle Services, Son en Breugel; earlier AME, HQ Pack, RPI Print, HKC Europe) continuously since 2022. He cannot be running a custom-PC business in Kraków day-to-day.
- Evidence I have: his LinkedIn employment history (screenshots); the operational-impossibility argument; Waldek (unregistered) acting as de facto operator.

**Misrepresentation 2 — website: "We present selected products in our store."**
- Reality: there is no store. The CEIDG / Google Maps address (ul. Mogilska 16/7) is a **virtual office**; the Google Maps pin is labelled "Our office" at premises where they almost certainly have no presence and hold no stock.
- Evidence I have: the website text; the virtual-office nature of Mogilska 16/7; the Google Maps listing; Wayback Machine captures of the site (2016/2018/2019).

**Misrepresentation 3 — website: "Each shipment is carefully packaged and insured for its full value."**
- Reality: my goods were never delivered, and no insurance, claim, or cover ever materialised. Either (a) the shipment was never made (their own accounts contradict each other: 24.02.2026 "customs cleared the goods today" vs. 13.05.2026 "the Hong Kong supplier went bankrupt and the parts never arrived"), or (b) if anything was shipped and "lost," the promised full-value insurance plainly did not exist.
- Evidence I have: the website text; the two contradictory emails; non-delivery; the absence of any insurance payout or even a claim being offered.

**Aggregate theory — a staged appearance of safety and a "guaranteed" purchase.**
- Taken together, the store claim + insurance claim + Kraków-business appearance sell *legitimacy and security* that does not exist. Had I known it was a virtual-office shell run remotely by an unregistered person while the owner works abroad, with no real store and no real insurance, **I would not have transferred 155,000 PLN.**

## What I need

1. **Element-by-element fit to art. 286 § 1 KK** for each misrepresentation (1, 2, 3) and for the aggregate "fake front" theory:
   - Is each a *wprowadzenie w błąd* (active deception) about a **present fact**, as opposed to a future promise or mere marketing?
   - Causation: how does Polish law treat the link between the deception and the *niekorzystne rozporządzenie mieniem*? Is **subjective materiality** ("I would not have paid had I known") sufficient, or must the misled fact be **objectively material**? Cite authority.
   - Intent/timing: do pre-payment website representations help satisfy *dolus directus coloratus at contract formation* better than post-payment conduct?

2. **Puffery / *dolus bonus* / advertising boundary.** Where is the line between criminally relevant false statements and non-actionable marketing exaggeration? Is "we present products in our store" or "insured for full value" specific enough to be a factual representation a court would treat as deception? Cite case law on false statements in commercial advertising / e-commerce as *oszustwo*.

3. **The "fake front" / identity-and-capacity deception.** Is misrepresenting the *nature, location, and operational reality* of a business — presenting a remote shell as a bona fide operating merchant — itself deception about the seller's identity and capacity to perform? Address any overlap with **firmanctwo** (the registered owner as a front for the unregistered de facto operator) and whether that strengthens the art. 286 picture.

4. **Evidence sufficiency and ranking.** Rank the three misrepresentations by (a) how provable they are with what I already hold, and (b) legal strength. Which could stand alone, and which work only as corroboration? Identify the **gaps** the prosecutor would need to close (e.g. who authored/controlled the website; whether goods were ever actually shipped; whether any insurance policy ever existed; whether the site text was live at the date I paid).

5. **Attribution.** The website claims must be tied to a person. If Waldek built/ran the site (3dkrakow branding) while Mateusz is the JDG owner, how does that affect who is the *sprawca* of the deception — and does it reinforce the co-perpetration theory (art. 18 § 1 KK)?

6. **Honest likelihood assessment.** Given this is **affirmative pre-payment misrepresentation** (not just non-performance), how much does it raise the odds of (a) surviving the "mere civil dispute" screening, (b) an investigation being opened, and (c) charges? Compare to the concealment-only theory.

7. **Drafting payoff.** Give the concrete Polish wording I should use in the *zawiadomienie* to plead this theory — framing the website statements and the virtual-office/abroad-worker reality as the *method* of *wprowadzenie w błąd* that caused the disposition of 155,000 PLN.

## Evidence-preservation note (flag in the answer)
The website text is editable at will. Advise on how to preserve it to evidentiary standard **now**: dated full-page screenshots, Wayback Machine archiving of the live pages, and whether a notarised capture (*protokół z otwarcia strony internetowej* by a notariusz, or a print with metadata) is worth it for the key pages (the "store" claim, the "insured for full value" claim, the "Our office" map).

## Output format
Per-misrepresentation analysis (element fit + evidence + gaps), then the puffery boundary, then the ranked strength table, then an honest likelihood verdict comparing this theory to the concealment theory, then the Polish pleading wording.
