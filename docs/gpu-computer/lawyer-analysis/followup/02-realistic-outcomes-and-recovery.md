# Prompt 2 — Realistic outcome of the fraud complaint, and whether it ever returns my money

## Role
You are a Polish criminal litigator giving a brutally honest expected-value assessment to a victim who is deciding whether the criminal route is worth his time. Do not oversell. Use Polish statistics/practice where available, and be explicit about probabilities and time horizons. Distinguish sharply between "leverage/pressure value" and "actual cash in my hand."

## Case facts
(Same matter as the rest of this folder — summary:)
- 155,000 PLN paid Jan 2026 to Mateusz Szklarski / JDG GPUcomputer (NIP 8661681248, ul. Mogilska 16/7, Kraków) for an undelivered custom workstation, on a proforma issued by his firm.
- Written refund promise 10.03.2026 (155k + interest, 14 business days); default 31.03.2026.
- Contradictory excuses (customs cleared 24.02 vs. Hong Kong supplier bankruptcy 13.05); KRZ shows no insolvency.
- A second man, Waldek (Waldemar Łopata), ran the @gpucomputer mailboxes and made the refund promise; probable relative.
- Mateusz lives/works full-time in the Netherlands; the Kraków JDG is run remotely.
- EPU civil payment-order claim already filed (Nc-e 552126/26).
- The lawyer said the criminal route is the one that could also reach Waldek, but earlier research suggested the criminal track is "mostly pressure, may not return money."

## What I need
A realistic, end-to-end picture:

1. **Outcome decision tree after filing**, with rough probabilities for this specific fact pattern (sub-200k PLN, single known victim so far, foreign victim, debtor abroad):
   - odmowa wszczęcia / umorzenie at the screening stage,
   - investigation opened but later umorzone,
   - charges (przedstawienie zarzutów) → debtor becomes podejrzany,
   - indictment (akt oskarżenia),
   - conviction.
   Give realistic percentages and the typical time at each stage.

2. **What concretely changes the odds in my favour** — e.g. surfacing other victims (multi-victim pattern), the bank-trail showing the 155k was never used to order anything, proof there was no real Hong Kong supplier, the contradictory written statements. Rank these by impact.

3. **The actual money-recovery mechanisms inside the criminal process**, explained properly (not just named):
   - **art. 46 § 1 KK** obowiązek naprawienia szkody — when can I get it, what must I do (art. 49a KPK motion timing), is it automatic on conviction, and is it an enforceable execution title on its own (without a separate civil judgment)?
   - **art. 291–295 KPK** zabezpieczenie majątkowe — at what point does it become available (only once someone is podejrzany?), what assets it can freeze, who decides, cost to me, and how it compares to the civil zabezpieczenie the lawyer says I can't get.
   - Restitution / mediation / voluntary repayment as a sentence-mitigation incentive (does the threat of conviction realistically push a JDG to settle, and at what stage?).

4. **The honest bottom line: will this route return the 155k?** Separate clearly:
   - cases where it does (debtor solvent and settles under pressure, or art. 46 KK + successful enforcement),
   - cases where it returns nothing in cash but still delivers strategic value (asset freeze, non-dischargeability — see prompt 7 — record/business-ban pressure),
   - cases where it's a pure dead end.

5. **Cost vs. benefit if I run it myself** (prosecutor-driven, minimal paid counsel — see prompt 8): is the expected value positive given the filing is free and the prosecutor bears investigation cost?

## Output format
Decision tree first, then the recovery-mechanism deep dive, then a one-paragraph honest verdict on "money back: yes / no / conditional and on what."
