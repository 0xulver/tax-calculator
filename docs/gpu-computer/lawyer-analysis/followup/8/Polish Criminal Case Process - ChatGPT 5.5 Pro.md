## Plain answer: the prosecutor runs the criminal case; you do **not** need to pay a lawyer to “prosecute” it

For suspected **art. 286 § 1 KK fraud** against you as an unrelated commercial victim, this is a **public prosecution / ex officio criminal matter**, not a private prosecution. Your job is to file a solid notification, give evidence, testify, and monitor key decisions. The **prosecutor and police carry the burden of running the investigation** and, if the evidence supports it, bringing and supporting the indictment. You do **not** have to hire a lawyer for the State to investigate bank records, witnesses, supplier facts, communications, and other evidence. The KPK legalism rule says procedural authorities act ex officio, and the prosecuting authority must initiate and conduct preparatory proceedings and the public prosecutor must bring/support the accusation for offences prosecuted ex officio. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

The important limitation is this: the State is obliged to investigate when there is a **justified suspicion of a crime**, not to indict automatically. They may refuse or discontinue if they conclude there is no sufficient data, no criminal offence, no intent to deceive at the time of the transaction, limitation, jurisdiction problem, etc. KPK art. 303–305 requires a decision to open or refuse after a notification; if preliminary fact-checking is needed, the open/refuse decision should be issued within 30 days. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

## 1. Who prosecutes art. 286 KK fraud

Art. 286 § 1 KK covers causing another person, for financial gain, to make an unfavourable disposal of property by misleading them, exploiting their mistake, or exploiting inability to understand the action; the penalty is 6 months to 8 years. The only art. 286-specific “motion by victim” caveat is § 4: where the fraud is committed to the detriment of a **close person**. That is not your fact pattern: Mateusz Szklarski / GPUcomputer and any business co-perpetrator are not your close relatives. ([Dziennik Ustaw][2])

So, your case is **publicznoskargowe / ścigane z urzędu**. You are not expected to act as a private prosecutor. After you file a credible **zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu popełnienia przestępstwa**, the State’s preparatory proceeding is meant to determine whether a crime occurred, detect the perpetrator, clarify the facts including the victim and amount of damage, and collect/preserve evidence for court. The prosecutor conducts or supervises the preparatory proceeding, while the police may conduct it within statutory limits. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Practically, that means you should **ask** for bank-record checks, witness questioning, supplier verification, courier/shipping verification, business records, device/account/communications evidence, and questioning of Waldek Łopata if relevant. But you do not personally have to obtain bank secrecy material or compel witnesses; that is precisely why the case is run by the State.

## 2. Your role and rights as pokrzywdzony

During the investigation, you are a **party** to the preparatory proceeding. The authorities must instruct you about your victim status and rights, including evidence motions, participation in investigative acts, file access, interpreter rights, appeals against refusal/discontinuance, and the possibility of a court-appointed representative. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Your most important practical rights are:

| Right                                                         | What it means in your case                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **To be informed**                                            | You should receive notice of opening, refusal, or discontinuance. If you receive nothing within 6 weeks after filing the notification, you can complain to the superior prosecutor / supervising authority. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Evidence motions — wnioski dowodowe**                       | You can file written requests for specific investigative acts, for example obtaining bank statements for the receiving account, questioning identified witnesses, verifying supplier stock/order history, securing correspondence, and checking whether the same seller defrauded others. KPK art. 315 expressly allows the victim to file motions for investigative acts. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1]) |
| **Participation in some acts**                                | If an act is performed on your motion, you and your representative generally cannot be refused participation if you requested it, subject to investigative exceptions. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Access to files**                                           | You may request file access/copies. During the active investigation this can be refused if needed to protect the proper course of proceedings or important State interests; later, especially near the end of preparatory proceedings, refusal becomes more limited. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])                                                                                                       |
| **Appeal refusal or discontinuance — art. 306 KPK zażalenie** | As victim, you may appeal refusal to initiate and, as a party, discontinuance. ([Gov.pl][3])                                                                            |

## 3. Oskarżyciel posiłkowy after indictment

If the public prosecutor files an indictment, you can become an **oskarżyciel posiłkowy** by making a simple statement before the start of the court trial’s evidentiary phase. This lets you act as a court party alongside the prosecutor: attend, submit evidence motions, ask questions, make submissions, and potentially appeal. KPK art. 53–54 provides that in public-prosecution cases the victim may act as an auxiliary prosecutor, and if the public prosecutor filed the indictment, the victim may declare auxiliary-prosecutor participation until the start of the main trial. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

This does **not** require a lawyer. A simple statement is enough, for example:

> **Oświadczam, że będę działał w sprawie w charakterze oskarżyciela posiłkowego.**

The main reason to join is control: you receive more procedural rights in court and are not merely a witness. The cost risk is limited in ordinary public prosecution, but be careful with appeals: if only you, as auxiliary prosecutor, file an appeal and it fails, appeal-stage costs may be shifted to you under KPK cost rules. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

## 4. Subsidiary indictment: the backstop that **does** require a lawyer

The **subsydiarny akt oskarżenia** is not the normal path. It is a backstop if the State refuses/discontinues and, after the statutory appeal route, still refuses.

The sequence is roughly:

1. Prosecutor/police refuse to initiate or discontinue.
2. You file **zażalenie under art. 306 KPK**.
3. The court overturns the refusal/discontinuance and gives binding instructions.
4. The prosecutor still refuses/discontinues again.
5. You complain to the superior prosecutor.
6. If the superior prosecutor maintains the negative decision, you have **one month** to file the subsidiary indictment under art. 55 KPK. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

That step has **przymus adwokacko-radcowski**: the subsidiary indictment filed by the victim must be drafted and signed by an advocate, legal adviser, or Prokuratoria Generalna lawyer. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

## 5. Do you have to hire and pay a pełnomocnik?

No. A **pełnomocnik** for a victim is optional. KPK art. 87 says a party other than the accused may appoint a representative; KPK art. 88 says a representative may be an advocate, legal adviser, or Prokuratoria lawyer. That is a right, not an obligation, except for specific filings such as the subsidiary indictment. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

### What is realistically DIY-able

DIY-able without paying a lawyer:

* filing the **zawiadomienie**;
* attaching a chronology, invoices, bank transfer proof, correspondence, seller details, and loss calculation;
* filing evidence motions;
* asking for interpreter, remote testimony, file access, and updates;
* joining as **oskarżyciel posiłkowy** after indictment;
* making a compensation/restitution request.

Worth considering paid or court-appointed help:

* a refusal/discontinuance appeal where the prosecutor says “civil dispute, no criminal intent”;
* complex evidence motions after file access;
* trial advocacy if the case becomes contested;
* appeal against a judgment;
* subsidiary indictment, where professional drafting/signature is mandatory.

## 6. Court-appointed pełnomocnik z urzędu

A victim can request a **pełnomocnik z urzędu** through KPK art. 88, which applies art. 78 correspondingly. The means test is whether you properly show that you cannot bear the cost of representation **without prejudice to necessary maintenance of yourself and your family**. The same rule also allows appointment for a specific procedural act. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Your Swedish nationality or foreign income does not disqualify you. It may make proof more work-heavy. Attach evidence: rent, income, bank balances, debts, dependants, medical costs, Swedish/Polish tax or income documents, and a short Polish explanation of monthly income/expenses. If documents are Swedish/English, attach a practical Polish summary; certified translations may help for key financial documents.

Useful wording:

> **Wniosek o wyznaczenie pełnomocnika z urzędu dla pokrzywdzonego**
> Na podstawie art. 88 § 1 w zw. z art. 78 § 1 KPK wnoszę o wyznaczenie mi pełnomocnika z urzędu, ponieważ nie jestem w stanie ponieść kosztów pełnomocnika bez uszczerbku dla niezbędnego utrzymania siebie i rodziny. Proszę o wyznaczenie pełnomocnika do całego postępowania / ewentualnie do sporządzenia zażalenia / ewentualnie do sporządzenia subsydiarnego aktu oskarżenia.

File it with the prosecutor/police handling the case and ask that it be forwarded to the competent court, or file directly with the court competent to hear the criminal case if known. The president or court referendarz of the competent court appoints the representative. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

## 7. What you personally must do or attend

You will likely be summoned at least once as a **witness-victim**. A witness has a duty to appear and testify. You can and should ask to be heard in Warsaw or by videoconference, especially if the case is handled in Kraków. KPK art. 177 § 1a allows witness examination by distance technology with live audio-video; in preparatory proceedings the prosecutor or relevant authority must provide the technical conditions. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Pure “written testimony” is not normally a full substitute for witness evidence. You can submit a written chronology and exhibits, but KPK does not allow witness testimony to be replaced by letters or notes; the authority will usually want a protocolled oral statement, possibly by video or at a local Warsaw unit. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Ask in the notification:

> **Wnoszę o przesłuchanie mnie w charakterze świadka przy użyciu urządzeń technicznych umożliwiających przesłuchanie na odległość, ewentualnie przez jednostkę Policji/Prokuratury w Warszawie w drodze pomocy prawnej, z uwagi na moje miejsce zamieszkania w Warszawie, koszty podróży oraz potrzebę udziału tłumacza.**

## 8. Interpreter rights

If you do not speak Polish at legal-procedural level, ask for an interpreter immediately. KPK art. 204 requires an interpreter where there is a need to question a person who does not know Polish, or to translate a foreign-language document into Polish or vice versa, or to acquaint a party with evidence. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

For official questioning and court/prosecutor actions, the interpreter is a process cost, not your private lawyer-style cost. KPK art. 618 includes witness and interpreter dues among State Treasury expenses, and KPK art. 619 says expenses are temporarily advanced by the State unless the law says otherwise. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

Private translations of your own attachments are different. You do not legally have to translate every exhibit before filing, but translating the most important documents can prevent delays or “we cannot assess the material” problems. For Swedish sworn translation, commercial prices I found are around **80 PLN gross per sworn page** in one current price list; sworn translation pages are typically counted differently from ordinary A4 pages, with one listed source using 1125 characters for sworn pages. ([mTlumaczenia][4])

## 9. Realistic out-of-pocket costs

### If you rely on the prosecutor and use no private counsel

| Item                                                 |                                                                                                                                                                                                                         Expected cost to you |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |
| Filing the criminal notification                     |                                                                                                                                                                            **0 PLN** filing fee in practice; possible postage/printing only. |
| Interpreter for official questioning/court           |                                                                                                                                       **0 PLN to you** as a process cost when the authority uses an interpreter. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1]) |
| Selected sworn translations you choose to commission |                                                                         Often **300–2,000 PLN**, depending on volume/language/urgency; Swedish can be around **80 PLN gross per sworn page** in current market examples. ([mTlumaczenia][4]) |
| Travel to Kraków if summoned                         | You may need to advance the money, but witnesses can claim travel, accommodation/subsistence, and lost income within statutory limits; the reimbursement request should be made within **3 days** after the act. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1]) |
| Copies/certified copies                              |                                                                               Usually small unless you request paid copies/certified copies. Self-made copies from files are not charged under KPK art. 156 § 2. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1]) |

A realistic no-lawyer budget is therefore:

* **Low-cost scenario:** **0–300 PLN** if you file electronically/by post, do not pre-translate much, and are heard in Warsaw/video.
* **Typical cautious scenario:** **500–2,000 PLN** for selected sworn translations, copies, postage, and incidental travel.
* **Conservative scenario:** **2,000–4,000 PLN** if you translate a larger Swedish/English document set or must travel to Kraków once or twice before reimbursement.

### If you hire a lawyer throughout

Published Kraków criminal-law price lists show consultations around **300 PLN/hour**, preparatory-proceeding participation around **350 PLN/hour**, court hearing participation around **500–700 PLN/hour**, and criminal-case stage fees starting around **2,500 PLN for investigation** and **3,000 PLN for court**, with higher fees depending on complexity. ([pacholska.eu][5])

For a 155,000 PLN business-fraud matter, full representation from notification through investigation and first-instance trial would realistically often be **8,000–25,000+ PLN**, more if there are many hearings, appeals, complex financial evidence, multiple suspects, or travel/substitution costs.

### If the case reaches subsidiary-indictment stage

Budget separately for that backstop: the subsidiary indictment needs a lawyer’s draft/signature, and because cost rules for private prosecution apply correspondingly when the auxiliary prosecutor brings the indictment in a public-prosecution case, there can be a **1,000 PLN flat expense amount** unless you obtain exemption. The 1,000 PLN figure is set by the 2025 Justice Minister regulation effective from 1 July 2025. ([monitorpolski.gov.pl][1])

## Bottom line for your decision

You do **not** need to pay a lawyer to “run” an art. 286 KK criminal case. The State runs it. Your best cost-efficient strategy is to file a strong, organized notification yourself, include precise evidence motions, request interpreter and remote/Warsaw testimony, monitor for a decision, and only spend money on targeted translations or targeted legal help if the prosecutor refuses/discontinues or the case reaches trial/subsidiary-indictment territory.
