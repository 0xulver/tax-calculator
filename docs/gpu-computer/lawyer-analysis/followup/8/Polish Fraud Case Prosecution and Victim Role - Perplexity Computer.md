The plain answer
Yes — the State runs this case for you, for free. Art. 286 § 1 KK fraud is a publicly-prosecuted offence (przestępstwo publicznoskargowe, ścigane z urzędu). Once you file a credible zawiadomienie, the prosecutor and police are legally obliged — under the zasada legalizmu in art. 10 § 1 KPK — to open and conduct the investigation, gather evidence, and, if there are grounds, draft and file the indictment and prosecute it in court at State expense. The relevant text is unambiguous: the body appointed to prosecute crimes "jest obowiązany do wszczęcia i przeprowadzenia postępowania przygotowawczego, a oskarżyciel publiczny także do wniesienia i popierania oskarżenia — o czyn ścigany z urzędu".

So you do not run this as a private prosecution, and you are not required to hire a lawyer. A pełnomocnik is optional for a victim at every ordinary stage. The prosecutor uses powers you simply don't have — pulling bank records, compelling witnesses, ordering production from suppliers — and bears the cost.

The one place where the burden shifts onto you and a lawyer becomes mandatory is the subsidiary indictment (subsydiarny akt oskarżenia) — the backstop if the prosecutor refuses twice. More on that below.

The art. 286 § 4 caveat does not apply to you
There is one narrow exception that converts art. 286 fraud into a "motion" offence (wnioskowy): art. 286 § 4 KK provides that "jeżeli czyn ... popełniono na szkodę osoby najbliższej, ściganie następuje na wniosek pokrzywdzonego" — i.e., only when the fraud is committed against a close family member (osoba najbliższa: spouse, ascendant, descendant, sibling, etc.). Mateusz Szklarski / GPUcomputer and Waldek Łopata are arm's-length commercial counterparties, not your osoba najbliższa, so § 4 is irrelevant. Your case stays squarely ex officio: the State prosecutes whether or not you ask it to (your zawiadomienie merely triggers it).

Your role and rights as pokrzywdzony
During the preparatory proceedings (postępowanie przygotowawcze) you are a party (strona) with real, usable rights — none of which require a lawyer:

Right to be informed and to file evidence motions (wnioski dowodowe): you can ask the prosecutor in writing to question specific witnesses, obtain the supplier's records, secure the suspects' bank statements, etc. You can submit these yourself.

Right to access the case file (wgląd do akt); the prosecutor may even make it available electronically.

Right to appeal a refusal or discontinuance — your most important lever. Under art. 306 KPK, a zażalenie lies against both a refusal to open the investigation (§ 1, expressly granted to "pokrzywdzonemu") and a discontinuance/umorzenie (§ 1a, granted to "stronom"). You also get the file to prepare the appeal (§ 1b), and if you're not told within 6 weeks whether an investigation was opened, you can complain to the superior prosecutor (§ 3). You can write and file this zażalenie yourself.

Oskarżyciel posiłkowy (auxiliary prosecutor)
Once the prosecutor files the indictment, you may declare — up to the start of the trial (przewód sądowy) — that you wish to act as an oskarżyciel posiłkowy alongside the prosecutor. This upgrades you from "victim who reported it" to an active party at trial: you can ask questions, file motions, address the court, and appeal the judgment. It does not require a lawyer — you can do it yourself with a short written statement. It also costs no court fee. This is the cheapest way to stay actively involved while the State still carries the prosecution.

The subsidiary indictment backstop — and the one place a lawyer is mandatory
If the prosecutor won't prosecute, the "two refusals" mechanism in art. 330 KPK is your route to court:

Prosecutor discontinues/refuses → you file a zażalenie (art. 306).

The court can overturn the decision and give binding instructions (art. 330 § 1).

If the prosecutor still finds no grounds and discontinues again, that second decision is appealable only to the superior prosecutor (art. 330 § 2).

If the superior prosecutor upholds it, then a victim who has "co najmniej dwukrotnie wykorzystał uprawnienia przewidziane w art. 306" may file a subsidiary indictment under art. 55.

Here the burden genuinely shifts to you — and counsel becomes compulsory. Art. 55 § 2 KPK states that the victim's act of indictment "powinien być sporządzony i podpisany przez adwokata, radcę prawnego albo radcę Prokuratorii Generalnej" — the przymus adwokacko-radcowski. You cannot draft or sign a subsidiary indictment yourself; it must be prepared and signed by an advocate or legal counsel. (Note: if you reach this point and then a prosecutor later decides to step back in, they can rejoin as oskarżyciel publiczny under art. 55 § 4, and you revert to auxiliary-prosecutor status.)

Do you have to hire and pay a lawyer?
Mandatory? No — not at any ordinary stage. The only mandatory-lawyer step is the subsidiary indictment. Everything else (zawiadomienie, evidence motions, zażalenie, oskarżyciel posiłkowy declaration, testifying) is DIY-able.

Free court-appointed pełnomocnik (z urzędu)? Yes, available to a victim. Art. 88 § 1 KPK expressly applies the rules on defence counsel — including art. 78 KPK — "odpowiednio" to a pełnomocnik. The means-test is that you must "w sposób należyty wykaże, że nie jest w stanie ponieść kosztów ... bez uszczerbku dla niezbędnego utrzymania siebie i rodziny" — credibly show you cannot bear the cost without harm to your and your family's basic upkeep. You can even request one just for a specific act, such as drafting the subsidiary indictment (art. 78 § 1a, applied via art. 88).

How to apply: file a written request (wniosek o wyznaczenie pełnomocnika z urzędu) with the court competent to hear the case, attaching a statement of your financial situation (an oświadczenie o stanie majątkowym / rodzinnym). The appointment is made by the court president or referendarz.

Your foreign residence/income: this doesn't bar you — eligibility turns on means, not nationality or where income arises. In practice it makes the means assessment slightly more involved, because the court will want a credible picture of your total income/assets (including any abroad). Modest means + honest, documented disclosure is what matters. Using free legal aid in the meantime cannot be a ground to refuse you appointed counsel (art. 78 § 1, as amended).

What you must personally do/attend
Testifying: Yes, you will be summoned as a witness; "każda osoba wezwana w charakterze świadka ma obowiązek stawić się i złożyć zeznania". But you can avoid repeated Kraków trips: under art. 177 § 1a KPK, questioning "może nastąpić przy użyciu urządzeń technicznych umożliwiających przeprowadzenie tej czynności na odległość z jednoczesnym bezpośrednim przekazem obrazu i dźwięku" — i.e. by videoconference, with a court/prosecution official present at your location. There's no absolute right to it (the organ decides), but request it expressly in your filings, citing your Warsaw residence and limited means; courts grant remote questioning routinely for out-of-city witnesses. A witness who can't appear due to an insurmountable obstacle can also be questioned at their place of stay (art. 177 § 2).

Interpreter: For your questioning, yes — provided and State-paid. Art. 204 § 1 KPK makes calling an interpreter mandatory ("należy wezwać tłumacza") to question "osoby nie władającej językiem polskim," and § 2 covers translating foreign-language documents. One important nuance to flag honestly: the strongest free-interpreter guarantee (art. 72 KPK) is written for the accused, and Polish doctrine notes a gap — a victim acting purely as a party (e.g. as oskarżyciel posiłkowy) is not given the same explicit statutory guarantee for non-questioning participation. In practice, for your own questioning the interpreter is provided and paid by the State, and at the court (jurisdictional) stage art. 5 § 2 of the Law on Courts (u.s.p.) gives a non-Polish speaker the right to appear in a known language with free interpreter assistance. What is not automatically free is interpreting/translation you arrange for your own convenience outside official acts.

Route B — Hiring a pełnomocnik throughout
Lawyers set fees freely; the statutory minima (Min. of Justice fee regulation, PZ Adwokaci) are a floor, not the market price:

Minima: ~600 PLN for the investigation (śledztwo) stage as victim's counsel, ~840–1,200 PLN before the court of first instance, with +20% per extra trial day.

Real-world full representation in a contested 155,000 PLN fraud: typically ~4,000–12,000+ PLN all-in, depending on complexity and trial length.

A subsidiary indictment alone (if you ever need it): drafting + filing commonly ~2,000–4,000+ PLN, since it's mandatory-lawyer work. Plus translations as above.

Represented total: roughly 5,000–18,000 PLN, plus translations.

A cost-recovery sweetener: if the accused is convicted, the court can order them to reimburse your costs, including reasonable counsel fees and the expenses of your participation — so represented costs are potentially recoverable from the defendant on a guilty verdict (subject to the court's award and the defendant's solvency).

Bottom line for your situation
Start in Route A: file the zawiadomienie yourself, join as oskarżyciel posiłkowy after indictment, request videoconference questioning and a State interpreter, and use the art. 306 zażalenie if the prosecutor stalls. Keep a lawyer in reserve for two triggers only: (1) if you hit the subsidiary-indictment wall (then counsel is legally required), or (2) a contested trial where you want active advocacy. Given your modest means and foreign residence, apply for a pełnomocnik z urzędu under art. 88 + art. 78 the moment a lawyer becomes necessary — eligibility is means-based, and your non-Polish status doesn't disqualify you.

One caveat worth stating plainly: this is general procedural information based on the current KPK/KK and published fee schedules, not individual legal advice. Before any deadline-sensitive step (especially the zażalenie and the subsidiary-indictment route), confirm the specifics with a Polish advocate or the free state legal aid scheme (nieodpłatna pomoc prawna), which you qualify to use and which cannot be held against your z urzędu request.
