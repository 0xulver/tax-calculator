The governing law (so each element below has an anchor)
Art. 286 § 1 KK punishes whoever, in order to obtain a material benefit, brings another person to a disadvantageous disposition of property by means of leading them into error, exploiting an error, or exploiting incapacity to properly comprehend the action. Three points control your case:

The method is a closed list. SN, III KK 216/25 (17.06.2025): the perpetrator's conduct "sprowadza się do trzech wymienionych w tym przepisie sposobów" and "wszystkie inne sposoby wywołania skutku w postaci niekorzystnego rozporządzenia mieniem pozostają poza zakresem kryminalizacji". Taking a prepayment and not delivering/refunding "stanowi jedynie podstawę odpowiedzialności kontraktowej, nie zaś przestępstwo oszustwa." You must name a sposób and a fact.

The error must concern a present fact material to the disposition. The misled person's "wyobrażenie o rzeczywistości nie odpowiada faktom w zakresie istotnym dla podjęcia decyzji o określonym rozporządzeniu mieniem".

Intent — dolus directus coloratus — at the moment of contracting. The directional, specially-coloured intent must cover both the deception and the disadvantageous disposition, and must exist "od samego początku" (kancelaria-adwokata.pl on dolus directus coloratus; SA Katowice II AKa 294/17, orzeczenia.katowice.sa.gov.pl).

On causation/materiality there are two strands you must understand, because your whole theory turns on them:

Subjective sufficiency strand: "Dla zaistnienia przestępstwa oszustwa wystarczające jest ustalenie, że pokrzywdzony nie zawarłby umowy, gdyby wiedział o okolicznościach, które były przedmiotem wprowadzenia go w błąd" (SA Katowice II AKa 194/16, quoted in II AKa 294/17). On its face this supports "I would not have paid had I known."

Objective-materiality counterweight: the dominant line ties the error to "okoliczności, która ma wpływ na możliwość realizacji warunków transakcji" — i.e. a circumstance objectively affecting the seller's ability to perform. Courts blend the two: the "I wouldn't have paid" test is necessary but is assessed against whether a reasonable contracting party would treat the fact as decision-relevant. Pure subjective idiosyncrasy is not enough; an objectively decision-relevant fact that you also subjectively relied on is.

Implication for your drafting: plead every misrepresentation as a fact that objectively bore on GPUcomputer's capacity and reliability to perform — not merely as something that personally reassured you.

Misrepresentation 1 — "this is a genuine, operating Kraków computer business"

The implied representation — that there is a real, operationally-staffed Kraków undertaking able to build and ship a 155,000 PLN custom workstation — is a present-fact representation about capacity to perform. The puffery / dolus bonus boundary
Polish law (like most systems) does not criminalise dolus bonus — permissible commercial exaggeration a reasonable recipient discounts ("best workstations," "perfect," "unbeatable performance"). The line is specificity and verifiability:

Non-actionable puffery: "Perfekcyjne stacje robocze", "sprawdź ile zaoszczędzisz", "czas to pieniądz", "sprzęt działa bezawaryjnie" — vague, evaluative, not checkable. A court treats these as marketing.

Actionable factual representation: "ubezpieczona do pełnej wartości" — concrete, binary, verifiable (a policy either exists or it does not). This is the same logic courts apply to false online-shop listings being charged as art. 286 (e.g. SAOS II K 534/21, where the analysis turns on whether the seller made a checkable false representation with directional intent — the acquittal there was for lack of intent, not because online-shop statements can't be fraud). False statements in e-commerce are squarely within art. 286 when specific and intentional (kancelariamw.pl, oszustwo 2026; CERT Polska on advertising fraud, cert.pl).

So: M3 clears the puffery line cleanly. M1 is mixed (implied factual core, wrapped in puffery). M2's surviving fragment is factual (address/seat) but low-materiality.

The fake-front / identity-and-capacity deception, and firmanctwo overlap
Misrepresenting the nature, seat, and operational reality of a business — presenting a remotely-run shell with an absent registered owner as a bona fide operating merchant — is deception about the seller's identity and capacity to perform, which Polish doctrine recognises as a classic wprowadzenie w błąd about a circumstance affecting możliwość realizacji. This is the correct home for the M1/M2 material.

Firmanctwo (art. 55 KKS) — the registered owner (Mateusz) used as a firmant fronting for the unregistered de facto operator (Waldek) — is a separate fiscal offence: using another's name/firm to conceal who really conducts the business and thereby exposing tax to depletion (Kancelaria Gatner; adwokat-skoczylas.pl). It is not itself oszustwo, and you should not conflate them in the zawiadomienie. But it strengthens the art. 286 picture two ways: (1) it independently evidences that the "registered Kraków business" presentation was a deliberate construction concealing the real operator — i.e. it corroborates the fake-front deception and the intent; (2) it gives the prosecutor a second, fiscal hook and a motive to investigate. Flag it as a related offence (note the JDG is registered, so the firmanctwo fit depends on whether Waldek operates on his own account behind Mateusz's NIP — that's a factual question for the organs, not something you assert as proven).
