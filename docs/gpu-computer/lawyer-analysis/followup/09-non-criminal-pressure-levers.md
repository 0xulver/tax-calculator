# Prompt 9 — Non-criminal pressure levers tied to the clean KRZ (business ban + KAS/VAT)

## Role
You are a Polish insolvency- and tax-enforcement specialist. Besides the civil EPU claim and the criminal fraud complaint, I want to know what *other* low-cost pressure levers exist — particularly ones triggered by the fact that the debtor delayed payment for months yet the KRZ shows he never filed for bankruptcy.

## Case facts
- Debtor: Mateusz Szklarski, sole proprietor (JDG GPUcomputer, NIP 8661681248), Kraków; physically in the Netherlands.
- He told me (by email) he had "filed for bankruptcy protection" and cited a Hong Kong supplier's collapse — but **KRZ shows no bankruptcy or restructuring proceeding** against him or the business as of 28.05.2026. So either the claim was false, or he never filed despite being unable to pay a 155,000 PLN debt due since 31.03.2026 (and likely other obligations).
- The transaction: 155k prepayment for an undelivered workstation; written refund promise broken.
- He charged on a proforma; whether a VAT invoice (faktura) was issued and VAT accounted for is unclear. The second operator, Waldek, is **not VAT-registered**.

## What I need

1. **Obligation to file for bankruptcy and the consequences of not doing so (JDG).**
   - **art. 21 PU** — a JDG entrepreneur must file for bankruptcy within **30 days** of becoming insolvent; **art. 11 § 1a PU** presumption of insolvency after a 3-month payment delay. Has that window plausibly lapsed for him already?
   - **art. 21 § 3 PU** — personal civil liability of the entrepreneur to creditors for damage caused by failing to file in time. Could I claim under this?
   - **art. 373 PU** — court-imposed **business activity ban (zakaz prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej) for 1–10 years** on an entrepreneur who failed to file in time. **Can I, as a creditor, apply for this ban directly to the insolvency court, independent of the criminal and civil tracks?** Procedure, cost, evidence needed, and realistic prospects. How professionally damaging is it for him?

2. **Can a creditor force a bankruptcy?**
   - Filing a **creditor's bankruptcy petition (wniosek wierzyciela o ogłoszenie upadłości)** against his JDG — threshold, cost (incl. any zaliczka), risk, and whether it's a credible pressure tool or a money-sink given he may have no assets. When does it make sense vs. not?

3. **The KAS / tax (VAT) lever.**
   - If VAT was due on the 155k advance (tax point on receipt of prepayment) and he didn't issue a proper faktura or account for it, a **zawiadomienie/donos to the Urząd Skarbowy / KAS** could trigger an audit. Relevant: art. 56, 62, 76 KKS.
   - I have **no standing as pokrzywdzony** in a KKS matter (the State is the victim) — confirm — but does the audit pressure itself have leverage value at near-zero cost to me? How do I file such a notice?
   - The fact that Waldek operates the business but is not VAT-registered — does that itself suggest a tax irregularity worth flagging?

4. **UOKiK / consumer angle** — only briefly: given I bought (arguably) for personal use, is a UOKiK notice or any consumer-protection mechanism worth filing as supplementary pressure, or is it a dead end for a single B2B-looking transaction?

5. **Sequencing / proportionality.** Rank these levers by (pressure created ÷ cost & effort to me), and flag any that could **backfire** (e.g. pushing him into a bankruptcy that suspends my EPU and dumps me into a near-empty Category III estate — cross-reference the discharge/non-dischargeability analysis in prompt 7).

## Output format
Lever-by-lever analysis with cost, standing, and procedure for each, then a ranked "best bang-for-buck pressure moves" list with explicit backfire warnings.
