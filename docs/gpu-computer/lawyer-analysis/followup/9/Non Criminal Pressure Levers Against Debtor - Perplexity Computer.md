Lever 1 — Bankruptcy-filing duty and its consequences (art. 21, 11, 373 PU)
1a. Has the filing window plausibly lapsed? (art. 11 §1a + art. 21 ust. 1 PU)
Yes — almost certainly, on your timeline.

art. 11 ust. 1a PU presumes a debtor has lost the ability to pay matured monetary obligations once the payment delay exceeds three months. This is a rebuttable presumption (domniemanie wzruszalne) that shifts the burden onto the debtor to prove he was actually solvent (CEJSH – Michta, on art. 11 ust. 1a; Kancelaria Świąder).

art. 21 ust. 1 PU then gives the debtor 30 days from the moment insolvency objectively arose to file for bankruptcy.

Applied to your facts: the 155k was due 31.03.2026. Three months' delay lands at ~30.06.2026, and the presumption of insolvency crystallises then; the 30-day window would close ~30.07.2026. Important nuance: the 3-month presumption is an evidentiary shortcut, not the only route to insolvency. Insolvency is defined functionally as loss of the ability to pay matured debts (art. 11 ust. 1 PU) — and a debtor who could not refund 155k since March, told you he "filed for bankruptcy protection," and cited a supplier collapse has effectively admitted he could not meet obligations well before the 3-month mark. So the duty arguably arose earlier than end-June; the presumption merely makes it trivially easy to prove from ~end-June onward.

Practical read: by late summer 2026 you will have a clean, presumption-backed case that he was obliged to file and didn't. Right now (early June) you are close but the cleanest window is a few weeks out. This timing matters for the ban (1b) and the creditor petition (Lever 2).

1b. Personal civil liability to creditors (art. 21 ust. 3 PU)
Yes, this is a live claim — and it is conceptually distinct from your EPU contract claim.

art. 21 ust. 3 PU makes the entrepreneur personally liable for damage caused by failing to file in time. Crucially, the evidentiary burden is light: the creditor only has to make plausible (uprawdopodobnić) that the petition was filed late (i.e. past the 30-day window); the debtor must then disprove fault and disprove the damage.

What "damage" means here, and the catch: the damage is typically the difference between what you would have recovered had he filed on time versus what you actually recover. If he was already asset-empty in March, the late filing may have caused you little or no incremental damage — making the recoverable amount under art. 21 ust. 3 potentially small or nominal, even though liability exists.

Verdict on 1b: Real, low evidentiary threshold, but for a JDG (unlike a sp. z o.o. board under art. 299 KSH) it largely duplicates your existing 155k claim, because a sole proprietor is already personally liable with his entire estate for the underlying debt (as your komornik brief established — no firm/private asset separation). The added value of art. 21 ust. 3 is therefore modest for a JDG: you don't need it to reach his personal assets. Treat it as a secondary pleading, not a standalone lever. Its real use is rhetorical/pressure — citing it in correspondence signals you understand his exposure.

1c. Business-activity ban — art. 373 PU (the strongest non-criminal lever)
This is the lever with the highest pressure-to-cost ratio, and yes — you can apply directly as a creditor, independent of the criminal and civil tracks, and without any bankruptcy ever being opened.

Standing. A creditor is expressly named among those entitled to move for the ban. The Supreme Court has confirmed the proceeding can be initiated solely on the application of a person listed in art. 376 ust. 1 PU, and that list includes the creditor (wierzyciel) (Sąd Najwyższy IV CSK 564/14; Kancelaria Rychlewska-Hotel on art. 373).

No bankruptcy required. The same SN ruling makes the key point for your situation directly: the ban proceeding can be brought even where no bankruptcy proceeding was ever opened — i.e. precisely the "clean KRZ" scenario. You don't have to first force his bankruptcy. You simply have to prove you hold a claim that would be satisfiable from the bankruptcy estate under PU rules.

The ground. art. 373 ust. 1 pkt 1 PU: the court may impose the ban on someone who, being obliged by law, failed to file the bankruptcy petition within the statutory deadline — exactly the art. 21/art. 11 §1a fact pattern from Lever 1a.

The sanction. Deprivation, for 1 to 10 years, of the right to:

run a business on one's own account or in a civil-law partnership;

act as zarządca sukcesyjny, member of a supervisory board / audit committee, representative or proxy of a sole trader, commercial company, state enterprise, cooperative, foundation or association.

How professionally damaging is it for him? Severe and durable. The ban is entered in the KRZ/public registers, it kills his JDG, bars him from board/proxy roles, and is visible to counterparties and banks. For someone whose livelihood is running "GPUcomputer," a ban of even a few years is functionally a career interdiction in his field and a permanent reputational marker. This is the single most personally threatening civil consequence available to you.

Critical deadline — art. 377 PU (don't miss this). Where no bankruptcy petition was filed, the ban proceeding must be initiated within 3 years from the day the state of insolvency ceased or the filing obligation expired (SN V CSKP 8/21; orzeczenie XIII Ga 1122/15). Since his insolvency is ongoing (debt unpaid), the clock has not even started — you have ample time, but file once Lever 1a's window has clearly lapsed (late summer 2026+) so the "failed to file in time" element is unimpeachable.

Procedure / cost / evidence:

File a wniosek o orzeczenie zakazu prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej with the insolvency division of the rejonowy court competent for the debtor (Kraków).

Evidence you need: (i) proof you are a creditor whose claim would be satisfiable from the estate — your EPU nakaz/title and the underlying contract, proforma, transfer, and broken refund promise; (ii) proof the filing duty arose and lapsed — i.e. the 3-month delay (art. 11 §1a) plus the 30-day window (art. 21), evidenced by the due date, his admissions, and the clean KRZ printout (28.05.2026) showing he never filed.

Cost: modest court fee (this is a non-contentious insolvency-division matter, not a 1000 PLN upadłość petition) plus your own time; far cheaper than forcing a bankruptcy. No large zaliczka is required because you are not opening an estate.

Realistic prospects: strong on the merits if you nail the timing element. The main defence routes for him are (i) proving he was actually solvent (hard, given his own statements), or (ii) showing he timely filed a restructuring petition (KRZ says he didn't), or (iii) art. 373 ust. 2 — court discretion to dismiss where a restructuring was opened and creditor harm is slight (inapplicable: no restructuring, harm is 155k). Note the court may (discretionary "może orzec") decline or shorten — but the fact pattern is squarely within the core scenario the provision targets.

Lever 2 — Can a creditor force the bankruptcy? (creditor's petition)
Standing & threshold. Yes — as a creditor you may file a wniosek wierzyciela o ogłoszenie upadłości. You must make your claim plausible (uprawdopodobnić wierzytelność) and show the debtor is insolvent — and here the art. 11 §1a 3-month presumption does the heavy lifting for you.

Cost.

Court fee: 1 000 PLN fixed (art. 74 ustawy o kosztach sądowych w sprawach cywilnych) (Kancelaria RPMS; CNO Legal).

Zaliczka on procedural costs: the court will demand an advance roughly equal to one average monthly salary — practitioners cite figures around 7 000+ PLN. You front this.

The fatal problem — art. 13 PU. The court must dismiss the petition (oddala wniosek) if the debtor's assets are insufficient to cover even the costs of the proceeding, or suffice only to cover those costs (orzeczenie VI Gz 37/18, Rzeszów; UEK materiały – art. 13 PU). Given your komornik analysis already points to an asset-empty debtor who has fled to NL, the realistic outcome is dismissal under art. 13 — you lose the 1000 PLN fee and risk part of the zaliczka, and get no estate.

Is it credible pressure or a money-sink? Mostly a money-sink against an empty JDG, with two redeeming features:

The threat itself (a credible, drafted petition served on him) can be a potent settlement lever before filing — for someone in business, an open bankruptcy petition is alarming.

A dismissal under art. 13 is not wasted ammunition for Lever 1c: the court's finding that he is insolvent with no assets is itself strong evidence that the art. 21 filing duty arose and lapsed — feeding directly into the business-ban application. And the art. 377 ban deadline after an art. 13 dismissal is only 1 year, so calendar it.

When it makes sense vs. not. File the actual petition only if you have concrete reason to believe there is a recoverable estate (e.g. komornik finds a vehicle, real property, or receivables). Otherwise use it as a drafted-but-unfiled threat and rely on Lever 1c for the lasting damage.

Lever 3 — The KAS / VAT (KKS) lever
The VAT logic
If a VAT invoice was due, the tax point on an advance payment (zaliczka) arises on receipt of the prepayment (art. 19a ust. 8 ustawy o VAT) and a faktura zaliczkowa is generally required under art. 106b ust. 1 pkt 4. If he took 155k and neither issued a proper faktura nor accounted for the output VAT, that is a tax irregularity. Relevant KKS provisions:

art. 62 KKS — failing to issue an invoice when obliged, or issuing it defectively/unreliably (penalty scales from a wykroczenie skarbowe fine up to a przestępstwo skarbowe with up to 720 daily-rate fines / prison for large amounts) (Kancelaria Skarbiec – art. 62 KKS; adwokat-szymczyk on art. 62 §2).

art. 56 KKS — false/omitted tax declaration (under-declaring liability).

art. 76 KKS — wrongful refund/over-claim.

Your standing — confirmed, you have none as pokrzywdzony
Correct. In a KKS matter the injured party is the State Treasury (Skarb Państwa), not you. You cannot be a pokrzywdzony, cannot drive the proceeding, and have no procedural rights in it. What you can do is file a zawiadomienie / "donos" do urzędu skarbowego (KAS) — a non-binding notification that may trigger a czynności sprawdzające or a full audit (kontrola). The tax authority decides whether to act; you are a tipster, not a party.

Does the audit pressure have leverage value at near-zero cost?
Yes — this is the cheapest lever you have, and its value is purely in terrorem:

Cost: effectively zero — a short written notice (post, ePUAP, or in person) to the naczelnik of his competent urząd skarbowy, identifying GPUcomputer/NIP 8661681248, describing the 155k advance, the proforma, the absence of a faktura/VAT settlement, and attaching what you have.

Leverage: an in-progress KAS audit creates exactly the kind of administrative pressure (back-tax + interest + possible 100% sanction, plus KKS exposure) that motivates a debtor to settle to make the broader problem disappear.

You get no money from it — any recovered tax flows to the State, not to you. Its only function is pressure and, indirectly, evidence (an audit may surface his asset/income picture, useful elsewhere).

Waldek operating without VAT registration
Worth flagging in the same notice, yes — but frame it as an observation, not a conclusion. The signal: if Waldek actually operates the business (issues quotes, takes orders, runs sales) yet is not VAT-registered, that points to one of two irregularities the KAS cares about — either turnover is being run through a non-registered person to stay under/around VAT obligations, or there is an undeclared division of activity. Either way it is a legitimate "worth-checking" flag for the tax office. You are not adjudicating it; you are giving KAS a thread to pull. Keep your language factual ("Waldek X appears to operate the business and is, to my knowledge, not VAT-registered") to avoid any defamation exposure.

How to file
Address a written zawiadomienie o możliwości popełnienia czynu zabronionego / nieprawidłowościach podatkowych to the Naczelnik Urzędu Skarbowego competent for the debtor's registered seat (Kraków), or to the relevant Urząd Celno-Skarbowy / KAS. State facts, attach evidence (emails, proforma, transfer confirmation, refund promise), give your contact details (anonymous tips are accepted but carry less weight). No fee.

Lever 4 — UOKiK / consumer angle (brief)
Largely a dead end as a pressure lever, with one small qualification.

UOKiK doesn't resolve individual disputes. The President of UOKiK pursues collective consumer-interest infringements; it will not chase your single 155k transaction or recover your money for you.

Individual help routes are the rzecznik konsumentów (municipal/county consumer ombudsman) and out-of-court dispute resolution (e.g. Inspekcja Handlowa's permanent arbitration courts) — but both require a genuine B2C relationship, and your transaction looks B2B (workstation, proforma, likely his JDG to your business context) (Gov.pl – pomoc dla konsumentów; UOKiK – rzecznicy konsumentów).

The qualification: if you can genuinely characterise the purchase as personal use (consumer), a rzecznik konsumentów intervention is free and can generate a semi-official letter to the trader — mild added pressure. But for a single, B2B-looking, already-litigated claim where you hold an EPU title, it adds almost nothing over what you already have. Skip it unless the consumer framing is clearly defensible and you want one more low-effort letter in the file.
