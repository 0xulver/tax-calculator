## Bottom line

The **clean KRZ is useful pressure evidence**, but it does **not yet prove a missed bankruptcy-filing duty based only on your PLN 155,000 debt due 31.03.2026**.

Under Polish Bankruptcy Law, insolvency is presumed when a debtor’s delay in paying monetary obligations exceeds **3 months**, and the debtor must file a bankruptcy petition within **30 days** from the date the basis for bankruptcy arose. On your single debt alone, the 3-month presumption would plausibly mature around **1–2 July 2026**, not on 28.05.2026; the 30-day filing window would then expire around **end July / early August 2026**. It may already have lapsed only if you can show **older unpaid obligations, other creditors, ZUS/tax arrears, failed enforcement, or admissions that he was already unable to pay earlier**. ([Isap][1])

The best low-cost levers are: **KAS/VAT notice now**, **business-ban threat/draft now**, and **actual art. 373 PU ban application after the 3-month presumption or once you have evidence of other unpaid creditors**. A **creditor bankruptcy petition** is much more expensive and can backfire by dragging your claim into a low-recovery bankruptcy process.

# 1. Art. 21 PU duty to file + civil consequences for JDG

## Duty to file

Mateusz as a **JDG entrepreneur** is a bankruptcy-capable debtor. Bankruptcy Law applies to entrepreneurs, and a debtor is insolvent if he has lost the ability to pay due monetary obligations. There is a statutory presumption that this loss exists when payment delay exceeds **3 months**. The debtor must file a bankruptcy petition within **30 days** from the date the basis for bankruptcy arose. ([Isap][1])

For your timeline:

| Date                             | Significance                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **31.03.2026**                   | Refund/debt due date you gave                                                |
| **01.04.2026 approx.**           | Payment delay starts in practical terms                                      |
| **01–02.07.2026 approx.**        | 3-month art. 11 PU presumption plausibly matures                             |
| **End July / early August 2026** | 30-day art. 21 PU filing deadline would plausibly lapse if no petition filed |

So: **as of 28.05.2026, the art. 11 § 1a / art. 21 PU route is not yet ripe on your claim alone**. The clean KRZ still matters because it contradicts his “I filed for bankruptcy protection” email, but the stronger legal conclusion is: either he lied/misled you, or he referred to something outside Polish bankruptcy/restructuring proceedings, or he has not filed despite being in financial distress.

## Could the deadline already have lapsed?

Yes, but only if insolvency arose earlier than your due date. Evidence that would move the clock back includes:

* other unpaid customers or suppliers;
* ZUS / VAT / PIT arrears;
* bounced payments, account seizures, debt collection letters;
* admissions such as “I cannot pay because the supplier collapsed” before 31.03.2026;
* continued taking prepayments while unable to deliver/refund;
* failed enforcement or lack of attachable assets;
* public complaints showing older unpaid orders.

Without that, an art. 373 ban application filed before early July is more vulnerable to being treated as a **one-creditor collection dispute** rather than insolvency misconduct.

## Art. 21 § 3 PU civil liability

Art. 21 PU also creates liability for damage caused by failure to file in time. In a creditor’s claim against an insolvent debtor, the damage is presumed to include the amount of the unsatisfied claim, unless the debtor proves no fault or that timely restructuring-type protection existed. ([Isap][1])

Practical value in a **JDG** case is limited. Mateusz is already personally liable for his JDG debt; there is no company veil to pierce. A separate art. 21 § 3 lawsuit would mostly duplicate your existing PLN 155,000 claim unless you are trying to claim extra damage caused by the late filing.

Use it mainly as:

* an allegation in a demand letter;
* supporting material for an art. 373 business-ban petition;
* supporting material in later insolvency/discharge objections;
* evidence of bad-faith financial conduct.

It is **not** the first pressure lever I would spend money on.

# 2. Art. 373 PU business activity ban

## Can you apply directly as creditor?

Yes. A creditor can directly apply to the bankruptcy court for a **zakaz prowadzenia działalności gospodarczej** under art. 373 PU. This is independent of your EPU/civil claim and independent of a criminal complaint. Proceedings for the ban can be initiated on application of, among others, a creditor, and payment to the applicant during the ban case does not automatically discontinue the proceeding. ([Isap][1])

## What the ban does

The court may impose a ban for **1–10 years** on a person who, at fault, failed to file a bankruptcy petition within the statutory deadline. The ban may cover running business as a sole proprietor and holding management, supervisory, representative or proxy roles in companies, cooperatives, foundations, associations and similar entities. The court considers the degree of fault, consequences, enterprise-value deterioration and harm to creditors. ([Isap][1])

For someone trading in hardware, taking prepayments, dealing with banks, suppliers and customers, this is professionally damaging because it is a formal court restriction, publicly announced in the insolvency system, and directly attacks his ability to continue as a trusted entrepreneur.

## Cost

The filing fee for a business-ban application is **PLN 100**. This is materially cheaper than a creditor bankruptcy petition. ([Inforlex][2])

## Court and procedure

Likely venue: the bankruptcy court competent for Mateusz / the business, probably Kraków if the JDG, business address, COMI or assets remain in Poland. His physical presence in the Netherlands may complicate service and COMI arguments, but Polish jurisdiction remains plausible where the debtor conducts business, has assets, residence or centre of main interests in Poland. ([Isap][1])

Procedure:

1. File a written application to the bankruptcy court.
2. Identify Mateusz precisely: name, JDG name, NIP 8661681248, address, CEIDG/KRZ references.
3. State that you are a creditor and attach proof.
4. Describe insolvency indicators and failure to file.
5. Attach KRZ printout showing no bankruptcy/restructuring proceeding.
6. Attach the email where he claimed “bankruptcy protection.”
7. Ask for a 1–10 year ban; in practice, ask for a proportionate but serious ban, for example 3–5 years, leaving the court discretion.

## Evidence package

Minimum evidence:

* order/proforma/contract;
* proof of PLN 155,000 transfer;
* proof of due date 31.03.2026;
* refund promise and broken refund deadline;
* demand for payment;
* KRZ search result as of 28.05.2026;
* email where he claimed he had filed for bankruptcy protection;
* evidence of continued business activity or continued solicitation of orders;
* evidence that Waldek was operating or managing the business, if relevant.

Better evidence:

* other unpaid creditors or customers;
* screenshots/reviews/complaints;
* ZUS/tax arrears if known;
* failed enforcement;
* admissions that he could not pay because funds were gone;
* evidence that he kept taking prepayments after he knew he could not fulfil or refund.

## Prospects

**Before early July 2026:** weak-to-moderate if based only on your claim, because the 3-month presumption has not matured yet.

**After early July 2026:** stronger, because the art. 11 presumption can be invoked.

**After end July / early August 2026:** stronger still, because the 30-day art. 21 filing window would plausibly have expired, assuming no earlier bankruptcy/restructuring petition appears in KRZ.

**Immediately strong only if** you can prove older insolvency: multiple creditors, older unpaid debts, failed enforcement, tax/ZUS arrears, or admissions showing inability to pay before your due date.

## Pressure value

High. This is the cleanest “non-criminal pressure” lever because it is cheap, targeted, reputationally damaging and independent of your civil/criminal tracks.

The best use is:

1. send a final demand with a **draft art. 373 petition** attached;
2. state that the petition will be filed after the statutory insolvency window matures unless payment/security is provided;
3. file earlier only if you gather evidence of older insolvency or other creditors.

Backfire: once filed, payment by Mateusz does **not** automatically stop the ban case. That can be good pressure, but it also means escalation becomes harder to unwind. ([Isap][1])

# 3. Creditor bankruptcy petition against the JDG

## Can you file?

Yes. A personal creditor may file a debtor’s bankruptcy petition. A creditor filing must make the claim plausible, and the court examines insolvency and other statutory conditions. ([Isap][1])

You cannot literally “force” bankruptcy. You can file the petition; the court decides.

## Threshold and risks

You need more than “he owes me money.” You need to show insolvency, not merely non-performance of one contract.

Important risks:

* If the debtor shows the claim is wholly disputed and the dispute arose before the filing, the court may dismiss the petition.
* The court dismisses a petition if the debtor’s assets are insufficient to cover bankruptcy costs or only cover those costs.
* In practice, bankruptcy is a collective creditor remedy, so a one-creditor case is vulnerable unless you can show broader insolvency. ([Isap][1])

## Cost

Expect a significant cash outlay:

| Item                                  |                              Approximate amount |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------: |
| Court fee for bankruptcy petition     |                                   **PLN 1,000** |
| Mandatory art. 22a PU advance in 2026 |                                **PLN 8,851.42** |
| Initial outlay                        | **PLN 9,851.42** plus lawyer/copy/service costs |

The art. 22a advance equals the average monthly enterprise-sector wage without profit awards for Q3 of the previous year; for Q3 2025, GUS announced **PLN 8,851.42**, so that is the relevant 2026 figure. The court may also demand further advances for expenses. ([Przepisy Gofin][3])

There are cost-shifting rules if the debtor pays and you withdraw, or if the petition is dismissed because there are no assets, but practical recovery from an insolvent debtor may be poor. Bad-faith filing can expose the creditor to costs, publication of a statement, and damages. ([Isap][1])

## When it makes sense

A creditor bankruptcy petition can make sense if:

* you have evidence of **multiple creditors**;
* you believe there are assets: stock, receivables, bank balances, vehicles, equipment, supplier claims;
* there are suspicious transfers or asset movements that a trustee could attack;
* you want to stop dissipation of assets;
* you can afford the initial outlay;
* you accept that recovery may be slow and partial.

It is a credible pressure tool if he still cares about operating in Poland, banking, suppliers, customer trust or future business.

## When it is a money-sink

It is likely a money-sink if:

* you are the only known creditor;
* he has no assets in Poland;
* he is already physically in the Netherlands;
* the debt is disputed;
* your real goal is fast payment;
* you cannot absorb the PLN 9,851.42+ initial outlay;
* there is a real risk the court dismisses for lack of assets.

## Main backfire

If bankruptcy is declared, your EPU/civil/enforcement path is disrupted. Proceedings concerning the bankruptcy estate proceed through or against the trustee, pre-bankruptcy claim proceedings are constrained, enforcement against estate assets is suspended and then discontinued, and new enforcement/security against estate property is generally inadmissible. ([Isap][1])

Also, a correction to the “Category III” shorthand: under the current art. 342 PU structure, ordinary unsecured claims are generally **Category II**, while Category III includes items such as interest on higher-category claims, fines and donations. That said, the practical problem remains: costs of the bankruptcy estate and other priority items are paid before ordinary creditor distributions, and if the estate is empty, the category label may not help much. ([Isap][1])

Cross-reference to discharge: an ordinary refund claim may be vulnerable in personal bankruptcy discharge dynamics. The safest non-discharge path is usually a final criminal judgment/restitution or damage-from-crime finding, because Bankruptcy Law excludes certain criminally grounded obligations from discharge. ([Isap][1])

# 4. KAS / VAT lever

## VAT issue

If Mateusz was a VAT taxpayer and received PLN 155,000 as an advance for a domestic supply of a workstation, VAT generally arises upon receipt of the advance, to the extent of the amount received. A proforma is not the same as a VAT invoice. For taxable sales/advances, invoice obligations can arise, including advance invoices generally by the 15th day of the month following receipt of payment. ([Isap][4])

Important distinction: failure to issue a VAT invoice is not the same as failure to account for VAT. He may have accounted for VAT in JPK/VAT without sending you a proper invoice. But if he took the money and did neither, the tax lever becomes serious.

Potential KKS angles:

* **art. 62 KKS** — failure to issue an invoice, defective invoice, refusal to issue;
* **art. 56 KKS** — false declaration or concealment exposing tax to depletion;
* **art. 76 KKS** — more relevant if there was undue refund/input VAT abuse, so mention it cautiously unless you have evidence of a refund scheme. ([SIP Lex][5])

## Your standing

You generally do **not** have pokrzywdzony status in a KKS tax offence matter. The protected interest is the State Treasury/public revenue, not your private claim. You are a notifier and potential witness; you should not expect case control, party rights or detailed audit updates because of tax secrecy. ([| Co do zasady][6])

## Leverage value

High relative to cost. A factual KAS notice can trigger checking activities, audit questions, JPK/VAT review, bank-account review, invoice review and turnover analysis. Even if you get no feedback, the pressure on him can be real.

This is near-zero cost to you and does not suspend your EPU claim.

## How to file in 2026

For tax/KAS filings, do **not** rely on ordinary ePUAP to tax authorities in 2026. From 1 January 2026, ePUAP filings by individuals and non-public entities to tax/KAS bodies are generally not effective, except special cases; electronic filings should go through e-Doręczenia or dedicated systems such as e-Urząd Skarbowy / PUESC. ([Gov.pl][7])

Practical channels:

1. **e-Urząd Skarbowy** — use **Pismo ogólne** to the competent Urząd Skarbowy / KAS body. e-US allows general letters to tax offices, customs-tax offices, tax administration chambers, the Ministry of Finance, Head of KAS and KIS. ([Podatki][8])
2. **Paper letter** — send by registered mail to the competent Naczelnik Urzędu Skarbowego for the business/person, asking for forwarding if jurisdiction differs.
3. **KAS intervention channel** — phone **800 060 000** or email **[powiadomKAS@mf.gov.pl](mailto:powiadomKAS@mf.gov.pl)** for tax/customs irregularity signals; the channel can receive anonymous reports, but for your evidentiary package a signed written notice is stronger. ([Podatki][9])

Suggested Polish title:

> **Zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu nieprawidłowości podatkowych / czynu z art. 56 i 62 KKS – GPUcomputer Mateusz Szklarski, NIP 8661681248, transakcja 155 000 zł**

Suggested neutral wording:

> Wnoszę o weryfikację, czy w związku z otrzymaniem przedpłaty 155 000 zł na poczet dostawy stacji roboczej podatnik prawidłowo rozpoznał obowiązek podatkowy w VAT, wykazał sprzedaż / zaliczkę w ewidencji i JPK_V7 oraz wystawił wymaganą fakturę zaliczkową lub fakturę na żądanie nabywcy. Nie mam wiedzy, czy podatek został rozliczony; załączam dokumenty transakcji i korespondencję.

Attach:

* proforma/order;
* bank transfer confirmation;
* proof of payee account;
* refund promise;
* your request for invoice/faktura, if any;
* lack of delivery/refund;
* KRZ search as background;
* communications about “bankruptcy protection”;
* any VAT white-list result you have;
* evidence of Waldek’s operational role.

Do not write “he committed VAT fraud” unless you can prove it. Write “I request verification.”

## Waldek not VAT-registered

Waldek not being VAT-registered is **not automatically illegal**. Some small sellers may use a VAT exemption; in 2026, the general Polish small-taxpayer exemption threshold is PLN 240,000, and exempt taxpayers may be outside active VAT registration. ([Isap][4])

But it is worth flagging if he is actually operating hardware sales. VAT exemption has exclusions, including certain distance sales through organised distance-sale systems for categories such as computers/electronics/optical products and electrical equipment. If Waldek is taking workstation/computer-hardware orders remotely, receiving payments, using Mateusz’s brand/NIP, splitting turnover, or acting as a shadow operator, that is a legitimate factual issue for KAS to verify. ([Isap][4])

# 5. UOKiK / consumer angle

This is supplementary only.

If you bought for personal use, you may have a consumer-law framing. But UOKiK is not a private debt collector and generally focuses on practices affecting collective consumer interests, not one individual transaction. UOKiK itself distinguishes individual matters from collective consumer-interest cases, though anyone can notify suspected collective infringements. ([Urząd Ochrony Konkurencji i Konsumentów][10])

Worth filing only if you can show a pattern:

* online sales to consumers;
* repeated prepayment non-delivery;
* misleading availability claims;
* misleading refund promises;
* misleading “bankruptcy protection” statements to more than one customer;
* standard terms that harm consumers.

For a single transaction that looks B2B because of proforma/business context, UOKiK is likely low-yield.

If you are genuinely a consumer, the more practical consumer route is a **miejski/powiatowy rzecznik konsumentów** or Inspekcja Handlowa/ADR. Consumer ombudsmen can provide free legal help, draft letters and in some cases sue or join proceedings. ([Urząd Ochrony Konkurencji i Konsumentów][11])
