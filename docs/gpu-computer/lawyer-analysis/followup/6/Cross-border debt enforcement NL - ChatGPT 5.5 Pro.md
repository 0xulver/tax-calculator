## Bottom line

Yes, Dutch collection is legally possible and, on your facts, **worth starting**, because you have a known Dutch employer. But the economics are uneven: **wage garnishment is reliable but slow**, while a **bank freeze/account attachment is fast but only valuable if he has a balance when it hits**. The biggest legal risk is **service**: if the Polish EPU order becomes final only through Polish fiction-of-service at an old/Polish address while he actually lives in the Netherlands, that can undermine both an EEO certificate and Dutch enforcement resistance under Brussels Ia.

At the ECB reference rate for **3 June 2026, 1 EUR = 4.2383 PLN**, the **155,000 PLN principal is about €36,571**, before interest and enforcement costs. ([European Central Bank][1])

## Does an EPU nakaz final by fiction-of-service qualify as “uncontested” for EEO?

**Not safely.** The claim can be “uncontested” if he did not object, but **the EEO certificate depends on EU minimum service standards**, not merely on Polish procedural finality. Reg. 805/2004 allows no-objection claims to be uncontested, but certification in such cases requires compliant service and proper information on the claim and how to contest it. Service without proof of receipt is expressly not admissible if the debtor’s address was not known with certainty. ([EUR-Lex][2])

For your facts, that means:

**If the EPU order is served at his actual Dutch address, by a method compliant with EU service rules, and he does not oppose**, EEO is plausible.

**If it becomes final because papers were deemed served at a Polish CEIDG/residential address while he has actually lived and worked in the Netherlands since 2022**, do not rely on EEO. The debtor may attack the EEO certificate in Poland as wrongly granted, and the Dutch side may limit/stay enforcement if the certificate or judgment is challenged. Reg. 805/2004 allows withdrawal where an EEO certificate was clearly wrongly granted, and Dutch enforcement is governed by Dutch enforcement law after the EU title is accepted. ([EUR-Lex][2])

Brussels Ia is more forgiving procedurally because it does not require “uncontested” certification, but it still has the default-judgment service objection: Dutch recognition/enforcement can be refused if he was not served in sufficient time and in such a way as to arrange a defence, unless he could have challenged it and failed to do so. ([EUR-Lex][3])

## Recommended route for your EPU nakaz

### 1. Make the Polish title service-proof first

Do not build the Dutch plan on fiction-of-service if you know he resides in the Netherlands. For cross-border service of judicial documents in civil/commercial matters, the EU Service Regulation applies, and direct postal service in another Member State by registered letter with acknowledgement or equivalent is one allowed route; the defendant also has language-refusal rights if the document is not in a language he understands or the official language of the place of service. ([EUR-Lex][6])

Practical move: in the EPU file, preserve evidence that the court had or was given the correct Dutch address/employment information. If the EPU court cannot handle the service cleanly, expect either transfer to ordinary proceedings or the need to re-route via a more service-robust procedure.

### 2. Once final/enforceable, use **Brussels Ia Article 53** as the main enforcement package

Ask the Polish court of origin for:

1. an authenticated copy of the nakaz zapłaty / judgment;
2. confirmation that it is enforceable/final under Polish law;
3. the **Article 53 Brussels Ia certificate**;
4. a clear interest calculation from 31.03.2026;
5. certified Dutch translations where needed.

Under Brussels Ia, the Dutch enforcement authority needs the judgment copy and Article 53 certificate, and the certificate should contain enforceability, recoverable costs and interest information where relevant. ([EUR-Lex][3])

The Article 53 certificate must be served on the debtor before the first enforcement measure, with the judgment if it has not already been served; if he requests a translation, only protective measures can proceed until the translation is provided. For speed, provide Dutch translations upfront. ([EUR-Lex][3])

### 3. Request EEO only as an optional accelerator

Request the EEO certificate only if you can honestly show EU-compliant service and no opposition. If service is questionable, skip EEO and use Brussels Ia. A bad EEO certificate can become a detour because the debtor can apply in Poland to have it withdrawn or can seek limitation/stay in the enforcement state while challenges are pending. ([EUR-Lex][2])

### 4. Use EAPO after the title to locate/freeze his Dutch bank account

After you have the Polish judgment, the Polish court that issued the judgment has jurisdiction to issue an EAPO for the claim in that judgment. EAPO is available after a judgment, and where a judgment already exists, jurisdiction lies with the court of the Member State where the judgment was issued. ([EUR-Lex][5])

Your EAPO application should say:

* he is resident/employed in the Netherlands;
* he has Dutch wages and likely a Dutch salary account;
* Polish assets appear minimal;
* he promised refund but did not pay;
* there is a real risk ordinary enforcement will be impeded if warned first;
* you request Dutch bank-account information under Article 14 because you have reason to believe he holds Dutch accounts but do not know the bank/IBAN.

Article 14 allows the court, where the creditor has an enforceable judgment and believes the debtor has accounts in a specific Member State, to request the enforcement-state information authority to identify the bank/accounts. In the Netherlands, the designated mechanism uses the bailiff: the bailiff can request account information from Dutch banks, and banks must reply promptly. ([EUR-Lex][5])

EAPO is ex parte: the debtor is not notified or heard before the order is issued. If you already have a judgment, security is discretionary; before judgment, security is normally required unless the court dispenses with it. ([EUR-Lex][5])

## Enforcement on the ground in the Netherlands

Dutch enforcement is done through a **gerechtsdeurwaarder**. Bailiffs are authorised to enforce judgments, act on creditor instructions, serve the enforceable document, demand payment, receive payment, seize assets, and request police assistance where necessary. Dutch enforcement normally requires an enforceable title and prior service on the debtor. ([e-justice.europa.eu][7])

You can instruct a Dutch bailiff remotely. Under Brussels Ia, a party seeking enforcement of a foreign EU judgment is not required to have a postal address in the Member State addressed and is not required to have a representative unless such representation is mandatory regardless of nationality/domicile. ([EUR-Lex][3])

### Wage garnishment: loonbeslag at Sims Lifecycle Services

This is your most reliable route because the employer is known. The bailiff serves a third-party attachment on the employer, and the employer withholds the attachable part of wages. Dutch law protects a **beslagvrije voet**, the protected minimum the debtor must keep for living expenses. Dutch government guidance states that, when income is attached, the debtor has a right to a minimum amount, and at least **5% of net income** goes to creditors. ([Rijksoverheid][8])

The protected amount depends on household, income and housing situation. For 2026, Juridisch Loket lists maximum beslagvrije voet amounts of **€2,191.42** for a single person without children, **€2,526.69** for a single parent, **€2,881.41** for a couple without minor children, and **€3,155.31** for a couple with minor children. ([juridischloket.nl][9])

The Netherlands minimum wage for age 21+ from 1 January 2026 is **€14.71 gross/hour**, and since 2024 there is no fixed monthly minimum wage; monthly pay depends on working hours. At 40 hours/week, that is roughly **€2,550 gross/month** before overtime/shift allowances. ([Government of the Netherlands][10])

### Realistic monthly recovery

Because the protected minimum is high, a production-line worker’s attachable wage may be modest unless he has overtime, shift allowance, no dependants, and no competing attachments.

Approximate stress test, using principal only (~€36,571) and ignoring interest/costs:

| Approx. net monthly income | If single/no children, rough attachable amount | Time to recover principal only |
| -------------------------: | ---------------------------------------------: | -----------------------------: |
|                     €2,200 |        ~€110/month, because minimum 5% applies |                      ~28 years |
|                     €2,400 |                                    ~€209/month |                      ~15 years |
|                     €2,600 |                                    ~€409/month |                     ~7.5 years |
|                     €2,800 |                                    ~€609/month |                       ~5 years |

If he has a partner/children, the protected amount may be higher, but the 5% minimum still matters. If he has other creditors, a bailiff must check existing attachments; Dutch bailiffs register income/goods seizures in the central Digital Register of Attachments and must verify whether assets are already seized. ([business.gov.nl][11])

### Bank attachment: bankbeslag

A Dutch bank attachment is worth doing if you know the bank, or after an EAPO/account-information request identifies it. It catches the balance at the time of seizure; later salary payments are generally not included in that same seizure. The Dutch bailiff leaflet explains that assets in the account are seized and the bank usually freezes the account, but payments made into the account after the seizure are not included. ([KBvG][12])

This means the best bank strategy is timing: attach shortly after salary payment, or use EAPO to freeze existing balances before he is warned. But if he lives paycheck-to-paycheck, bankbeslag may produce little.

### Other assets

Dutch executory attachment can reach movable property, shares/securities, third-party debts, real estate, ships/aircraft, and bank/wage claims, subject to exemptions for basic necessities and protected wage/benefit portions. In practice, for a production worker with no known real estate, the best assets are salary, bank balance, vehicle, tax refunds, and any savings/investment account. ([e-justice.europa.eu][7])

## Costs and timing

### Official Dutch bailiff costs

Dutch bailiff official acts have statutory tariffs, but your commercial arrangement/retainer with the bailiff is negotiated; costs are generally for the debtor’s account, but you often front them and only recover if collection succeeds. ([KBvG][13])

For 2026, the KBvG tariff table lists, among others:

| Act                                                                    | 2026 tariff excl. VAT | 2026 tariff incl. VAT |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------: | --------------------: |
| Service of a title                                                     |               €136.71 |               €165.42 |
| Bank/ordinary third-party attachment, incl. EAPO execution-type act    |               €260.68 |               €315.42 |
| Third-party attachment on periodic payments, i.e. wage-type attachment |               €184.91 |               €223.74 |
| Information request, including EAPO-related request                    |               €105.13 |               €127.21 |



### EAPO costs

EAPO does not require mandatory lawyer representation at EU level, and court fees may not be higher than equivalent national protective-order fees. Bank/authority costs are regulated and cannot exceed equivalent national-order/account-information costs. In the Netherlands, banks are not allowed to charge fees for implementing an equivalent national order, and Dutch EAPO enforcement/service/account-information functions are handled by the bailiff. ([EUR-Lex][5])

### Practical budget

For a Brussels Ia enforcement package plus first Dutch enforcement steps, budget roughly:

* **€300–€900** for sworn/certified translations, depending on pages and urgency;
* **€500–€1,200** for first Dutch bailiff acts and disbursements if doing service + wage attachment + information checks;
* **€300–€700 extra** if adding bank attachment/EAPO execution steps;
* **€1,000–€2,500 total initial budget** for an uncontested, document-ready start;
* **€3,000–€8,000+** if the debtor contests service/enforcement and Dutch counsel becomes necessary.

Those are working estimates; the statutory act fees are recoverable in principle from the debtor, but unrecovered enforcement costs remain an economic risk to you.

### Timing

A realistic uncontested sequence is:

1. Polish title becomes final/enforceable;
2. Article 53 certificate: days to weeks, court-dependent;
3. translations: several days;
4. Dutch bailiff service/demand: about 1–2 weeks after receiving documents;
5. wage garnishment: often effective from the next payroll cycle after employer service;
6. EAPO after judgment: the Regulation sets a 5-working-day decision target after a complete application when a judgment already exists, though account-information handling can add time. ([EUR-Lex][5])

If the debtor contests service or the Polish order is vulnerable, add months.

## Economic verdict for 155,000 PLN

**Yes, it is economically rational to pursue Dutch enforcement, but not because wage garnishment will quickly pay €36k.** It is rational because:

1. you know his employer;
2. the Dutch system has functioning wage attachment;
3. a bank freeze/account search may reveal attachable funds;
4. enforcement pressure may force a settlement;
5. the claim is large enough to justify a €1k–€2.5k initial enforcement spend.

But the honest recovery picture is:

* **Wage-only recovery may take 5–15+ years**, and longer if he has dependants, low net income, other creditors, or job changes.
* **Bank attachment/EAPO is the only route likely to produce meaningful fast money**, but only if he has savings or a salary balance when hit.
* **The strongest leverage package** is: Article 53 certificate → Dutch bailiff service/demand → wage attachment at Sims → EAPO/account-information request → bank attachment → settlement proposal backed by continuing loonbeslag.

For settlement leverage, a credible offer could be: immediate lump sum plus wage-backed instalments, with suspension of further attachments only after first payment clears. Do not release the title or withdraw enforcement without secured payments.

## Criminal-track interaction

A Polish criminal asset freeze under **art. 291 KPK** is a different channel from your civil enforcement. To reach Dutch assets, the Polish prosecutor/court would use **Regulation (EU) 2018/1805** on mutual recognition of freezing/confiscation orders. For a freezing order concerning money, if the issuing authority has reasonable grounds to believe the person has property or income in another Member State, it transmits a freezing certificate to that Member State; the executing authority recognises and executes it like a domestic freezing order unless a refusal/postponement ground applies. ([EUR-Lex][14])

That criminal freeze can be powerful, but you do not control it like a creditor instructing a bailiff. You can request it as the injured party and give the prosecutor the Dutch employer/residence/bank-risk facts.

For a final criminal restitution/compensation order under **art. 46 KK**, the route depends on how the order is framed. Regulation 2018/1805 can deal with victim restitution/compensation out of frozen/confiscated property where the issuing authority includes that victim decision in the freezing/confiscation certificate. ([EUR-Lex][14])

Separately, Framework Decision 2005/214/JHA covers certain criminal “financial penalties,” including compensation imposed in the same criminal decision for victims where the victim may not be a civil party and the court acts in criminal jurisdiction, but it excludes civil-nature damages/restitution orders enforceable through civil recognition/enforcement. ([EUR-Lex][15])

So: **criminal freezing is potentially useful against Dutch assets, but it is not a substitute for civil Dutch enforcement.** Run the civil route yourself once you have the Polish title, and push the prosecutor separately for a Regulation 2018/1805 freezing certificate if the fraud case is active.
