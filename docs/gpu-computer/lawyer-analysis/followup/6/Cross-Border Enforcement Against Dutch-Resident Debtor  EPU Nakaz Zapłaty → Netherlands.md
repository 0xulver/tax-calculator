## 1. Turning the Polish EPU Nakaz Zapłaty into an Enforceable Instrument in NL

### Route A: European Enforcement Order (Regulation 805/2004)

The EEO was designed precisely for this situation: a creditor with an uncontested judgment in one Member State wishing to enforce directly in another without exequatur. A judgment is certified as a EEO when the debtor "has never objected to it in the course of the court proceedings" — which is precisely what an EPU nakaz zapłaty that became final by non-opposition achieves.[^2][^4][^5]

**The critical service problem with EPU and EEO.** The EPU system at Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód serves the payment order electronically to the creditor, but serves the debtor by postal delivery to the Polish address on record. When the debtor is physically resident in the Netherlands, service is often effected via *fiction of service* (Art. 139 k.p.c.) — the order is left at the Polish address, and if unclaimed, is deemed served after a 14-day waiting period. This is the core EEO vulnerability: Article 14 of Regulation 805/2004 permits service by fiction (such as deemed delivery by post to a last-known address) only if the debtor's address is "known with certainty." If the debtor's actual address is in the Netherlands and service was made to a Polish address that is no longer his habitual residence, the EEO certificate can be challenged or withdrawn. Polish courts issuing EEO certificates for EPU nakazes where the debtor was served by fiction-of-service are consequently at risk — the issuing court in Lublin must certify compliance with the service standards, and if the Polish address was not the debtor's actual address at the time, the certificate could fail.[^5][^10]

**Practical consequence:** If the EPU nakaz was served by Art. 14 deemed service to an outdated Polish address while the debtor was already in the Netherlands, applying for the EEO certificate is risky. The Lublin court will check the service method when issuing the EEO certificate, and if service was not compliant with Arts 13–15 of Regulation 805/2004, certification will be refused or the certificate subsequently withdrawn upon debtor challenge.[^10]

### Route B: Brussels Ia Regulation (1215/2012) — Recommended Primary Route

Under Brussels Ia, any final Polish civil/commercial judgment is automatically recognised and enforceable throughout the EU without any declaration of enforceability. There is no exequatur. The Polish court (Lublin-Zachód) issues an **Article 53 certificate** (standard form, Annex I to the Regulation), which the creditor presents to a Dutch bailiff together with a copy of the judgment.[^7][^3][^11]

**Why Brussels Ia is better for an EPU nakaz than EEO:**

- Brussels Ia does *not* impose minimum service standards on the proceedings in the Member State of origin. It does not second-guess how the Polish court served the debtor.[^11]
- The grounds for refusal in NL are limited (Art. 45): manifest public policy violation, due process (which requires specific challenge by debtor), irreconcilable judgments. A default EPU nakaz where the debtor simply did not oppose will generally not trigger these.[^11]
- The debtor can challenge recognition in NL under Art. 47, but the Dutch court cannot review the merits of the Polish judgment.[^11]
- The procedure is bilateral: get the Art. 53 certificate from Lublin-Zachód, provide a sworn Dutch translation of the certificate (the judgment itself need not always be translated if the certificate is self-contained, but best practice is to translate both).[^12]

**One practical risk for Brussels Ia too:** If the debtor later argues before a Dutch court that the EPU nakaz was served in a manner that denied him a real opportunity to contest it (Art. 45(1)(b) Brussels Ia — due process), and demonstrates he had no knowledge of the proceedings because he was abroad, the Dutch court *could* refuse enforcement. This is the same underlying risk as with EEO, but the Brussels Ia refusal standard is higher and requires the debtor to proactively challenge. In practice, enforcement begins immediately; the debtor must apply to stop it.

**Obtaining the Art. 53 certificate in Poland:** File an application at the Sąd Rejonowy Lublin-Zachód (the same court) once the nakaz is final (i.e., after the opposition period expires without challenge). The application uses the standard EU form; court fees are minimal (approximately 100–200 PLN). Timeline: 2–6 weeks typically.[^8]

### Route C: European Order for Payment (Regulation 1896/2006)

The EOP is a separate EU-wide payment order procedure filed directly in EU cross-border cases using standard EU forms. It would require re-filing the entire claim from scratch using Form A, and the debtor would be served via the EU Service Regulation. This route would have been cleaner from the outset for NL enforcement (because service is performed in compliance with the EOP Regulation, eliminating the service-standard issue), but since the EPU nakaz is already underway, switching to EOP now means losing the work done and re-incurring filing fees. It is **not advisable to abandon** the EPU route. Note: An EOP can theoretically be filed in parallel for a fresh proceeding, but that creates complexity and cost without significant benefit given Brussels Ia is available.[^13]

## 2. European Account Preservation Order (EAPO — Regulation 655/2014)

### Can a Polish Court Issue an EAPO Targeting a Dutch Bank Account?

Yes — this is the core purpose of Regulation 655/2014. The regulation applies to cross-border cases, and the case is cross-border because the bank accounts to be frozen are in the Netherlands while the creditor is domiciled in Poland. An EAPO issued by a Polish court is automatically recognised in the Netherlands without any special procedure or declaration of enforceability.[^14][^15][^16]

### Before or After Having a Title?

The EAPO can be obtained **at any stage**:[^14]
- **Before** EPU proceedings are filed (pre-action)
- **During** the EPU proceedings (parallel to the nakaz application)
- **After** the nakaz is obtained (post-judgment)

If applying before or during proceedings, the creditor must demonstrate both (a) urgency/risk of dissipation and (b) likelihood of success on the merits. Once a final nakaz (or Art. 53 certificate) exists, only the urgency criterion needs to be met — a stronger procedural position.[^17][^16][^14]

### Evidence Threshold

The Polish court must be satisfied that there is a **real risk** that subsequent enforcement will be impeded or substantially more difficult without the preservation measure. With a debtor who has moved abroad, left minimal Polish business assets, and whose Polish JDG shows signs of wind-down, this risk should be demonstrable. Evidence to adduce: the proforma invoice, proof of payment, the written refund promise, the debtor's relocation to NL, absence of known Polish assets, any evidence of asset transfers or account closures.[^14]

### Finding the Dutch Bank Account: Article 14 Information Request

If the account details (bank name or IBAN) are unknown, the creditor can ask the Polish court to request the Dutch **information authority** to locate the account. In the Netherlands, individual *gerechtsdeurwaarders* (judicial officers) have been designated as competent to receive, transmit, and serve EAPO documents, and the KBvG (Royal Professional Organisation of Bailiffs) coordinates this. The Article 14 mechanism requires:[^18][^19][^16]
- The creditor must already have an enforceable title (a final nakaz) OR have obtained an EAPO on the merits[^17]
- The creditor must have **reasonable grounds to believe** the debtor holds an account in the Netherlands — knowing he is employed at Sims Lifecycle Services in Son en Breugel is a very strong basis[^18]
- The information authority queries banks and reports the account to the court (not directly to the creditor, for data protection reasons)[^18]
- The debtor is not notified of this bank-information request for 30 days to prevent dissipation[^17]

**Before you have a final title:** The Art. 14 information mechanism is only available **once you have an enforceable title**. During EPU proceedings, you can apply for the EAPO itself (with the higher dual-threshold — merits likelihood + urgency), but the account search requires the title. Plan accordingly: obtain the final nakaz first, then move immediately to EAPO + Art. 14 request simultaneously.[^17]

### Ex Parte Nature and Security Deposit

The EAPO is obtained **ex parte** — the debtor is not heard before the order is issued. This is the "surprise effect" that prevents fund movement before the freeze. However:[^15]

- The Polish court **may** require the creditor to post a security deposit (cautio) if the debtor has not yet been heard and there is a risk of wrongful attachment causing damage. The amount is at the court's discretion.[^14]
- In many cases for documented monetary claims, courts do not require security if the claim is well-evidenced.
- If the EAPO is wrongfully obtained, the creditor can be liable for damages.

### EAPO Costs (Polish Side)

EAPO application filing fees in Poland are governed by Art. 33a of the Ustawa o kosztach sądowych (court fees act). Court fee is typically a flat PLN 300–500 for the preservation application. Legal representation is advisable but not mandatory.[^20]

### Is EAPO the Cross-Border Equivalent of the Domestic Freeze?

Yes — the EAPO is precisely the cross-border alternative to the Polish *zabezpieczenie* order that your domestic lawyer says is unavailable within the EPU track. The domestic limitation arises because Polish EPU proceedings are simplified and do not support interim asset-preservation orders. The EAPO regulation gives you a dedicated EU-law route to do exactly what the Polish procedure denies: freeze assets before/during litigation on a documented civil claim. This is strongly recommended as a parallel action.[^20][^14]

## 3. Enforcement on the Ground in the Netherlands

### Role of the Dutch Gerechtsdeurwaarder

All physical enforcement in the Netherlands is carried out exclusively by a *gerechtsdeurwaarder* (sworn judicial officer, equivalent to a French huissier de justice). No bailiff, debt collector, or lawyer can levy attachment themselves — only the gerechtsdeurwaarder. All Dutch gerechtsdeurwaarders are members of the KBvG (Koninklijke Beroepsorganisatie van Gerechtsdeurwaarders).[^21][^22][^11]

**Engaging one remotely:** Fully possible. The gerechtsdeurwaarder operates throughout the Netherlands regardless of registration district. You instruct one by email or post, providing: (1) the authenticated copy of the Polish judgment, (2) the Art. 53 Brussels Ia certificate (or EEO certificate), (3) a sworn Dutch translation of the certificate/relevant parts, and (4) debtor's known details (name, employer, address). The bailiff serves the judgment on the debtor before commencing enforcement. Find one via the KBvG register at registergerechtsdeurwaarders.nl.[^23][^19]

### Wage Garnishment (Loonbeslag) Against Sims Lifecycle Services

The loonbeslag (wage attachment) is likely your most reliable enforcement tool given the debtor has stable employment at Sims Lifecycle Services, a known employer with a fixed Dutch address. The gerechtsdeurwaarder serves the attachment notice (*beslagexploot*) on Sims as the employer. Sims then becomes legally obligated to withhold the attachable portion of wages and remit it to the bailiff. Failure by the employer to respond to the questionnaire within 4 weeks can make the employer liable for the debt.[^21]

**The beslagvrije voet — Protected Earnings Floor:**

The *beslagvrije voet* (protected earnings floor) sets the minimum amount the debtor must retain to cover basic living costs; everything above it can be attached.[^24][^25]

From 1 July 2025, the maximum beslagvrije voet for an **alleenstaande zonder kinderen** (single person without children) is **€2,093.67/month net**. For middle incomes, it is calculated by formula under the Dutch Civil Procedure Code.[^24]

**Production worker wage calculation at Sims Lifecycle Services:**

- Average gross production worker wage in the Netherlands: approximately **€2,184–€2,784/month** gross (€14–€16/hour × 36–40h)[^26]
- Equivalent net wage (after Dutch tax/social contributions): approximately €1,700–€2,100/month net for this income bracket
- Workers earning at or below the social minimum (bijstandsnorm) retain 95% of net income, leaving only 5% attachable capacity[^24]
- Workers with net income *above* the beslagvrije voet threshold have the excess attached

For a single production worker at Sims earning approximately €2,100–€2,400/month gross:
- Net income ≈ €1,650–€1,900/month
- Beslagvrije voet (single, no children) ≈ €1,600–€2,093 depending on income level and individual circumstances[^27][^24]
- **Realistically attachable per month: €50–€300/month** — the low end of the income scale means the protected floor consumes most or all of the income

**Implications for the 155,000 PLN claim (≈ €36,500):**

At €150/month attachable income, recovery of €36,500 would take **approximately 20+ years** via wage garnishment alone. Even at €300/month it would take over 10 years. This is the fundamental economic constraint of NL loonbeslag for this claim at this wage level.

**Important caveat:** Holiday allowance (*vakantiebijslag*, statutorily 8% of gross wages in the Netherlands), year-end bonuses, and overtime payments in excess of the beslagvrije voet are also attachable and not protected by the floor. These could accelerate recovery but are unlikely to change the order of magnitude.[^24]

### Bank Account Attachment (Bankbeslag)

Bank account garnishment (*bankbeslag*) is potentially more effective if there is a meaningful balance. A Dutch account can be frozen/garnished by the gerechtsdeurwaarder once the enforcement title is presented. The EAPO preserves the balance; subsequent execution converts the freeze into an actual transfer to the creditor. The bank is notified and must not release funds up to the amount of the claim.[^16][^11]

The limitation: production-line workers typically do not hold significant bank balances. The EAPO/bankbeslag is most valuable as a **leverage and surprise tool** — it forces the debtor to engage with settlement rather than ignore the claim. The psychological effect of a frozen salary account (affecting rent, groceries, standing orders) is considerable even if the recoverable balance is small.

These enforcement costs are recoverable from the debtor as execution costs under Dutch law, added to the claim.[^21][^11]

| Art. 53 certificate issued by Polish court | 2–6 weeks |
| Sworn translation | 3–5 business days |
| Gerechtsdeurwaarder serves debtor and levies attachment | 1–3 weeks |
| First wage attachment payment | 1 month after loonbeslag |

### Economic Verdict

**Is NL enforcement economically rational?**

| Scenario | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **EAPO + bankbeslag as leverage** | **Highly rational.** Cost: ~€1,000. Effect: freezes salary account, forces the debtor to engage. High probability of settlement. |
| **Loonbeslag as standalone recovery tool** | **Economically marginal.** At likely net wages of a production-line worker, the beslagvrije voet absorbs most income. Recovery timeline for €36,500 could be 10–20+ years. Not a viable standalone strategy. |
| **Full NL enforcement to final recovery** | **Rational only if debtor acquires wealth, inheritance, or changes job** — upward wage mobility or career change changes the math. Keep the title alive (20-year limitation in NL). |
| **EAPO + loonbeslag as permanent pressure** | **Valuable as a combination.** Even partial recovery plus settlement pressure justifies the ~€1,000 in procedural costs on a €36,500 claim. |

**The strategic calculus:** The primary value of NL enforcement is **leverage toward settlement**, not full amortization via wage garnishment. A frozen Dutch bank account and a standing wage attachment make the debtor's daily life in the Netherlands uncomfortable and credit-impaired. The cost of achieving this is low (< 3% of claim value) and the settlement-inducing effect is high. The economic case for initiating EAPO + Brussels Ia enforcement is therefore **yes — do it**, with realistic expectations that recovery will be partial or settlement-based.

## Recommended Route for the EPU Nakaz

**Step 1 — Let the EPU proceed to a final nakaz.** Do not withdraw or switch to EOP. The current EPU proceeding at Lublin-Zachód should be allowed to run to completion. Once the opposition period expires without challenge, the nakaz becomes final.

**Step 2 — Simultaneously apply for an EAPO.** As soon as the nakaz is final (or arguably even before, on the dual-threshold standard), apply to Lublin-Zachód for an EAPO targeting the debtor's Dutch bank account. Also request the Art. 14 account-information mechanism from the Dutch information authority via the Polish court.

**Step 3 — Obtain the Art. 53 Brussels Ia certificate.** File a separate application at Lublin-Zachód for the Art. 53/Annex I certificate. This is the basis for direct enforcement in NL via Brussels Ia — cleanest and most defensible route for an EPU nakaz (avoids the EEO service-standard issue).

**Step 4 — Commission a sworn Dutch translation** of the Polish nakaz (or the operative part) and the Art. 53 certificate. Polish is not English/French/German, so sworn Dutch translation is required in practice.[^12]

**Step 5 — Instruct a Dutch gerechtsdeurwaarder.** Provide the Art. 53 certificate, the authenticated nakaz, and the sworn translation. Instruct both: (a) serve the EAPO on Sims Lifecycle Services' bank (once account identified) and (b) levy *loonbeslag* on the debtor's wages at Sims.

**Do NOT use the EEO route** unless it can be confirmed that the EPU service was made in full compliance with Arts 13–15 of Regulation 805/2004 (e.g., service was effected at the debtor's actual Dutch address via the EU Service Regulation or by personal delivery). For a domestic EPU where service was by Polish deemed delivery to a Polish address while the debtor was in the Netherlands, Brussels Ia is safer.

## 5. Criminal Track: Cross-Border Asset Freeze and Art. 46 KK Restitution

### Regulation (EU) 2018/1805 — Mutual Recognition of Freezing and Confiscation Orders

If Polish criminal proceedings are underway (e.g., for fraud under Art. 286 KK or misappropriation under Art. 284 KK), prosecutors can seek a **criminal asset freezing order** under Arts 291–295 of the Polish Code of Criminal Procedure. Since 19 December 2020, Regulation (EU) 2018/1805 provides a direct mechanism for mutual recognition of Polish criminal freezing orders in the Netherlands.[^31][^32][^33]

**Key features of Regulation 2018/1805 in this context:**

- The Polish freezing order is issued using the standard EU certificate form (Annex I to the Regulation)[^32]
- The certificate is transmitted by the Polish issuing authority (typically the prosecutor or court) **directly** to the competent Dutch executing authority (usually the Public Prosecution Service / Openbaar Ministerie)[^33]
- The Netherlands must execute the order within **48 hours** of the decision to recognise and execute it[^33]
- Grounds for non-recognition are narrow: fundamental rights violations, *ne bis in idem*, the offence is not a crime in NL (though most fraud offences qualify), or public order[^33]
- **Assets covered:** The regulation applies to assets held by the suspect in any form — including Dutch bank accounts and wages[^32][^33]
- **Art. 46 KK restitution order:** If a Polish court issues a restitution order (obowiązek naprawienia szkody) under Art. 46 KK as part of criminal sentencing, this is a criminal matter and would also fall within Regulation 2018/1805 for cross-border enforcement[^33]

**Practical reality:** Criminal asset freezing via Regulation 2018/1805 is fast and powerful, but it requires active criminal proceedings — the prosecutor must open an investigation and seek the freezing order. Given the documented nature of the claim (proforma, payment proof, written refund promise), a criminal complaint under Art. 286 KK (oszustwo) is viable. The combination of: (1) EAPO on the civil side and (2) criminal freezing order under 2018/1805 on the criminal side creates dual-track pressure and dual legal authority to freeze Dutch assets.

**Denmark/Ireland exception note:** Regulation 2018/1805 does NOT apply to Denmark or Ireland, but the Netherlands is fully within scope.[^33]

## Practical Action Checklist

1. **Now (parallel to EPU):** File criminal complaint with Polish police/prosecutor (Art. 286 KK) to open criminal track — this enables Art. 291 KPK freezing order → 2018/1805 cross-border effect.
2. **On nakaz becoming final:** Immediately apply at Lublin-Zachód for (a) Art. 53 Brussels Ia certificate and (b) EAPO with Art. 14 bank-information request.
3. **Upon receipt of certificates:** Commission sworn Polish→Dutch translation (3–5 days).
4. **Instruct a Dutch gerechtsdeurwaarder:** Serve EAPO + levy loonbeslag on Sims Lifecycle Services.
5. **Open settlement negotiation** in parallel once debtor's account is frozen — this is when debtors typically engage.
6. **If debtor contests Brussels Ia enforcement** (Art. 47 NL): have Polish counsel provide evidence of service and final status of nakaz.
