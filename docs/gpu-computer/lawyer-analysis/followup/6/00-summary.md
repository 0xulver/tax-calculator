# Research summary — Collecting in the Netherlands

> All four models agree: once the EPU nakaz is final, Dutch enforcement is worth doing, but via Brussels Ia (Art. 53 certificate), not the EEO — the EPU's fiction-of-service makes EEO certification likely to fail or be withdrawn. Realistic wage garnishment yields only ~€100–400/month against a ~€36.5k claim (10–20+ years), so the campaign's real value is an EAPO bank freeze plus loonbeslag as settlement leverage, at a modest €1,000–2,500 upfront cost.

## Key findings (consensus)

- **Instrument choice.** Brussels Ia (Reg. 1215/2012) is the default: no exequatur, the Lublin court issues an Article 53 certificate (~100–200 PLN, 2–6 weeks), and the Dutch bailiff enforces directly. The EEO (Reg. 805/2004) is rejected by all four models for this case: Art. 14 service-without-proof-of-receipt is only valid if the debtor's address is "known with certainty," and an EPU order final by fikcja doręczenia at a Polish address while he lives in NL fails that test (Gemini cites CJEU *Imtech Marine* C-300/14). Brussels Ia carries the same underlying risk but only as a debtor-initiated Art. 45 challenge after enforcement starts. EOP re-filing is the fallback, not the current move.
- **EAPO (Reg. 655/2014).** A Polish court can freeze his Dutch account ex parte; post-judgment, the threshold drops to "real risk," security is usually waived, and the decision target is 5 working days. The Art. 14 account-discovery mechanism (requires an enforceable title) goes through Dutch bailiffs/KBvG as the information authority — his Sims employment is strong grounds. This is the cross-border equivalent of the zabezpieczenie unavailable in EPU, but it freezes only the balance at the moment of attachment, not future wages.
- **Loonbeslag math.** 2026 beslagvrije voet for a single person caps at €2,191.42/month; minimum 5% of net income goes to creditors. A production worker netting ~€1,700–2,400 yields roughly €100–400/month attachable → 8–20+ years for the principal. Gemini notes Polish statutory interest on 155k PLN may outpace ~€2,500/year recovery, so the debt could grow in real terms.
- **Costs.** BTAG 2026 tariffs: ~€165 service of title, ~€224 wage attachment, ~€315 bank attachment, ~€127 information request (incl. VAT). Total uncontested package incl. sworn translations: roughly €800–2,500; €3,000–8,000+ if contested. Costs are recoverable in principle but fronted by the creditor. First attachment ~2–4 months after the nakaz is final.
- **Framing.** All models converge: this is a pressure-and-settlement campaign, not passive liquidation. A frozen salary account plus standing loonbeslag is the leverage; a lump-sum settlement (often family-funded) is the realistic endgame.
- **Criminal track.** Reg. 2018/1805 lets a Polish prosecutor project an art. 291 KPK freeze into NL (48h recognition + 48h execution, victim restitution prioritized), but only the prosecutor controls it — press them with the Dutch employer/residence facts; it complements, not replaces, civil enforcement.

## Where the models disagree or add unique points

- **Gemini PDF** is uniquely pessimistic about the EPU title itself: Art. 505³⁴ §2 KPC obliges the e-court to repeal the order ex officio if it can't be served domestically, and the debtor can later reinstate his sprzeciw deadline, collapsing everything. It also notes the Hoge Raad applies beslagvrije-voet-style proportionality even to criminal freezes, and quotes ~€89 for the Art. 14 search.
- **ChatGPT 5.5 Pro** uniquely recommends fixing service first (EU Service Regulation to his actual Dutch address) before building on the title.
- **The "Cross-Border Enforcement Against Dutch-Resident Debtor" file** is the most action-oriented (checklist, employer's 4-week questionnaire liability, 20-year NL title life) but is loosest on sourcing; its claim that an Art. 46 KK order straightforwardly travels via 2018/1805 is contradicted by ChatGPT Deep Research, which calls that picture "less clean."
- **Fee discrepancies:** Polish EAPO court fee quoted as PLN 100 (ChatGPT DR) vs PLN 300–500 (Perplexity file); Gemini says the Art. 53 certificate often needs no translation, others budget €300–900 for sworn translations.

## Action checklist

1. Verify/repair service in the EPU file (real Dutch address) before relying on the title.
2. Once final: apply at Lublin-Zachód for the Art. 53 certificate and, simultaneously, an EAPO with Art. 14 account-information request.
3. Commission sworn Polish→Dutch translations.
4. Instruct an Eindhoven-area gerechtsdeurwaarder remotely: serve title, levy loonbeslag on Sims, execute bankbeslag once the account is identified.
5. Open settlement talks the moment the account freezes; do not lift attachments until payments clear.
6. Separately push the Polish prosecutor for a 2018/1805 freezing certificate if the criminal case advances.

## Caveats / open questions

- The EPU service history is the single biggest unknown — it drives EEO viability, Brussels Ia challenge risk, and (per Gemini) whether the title survives at all.
- His household composition, net pay, rent, and any prior attachments determine the actual attachable margin; all monthly figures are scenario math, not predictions.
- Polish EAPO fee, translation requirements, and exact BTAG recoverability differ between models — confirm with Polish counsel and the chosen bailiff.
- Whether an art. 46 KK restitution order is enforceable in NL in your own hands remains unresolved; treat civil enforcement as the reliable path.
