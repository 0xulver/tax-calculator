# Research summary — Realistic outcomes and money recovery

> All six models converge: file the complaint because it is free and the expected value is positive, but treat it as a pressure-and-discovery tool, not a collection mechanism. Realistic chance of conviction from filing is roughly 10–20%; chance of meaningful cash from the criminal track alone is ~15–30%, with a better-than-even chance of zero cash. The money, if it comes, comes from settlement under pressure after charges — not from a verdict years later. Everything hinges on two things you don't yet have: the bank trail and other victims.

## Key findings (consensus)

- **Decision tree.** Screening refusal (odmowa wszczęcia): ~30–50%, decided in 1–3 months. Opened then discontinued: ~30–40%. Charges (podejrzany status): ~15–35% cumulative, 6–24 months. Indictment: ~10–30%. Conviction from filing: ~8–22% (but 60–90% conditional on indictment). Total to final judgment: 2–5 years.
- **What moves the odds.** Top two everywhere: (1) the **bank trail** showing the 155k never went to any supplier — closest thing to direct proof of intent, and only the prosecutor can compel it; (2) **other victims** — converts "civil dispute" into a scheme, and one more victim with ≥45k PLN pushes the aggregate past the 200k art. 294 KK threshold (1–10 years, higher priority). Then: proof there was no real Hong Kong supplier. The contradictory excuses and refund promise are good complaint material but corroborative, not decisive.
- **Art. 46/49a mechanics.** On the victim's art. 49a KPK motion (deadline: close of the main trial — file early), the court **must** order damage repair on conviction; with an enforcement clause it is a standalone execution title, no separate civil suit needed. It also survives consumer bankruptcy (non-dischargeable).
- **Anti-cumulation (art. 415 § 1 KPK).** The pending EPU claim (Nc-e 552126/26) for the same 155k can **block** an art. 46 order. All models flag this; severity disputed (see below). Practical line: EPU remains the faster title and the only Brussels-I cross-border instrument for NL assets; art. 46 is the domestic, bankruptcy-proof backup.
- **Asset freeze (art. 291–295 KPK).** Free, prosecutor-driven, immediately enforceable, no bond — but generally only available once someone is formally a suspect. Your job is feeding asset intelligence.
- **Realistic cash route.** Settlement after charges/before indictment, leveraged by art. 295 KK (full repayment → extraordinary mitigation, possibly no punishment) and negotiated outcomes (art. 335/387 KPK). If cash comes, it comes in year 1–2.5, not after trial.

## Where the models disagree or add unique points

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro (PDF)**: most optimistic (27% cumulative conviction); hardest line on anti-cumulation — claims EPU "completely neutralizes" art. 46 and forfeits art. 244c KK leverage. Unique: art. 106a Banking Law 6-month account blockade and the "money as material evidence" pre-charge freeze workaround.
- **Kimi K2.6 (docx)**: most pessimistic on cash (1.2–2.1% to cash via full pipeline; expected cash 13.5–20k PLN); says anti-cumulation is **manageable** (EPU first, art. 46 for shortfall). Unique: Brussels I excludes criminal judgments → art. 46 useless against Dutch assets; remote participation (video testimony, free translation); five liability pathways for Waldek; art. 244c KK 30-day repentance window.
- **ChatGPT 5.5 Pro**: lowest conviction estimate (8–14%); warns prosecutors may deprioritize because "you already have a civil case."
- **ChatGPT Deep Research**: art. 46 § 3 KK lets you pursue any unsatisfied remainder civilly; subsidiary indictment needs an advocate's signature.
- **Perplexity Computer**: odmowa is not res judicata — refile when a second victim surfaces; freeze reaches assets transferred to third parties (art. 45 § 2–3 KK).
- **Perplexity Deep Research**: EAW/ENA pressure angle (NL executes them); suspended sentence conditioned on damage repair (art. 72 § 2/75 KK) as the strongest payment motivator post-conviction.

## Caveats / open questions

All percentages are model estimates, not case statistics. The anti-cumulation effect of a *pending* (vs. final) EPU is genuinely contested between models — verify with counsel. Gemini's art. 106a/material-evidence freeze is described as controversial practice. Cross-border enforcement of art. 46 in the Netherlands is doubtful. The decisive unknown is Szklarski's actual solvency — no mechanism manufactures money that isn't there.
