## Decision tree after filing

Baseline: this is a **credible criminal complaint**, but not a slam-dunk fraud case. The hard legal issue is not “he did not deliver” or “he promised a refund and defaulted.” The hard issue is proving **dishonest intent at the moment you paid**: that Mateusz, Waldek, or both induced the 155,000 PLN payment while knowing the workstation/order/refund story was false or materially misleading. Art. 286 § 1 KK covers fraud by misleading someone into an unfavourable property disposition for gain, punishable by 6 months to 8 years; 155,000 PLN is below the 200,000 PLN “mienie znacznej wartości” threshold, so on the present facts this is normally basic Art. 286, not Art. 294 aggravated fraud. ([Isap][1])

Official aggregate prosecution data are a useful reality check, but not a case-specific forecast: in 2024 Polish prosecutors disposed of 1,129,185 criminal preparatory proceedings; 28.7% ended in **refusal to initiate**, 37.9% in **discontinuance**, and 22% went to court with an indictment or equivalent motion. For your single-victim commercial non-delivery fact pattern, I would move the refusal/discontinuance risk **above average** unless the complaint quickly produces bank-trail, supplier, or other-victim evidence. ([IWS][2])

## Recovery-mechanism deep dive

### 1. Art. 46 § 1 KK — obowiązek naprawienia szkody

Art. 46 § 1 KK is the criminal court’s compensation order. On conviction, the court **may** order damage repair on its own, but **must order it on the victim’s motion** if the statutory conditions are met: damage caused by the offence, amount proven, convicted person responsible, and no procedural anti-duplication block. Art. 46 allows repair of the whole or part of the damage, applying civil-law rules; if exact ordering is difficult, the court may instead order a **nawiązka** up to 200,000 PLN. ([Isap][1])

You must file the motion under **Art. 49a KPK**. Timing: the victim or prosecutor may file the Art. 46 motion **until the close of the judicial proceedings at the main trial**. Do not wait. Put it into the criminal complaint now, repeat it after indictment, and, if there is a trial, make sure it is in the court file before the przewód sądowy closes. ([Isap][3])

It is **not automatic cash**. It is a judgment obligation. After a final criminal judgment, you request an enforcement clause; KPK Art. 107 says the court or referendarz grants a clause of enforceability for an enforceable decision, and compensation/nawiązka obligations to the victim are treated as property claims if fit for civil enforcement. So yes: if Art. 46 is ordered, you do **not** need a separate civil judgment for that same compensation order. But you still need enforcement against actual assets. ([Isap][3])

The catch in your case is the already-filed EPU claim. KPK Art. 415 § 1 has an anti-duplication rule: the criminal court does not order damage repair/nawiązka/compensation if the same offence-derived claim is already the subject of another proceeding or has already been finally adjudicated. Because Nc-e 552126/26 is already pending for the same 155,000 PLN, the criminal court may later refuse to duplicate the civil claim under Art. 46. This is not a reason to abandon EPU; civil/EPU may be faster for an enforceable title. It means Art. 46 is not your cleanest money route if the civil case is active. ([Isap][3])

For Waldek, Art. 46 only helps against him if he is convicted. If Waldek remains a witness or informal helper, no criminal compensation order is made against him.

### 2. Art. 291–295 KPK — zabezpieczenie majątkowe

This is the most important criminal-process money tool, but it is usually misunderstood. **Zabezpieczenie majątkowe is a freeze, not a payout.** It preserves assets so that a later fine, forfeiture, compensation order, or return obligation can be enforced.

For ordinary securing of a future compensation obligation, the practical trigger is usually: someone becomes **podejrzany**. Art. 291 § 1 KPK says that when the accused is charged with an offence for which a fine, forfeiture, compensatory measure, or return of gain may be ordered, security may be imposed ex officio on the accused’s assets if there is justified fear that enforcement would otherwise be impossible or significantly hindered. Because KPK Art. 71 says “oskarżony” generally includes “podejrzany,” this usually becomes live after formal charges. ([Isap][3])

There is a narrower earlier route: Art. 291 § 2 allows certain security for property subject to forfeiture or return even independently of whether formal charges have been issued. In practice, that is strongest where the 155,000 PLN or directly traceable proceeds are located. It is not a general pre-charge civil-style freeze of all Mateusz’s assets. ([Isap][3])

What can be frozen: bank accounts, receivables, movable assets, other property rights, vehicles, equipment, and real estate by a ban on sale/encumbrance entered in the land register; KPK also allows civil-procedure methods and, in some cases, compulsory management of an enterprise. In preparatory proceedings the prosecutor issues the security order; after indictment the court does. The order specifies the amount and method of security, is appealable, and itself becomes an enforcement title when issued. ([Isap][3])

Police also have an emergency tool: temporary seizure of movable property of a suspected person where there is fear of removal, but it lapses if no security order is issued within 7 days. This is useful only if there is an identified asset to seize. ([Isap][3])

Cost to you: no filing fee for requesting it in the criminal complaint. Your cost is supplying asset leads and pushing the prosecutor. The state bears the investigation cost, but the prosecutor controls the tool. You cannot force criminal security the way a claimant litigates a civil injunction. You can request it, justify it, appeal certain refusals/inaction where procedurally available, and keep giving asset information.

Compared with civil zabezpieczenie: criminal security is better because it can ride on prosecutorial powers, bank inquiries, and ex officio action; it is worse because it usually arrives later, after the case has passed the criminal-intent threshold. Civil security is theoretically earlier but requires satisfying civil procedural standards; if your lawyer says you cannot realistically get it, the criminal route may be the only practical freeze route, but only after the prosecutor takes the case seriously.

For Dutch assets, EU criminal freezing/confiscation cooperation exists, and Regulation 2018/1805 is specifically about mutual recognition of freezing and confiscation orders in cross-border EU asset recovery. In practice, however, freezing a Dutch salary account or Dutch assets is slower and less predictable than freezing a Polish business bank account. ([Eurojust][4])

### 3. Restitution, mediation, voluntary repayment, sentence mitigation

This is the most realistic money route. Not Art. 46 after years. Not symbolic conviction. The realistic route is: **they pay because the criminal case becomes dangerous**.

The pressure windows are:

| Stage                                                             | Settlement leverage | Cash realism                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before refusal/opening decision                                   | Low to medium       | They may pay something to prevent the complaint from escalating, but many debtors gamble on refusal.                                |
| After investigation opens and bank/supplier records are requested | Medium              | This is often the first real fear point. They no longer control the narrative.                                                      |
| After charges                                                     | High                | This is the best pressure stage. A suspect now faces criminal record, security, indictment risk, and sentencing exposure.           |
| Before indictment / negotiated conviction motion                  | High                | Payment can be used to argue for leniency or a consensual outcome.                                                                  |
| Before sentencing                                                 | Medium-high         | Still useful, but if the court process is already underway, payment may be more about sentence mitigation than avoiding conviction. |

Art. 295 KK gives a real statutory incentive in property crimes including fraud: if the offender voluntarily repairs the damage in full, the court may apply extraordinary mitigation and even waive punishment; if the offender repairs a substantial part, extraordinary mitigation may be available. That is a genuine lever. ([Isap][1])

But do not overstate it. Basic Art. 286 § 1 carries up to 8 years, and conditional discontinuance generally does not apply to offences punishable by more than 5 years. So full payment does not automatically make the case disappear. It can still strongly affect charging, plea posture, sentencing, and whether the defendant fights or settles. ([Isap][1])

The business-ban pressure is real but secondary. Art. 41 KK allows the court, on conviction for an offence connected with business activity, to prohibit conducting a specified business activity if continued activity threatens legally protected interests. For a JDG whose business depends on trust, the threat of conviction, public judgment, and business restriction can matter. It only matters if Mateusz or Waldek have a reputation, future business, employment, or licensing concern worth protecting. ([Isap][1])

## When the criminal route actually returns money

**It returns money when the debtor is solvent and scared.** The best factual version for you is: investigation opens, bank records show no real workstation order, Waldek’s role is documented, other victims appear, assets are found, and Mateusz/Waldek realise that paying 155,000 PLN is cheaper than charges/indictment/conviction. In that version, settlement is possible before indictment, and criminal pressure has real cash value.

**It may return money later through Art. 46 KK or secured assets.** This requires conviction, a compensation order not blocked by the pending civil claim, and successful enforcement. If criminal zabezpieczenie freezes money or assets early, your odds improve sharply. Without a freeze, an Art. 46 order years later may be just another paper title against an empty debtor.

**It may return no cash but still deliver strategic value.** It can force bank-trail checks, supplier verification, email/domain evidence, identification of Waldek’s role, and asset searches that you cannot easily obtain in EPU. It may also create settlement pressure, support future non-dischargeability / bad-faith arguments, and create criminal-record or business-ban risk. That is leverage value, not cash in hand.

**It is a dead end if the file stays single-victim and civil-looking.** If there is a plausible supplier, some evidence of a real order, no other victims, no fake documents, no asset trail, and no proof of initial dishonesty, expect refusal or umorzenie. You may get a few months of procedural friction and no money.

## Cost vs. benefit if you run it yourself

If you run it yourself with minimal paid counsel, the expected value is positive. The complaint is effectively free to file, the prosecutor/police bear the investigation burden, and your main cost is time: preparing a clean evidence pack, responding to requests, pushing for bank/supplier/email evidence, and appealing a bad refusal or discontinuance. KPK expressly allows any person aware of an ex officio offence to notify the prosecutor or police, and the authorities then decide whether to initiate or refuse. ([Isap][3])

I would not spend heavily at the outset. The sensible spend is targeted: a lawyer to polish the criminal complaint, draft the Art. 46 / zabezpieczenie requests, and handle a zażalenie against refusal or umorzenie. If charges are filed or assets are found, then paying counsel becomes more rational. Spending 20,000–40,000 PLN early can easily consume the expected cash value of the criminal route.

## Honest verdict

**Money back: conditional, not yes.** On current facts, the criminal route is worth filing because it is cheap, can reach Waldek in a way EPU cannot, and may unlock bank/supplier/asset evidence. But I would treat it primarily as **pressure and discovery**, not as a reliable collection path. Full 155,000 PLN from the criminal route alone is a low-probability outcome unless you get at least one of these: other victims, bank-trail diversion, proof the supplier/customs story was false, identifiable assets, or a suspect who decides repayment is the price of avoiding criminal escalation. Without those, expect leverage value, not cash.
