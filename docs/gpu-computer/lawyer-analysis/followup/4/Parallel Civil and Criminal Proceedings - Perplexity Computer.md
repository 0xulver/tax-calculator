This is general legal information based on current Polish statute and case law, not formal legal advice in your specific matter. Confirm the live procedural posture with your acting pełnomocnik before you move.

Direct answers
Can both run at once? Yes. There is no procedural bar. You can have an open EPU/civil claim and a criminal zawiadomienie for the same facts simultaneously. Nothing in the KPC or KPK requires you to choose.

Will the civil case be suspended because of the criminal case? Probably not, and almost certainly not while it sits in EPU. Suspension under art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC is discretionary, not mandatory, and Sąd Najwyższy case law says criminal proceedings "as a rule do not lead to suspension" of the civil case. The risk rises only modestly after a sprzeciw converts the case to ordinary proceedings.

Can you hold both a civil title and a criminal art. 46 KK restitution order for the same 155k? Generally no — not for an identical claim. The anti-cumulation clause (klauzula antykumulacyjna) in art. 415 § 1 zd. 2 KPK forbids the criminal court from ordering naprawienie szkody where the same claim is already the subject of another proceeding or has been finally adjudicated. But there is a real exception turning on identity of debtor that may work in your favour — see below.

Can you collect twice? No. You can collect the 155k once. Even if two titles somehow exist, the moment the debt is satisfied the second title is neutralized via a powództwo przeciwegzekucyjne (art. 840 § 1 pkt 2 KPC).

The suspension analysis (art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC)
Discretionary, and the courts read it narrowly. The provision says the court "may" (może) suspend — it is fakultatywne, never obligatoryjne, when the trigger is a parallel criminal case. The settled SN line is that the civil court should run its own evidence first and suspend only if (a) the civil evidence gathered is insufficient to grant the claim, and (b) a criminal conviction could plug that gap. Suspension without first taking civil evidence is unjustified (SN II PZ 20/65, cited in the same ruling).

What this means for you:

In EPU itself: suspension is effectively a non-issue. EPU is a documentary chase-the-order track with no evidentiary hearing; the e-sąd either issues the nakaz or it doesn't. Art. 177 suspension is a tool of the merits court, not the e-sąd. So as long as your case stays in EPU, art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 simply doesn't bite.

Who requests it matters in practice, not in law. Suspension is ordered z urzędu (on the court's own motion). The debtor can move for it as a delaying tactic, but he cannot force it — the court must weigh celowość. A documentary claim where your civil evidence already supports the debt (invoices, contract, transfer confirmations) is exactly the scenario where SN says don't suspend, because the civil court can decide on its own record. You, as creditor, should affirmatively oppose any such motion and point to that SN line.

Risk rises — modestly — only after a sprzeciw. A sprzeciw kills the nakaz and transfers the case to the sąd właściwości ogólnej (here the debtor's general venue) for ordinary proceedings. Once it's a contested merits trial, the debtor will likely move to suspend pending the karne. Your counter remains the same: the court must take civil evidence first and may suspend only if that evidence is inconclusive. With a documented 155k debt that bar is hard for him to clear.

Can the two cases help each other? Yes, in one direction strongly:

A final criminal conviction binds the civil court (art. 11 KPC — wiążące ustalenia wyroku skazującego as to commission of the crime). So a skazanie for art. 286 § 1 KK would lock in the civil court's findings in your favour. That is the upside the debtor fears, and why he might want suspension — to delay your title until the karne resolves.

The reverse is weaker: a civil nakaz/judgment does not bind the criminal court, but it is powerful evidence of the debt and its amount, and — critically — it can trigger the anti-cumulation clause and shape what the prosecutor/criminal court can order (next section).

The double-recovery rule
The governing rule: identity of claim, judged by parties + same factual event.

The anti-cumulation clause blocks a second title only where the claim is identical — same roszczenie arising from the same historical event, between the same parties (SN IV KK 234/17; Mozgawa, gloss, gov.pl). Two consequences flow from this, and your specific facts sit right on the seam:

(a) If the EPU title and the criminal restitution would be against the same debtor (Mateusz the natural person):
Then the claims are identical. Once you have a final nakaz zapłaty against Mateusz, the criminal court cannot also impose an art. 46 KK obowiązek naprawienia szkody on him for the same 155k — and this holds regardless of whether your civil judgment has actually been enforced/collected (SN IV KK 234/17; IV Ka 702/24). You get one title, not two. This is the key point about JDG GPUcomputer: a JDG (jednooosobowa działalność gospodarcza) has no separate legal personality — "Mateusz Szklarski / JDG GPUcomputer" is Mateusz the natural person. So your EPU defendant and your art. 286 accused are the same legal subject. That means an EPU title likely forecloses an art. 46 KK order against Mateusz for the same sum. You'd still get the conviction (and its non-dischargeability effect from prompt 7 attaches to the criminal debt/judgment, but the restitution component itself would be barred).

(b) Where the anti-cumulation clause does not apply — different debtor:
The clause requires identity of parties, not just the event. The classic SN scenario: creditor has a nakaz against a spółka z o.o., then seeks art. 46 KK restitution against a board member personally — those are different subjects, so no identity of claim, and the criminal restitution is permitted. This is exactly your Waldek angle from prompt 3. Your EPU is against Mateusz/JDG. If Waldek is also charged under art. 286, an art. 46 KK restitution order against Waldek is not blocked by your nakaz against Mateusz — different defendant, no identity. That is a legitimate route to a second, separate title against a second person.

Even if two titles exist, you collect the 155k once. The whole architecture is built to prevent double satisfaction (zakaz podwójnego zaspokojenia). If you ever hold two enforceable titles for the same debt and one is satisfied, the debtor (or co-debtor) defeats the second by a powództwo o pozbawienie wykonalności tytułu wykonawczego under art. 840 § 1 pkt 2 KPC — the obligation has expired (wygasło) by satisfaction, so the second title loses enforceability; a co-debtor can alternatively move to discontinue execution under art. 825 pkt 2 KPC. The bailiff is not allowed to hand you 310k for a 155k loss.

Is there any advantage to holding two titles? Yes, but only the Waldek/different-debtor kind:

One title enforceable cleanly in Poland (the EPU nakaz against Mateusz) plus a separate restitution title against Waldek gives you two pockets to chase for one debt — useful if Mateusz is judgment-proof in PL but assets sit elsewhere, or vice versa. You still only collect 155k total, but you maximise the chance of collecting it from someone.

The criminal route also carries the non-dischargeability-in-bankruptcy effect you flagged in prompt 7: debts from intentional tort/crime survive konsumencka/upadłość discharge in a way an ordinary civil debt may not. So a criminal restitution title (where available — i.e. against Waldek, not blocked-out Mateusz) is qualitatively more durable than a plain civil one.

Against Mateusz specifically, the durable-debt benefit you want is better obtained by securing the conviction (art. 11 KPC binding effect + the criminal record establishing intentional fraud), which you can then leverage even though the art. 46 restitution order itself is barred by your nakaz.

A correction on the EPU "fiction of service" premise
This is the most important practical flag, and it cuts against the stated rationale for keeping EPU running. The fikcja doręczenia only works on a Polish PESEL address. The mechanic: after two failed delivery attempts, the nakaz is deemed served only if the address matches the one in the PESEL register. And two hard statutory limits apply when the debtor is abroad:

Art. 505²⁸ § 2 pkt 2 KPC: a nakaz zapłaty cannot be issued in EPU at all if service would have to occur abroad (poza granicami kraju).

Art. 505³⁴ § 2 KPC: if after issuing the nakaz it turns out it cannot be served on the debtor in Poland, the court uchyla nakaz i umarza postępowanie (quashes the order and discontinues), unless you remove the service obstacle within ≤1 month.

So the EPU-because-of-fikcja-doręczenia advantage is real only if Mateusz still has a registered residence address in the PESEL register inside Poland that you can serve to. If his only address is in the Netherlands, EPU will not produce a nakaz — it will be quashed/discontinued. Verify with your pełnomocnik that Mateusz retains a Polish PESEL-registered address. If he does, the lawyer's strategy is sound. If he doesn't, EPU is a dead end and you should pivot to ordinary postępowanie upominawcze or a European Payment Order. This is worth confirming before you invest more in the EPU track.

Recommended sequencing
Assuming Mateusz retains a serviceable Polish PESEL address (so EPU can actually yield a nakaz):

Push EPU to a final nakaz first — keep it running, don't withdraw. Your lawyer is right not to withdraw it for a civil asset freeze; withdrawal in EPU forfeits the fee and the fiction-of-service path. A final nakaz gives you (i) a clean Polish execution title, (ii) the bezskuteczność egzekucji evidence you'll want later, and (iii) the document that triggers the anti-cumulation clause in your favour.

File the criminal zawiadomienie in parallel — now, not after. Filing now does not jeopardise the EPU (no suspension risk while in EPU), and it starts the prosecutor's clock on the asset-securing measures (zabezpieczenie majątkowe, art. 291+ KPK) and the pressure. The earlier the karne is on foot, the sooner a prosecutor can freeze assets — something the civil track can't do as fast against an absent debtor. There is no downside to filing early here; the "wait for the nakaz" caution that exists in some matters is about evidence, and you already have the documentary debt.

Direct the criminal restitution at the right defendant. Against Mateusz, expect the art. 46 KK order to be barred by your nakaz (identity of claim) — so frame the karne against him around the conviction itself (art. 11 KPC binding effect + the prompt-7 non-dischargeability of the debt). Against Waldek (if charged), affirmatively request an art. 46 KK obowiązek naprawienia szkody, because your nakaz against Mateusz does not block it — that's your legitimate second title against a second pocket.

Enforce whichever bites first; let the anti-double-recovery rule clean up the rest. Chase assets under the EPU nakaz in Poland and under any Waldek restitution order wherever his assets are. Once 155k is recovered from any source, the other title is neutralized by the debtor/co-debtor via art. 840 § 1 pkt 2 (or art. 825 pkt 2) KPC. You are not exposed to a double-payment clawback — you simply stop being able to enforce the satisfied debt twice.

The wniosek o przyspieszenie rozpoznania sprawy — worth filing, low cost, modest effect. It's a short informal motion to the e-sąd asking it to expedite issuance of the nakaz, typically citing: the case reference (Nc-e 552126/26), the date filed (08.04.2026), the simple documentary nature of the claim, the absence of any complicating factor, and your interest in a prompt title given the debtor's flight risk / asset dissipation. It costs nothing and signals urgency. Be realistic: Lublin-Zachód is the single national e-sąd and chronically backlogged, so a przyspieszenie request nudges rather than guarantees. If the delay becomes genuinely excessive, the heavier instrument is a skarga na przewlekłość postępowania (complaint on undue delay) — but that's premature for a claim filed in April being reviewed in late May; reserve it.
