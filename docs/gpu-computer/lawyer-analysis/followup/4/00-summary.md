# Research summary — Running EPU and the criminal case in parallel

> All six models agree you can run the EPU claim and an art. 286 § 1 KK fraud complaint at the same time, that suspension of the civil case is discretionary and unlikely, and that you can only ever collect the 155,000 PLN once — the real decision the anti-cumulation rule (art. 415 § 1 KPK) forces is *which* title you want against Mateusz, because a pending or final civil claim blocks a criminal art. 46 KK restitution order against the same person. Four of six say keep pushing EPU and file the criminal complaint now; Kimi says criminal-second, Gemini says criminal-first and warns the EPU service advantage may be illusory for a Netherlands-resident debtor.

## Key findings (consensus)

- **Parallel tracks are legal.** No KPC/KPK bar; the tracks are independent (art. 8 KPK) and answer different questions (debt vs. fraudulent intent).
- **Suspension risk is low.** Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC says the court "may" suspend — facultative, never mandatory; SN case law discourages hasty suspension. In the pre-nakaz EPU phase it is practically a non-issue. Risk rises modestly only if a sprzeciw pushes the dispute into ordinary proceedings; a debtor's suspension motion is a stalling tactic the court need not honour, and you should actively oppose it.
- **Anti-cumulation (art. 415 § 1 zd. 2 KPK).** Because a JDG has no separate legal personality, the EPU defendant and the would-be accused are the same person, so the pending EPU blocks an art. 46 KK order against Mateusz for the same 155k. SN (III KK 270/11) holds this applies even if civil enforcement proves ineffective. You can still "win" both — civil title plus conviction — just not two compensation titles against him.
- **No double recovery.** One loss, one satisfaction; a second title is neutralised after payment via art. 840 § 1 pkt 2 KPC.
- **Cross-effects are one-way.** A final conviction binds the civil court (art. 11 KPC); a civil nakaz doesn't bind the criminal court but is strong evidence of debt and loss.
- **EPU timing.** Lublin-Zachód is chronically backlogged (Perplexity DR cites ~70-day CEPEJ disposition in 2025, ~350k case backlog). The wniosek o przyspieszenie is cheap, risk-free and worth filing (five of six models), but is a nudge, not a lever; the formal remedy (skarga na przewlekłość) should be held in reserve.

## Where the models disagree or add unique points

- **Sequencing.** ChatGPT 5.5 Pro, ChatGPT Deep Research, both Perplexity files: file the zawiadomienie **now** (early prosecutor asset-tracing, art. 291 KPK security, no real suspension risk). **Kimi K2.6 Swarm**: civil-first — wait for a final nakaz plus bezskuteczność evidence (~month 3) to avoid tipping off the debtor into a sprzeciw. **Gemini 3.1 Pro** is the outlier: it calls a final nakaz a "fatal strategic error" because it permanently locks out art. 46 KK and the art. 244c KK prison-for-evasion leverage, recommends treating EPU as a decoy, and touts EU Reg 2018/1805 cross-border criminal freezing.
- **Fiction of service.** Gemini argues post-2019 art. 139(1) KPC bailiff-verification kills the fikcja for a debtor known to live abroad; Perplexity Computer and Kimi say it works **only if** Mateusz retains a Polish PESEL-registered address (art. 505²⁸/505³⁴ KPC: no nakaz if service must occur abroad) and flag PESEL verification as the critical check.
- **Waldek angle (Perplexity Computer).** Anti-cumulation requires identity of debtor — an art. 46 KK order against Waldek is **not** blocked by the nakaz against Mateusz: a legitimate second pocket.
- **Sprzeciw mechanics.** ChatGPT DR: current law = umorzenie plus a 3-month window to refile preserving the filing date; both Perplexity files describe transfer to the general-venue court (likely outdated).

## Action checklist

1. Verify whether Mateusz retains a Polish PESEL-registered address — the whole EPU strategy hinges on it.
2. File the wniosek o przyspieszenie now (neutral tone, cite case number, filing date, debtor abroad, asset-dissipation risk; do not mention the criminal plan).
3. Decide sequencing presenting the dissent: majority say file the fraud complaint now; weigh Gemini/Kimi's art. 46/244c-preservation argument — clearest remaining question for a one-off lawyer consult.
4. In the zawiadomienie: request pokrzywdzony status, evidence preservation, and zabezpieczenie majątkowe; disclose the EPU number; aim any art. 46 KK motion at Waldek, not Mateusz.
5. If a sprzeciw kills the nakaz, refile in ordinary proceedings within 3 months; oppose any suspension motion.
6. Once a nakaz is final: clause, Polish enforcement, document bezskuteczność, then Netherlands enforcement under Brussels I bis. Credit every payment against the single 155k loss.

## Caveats / open questions

- The civil-title-vs-criminal-title trade-off (Gemini's core claim) is consequential and unresolved between the models — it directly contradicts the lawyer's wait-for-EPU strategy and needs a professional ruling.
- Perplexity DR floats a contested exception (contractual nakaz vs. delict-based art. 46 KK may not be the "same claim"); treat as unsettled.
- Backlog statistics and the post-2019 service-fiction mechanics are unverified; sprzeciw mechanics differ between models. None of this is legal advice.
