## Direct answers

1. **Yes — both can proceed simultaneously.** An open EPU/civil claim does **not** block a criminal complaint under **art. 286 § 1 KK** for the same factual transaction. Polish law expressly allows property claims arising from an offence to be pursued in civil proceedings or, where the criminal procedure allows it, in criminal proceedings; a final criminal conviction can also later bind the civil court as to the fact that the offence was committed. ([Isap][1])

2. **No — the EPU/civil case is not automatically suspended.** Suspension under **art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC** is discretionary: the court **may** suspend, not must suspend. For a clean, document-based EPU payment claim, suspension is unlikely at the EPU stage. It becomes more arguable only if the case leaves EPU and becomes a contested ordinary civil case where the criminal findings could materially affect the civil judgment. ([Isap][1])

3. **You can run both tracks, but you cannot collect the same 155,000 PLN twice.** In fact, a criminal court generally should **not** impose an **art. 46 KK** compensation order for the same claim if that claim is already pending in EPU/civil proceedings or has already been finally adjudicated. That is the anti-cumulation rule in **art. 415 § 1 KPK**. If two titles somehow exist, every payment must be credited against the same loss; the second route cannot be used for double satisfaction. ([Isap][2])

4. **Recommended sequence:** keep EPU running, push for the nakaz, file the criminal complaint in parallel if the fraud evidence is strong enough, and later supplement the criminal file with the nakaz, enforcement attempts, and any bezskuteczność. Do not withdraw EPU just because a criminal case is being filed. The criminal case may help with pressure, evidence, and possible asset security, but it is not a faster substitute for a civil execution title.

5. **The wniosek o przyspieszenie is worth filing, but expectations should be modest.** It is low-cost and procedurally harmless if drafted neutrally. It may put the case back on the court’s screen, but it is not a statutory accelerator and Lublin-Zachód’s EPU workload means it may have limited practical effect. The legal tone can rely on **art. 6 § 1 KPC**, under which the court should counteract delay. ([Isap][1])

## 1. Suspension analysis — EPU/civil vs criminal case

### The starting point: no procedural bar

The same transaction can generate two different legal tracks:

**Civil/EPU:** recovery of the 155,000 PLN plus statutory delay interest and costs.

**Criminal:** investigation/prosecution for suspected fraud under **art. 286 § 1 KK**, meaning obtaining a financial benefit by causing another person to make an adverse disposition of property through deception, exploitation of error, or inability to properly understand the action. ([Isap][2])

Those tracks are not mutually exclusive. The civil case answers: “Is the defendant obliged to pay?” The criminal case answers: “Was an offence committed, and by whom?” The overlap is obvious, but the procedural purposes are different.

### Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC: discretionary, not mandatory

**Art. 177 § 1 KPC** says the civil court **may** suspend proceedings ex officio in the listed cases. Point 4 covers a situation where a fact is revealed whose establishment in criminal or disciplinary proceedings **could affect** the civil case. The key word is **“może”** — the suspension is discretionary. ([Isap][1])

The Supreme Court’s line is useful for your situation: suspension under art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC is not automatic merely because a criminal case exists. The civil court should consider the purpose of suspension, the parties’ interests, expected duration of the criminal case, probability that a conviction will be issued, the stage and character of the civil case, and the need for speedy civil adjudication. The Supreme Court has also warned against hasty suspension where the criminal case is not truly necessary for the civil judgment. 

### EPU stage: suspension risk is low

For your current case — **Nc-e 552126/26**, EPU, 155,000 PLN plus interest — the practical suspension risk is low **before** any sprzeciw.

EPU is designed for a simplified payment-order assessment. It is not a forum for extensive evidence-taking. In EPU, evidence is indicated rather than fully litigated in the same way as ordinary proceedings, and the court either issues the nakaz or refuses/handles procedural obstacles under the EPU rules. ([Isap][1])

That matters because a criminal fraud investigation is usually not necessary to decide whether a payment-order claim is formally capable of being pursued. If your civil claim can be framed as a straightforward debt/refund/payment obligation, the criminal question of fraudulent intent at the moment of contracting/payment should not normally be a precondition to issuing a civil payment order.

### Who requests suspension matters tactically

If **you** request suspension, you are effectively telling the civil court that the criminal case is important enough to pause your own money claim. That would usually be contrary to your current strategy. I would not do it unless there is a specific evidentiary reason why the civil court cannot sensibly decide without the criminal file.

If **the debtor** requests suspension, treat it as a likely stalling tactic unless he identifies a precise criminal issue that is genuinely prejudicial to the civil case. Your response should be: the civil claim is for payment/refund; the criminal case may proceed independently; criminal findings are not necessary to decide the monetary obligation; delay would prejudice the creditor; and the Supreme Court discourages automatic suspension merely because criminal proceedings are pending. 

### If there is a sprzeciw, the risk rises — but note the EPU mechanics

Strictly, under the current EPU model, after an effective **sprzeciw**, the case does not simply “convert” into ordinary proceedings in the old automatic sense. The EPU court discontinues the proceeding to the extent the nakaz lost force, and the claimant then has a three-month window to file the same claim in ordinary proceedings while preserving the legal effects of the EPU filing date. ([Isap][1])

In the ordinary case, suspension risk is higher than in EPU because the debtor may raise factual defences and request evidence. Still, it is not automatic. The court should suspend only if the criminal findings could materially affect the civil judgment. For example:

* **Low suspension risk:** unpaid invoice, refund obligation, written acknowledgment, bank transfer, clear contractual documents.
* **Higher suspension risk:** alleged forged documents, disputed identity of the contracting party, disputed authority to act, factual issues where the criminal file contains evidence the civil court cannot realistically assess without waiting.

Even then, the creditor’s position should usually be: the civil court can decide the debt on civil evidence now; a later criminal conviction may help, but it is not indispensable.

### Can one track help the other?

Yes.

A **final criminal conviction** helps the civil side because under **art. 11 KPC**, findings of a final criminal conviction as to the commission of the offence bind the civil court. That does not mean every civil issue is automatically resolved, but it is powerful on the offence facts. ([Isap][1])

A **civil nakaz/judgment** helps the criminal side differently. It does not prove fraud by itself, because fraud requires more than non-payment; it requires deceptive intent around the adverse disposition of property. But it helps prove the debt, the amount of loss, the debtor’s failure to perform, and later enforcement ineffectiveness. Those facts can support the criminal narrative, especially when combined with pre-payment representations, concealment, diversion of funds, false assurances, or asset dissipation.

## 2. Double-recovery rule — civil title and art. 46 KK

### Art. 46 KK exists, but art. 415 KPK blocks duplication

**Art. 46 § 1 KK** allows the criminal court, upon conviction, to order the offender to repair damage in whole or in part; on the victim’s motion, the court must order it if the statutory conditions are met. ([Isap][2])

But the crucial limitation is **art. 415 § 1 KPK**. The criminal court does **not** order compensation/nawiązka/zadośćuczynienie if the claim arising from the offence is already the object of another proceeding or has already been finally adjudicated. ([Isap][3])

For your facts, that means:

**Against Mateusz / JDG GPUcomputer:** because the EPU claim for the same 155,000 PLN is already pending, the criminal court should not duplicate that same claim through an art. 46 KK order against him.

**Against Waldek:** if Waldek is not a defendant in the EPU case, the analysis is more open, but the anti-double-recovery principle still applies. If he were convicted as a co-perpetrator or aider and ordered to repair damage, any money collected from him would reduce what can be collected from Mateusz for the same loss.

### Can you “win” both?

Yes, in the following sense:

You can obtain a **civil title** confirming Mateusz owes the 155,000 PLN plus interest/costs.

You can also obtain a **criminal conviction** confirming that the conduct was fraudulent.

But you normally should not receive two separate, enforceable compensation orders for the exact same 155,000 PLN against the same person. If the civil claim is pending or already adjudicated, the criminal court should either refrain from ordering art. 46 KK for that same claim or limit itself to any part not covered by the civil case. If a criminal compensation order covers only part of the damage, the victim can pursue the remaining part civilly. ([Isap][2])

### What if two titles somehow exist?

If, through timing or oversight, you end up with both a civil execution title and a criminal restitution title covering the same principal, you still collect the same damage **once**.

The practical rule is:

* collect under whichever title is effective first;
* credit every payment against the same 155,000 PLN loss;
* once the principal is satisfied, do not continue enforcement for that same principal under the second title;
* the second title remains relevant only for unsatisfied interest, costs, or amounts that are not duplicative, if any.

This is better understood as **zakaz podwójnego zaspokojenia** and procedural anti-cumulation than as a chance to multiply recovery. **Powaga rzeczy osądzonej** matters in the civil track, but in the criminal-compensation context the more specific rule is art. 415 KPK.

### Is there any advantage to having both tracks?

Yes, but not because you collect twice.

The advantages are:

**Civil title:** usually the better money-collection instrument. Once enforceable, a Polish civil judgment/payment order can be used for enforcement in another EU Member State, including the Netherlands, under the Brussels I Recast framework: a judgment enforceable in one Member State is enforceable in another without a declaration of enforceability, though actual enforcement follows the law of the state addressed. ([EUR-Lex][4])

**Criminal track:** may produce investigative pressure, evidence-gathering, witness interrogation, banking evidence, and — once the person has suspect/accused status and statutory conditions are met — possible **zabezpieczenie majątkowe** under **art. 291 KPK**. Asset security is not automatic on filing the complaint; it generally requires a concrete criminal-law basis and fear that future enforcement will be impossible or significantly hindered. ([Isap][3])

**Bankruptcy/insolvency advantage:** a criminal conviction or court-imposed obligation to repair crime damage can matter if the debtor later seeks consumer bankruptcy relief. Polish bankruptcy law excludes certain criminal-law obligations and crime-damage obligations confirmed by a final judgment from discharge. ([Isap][5])

That last point is a real strategic reason to pursue the criminal case even if the civil title remains your main collection tool.

## 3. Sequencing strategy for your facts

### Step 1 — keep EPU alive and push for the nakaz

I agree with the lawyer’s instinct **not to withdraw EPU** merely to chase a civil asset freeze. You already filed on **08.04.2026**. The claim is for a fixed monetary amount. If the EPU court issues a nakaz and it becomes final/enforceable, you have a clean civil route to execution.

However, the service point is important. EPU has a service advantage only within the statutory limits. The EPU rules say a payment order should not be issued if service on the defendant would have to take place outside Poland; they also contain a specific deemed-service mechanism where notices are left at an address matching the PESEL register. If it later turns out that the nakaz cannot be served in Poland, the EPU court may vacate the order and discontinue the proceeding unless the obstacle is removed. ([Isap][1])

So the lawful strategy is: use EPU if the Polish address/service basis is defensible, but do not build the case on a knowingly false service premise. If the Netherlands residence becomes a service obstacle, be ready for ordinary proceedings.

### Step 2 — file the criminal complaint now, not only after the nakaz

Filing the criminal complaint now is usually better than waiting, provided the facts support fraud rather than mere non-payment. Waiting for the nakaz gives you a cleaner civil document, but it also gives the debtor more time to move assets and lets electronic/banking/platform evidence get older.

The complaint should be framed as a fraud case, not as “please help me collect a debt.” Attach and emphasize:

* pre-payment representations and assurances;
* invoices/orders/contracts/messages;
* payment confirmation;
* non-delivery or non-performance;
* post-payment evasions, false excuses, concealment, or inconsistent explanations;
* any facts showing Mateusz knew at the time of taking the money that performance/refund was unlikely or not intended;
* links to Waldek only if there is evidence of participation, aiding, directing, receiving funds, or knowingly facilitating the scheme;
* the pending EPU case number, but with a clear statement that you are not seeking double recovery.

In the criminal complaint, request recognition as **pokrzywdzony**, evidence preservation, banking/telecom/platform evidence, interrogation of relevant persons, and consideration of **zabezpieczenie majątkowe** once procedural conditions are met. The formal art. 46 KK motion can be filed by the victim up to the close of the trial, but because your EPU claim is already pending, the motion should be worded carefully to avoid collision with art. 415 KPK. ([Isap][3])

### Step 3 — once the nakaz is issued, use it aggressively

If the EPU nakaz is issued and becomes final:

1. obtain the enforcement clause;
2. start Polish enforcement quickly;
3. check bank accounts, CEIDG-related assets, receivables, vehicles, and any Polish assets;
4. if Polish enforcement is ineffective, obtain documentation of **bezskuteczność**;
5. supplement the criminal file with the nakaz, enforcement attempts, and the ineffectiveness report;
6. consider enforcement in the Netherlands under EU civil-enforcement rules.

The bezskuteczność evidence is especially useful if the criminal case later needs to show ongoing harm, concealment, dissipation, or the practical impossibility of recovery.

### Step 4 — if there is a sprzeciw, preserve the filing effect

If the debtor files a sprzeciw and the EPU case is discontinued, file the ordinary civil claim within the statutory three-month window to preserve the legal effects of the EPU filing date. At that point, reassess civil asset security in the ordinary court. ([Isap][1])

In ordinary proceedings, oppose any debtor motion to suspend unless the debtor identifies a specific criminal issue that truly prevents the civil court from deciding the payment claim.

### Step 5 — use whichever route “bites,” but credit all payments

Your collection plan should be practical:

* if civil enforcement works first, collect there and inform the prosecutor/court of recovered amounts;
* if criminal asset security produces leverage, use it, but do not abandon the civil title;
* if a settlement appears, make it global: civil case, criminal restitution position, costs, interest, and withdrawal/limitation of enforcement only after cleared funds.

The operational rule is simple: **two tracks, one loss, one satisfaction.**

## 4. Wniosek o przyspieszenie rozpoznania sprawy in EPU

It is worth filing because it is cheap, polite, and may help. It should not be dramatic. It should not read like a complaint against the court. It should simply ask the EPU court to take the next procedural step.

A suitable version:

> **Wniosek o podjęcie czynności / przyspieszenie rozpoznania sprawy**
>
> Działając jako powód w sprawie **Nc-e 552126/26**, wnoszę o możliwie pilne podjęcie czynności w sprawie, w szczególności o rozpoznanie pozwu wniesionego w dniu **08.04.2026 r.** poprzez wydanie nakazu zapłaty, ewentualnie wydanie stosownego zarządzenia lub postanowienia, jeżeli Sąd dostrzega przeszkody do wydania nakazu.
>
> Pozew obejmuje wymagalne roszczenie pieniężne w kwocie **155.000 zł** wraz z odsetkami ustawowymi za opóźnienie od dnia **31.03.2026 r.** Do dnia sporządzenia niniejszego pisma w sprawie nie został wydany nakaz zapłaty ani inne rozstrzygnięcie nadające sprawie dalszy bieg. Upływ czasu zwiększa ryzyko utrudnienia przyszłej egzekucji. Mając na uwadze dyrektywę przeciwdziałania przewlekłości postępowania wynikającą z **art. 6 § 1 KPC**, wnoszę jak na wstępie.

I would keep it neutral on the Netherlands issue unless the court has already raised service/address concerns. Do not make any false statement about residence or address, but there is no need to turn a simple acceleration request into a full service-law submission unless your lawyer decides the address issue must be proactively addressed.

**Bottom line:** keep EPU moving, file the criminal complaint in parallel if the fraud evidence is there, resist suspension, and treat civil enforcement as the main recovery route while using the criminal track for evidence, pressure, security, and insolvency/non-dischargeability leverage.
