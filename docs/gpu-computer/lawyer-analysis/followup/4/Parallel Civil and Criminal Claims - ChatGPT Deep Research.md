# Parallel civil and criminal tracks for the same 155,000 PLN claim

## Bottom line

**Can both proceed simultaneously? Yes.** Polish law does not force you to choose between a pending civil payment claim and a criminal complaint based on the same facts. Your proposed fraud complaint would run under the criminal process for art. 286 § 1 KK, while the EPU remains a civil money-claim procedure; the interaction between them is governed mainly by the civil court’s discretionary suspension power in art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC, the binding effect of a final criminal conviction in art. 11 KPC, and the criminal court’s procedural autonomy in art. 8 KPK. citeturn32view0turn40view0turn34view0turn39view0

**Will the EPU/civil case automatically be suspended if you file the criminal complaint? No.** Suspension under art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC is facultative, not mandatory. The Supreme Court has repeatedly said courts should avoid hasty suspension; a mere allegation that the other side committed a crime is not enough. At the present EPU stage, the risk is lower still, because EPU is designed for straightforward fixed-sum claims and ordinarily proceeds without evidentiary hearings. citeturn40view0turn34view1turn34view2turn20view0

**Can you “win” both tracks? Yes, in the sense that you could obtain a civil title and also obtain a criminal conviction. Can you collect the same 155,000 PLN twice? No.** In your current posture, the more important point is that the already-pending EPU will ordinarily block the criminal court from imposing a second full compensatory order under art. 46 KK for that same claim, because art. 415 § 1 KPK forbids criminal compensation where the same claim is already pending elsewhere or has already been finally adjudicated. Even if overlapping titles somehow existed, the debt could still be satisfied only once. citeturn8view0turn8view1turn36view1turn41search8turn41search14

**What is the best sequence?** On these facts, I would keep the EPU alive and file the criminal complaint now if your goals are asset tracing, possible criminal freezing measures, and pressure. Filing now can matter because criminal procedure allows early property-securing measures; waiting until after a civil title does not improve your chances of getting a second collectible art. 46 order, because the pending civil claim already works against that outcome. citeturn13view0turn13view1turn8view0

**Is a wniosek o przyspieszenie worth filing? Yes, but only as a low-cost nudge.** The statutory remedy with real force is the skarga na przewlekłość during the pending case; an informal acceleration request is not the same thing. citeturn16view0turn16view1turn16view2

## Simultaneous proceedings

You can have an open EPU/civil claim and a criminal complaint over the same facts at the same time. Nothing in the current KPC or KPK creates a general election-of-remedies bar between those tracks. The criminal court remains autonomous under art. 8 KPK, and the civil court remains competent to decide the monetary claim unless it decides, exceptionally, to suspend under art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC. Fraud under art. 286 § 1 KK is a standard public-prosecution offense; the existence of a civil case does not immunize the conduct from criminal scrutiny. citeturn39view0turn40view0turn32view0

For your facts, that means the already-filed EPU in case **Nc-e 552126/26** can continue while you submit a zawiadomienie regarding the same non-payment / delivery facts. The important caveat is not whether both tracks may exist, but what each track can still give you once the other is pending. The criminal complaint can still lead to investigation, coercive measures, and—if the evidence supports it—a conviction. What it will not usually give you, once the EPU is already pending over the same 155,000 PLN against the same person, is a second full victim-compensation title for that same debt. citeturn13view0turn24view0turn8view0

That identity point matters here because a CEIDG sole proprietorship is not a separate legal person from the entrepreneur. Under the Entrepreneurs’ Law, the entrepreneur may be a natural person, and CEIDG is the register for persons running sole proprietorships. So suing “Mateusz Szklarski / JDG GPUcomputer” and accusing Mateusz as a natural person are, for anti-cumulation purposes, ordinarily the same debtor, not two different ones. citeturn38search5turn38search19

## Suspension risk

Art. 177 § 1 pkt 4 KPC says the court **may** suspend proceedings ex officio if a revealed act, to be determined in criminal or disciplinary proceedings, could affect the civil outcome. The statutory verb is permissive, not mandatory. Supreme Court case law is consistent that suspension on this ground is not automatic and depends on whether the criminal findings are genuinely prejudicial to the civil case. The court should consider practicality and fairness, including the expected length of the criminal case, the probability of a conviction, the stage of the civil case, and the importance of a quick civil decision for the claimant. citeturn40view0turn34view0turn34view1turn34view2

That also answers the “who asks for it?” question. Either side can try to push the issue, but the request itself does not control the outcome. Because art. 177 § 1 KPC is framed as a power of the court, a debtor cannot force suspension merely by filing a stalling motion and saying “there is a criminal case now.” The Supreme Court’s line is that the court should not suspend just because one party accuses the other of criminal conduct. citeturn40view0turn34view1turn34view2

At the **pure EPU stage**, suspension risk is usually lower as a practical matter. EPU is a streamlined, nationwide order-for-payment procedure handled by a single specialized e-court; it is intended for fixed monetary claims where the claimant identifies supporting documents but does not attach them, and the process ordinarily does not involve the kind of evidentiary hearing in which the court might feel dependent on a criminal file. That does not make suspension impossible, but it makes it less likely than in a later ordinary trial centered on disputed testimony and intent. This is an inference from the structure of EPU, not an absolute rule. citeturn20view0turn34view1turn34view2

One important correction to your question: under the **current** version of EPU, if the defendant files a sprzeciw, the case does **not** “convert” into ordinary proceedings in the same file. The EPU court **umarza** the proceeding in the scope in which the nakaz loses force, and you then have **three months** to bring an ordinary lawsuit while preserving the legal effects of the original EPU filing date. So the real question is not whether “converted ordinary proceedings” become easier to suspend, but whether a **new ordinary action filed after EPU umorzenie** would face a higher suspension risk. The answer is yes—somewhat—because an ordinary court is more likely than the EPU court to see a developed criminal file as useful in a factually contested fraud dispute. But even there the suspension remains discretionary, not automatic. citeturn31view0turn31view1turn22view0turn34view1

The interaction works more strongly in one direction than the other. A final **criminal conviction** binds the civil court under art. 11 KPC as to the commission of the offense. By contrast, a criminal **acquittal** does not bind the civil court in the same way, and the civil court may still independently conclude that the underlying conduct creates civil liability. The mirror image does not exist on the criminal side either: under art. 8 KPK, the criminal court is generally not bound by an earlier civil payment judgment, except for truly constitutive judgments shaping a legal status or relationship. A civil judgment therefore helps the criminal case evidentially, but not as a binding prejudicate on fraud. citeturn34view0turn23search0turn23search6turn23search9turn39view0turn30search0turn30search3

## Double recovery and overlapping titles

Art. 46 § 1 KK allows the criminal court to order repair of the damage caused by the offense, applying civil-law rules. Standing alone, that could suggest coexistence with a civil title. But art. 415 § 1 KPK contains a strong anti-cumulative clause: the criminal court does **not** order a victim-focused nawiązka, an obligation to repair damage, or zadośćuczynienie if the claim arising from the offense is already the subject of another proceeding or has already been finally adjudicated. Supreme Court decisions describe this as a categorical prohibition on multiplying enforcement titles for the same claim. citeturn24view0turn8view0turn8view1

That rule is especially important in a case like yours because the EPU claim is a money claim that may be framed civilly as a payment obligation, while the criminal complaint would frame the same factual loss as damage from fraud. The Supreme Court has expressly held that the anti-cumulation issue does **not** depend only on whether the two proceedings use the same legal label. Even when the civil decision rested on a contractual basis, the Court still treated it as the “same claim” where both claims arose from the same factual event and involved the same debtor-creditor identity. That is the load-bearing reason why your already-pending EPU normally blocks a second full art. 46 order for the same 155,000 PLN against Mateusz. citeturn8view0

In your specific posture, then, the likely answer is this: **you can have both tracks running, but you should not expect to hold both a final civil title and a final criminal compensation order for the same 155,000 PLN against the same Mateusz/JDG.** The criminal court should ordinarily decline the art. 46 remedy once it sees that the same claim is already pending in EPU, and the same bar would apply even more clearly if the EPU later ends with a final civil nakaz or judgment. citeturn8view0turn8view1turn36view1turn38search5turn38search19

If, however, a criminal court does impose an art. 46 obligation—whether because the civil track did not yet exist, or because only part of the loss was addressed—the criminal order is a real enforcement instrument. Under art. 107 KPK the court grants it a clause of enforceability on request, and art. 196 KKW treats it as a tytuł egzekucyjny for the victim. The Supreme Court has also stressed that a criminal art. 46 order has a genuinely compensatory, civil-equivalent character. citeturn28view0turn26view2turn36view1

But even then, the collection logic remains **single satisfaction, not double payment**. The law permits pursuit of the **unpaid remainder**, not duplicate recovery of the same sum. That is why art. 46 § 3 KK allows pursuit in civil court only for the **uncompensated part** of the claim, and why the Supreme Court frames the regime as avoiding multiple titles for the same claim. Payment on one title extinguishes the underlying obligation to that extent. If two overlapping titles somehow existed anyway, the second could be used only for any unpaid balance; once the debt is satisfied, the debtor could rely on the extinguishment of the obligation and seek to defeat further enforcement under art. 840 § 1 pkt 2 KPC. citeturn24view0turn8view0turn36view0turn37view1turn41search8turn41search14turn41search11

So the practical advantage of the criminal route is **not** “another 155,000 PLN.” Its real advantages are elsewhere: criminal investigative tools, the potential for property security, leverage on the debtor, and—if there is a conviction—the binding effect of that conviction in the civil case under art. 11 KPC. citeturn13view0turn13view1turn34view0

## Recommended sequencing

Given the facts you gave, I would **keep the EPU alive** and **file the criminal complaint now**, not later. There is no rule requiring you to withdraw the EPU before reporting fraud, and filing now preserves the possibility of criminal investigative action while the civil track continues. Criminal procedure allows property-securing measures once the statutory conditions are met, including police temporary seizure where there is a risk of removal and prosecutorial/court property security where there is a justified fear that later execution would become impossible or much harder. Waiting until after a civil title may simply burn time that could have been used to trace and secure assets. citeturn13view0turn13view1turn39view0turn40view0

I would **not** withdraw the EPU solely in order to restart civilly for a freeze. Civil security measures exist in general civil procedure if the claim and legal interest are shown, but that is a different move and does not require abandoning the criminal option. Withdrawal would surrender an already-filed payment-order path without solving your anti-double-recovery issue later. In other words, withdrawal does not buy you a lawful second bite at the same 155,000 PLN; it only trades one existing track for another. citeturn12view2turn12view3turn8view0

The one thing I would watch very closely is the **foreign-address problem**. Your lawyer’s “fiction of service” point is legally real in EPU, but it is narrower than people sometimes assume. EPU expressly says no nakaz may be issued if service to the defendant would have to occur **outside Poland**. EPU also has its own special service fiction: when the defendant does not collect the nakaz after repeated notice, the nakaz is deemed served only if the address used matches PESEL and the other statutory conditions are met. If, after issuance, it turns out that the nakaz cannot actually be served **in Poland**, the court must vacate the nakaz and dismiss the EPU unless you cure the obstacle in time. So the law supports your lawyer’s service advantage only **if** the address used truly fits the in-country EPU service regime. citeturn21view0turn22view0

That leads to the best tactical sequence on your exact facts as of **3 June 2026**. Let the EPU continue to its next procedural step. File the criminal complaint now, with a clean chronology and documents, and expressly ask to be recognized as the pokrzywdzony and for consideration of property-securing measures. If the EPU produces a final, unopposed nakaz or later a final judgment, move for clause and enforcement immediately. If the EPU dies because of a sprzeciw or because the court concludes service cannot be effected domestically, bring the ordinary suit within the statutory three-month period to preserve the original filing-date effects. citeturn31view0turn31view1turn13view0turn13view1

That civil title also has a meaningful structural advantage with a Netherlands-based debtor: a Polish civil judgment sits on the standard EU recognition-and-enforcement framework for civil and commercial matters under Brussels I bis. So even though the criminal track may create pressure and perhaps freezing, the civil title remains your clearest orthodox money-enforcement instrument for a debtor located in another Member State. citeturn29search0turn29search3

## Whether to seek acceleration in EPU

A **wniosek o przyspieszenie rozpoznania sprawy** is worth filing because it is cheap, easy, and may help put eyes back on the file. But it is not a statutory fast-track device. The formal instrument with real legal consequences is the **skarga na naruszenie prawa strony do rozpoznania sprawy bez nieuzasadnionej zwłoki**, which must be filed while the case is still pending and can lead to a finding of delay, directions to take action, and a monetary award. The informal acceleration request does not substitute for that remedy. citeturn16view0turn16view1turn16view2

The practical reason to keep expectations modest is that EPU is handled by one nationwide specialized court. The Ministry of Justice describes it as a single e-court for all of Poland and notes the very large scale of EPU usage. In that setting, an informal expedition request can help at the margin, but it is not something the court is legally obliged to honor ahead of other files. citeturn20view0

On your dates—EPU filed **8 April 2026**, still no nakaz by late May / early June 2026—I would treat the acceleration request as proportionate and sensible, but I would not yet assume that a formal skarga na przewlekłość is the best first move on this record alone. My practical sequence would be: file the acceleration request now, monitor the EPU closely, and keep the skarga remedy in reserve if the inactivity becomes materially longer and harder to justify. citeturn16view0turn16view1turn20view0

A useful acceleration request should be short and factual. It should identify the case number, filing date, the fact that the claim is a fixed monetary claim already filed in EPU, the fact that the defendant is abroad so delay increases enforcement risk, and a polite request for prompt examination or for issuance of the next procedural decision. A workable English rendering of the substance would read like this:

> In case Nc-e 552126/26, filed on 8 April 2026, I respectfully request prompt examination of the statement of claim. The claim concerns a fixed monetary amount of 155,000 PLN plus statutory delay interest. As of 3 June 2026, no nakaz zapłaty has yet been issued. Because the defendant is located abroad, further delay increases enforcement risk. I therefore ask for prompt examination of the file and issuance of the appropriate next order.

That kind of letter is worth sending. I would just keep your expectations calibrated: it is a procedural nudge, not a legal lever on the level of a formal delay complaint. citeturn16view0turn16view1turn20view0
