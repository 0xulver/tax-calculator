# Research summary — The bankruptcy shield (art. 491²¹ PU)

> All five models agree on the core mechanism: a bare EPU judgment for 155k PLN is fully dischargeable in Szklarski's consumer bankruptcy, but a final fraud conviction under art. 286 § 1 KK changes that permanently — either via an art. 46 KK restitution order (which alone is a non-dischargeable, directly enforceable title, no civil judgment needed) or via the "damage from a crime established by a final ruling" limb that protects the EPU title itself. The conviction must be final before his discharge order becomes final, and the art. 415 § 1 KPK anti-cumulation rule means the EPU and art. 46 routes must be deliberately sequenced, not run blindly in parallel.

## Key findings (consensus)

- **Art. 491²¹ ust. 2 PU is a closed, strictly interpreted catalogue.** Polish law has **no general "intentional tort" exclusion** (unlike German or US law). The phrase "czyn niedozwolony" does not appear in the statute. The exclusions apply in every discharge variant — repayment plan, no-plan (art. 491¹⁶), and conditional — and operate ex lege; under ust. 3 such debts remain enforceable after discharge.
- **Two hooks for the 155k:** (A) a court-ordered duty to repair damage — a final **art. 46 § 1 KK order alone is sufficient**, enforceable via komornik after a klauzula wykonalności; (B) a civil/EPU title for damage from a crime **established by a final criminal ruling**. A civil judgment that merely describes intentional conduct is not a safe trigger — only a criminal court "establishes" a crime.
- **The anti-cumulation trap (art. 415 § 1 KPK):** if the same 155k claim is already pending or finally adjudicated in EPU, the criminal court cannot issue an art. 46 order. The shield then survives only via Hook B (conviction + civil title), which is solid but slightly more contestable.
- **Timing:** get the final conviction before the discharge order is final. While the plan runs, art. 491¹⁹ ust. 4 lets late-confirmed creditors seek plan modification. Reviving an already-discharged debt with a later conviction is legally uncertain — do not build the strategy on it.
- **Strategy:** all models say the criminal track is justified despite 40–60% dismissal risk, mainly because it destroys his bankruptcy endgame and inverts his settlement incentive before the verdict becomes final.

## Where the models disagree or add unique points

- **Limitation period for an art. 46 title:** *ChatGPT 5.5 Pro* and *Perplexity Computer* apply the 10-year criminal execution limit (art. 103 KK); *ChatGPT Deep Research* cites Supreme Court authority that civil limitation rules (6 years, renewable) govern; the *Gemini PDF* claims 20 years under art. 442¹ § 2 KC. Unresolved — a lawyer question.
- **Post-discharge conviction:** the *Gemini PDF* uniquely claims a later conviction makes the discharge "wholly ineffective" for that debt; both ChatGPT files and Perplexity treat this as unsupported. Treat Gemini as optimistic outlier.
- **Dutch angle:** the *Gemini PDF* adds genuinely unique value — art. 358 lid 4 Faillissementswet excludes injured-party criminal claims (and post-conviction civil damages awards) from the Dutch schone lei, with Dutch case law cited. *Perplexity Computer*'s claim that an art. 46 title is "COMI-proof" because criminal matters fall outside the EIR is plausible but unverified.

## Caveats / open questions

- Limitation regime for art. 46 titles (6 vs 10 vs 20 years) is contested across models.
- Whether a post-discharge conviction revives the debt is unsettled.
- A genuine Dutch COMI shift means Dutch law governs discharge; the Gemini Dutch-law analysis needs verification by Dutch counsel.
- None of this matters without a conviction — the prosecutor-dismissal risk (40–60%) remains the gating factor.
