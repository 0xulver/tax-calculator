## 1. Statute-grounded map: how the consumer-bankruptcy discharge works

A deregistered JDG owner can fall into the consumer-bankruptcy title once he is no longer a person whose bankruptcy is handled under the entrepreneur provisions. The consumer title applies to natural persons whose bankruptcy cannot be declared under the general entrepreneur regime. ([Isap][1])

In consumer bankruptcy, the court usually ends with one of three discharge routes: a repayment plan, immediate discharge without a plan if the debtor is permanently unable to pay anything, or conditional no-plan discharge for five years if the inability is not permanent. The repayment plan normally lasts up to 36 months, but if the debtor intentionally or by gross negligence caused or materially worsened insolvency, the plan must be between 36 and 84 months. ([Isap][1])

The key provision is **art. 491²¹ PU**. After the debtor performs the repayment plan, the court issues an order confirming performance and discharging pre-bankruptcy debts unpaid through the plan. But art. 491²¹(2) lists debts that are **not discharged**. Current text:

> **“Nie podlegają umorzeniu zobowiązania o charakterze alimentacyjnym, zobowiązania wynikające z rent z tytułu odszkodowania za wywołanie choroby, niezdolności do pracy, kalectwa lub śmierci, zobowiązania do zapłaty orzeczonych przez sąd kar grzywny, a także do wykonania obowiązku naprawienia szkody oraz zadośćuczynienia za doznaną krzywdę, zobowiązania do zapłaty nawiązki lub świadczenia pieniężnego orzeczonych przez sąd jako środek karny lub środek związany z poddaniem sprawcy próbie, jak również zobowiązania do naprawienia szkody wynikającej z przestępstwa lub wykroczenia stwierdzonego prawomocnym orzeczeniem oraz zobowiązania, których upadły umyślnie nie ujawnił, jeżeli wierzyciel nie brał udziału w postępowaniu.”** ([Isap][2])

Two operational consequences follow. First, during the repayment plan, enforcement of ordinary pre-plan debts is barred, but **not** enforcement of debts falling under art. 491²¹(2). Second, after the final discharge order, enforcement of discharged pre-bankruptcy debts is barred, again **except** for art. 491²¹(2) debts. ([Isap][1])

Do not rely on the shorthand “intentional tort debts are non-dischargeable” unless you tie it to the actual statutory wording. The current art. 491²¹(2) does **not** contain a broad general exclusion for every civil “czyn niedozwolony popełniony umyślnie.” The safer and better reading is narrower: ordinary delict claims are dischargeable unless they are either covered by a criminal/probationary compensatory order, or are damages resulting from a crime or petty offence established by a final ruling. Commentary on art. 491²¹ makes the same distinction: ordinary delict claims are generally not excluded unless the delict is also a crime/offence confirmed by final criminal adjudication. 

For your 155,000 PLN claim, that distinction matters. A normal EPU payment order for “zapłata” gives you a title, but by itself it is likely an ordinary pre-bankruptcy monetary debt. It becomes bankruptcy-resistant only if you can anchor it to one of the art. 491²¹(2) categories: especially **art. 46 KK** or a final ruling that the loss resulted from **art. 286 § 1 KK fraud**.

## 2. What triggers the shield

A criminal complaint alone does not trigger the shield. An investigation, charge, indictment, or non-final conviction is not enough for the “przestępstwo … stwierdzone prawomocnym orzeczeniem” limb. The clean trigger is a **prawomocny wyrok skazujący** or another final criminal ruling that establishes the offence and imposes or supports the compensatory obligation.

Art. 286 § 1 KK fraud is an intentional offence in this context. The Criminal Code says a misdemeanour may be committed unintentionally only if the statute so provides, and art. 286 § 1 itself requires acting “w celu osiągnięcia korzyści majątkowej” by misleading or exploiting error to induce disadvantageous property disposition. ([Isap][3])

There are two main ways your claim becomes shielded:

**Route A — direct criminal compensatory title.** In the fraud judgment, the criminal court orders him under **art. 46 § 1 KK** to repair your damage, in whole or part, or awards compensation. Art. 46 says that in the event of conviction the court may order this, and on the victim’s motion must order it, applying civil-law rules; if ordering the obligation is significantly difficult, the court may instead award a **nawiązka** up to 200,000 PLN, and an art. 46 order does not block a civil case for the unsatisfied remainder. ([Isap][3])

**Route B — civil title plus final criminal finding.** Even if the criminal court does not order art. 46 because you already have or are pursuing an EPU/civil title, the civil debt can still fall under the later art. 491²¹(2) limb: “zobowiązania do naprawienia szkody wynikającej z przestępstwa … stwierdzonego prawomocnym orzeczeniem.” Commentary treats this as covering a civil damages obligation whose source is a crime/offence finally established by the criminal court. 

A civil judgment that merely says the conduct was intentional or tortious is **not** the same thing. A civil court may make factual findings, but it does not “establish” the commission of a crime in the criminal-law sense for this provision. The safer position is that the crime/offence must be established by a final criminal judgment or equivalent criminal/offence ruling. 

One important sequencing warning: if your EPU/civil claim for the same 155,000 PLN is already pending or finally adjudicated, the criminal court may be barred from ordering the same art. 46/nawiązka relief because KPK art. 415 § 1 says such compensatory orders are not made if the claim arising from the offence is the subject of another proceeding or has already been finally adjudicated. That does **not** destroy the bankruptcy shield route; it just shifts you from Route A to Route B. ([Isap][4])

## 3. Does art. 46 KK alone make the 155k non-dischargeable?

**Yes — if it is imposed in a final criminal judgment.**

A **prawomocny** art. 46 KK order requiring him to pay you the 155,000 PLN is itself within art. 491²¹(2): it is an obligation to perform a court-ordered duty to repair damage / compensate harm, and it also arises from a crime established by the final conviction. You do **not** need a separate civil judgment for that amount.

It is also enforceable as a money title. KPK art. 107 provides that the court or referendarz grants an enforcement clause to a ruling enforceable by execution, and expressly treats rulings imposing damage repair, compensation, or a nawiązka for the victim as rulings on property claims if they are suitable for civil enforcement. ([Isap][4])

The nuance is limitation. An art. 46 compensatory measure has a criminal-law execution limitation: KK art. 103 applies a 10-year period to compensatory measures. So art. 46 alone is bankruptcy-proof, but you should not treat it as automatically “life-long” unless enforcement is actively managed. A civil judgment tied to the criminal finding may be more renewable because civil limitation can be interrupted and restarted. ([Isap][3])

Best practical formulation: **art. 46 alone gives you a non-dischargeable and enforceable criminal-civil title; for maximum lifetime leverage, pair the final fraud finding with a civil/EPU title or preserve civil enforcement for any remainder.**

## 4. Timing: conviction before or after bankruptcy/discharge

You should aim to have the final conviction or art. 46 order **before** the consumer-bankruptcy discharge order becomes final.

If the conviction/order exists before the discharge decision, the bankruptcy court has a straightforward statutory basis to say: this debt is not discharged under art. 491²¹(2). If the title or final ruling comes after the repayment plan is set but before final discharge, art. 491¹⁹(4) gives a route for creditors whose pre-plan claims are later confirmed by final judgment, settlement, or final decision to seek modification of the plan. ([Isap][1])

After a final discharge has already become legally effective, the position is much less safe. Polish bankruptcy law does not give a simple general mechanism saying “a later fraud conviction automatically reopens a completed discharge.” A later art. 46 order may give arguments because it is a criminal compensatory order, but that is not the situation you want to rely on. The safe strategy is to appear in the bankruptcy, notify the trustee/court of the criminal track, object to discharge of your claim, and push for the criminal finding before the art. 491²¹ discharge order becomes final.

The last clause of art. 491²¹(2) gives a separate protection if the debtor **intentionally fails to disclose** your claim and you did not participate in the bankruptcy. But if he lists you and you participate, that omission-based exception is unavailable; you need the crime/art. 46 route. ([Isap][2])

## 5. Practical effect if the shield applies

If the shield applies, the 155,000 PLN is not wiped out by Polish consumer bankruptcy. It survives the repayment plan/no-plan discharge and remains enforceable against future income and assets, subject to limitation rules. Art. 491²¹(3) expressly preserves enforcement for claims arising from the non-dischargeable categories. ([Isap][2])

For a civil/EPU judgment, the core limitation mechanics are:

A property claim confirmed by a final court judgment or equivalent final ruling generally prescribes after **six years**, while future periodic payments, including future interest, prescribe after **three years**. Limitation is interrupted by acts before a court or enforcement authority undertaken directly to pursue, establish, satisfy, or secure the claim; after interruption, it runs anew, and if interrupted by proceedings it does not run anew until those proceedings end. ([Isap][5])

So “permanent” means **not dischargeable in bankruptcy**, not “no limitation ever.” With a civil title, you keep it alive by timely enforcement/interruption. With an art. 46 title, treat the 10-year criminal execution period as a hard operational deadline and enforce early. ([Isap][3])

## 6. Netherlands / COMI angle

If the relevant insolvency proceeding is Polish, the Dutch side should respect the Polish insolvency effect. Under EU Insolvency Regulation 2015/848, main insolvency jurisdiction is based on the debtor’s centre of main interests, and the law of the state opening the proceeding governs the proceeding and its effects, including creditors’ rights after closure. Judgments opening main proceedings are recognised automatically in other Member States, and they produce the same effects there as under the law of the state of opening. ([EUR-Lex][6])

That means: if Poland opens the main bankruptcy and Polish law says your art. 46/crime-based debt is **not discharged**, a Dutch court should not treat a Polish discharge as wiping out that debt. Insolvency closure judgments are also recognised without further formalities under the Regulation. ([EUR-Lex][6])

Different issue: if he genuinely relocates COMI and opens a Dutch main insolvency/debt-relief proceeding, Dutch insolvency law becomes the lex concursus. Polish art. 491²¹ would not automatically govern a Dutch discharge. The Polish fraud conviction/art. 46 order would still be powerful evidence/title material, but Dutch counsel should check the Dutch discharge exceptions and enforcement route.

## 7. Strategic value

The criminal track is worth serious pursuit if the evidence supports fraudulent intent at the time of contracting or taking the money. The upside is asymmetric: an ordinary EPU judgment may be neutralised by consumer bankruptcy, but a final art. 286 conviction plus art. 46 order, or a civil title tied to a final fraud finding, can convert the 155,000 PLN into a bankruptcy-surviving claim.

Even if the prosecutor-dismissal risk is 40–60%, the value is not just direct recovery from the criminal case. The value is eliminating his best endgame: “get civil judgment against me, then I file consumer bankruptcy and discharge it.” Once a final fraud finding/art. 46 order is realistically on the table, his rational settlement incentive increases because post-conviction bankruptcy no longer cleans the debt. Non-payment of a court-ordered compensatory measure can also create further criminal exposure under KK art. 244c if he evades performance of such a measure. ([Isap][3])

The honest caveat: prosecutors often try to classify commercial non-payment as a civil dispute unless the file shows deception, pre-existing inability or lack of intent to perform, false statements about stock/procurement/refunds, concealment, asset diversion, or a pattern of similar victims. The criminal complaint must be evidence-led, not framed as debt collection.

## 8. Failure to file / future filing

His past failure to file bankruptcy does **not** by itself make your 155,000 PLN non-dischargeable. It may support other levers, including plan refusal, longer repayment-plan treatment, or sanctions/ban arguments under the provisions you flagged for prompt 9, but the art. 491²¹ shield still depends on the debt falling into one of the statutory exceptions.

A future consumer-bankruptcy filing changes the urgency, not the legal test. If he files before you have the conviction/art. 46 order, you must participate, file the claim, notify the trustee/court of the criminal proceedings, and object to any discharge of the claim. If you already have the final art. 286/art. 46 result, the filing is much less threatening because the debt should survive the bankruptcy.
