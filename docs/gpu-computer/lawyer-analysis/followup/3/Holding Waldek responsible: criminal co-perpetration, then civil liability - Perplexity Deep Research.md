I. CRIMINAL CO-PERPETRATION — Art. 18 § 1 KK
The Core Theory: Waldek as Współsprawca or Sprawca Kierowniczy
Art. 286 § 1 KK criminalises deliberately leading another person to an unfavourable disposition of property through wprowadzenie w błąd (deception), wyzyskanie błędu (exploiting an error), or exploiting incapacity — done in order to obtain a financial benefit. The offence carries 6 months to 8 years' imprisonment.

The gateway to Waldek's criminal exposure is art. 18 § 1 KK, which recognises three forms of perpetratorship beyond the solo actor: the współsprawca (co-perpetrator who executes the crime jointly), the sprawca kierowniczy (directing perpetrator who does not personally execute but controls the act), and the sprawca polecający (ordering perpetrator who commands a subordinate). Polish Supreme Court (Sąd Najwyższy) doctrine applies a material-objective test for współsprawstwo: three elements must coincide — (1) explicit or tacit porozumienie (agreement) reached before or during the offence, (2) joint execution, and (3) the contributor's input being essential (istotny wkład) to completion of the crime. Importantly, the SN has consistently held that a co-perpetrator need not personally fulfil every znamię (element of the offence) — it is enough that they act with animus auctoris (the intent to be an author of the crime) and that their contribution, viewed as a whole, is constitutive.

For sprawstwo kierownicze specifically, the person need not set foot in the execution phase at all: the SN treats as a directing perpetrator someone who "conceives the criminal plan, designates targets, prepares the plan, recruits executors, provides tools, assigns roles, and specifies time and place". The formal figurehead (Mateusz's registered JDG) becomes merely the tool — the invoice-and-bank-account apparatus — through which Waldek's actual scheme operates.

III. THE CIVIL BRIDGE — HOW CRIMINAL FINDINGS BECOME CIVIL RECOVERY
This is the crux of your lawyer's concern. Here is the precise mechanism by which criminal findings unlock Waldek as a civil defendant.

Step 1: Art. 46 KK — Restitution in the Criminal Proceedings Themselves
Art. 46 § 1 KK provides that upon conviction, the court must (upon the victim's request, which you lodge under art. 49a KPK) order the convicted person to repair the damage caused by the crime, applying civil law rules. Critically, where there are multiple convicted co-perpetrators, Polish criminal courts apply the civil law of joint tortfeasors — meaning the art. 46 KK obligation can be imposed solidarnie (jointly and severally) on all convicted co-perpetrators. You do not need a separate civil action at all if both Mateusz and Waldek are convicted. File the art. 49a KPK request within the window the prosecutor will notify you of.

Practical action: Include in your zawiadomienie an express statement that you will request a środek kompensacyjny under art. 46 § 1 KK against all co-perpetrators.

Step 2: Art. 11 KPC — The Prejudykat Mechanism
Art. 11 KPC provides the civil law bridge your lawyer was referring to. A legally binding (prawomocny) conviction in criminal proceedings binds the civil court as to the commission of the crime — the civil court cannot re-open factual findings about whether the crime occurred and who committed it. This is the prejudykat effect. Specifically:

The civil court must accept that Waldek committed the offence (if he is convicted)

The civil court may make additional findings beyond what the criminal court established — e.g., that his culpability was higher than the criminal court found, since the civil standard of proof is lower and in dubio pro reo does not apply

The civil court cannot contradict the criminal finding — so an acquittal of Waldek in the criminal proceedings would not automatically bar a civil claim (you could still try to prove a tort), but it weakens it substantially

The practical sequence is therefore: criminal conviction of Waldek → art. 11 KPC locks in the fact of the delict → civil action against Waldek under art. 415 KC (general tort) proceeds without re-litigating the fraudulent scheme.

Step 3: Art. 422 KC + Art. 441 § 1 KC — Solidary Tort Liability
Art. 422 KC extends tort liability beyond the direct perpetrator to three categories: (1) a person who induced (nakłonił) the tortfeasor, (2) a person who aided (był pomocny), and (3) a person who knowingly benefited (świadomie skorzystał) from the damage. The third category is directly in play if funds flowed to Waldek.

However, the SN's uchwała of 21.12.2017 (III CZP 89/17) requires positive knowledge, not merely negligent ignorance, for the "knowingly benefited" prong. This is your main evidentiary challenge pre-conviction: you must prove not just that Waldek received a benefit, but that he knew the source was your defrauded payment. Given his operational control of the business, this inference is strong, but a civil court without a criminal precedent would scrutinise it closely.

Art. 441 § 1 KC then provides that once multiple persons are liable in tort, they are solidarnie (jointly and severally) liable for the full 155,000 PLN. You could sue Waldek and Mateusz jointly in civil proceedings, and either of them would owe the full amount — critically bypassing the privity problem that prevents a direct contractual claim against Waldek.

The bridge therefore looks like this:

Criminal conviction of Waldek (co-perpetrator) → art. 11 KPC prejudykat → art. 415 KC (general tort) + art. 441 § 1 KC (solidary liability) → Full 155,000 PLN judgment against Waldek personally

V. CIVIL THEORIES AGAINST WALDEK WITHOUT A CRIMINAL CONVICTION
Your lawyer is correct that contract privity blocks a breach-of-contract claim against Waldek. Here is an honest ranking of the non-criminal civil routes:

1. Art. 415 KC — General Tort (Delikt) — Moderate Strength
Art. 415 KC imposes liability on any person who causes damage to another through a culpable act. A civil claimant can independently prove that Waldek committed an act of deceit (podstęp) — a category of culpable act in Polish tort law — by personally making false representations in the emails and contradictory accounts. No criminal conviction is required. The difficulty is the evidentiary standard: in a civil action you bear the burden of proving Waldek's wina umyślna (intentional fault) and the causal link between his statements and your 155,000 PLN loss. The dual-mailbox control, repeated personal representations, and contradictory explanations are sufficient factual foundation, but a civil court without the prejudykat benefit will scrutinise this carefully. Realistic but not guaranteed pre-conviction.

2. Firmanctwo / Art. 55 § 1 KKS (Tax Penal Code) — Moderate Strength, Unusual Route
Firmanctwo is the practice of conducting business under another person's name (the "figurant") while being the real economic actor. Art. 55 § 1 KKS criminalises it as a tax offence. In the civil dimension, the "hidden" operator (Waldek as the real trader) bears commercial liability as if he were the registered trader: courts have applied art. 24 KC analogy and art. 55 KKS to pierce the nominal registration and hold the firmant (the hidden operator) liable to third parties who dealt in good faith with the nominal firm. This is a strong supporting argument in your civil case: you can argue that Waldek, as the firmant, is estopped from denying liability because the external appearance he created (branded emails from gpucomputer.pl, Wayback-documented 3dkrakow co-branding, personal correspondence as representative) reasonably led you to believe you were contracting with him or his entity. This is best deployed alongside the art. 415 KC claim rather than standalone.

3. Art. 422 KC (Knowing Benefit) — Weak Pre-Conviction, Strong Post-Conviction
As analysed above, the SN (uchwała III CZP 89/17) requires positive knowledge of benefiting from a tort. Pre-conviction, you would need to plead and prove: (a) Waldek received a financial benefit, and (b) he knew that benefit derived from the fraud on you. Without bank records (which you do not yet have), establishing (a) is speculative. And without the art. 11 KPC prejudykat, (b) is an uphill inference. Use this as a secondary/alternative cause of action, not your primary civil theory.

4. Art. 405 KC — Unjust Enrichment — Weak
Art. 405 KC applies where a person obtains a financial benefit at another's expense without legal basis. For Waldek, the problem is the same as with art. 422: you need to show the 155,000 PLN (or a portion of it) actually moved to Waldek. Under current doctrine, unjust enrichment claims must trace a direct transfer — your money went to Mateusz's JDG bank account, not directly to Waldek. You would need to demonstrate a subsequent transfer. This becomes viable only if bank records obtained in the criminal investigation reveal fund flows to Waldek.
