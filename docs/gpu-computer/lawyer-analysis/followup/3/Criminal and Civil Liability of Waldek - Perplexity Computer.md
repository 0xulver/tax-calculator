Part I — Criminal co-perpetration theory against Waldek
The core insight that defeats the "but the invoice was Mateusz's" objection
The decisive doctrinal point: art. 286 § 1 KK fraud is an ogólnosprawcze (general-subject) offence — anyone can commit it, and you do not need to personally realise every statutory element to be a perpetrator. Polish courts have repeatedly held that for współsprawstwo in fraud "nie jest konieczne, by każdy z współdziałających osobiście realizował wszystkie znamiona zarzucanego czynu (…), wystarczy, że osoba taka działa w ramach uzgodnionego podziału ról, umożliwiając innemu sprawcy wykonanie czynu" — it is enough that the person acts within an agreed division of roles, enabling the other to complete the act. The same source notes courts have specifically applied this to art. 286 fraud, holding that a co-perpetrator "nie musi sam działać na szkodę zindywidualizowanego pokrzywdzonego" — it suffices that he acts with intent to bring another to a disadvantageous disposition of property by inducing error, in order to obtain a material benefit.

So the fact that the invoice (proforma 2/01-2026) and bank account were Mateusz's does not shield Waldek. Whose name is on the paper is one element among several; the deception (wprowadzenie w błąd) is a separate element, and the evidence points to Waldek as the person who actually performed it.

art. 18 § 1 KK — współsprawstwo and sprawstwo kierownicze
The statute: "Odpowiada za sprawstwo nie tylko ten, kto wykonuje czyn zabroniony sam albo wspólnie i w porozumieniu z inną osobą, ale także ten, kto kieruje wykonaniem czynu zabronionego przez inną osobę lub wykorzystując uzależnienie innej osoby od siebie, poleca jej wykonanie takiego czynu". This single paragraph contains four forms: sole, co-perpetration (współsprawstwo), directing (kierownicze), and ordering (polecające).

Współsprawstwo (the primary theory). Two elements:

Element przedmiotowy (objective) — istotny wkład. Modern case law uses the material-objective theory: a co-perpetrator is anyone whose conduct constitutes a "konieczny lub bardzo istotny warunek" of the other's realisation of the offence — a necessary or very significant condition. The leading formulation: współsprawstwo exists where "wkład w popełnienie czynu zabronionego jest tak istotny, że bez niego (…) nie doszłoby do popełnienia czynu zabronionego w ustalony sposób" — the contribution is so essential that without it the offence would not have happened in the way it did. You do not need to have realised any znamię czasownikowe.

Element podmiotowy (subjective) — porozumienie. A shared understanding to commit the act together, with a division of roles. It need not be express: "może do niego dojść nawet w sposób dorozumiany" — it can arise impliedly. The co-perpetrators "muszą mieć świadomość wspólnego wykonania czynu zabronionego".

Why Waldek fits — "panowanie nad czynem": On the facts, Waldek is not a bystander. He conducted the dealings, controlled both mailboxes (the 13:10/13:11 near-identical refund texts from gpucomputer@ and waldek@ are direct evidence he commands the root account), personally made and repeated the refund promise, and gave the explanations — including the two mutually contradictory ones. This is the very "zorganizowanie i kierowanie akcją" that, at minimum, makes him a co-perpetrator and arguably a sprawca kierowniczy, whose essence is "panowanie nad czynem (…) i jego przewaga nad bezpośrednim wykonawcą" — control over the act and dominance over the direct executor.

The Mateusz-abroad fact is doctrinally powerful here. If Mateusz lives and works full-time in the Netherlands as a factory worker, the inference is that the operational conduct — the deception that induced your 155k transfer — was Waldek's, with Mateusz's JDG and bank account functioning as the narzędzie (instrument). That pattern fits sprawstwo kierownicze (Waldek runs the operation, Mateusz's firm is the vehicle) or, at minimum, co-perpetration with a clear division of roles.

One critical timing caveat for współsprawstwo. Co-perpetration covers conduct contemporaneous with realising the elements; conduct purely after the offence is complete is not współsprawstwo but at most pomocnictwo — or nothing. Fraud is a materialne offence completed at the moment of the disadvantageous disposition — i.e. when you transferred the 155k. So the conduct that matters for perpetration is what Waldek did before and up to your payment: the pre-payment correspondence and inducement. The post-payment refund promises and contradictory stories are not themselves the fraud act — but they are extremely valuable as circumstantial evidence of his role, control and intent, and they bear on a possible second theory (a separate deception to keep you from acting, or evidence of a stalling scheme). Frame the post-payment conduct as evidence of his dominion and intent, not as the actus reus itself.

art. 18 § 3 (pomocnictwo) and § 2 (podżeganie) — the fallbacks
Pomocnictwo (§ 3): "Odpowiada za pomocnictwo, kto w zamiarze, aby inna osoba dokonała czynu zabronionego, swoim zachowaniem ułatwia jego popełnienie". The threshold is lower than co-perpetration — merely facilitating — and crucially, for fraud it can be committed with zamiar ewentualny (dolus eventualis), not only direct intent. While art. 286 fraud by a perpetrator is a kierunkowe offence requiring direct intent, the SN held that for the pomocnictwo form "wystarczające jest wykazanie po stronie współdziałającego" either form of intent — including merely foreseeing and accepting the possibility. This is a useful fallback because the evidentiary bar on Waldek's mental state is easier. The distinction from co-perpetration: a pomocnik "uważa ów czyn za cudze przedsięwzięcie" — treats the act as someone else's enterprise and does not participate in it. On your facts, Waldek treats it as his enterprise (3dkrakow, his correspondence, his promises), which is why co-perpetration is the stronger fit and pomocnictwo the safety net.

Same timing trap: "Za nierealną należy uznać pomoc przy dokonaniu przestępstwa już dokonanego" — there is no such thing as assisting an already-completed offence; post-completion help is at most paserstwo (art. 291/292 KK), not pomocnictwo. So pomocnictwo, too, must rest on pre-payment facilitation.

Podżeganie (§ 2): "kto chcąc, aby inna osoba dokonała czynu zabronionego, nakłania ją do tego". Requires direct intent (chcąc) and an act of inducing Mateusz to commit the fraud. This is the weakest fit — you have no evidence that Waldek "talked Mateusz into" anything; the picture is joint/dominant operation, not instigation. Keep it only as a tertiary label for the prosecutor to consider.

art. 21 § 2 KK — individualised circumstances
The principle (art. 21 § 1): "Okoliczności osobiste, wyłączające lub łagodzące albo zaostrzające odpowiedzialność karną, uwzględnia się tylko co do osoby, której dotyczą". Personal circumstances attach only to the person they concern; § 2 then provides that where such a personal circumstance is itself a znamię of the offence, a co-actor is liable for that offence if he knew of the circumstance, even though it did not apply to him.

Why this matters for you: art. 286 § 1 KK is a general-subject crime with no personal-status element — there is no "VAT-registered entrepreneur" or "invoice-issuer" element built into fraud. So none of the things that are personal to Mateusz (the invoice being in his name, his being the JDG holder, his being the bank-account holder) are znamiona that bar Waldek's liability. The complementary rule of art. 20 KK reinforces this: "każdy ze współdziałających (…) odpowiada w granicach swojej umyślności lub nieumyślności, niezależnie od odpowiedzialności pozostałych" — each co-actor answers within the limits of his own intent, independent of the others. Practical upshot: art. 21 is not an obstacle to charging Waldek; it confirms that his liability is assessed on his conduct and his intent, regardless of Mateusz's formal status.

Direct answer to your italicised question
Yes — the fact that Waldek controlled the root mailbox, made the refund promises, and gave the false/contradictory explanations supports charging him as a perpetrator of the deception (wprowadzenie w błąd), independent of who issued the invoice. Issuing the invoice and deceiving the victim are distinct elements; case law squarely allows a co-perpetrator who realises the deception element (or even no verbal element at all, if his contribution is essential) to be a perpetrator of the whole fraud (Mozgawa, gov.pl; SO Białystok, VIII Ka 286/13). The one discipline to observe: anchor the deception in the pre-payment dealings, and use the post-payment material as proof of role and intent.

Part III — The civil bridge
This is the heart of your question, and the doctrine is favourable.

art. 422 KC + art. 441 § 1 KC — tort solidarity bypasses the privity problem
Your lawyer's "no basis to pursue Waldek civilly at this stage" is a contractual statement — correct, because your contract is with Mateusz's JDG. But tort (czyn niedozwolony) does not depend on contract. Delictual liability "powstaje niezależnie od jakiegokolwiek wcześniejszego stosunku między stronami (…) sprawca i poszkodowany mogą być dla siebie zupełnie obcymi osobami" — it arises independently of any prior relationship; perpetrator and victim can be complete strangers. That is exactly the wall-bypass you asked about.

art. 422 KC makes liable not only the direct wrongdoer but "ten kto do wyrządzenia szkody nakłonił inną osobę albo był jej pomocny lub też świadomie skorzystał z wyrządzonej drugiemu szkody" — the instigator, the helper, and the one who knowingly benefited from the harm. Critically, the same source confirms the cross-over: the circle of those civilly co-responsible "należy zaliczyć, zarówno przypadki sprawczego współdziałania w popełnianiu przestępstwa, jak też podżegania i pomocnictwa oraz skorzystania z przestępstwa" — co-perpetration, instigation, aiding, and benefiting all map onto art. 422 KC, and "Odpowiedzialność tych osób jest solidarna (art. 441 k.c.)".

art. 441 § 1 KC then makes co-tortfeasors solidarily liable for one indivisible harm. So: a finding that Waldek was a co-perpetrator (or aider, or knowing beneficiary) of the fraud makes him a solidary debtor for the full 155k via tort — you can claim the whole sum from him, regardless of contract privity. The pomocnik/co-perpetrator "odpowiada wobec poszkodowanego za całość szkody" — answers for the entire harm.

The "świadomie skorzystał" limb is worth flagging separately: if the funds flowed to Waldek, you must show he (1) obtained a benefit, (2) was aware of it, and (3) was aware the main perpetrator harmed you and that the benefit had its source in that harm.

art. 46 KK — restitution ordered directly against Waldek
If Waldek is convicted as co-perpetrator, the criminal court can order him to repay you directly. art. 46 § 1 KK: "W razie skazania sąd może orzec, a na wniosek pokrzywdzonego (…) orzeka, stosując przepisy prawa cywilnego, obowiązek naprawienia (…) szkody" — on a victim's motion the court must order it. It "ma charakter wyłącznie kompensacyjny" and applies civil-law principles. File the art. 46 motion in the criminal case — it is the cheapest, fastest path to a title against Waldek, with no separate civil filing fee, and because civil-law principles apply, the court can impose it solidarily with Mateusz.

art. 11 KPC — does a conviction bind the civil court? (prejudykat)
Yes, decisively, but with sharp limits. "Ustalenia (…) prawomocnego wyroku skazującego, co do popełnienia przestępstwa wiążą sąd w postępowaniu cywilnym". The civil court cannot make findings contrary to the criminal conviction; this is prejudycjalność and operates as a praesumptio iuris ac de iure — an irrebuttable presumption. But the limits matter:

It binds only a final conviction (wyrok skazujący) — not acquittals, not discontinuances, not warunkowe umorzenie.

It binds only as to the facts in the sentencing operative part (sentencja) — the commission of the crime and its qualification, i.e. the statutory elements — not the reasoning in the justification (Arczyński, Human Rights).

So if Waldek is convicted, a later civil court is bound that he committed the fraud — leaving you to prove only the residual civil questions (quantum, causation to your specific loss). If he is not convicted, you are not barred; art. 11 KPC binds only positively, so you can still prove the tort civilly on the ordinary balance of evidence.

Part IV — Should the zawiadomienie name Waldek from the outset?
Recommendation: name Waldek explicitly as a suspected co-perpetrator, alongside Mateusz, from the outset. The pros decisively outweigh the cons here.

Name him (recommended):

Pros: Directs the prosecutor to investigate his mailbox control, the fund flow, and the Łopata relationship — without naming him, an overworked prosecutor may treat it as a simple "JDG didn't deliver" matter and target only the invoice-holder. It frames the deception (the real heart of art. 286) around the person who performed it. It builds the record you need for the art. 46 motion and the art. 422/441 civil claim. It locks in the dual-mailbox and contradictory-stories evidence early, before it can be explained away.

Cons: If the deception evidence against Waldek is later found thin, the case against him may be discontinued (umorzenie) as to his person — but this does not harm the case against Mateusz, and an umorzenie does not bind you civilly under art. 11 KPC, so you keep the tort route.

Name only Mateusz and let the investigation reach Waldek (not recommended):

Pros: Lower risk of a discontinuance decision naming Waldek; lets the prosecutor "discover" him organically.

Cons: Real risk the prosecutor never expands the circle; you lose the framing advantage; you may have to file a supplementary zawiadomienie later anyway. Given how central Waldek is to your facts, this is unnecessarily passive.

Practical drafting note: in the zawiadomienie, describe Waldek's conduct as the wprowadzenie w błąd and present the podział ról expressly (Waldek operates/deceives; Mateusz's JDG and account are the vehicle), cite art. 286 § 1 KK w zw. z art. 18 § 1 KK as to Waldek, and append the dual-mailbox emails, the two contradictory explanations, the Wayback 3dkrakow captures, and the proforma. Plead the art. 18 § 3 pomocnictwo as an explicit alternative qualification so the prosecutor has a fallback that requires only zamiar ewentualny.

Part V — Civil theories that reach Waldek without a criminal conviction (honest strength ratings)
You can sue Waldek civilly in parallel, today, without waiting for the criminal court. The privity objection is contractual only; tort routes are open.

art. 415 KC delict (deceit) — STRONG-to-MODERATE. Pure tort: szkoda + zawinione bezprawne zachowanie + związek przyczynowy, "niezwiązana z żadną wcześniejszą umową". Your burden of proof is yours to carry (ciężar dowodu on the poszkodowany), but you do not need a conviction. If you can show Waldek's pre-payment misrepresentations caused your 155k loss, this stands alone. Strongest of the no-conviction routes because your evidence (his correspondence, his control, the contradictions) goes straight to fault. Limitation: 3 years from knowledge of harm and wrongdoer, but 20 years if the harm flows from a występek/zbrodnia — your fraud facts engage the 20-year window.

art. 422 KC (aided / knowingly benefited) + art. 441 solidarity — STRONG if funds traced to Waldek, otherwise MODERATE. Same statute as the criminal bridge but provable civilly without a conviction. If you can trace the 155k (or part) to Waldek, the "świadomie skorzystał" limb is very strong and makes him solidarily liable for the whole sum. Without a money trace, you fall back to the "był pomocny" limb, which is provable but evidence-dependent.

art. 405 KC unjust enrichment — MODERATE, and only if funds flowed to him. "Kto bez podstawy prawnej uzyskał korzyść majątkową kosztem innej osoby, obowiązany jest do wydania korzyści". Useful as an alternative if you can show money reached Waldek without legal basis, but weaker than tort because (a) it requires the enrichment to be at your cost and traceable to him specifically, and (b) it is subsidiary in character and courts are cautious where you paid a third party (Mateusz's JDG). Plead it as a secondary basis, not the lead.

Firmanctwo — WEAK as a recovery tool for you; useful only as corroboration. Firmanctwo (art. 113 Ordynacja podatkowa; art. 55 KKS) is about using another's name/firm to conceal one's own business and evade tax. It creates solidary liability for tax arrears between firmant and firmowany, owed to the State, not to you. And the family relationship does not exclude liability. It gives you no direct civil claim for your 155k. Its real value is evidential: a finding that Waldek (non-VAT-registered) was running the business behind Mateusz's name corroborates the de-facto-operator / panowanie nad czynem narrative for your art. 286/art. 422 case. You might mention the firmanctwo pattern to the tax authorities (US/KAS) as a parallel pressure point, but do not rely on it for recovery.

"Działanie faktyczne" / lifting the formal veil — NOT an independent basis in itself. There is no general Polish doctrine that a de facto operator of a JDG is automatically liable for the firm's debts the way you'd "pierce a corporate veil." A JDG is the sole proprietor's personal liability anyway — there is no separate legal person to pierce. So "działanie faktyczne" is not a free-standing cause of action; it is the factual theory that feeds your art. 415 / art. 422 / criminal co-perpetration claims. Treat it as argument, not as a statute.

Bottom line
Criminally: Waldek is a credible co-perpetrator of art. 286 § 1 KK under art. 18 § 1 KK (with sprawstwo kierownicze plausible given Mateusz's absence abroad), because fraud is general-subject and you need not realise every element — the deception he performed plus his essential, dominant role suffice. art. 18 § 3 pomocnictwo (provable on dolus eventualis) is the fallback. art. 21 § 2 is no obstacle — fraud has no personal-status element tying liability to the invoice-holder. Anchor the fraud act in pre-payment conduct; use the post-payment refund/contradiction material as proof of role and intent.

The civil bridge is real: a conviction binds the civil court under art. 11 KPC (positively, as to the sentencja); art. 46 KK lets the criminal court order Waldek to repay you directly; and art. 422 + 441 § 1 KC make him a solidary tort debtor for the full 155k, bypassing contract privity entirely.

You are not dependent on a conviction: art. 415 KC deceit (and art. 422 if you trace funds) lets you sue Waldek civilly now, in parallel, on the ordinary standard of proof, within a 20-year limitation window because the harm flows from a crime.
