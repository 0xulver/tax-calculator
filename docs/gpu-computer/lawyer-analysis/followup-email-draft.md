## Open questions worth his time (rationale, not for sending)

1. **EPU vs. criminal restitution (art. 415 §1 KPK).** Because the JDG is not a
   separate entity, a pending/final EPU claim for the same 155k may block an
   art. 46 KK restitution order against Mateusz — the one title that survives
   his bankruptcy. This cuts against the lawyer's "wait for EPU" plan. Models
   disagree on severity; needs his ruling. **Highest priority.**
2. **File now vs. wait.** Only the prosecutor can compel bank records / freeze
   assets; those tools vanish if money moves. Majority of research says file
   now; one strand warns of tipping off the debtor.
3. **Framing to clear the civil-dispute screen** (SN III KK 216/25): the method
   of deception must pre-date payment — the fake front + contradictory stories.
4. **Digital filing** via ePUAP "Pismo ogólne" signed with Profil Zaufany
   (legally equal to wet signature); no in-person appearance needed — but prefer
   he files it himself.
5. **Name Waldek** as criminal co-perpetrator now (invoice name is irrelevant
   for fraud); conviction bridges back to civil liability against him.
6. **The "where did the money go / there was a margin" point** — investigative;
   only the prosecutor can trace the 155k.
7. **Evidence preservation** — notarial *protokół z otwarcia strony* of the key
   website pages before any contact prompts edits.

## Draft email

**Subject: GPUcomputer / Szklarski — follow-up: the fraud complaint and how to proceed**

Dear Mr Proniewski,

Thank you for your analysis. I'd like to act on your suggestion in point 3b —
filing a criminal complaint for fraud (*zawiadomienie o podejrzeniu popełnienia
oszustwa*) — and I've gathered evidence that I think strengthens it well beyond
"they simply didn't pay me." I'm conscious the risk is that the prosecutor
treats this as a civil debt, so I've tried to focus on what happened **before**
I paid. I'd be very grateful for your view on the points below.

**The evidence I now have**

*1. The business was a staged front, not what it appeared to be.*

- Their website presented a legitimate, operating Kraków computer business with
  an office shown on Google Maps as "our office." In reality that address
  (Mogilska 16/7) is a **virtual office** — they have no real premises or stock
  there.
- The registered owner, Mateusz Szklarski, has **lived in the Netherlands and
  worked full-time on a factory production line since 2022** (I have his
  employment history). He cannot have been running a custom-PC business in
  Kraków day-to-day; in practice it was run remotely by Waldemar Łopata, who is
  not even registered.
- Their website stated that **every shipment is "insured to its full value."**
  No shipment, no insurance, no claim, and no payout ever materialised.

My point is that this appearance of a real, safe, insured Kraków merchant is
what induced me to transfer 155,000 PLN. Had I known it was a virtual-office
shell run remotely by an unregistered person while the owner works abroad, I
would never have paid.

*2. Their explanations contradict each other.*

- On 24 February they told me **customs had cleared the goods that day** and I'd
  have the machine shortly.
- On 13 May they told me a **Hong Kong supplier had gone bankrupt and the parts
  were never sent.**

Both cannot be true. I think this contradiction is itself evidence that I was
being misled — but I'd value your view on whether it helps show deceptive
intent.

*3. Where did the money actually go?*

Even if a supplier genuinely failed, the price I paid included their own margin
and profit — a significant part of 155,000 PLN. So how can the entire sum be
"lost" and nothing repaid? To me this suggests the money was never used to buy
my hardware at all. I understand only the prosecutor can compel their bank
records to trace this — which is one reason I think the criminal route matters.

**My questions**

1. **EPU vs. criminal restitution.** I've read that because his business is a
   sole proprietorship, a *pending or final* EPU claim for the same 155,000 PLN
   could block the criminal court from ordering him to repay me (and that a
   criminal restitution order is the one thing that would survive his
   bankruptcy). Is that correct? If so, does pushing the EPU case to a final
   order actually **harm** my ability to recover through the criminal case, and
   how should we sequence the two?

2. **Timing.** Should I file the criminal complaint **now** — to get the
   prosecutor tracing the funds and able to freeze assets — or wait, as you
   suggested, for the EPU order first?

3. **Framing.** Does the "staged front + contradictory stories + missing margin"
   theory, built on the evidence above, give a real chance of getting past the
   prosecutor's initial screening rather than being dismissed as a civil
   dispute? Which parts would you lead with?

4. **Waldek.** I understand there's no civil joint liability against him today.
   But should he be **named in the criminal complaint** as a likely
   co-perpetrator, given he ran the correspondence and the website while the
   owner was abroad — and could a criminal finding later open a civil route
   against him, as you mentioned?

5. **How it's filed — digitally.** I do not speak Polish and I'm not resident in
   Kraków, so I would strongly prefer **not to file or appear in person.** I do
   have a **Profil Zaufany** and already use it to file taxes and deal with the
   authorities. Can the complaint be filed electronically (ePUAP / *pismo
   ogólne*) signed with my Profil Zaufany — and, ideally, **would you prepare
   and file it on my behalf?**

6. **Preserving the evidence.** Their website can be edited at any time. Do you
   recommend a notarial capture (*protokół z otwarcia strony internetowej*) of
   the key pages before we take any step that might prompt them to change it? I
   have already saved dated screenshots and Wayback Machine archives.

Finally, could you let me know your fee for **(a)** preparing and filing the
complaint, and **(b)** a short call to settle the strategy above? I'd rather
invest in getting this framed correctly from the start.

Thank you,
Magnus Brantheim
