## 1. Bottom-line recommendation

For these facts, the working view is mostly right, with two important refinements.

**2023:** File a **PIT-38 correction** for 2023. The primary penal-fiscal shield is **KKS art. 16a**, not ordinary art. 16, because a return already exists and the remediation is a legally effective correction. Art. 16a says the offender is **not punishable** for a fiscal offence/petty offence concerning the filing of a declaration if, after the act, a legally effective correction is filed; if public-law tax was reduced, the amount must be paid promptly, no later than the deadline set by the financial preparatory-proceedings authority. The statute is not limited to corrections that increase current-year tax, so a correction of crypto cost/carry-forward fields should still fit art. 16a, provided the correction is legally effective. ([ISAP][1])

**2024:** First check e-Urząd / Twój e-PIT / Historia deklaracji. If **no PIT-38 exists**, file an **ordinary late first PIT-38** plus **czynny żal under KKS art. 16**. If a 2024 PIT-38 was **auto-accepted or already submitted**, file a **correction**, and art. 16a becomes the primary shield. A single art. 16 notice can still conservatively describe both 2023 and 2024, but it should not over-admit “tax evasion” or accuse the preparer. Official Twój e-PIT guidance says a 2024 PIT-38 can be automatically accepted on 30 April 2025 only in specified circumstances, generally where payer information such as PIT-8C was in the KAS system; official crypto guidance also says crypto exchanges/businesses have no legal duty to prepare PIT-8C/PIT-11 for users or tax authorities, so a purely crypto PIT-38 may well not have been auto-created. ([Podatki][2])

**2025:** Do **not** treat 2025 as part of the offence narrative. As of 27 April 2026, the 2025 PIT-38 deadline has not passed: PIT returns covered by PIT Act art. 45 are filed between 15 February and 30 April of the following year. File the 2025 PIT-38 normally before 30 April 2026, using the corrected 2024 carry-forward. ([ISAP][3])

**Sequence:** For the safest conservative posture: prepare the 2023 correction and the 2024 late filing/correction, then submit the **czynny żal first through e-Urząd Skarbowy**, then immediately file the PIT-38 correction/late return in the same session or the same day. Keep the e-US confirmation for the active-regret notice and the UPO/status 200 for each electronic PIT-38 filing. UPO is the only official confirmation that an electronic tax declaration was correctly filed. ([Podatki.gov.pl][4])

The draft is directionally good. The main changes I would make are: soften the “other persons” paragraph, avoid any language suggesting intentional evasion, explicitly identify 2024 as either **late first return** or **correction depending on e-US status**, state the 0 PLN tax due as “according to the filed correction/return,” and avoid relying on ordinary ePUAP in 2026. As of 1 January 2026, podatki.gov.pl states that ePUAP filings by individuals/private entities to tax/KAS authorities are generally not effective unless a special exception applies; electronic filings should be made through e-US/PUESC or the authority’s e-Delivery address. ([Podatki.gov.pl][5])

## 3. Black-letter law, official guidance, case law, inference

### Black-letter law

**KKS art. 16 is not merely mitigation.** The statute says the offender **“nie podlega karze”** — is not subject to punishment — if, after the act, the offender notifies the authority called to prosecute and discloses material circumstances, especially cooperating persons. If the act reduced a public-law receivable, payment must be made within the statutory/authority deadline. The notice may be written, oral to protocol, and may be filed through an e-Urząd Skarbowy account. ([ISAP][1])

**KKS art. 16 has hard cut-offs.** It is ineffective if filed when the enforcement authority already had a clearly documented notice of the fiscal offence/petty offence, or after an official action had started — especially search, checking activities, or audit — aimed at revealing the offence, unless that action gave no basis to initiate proceedings. It also does not apply to certain directing/organising/entrapment situations. ([ISAP][1])

**KKS art. 16a is the cleaner mechanism for already-filed wrong returns.** It covers an act concerning the filing of a declaration or sending of a book where a legally effective correction is filed for the obligation whose incorrect performance was the act. It is unavailable if, before the correction, preparatory proceedings were started or the offence was discovered in ongoing preparatory proceedings. ([ISAP][1])

**Ordynacja podatkowa art. 81 / 81b:** taxpayers may correct a previously filed declaration by filing a corrective declaration, but correction rights are suspended during tax proceedings or tax audit in the covered scope and then revive after the proceeding/audit in the scope not covered by the decision. ([ISAP][6])

**PIT crypto rules:** PIT Act art. 30b taxes income from paid disposal of virtual currencies at 19%; art. 22 ust. 14–16 governs direct acquisition/disposal costs and carry-forward of surplus costs; art. 30b ust. 6a requires showing virtual-currency costs even if there is no disposal revenue in the year; art. 45 requires PIT-38 filing by 30 April of the following year. ([ISAP][3])

**Jurisdiction:** for PIT returns, the PIT Act points to the office serving the naczelnik urzędu skarbowego competent by the taxpayer’s place of residence on the filing date, or last Polish residence if Polish residence ceased before filing. The general Ordynacja rule also uses residence/address unless special tax rules provide otherwise. ([ISAP][3])

### Official administrative guidance

Official crypto guidance confirms that a PIT-38 is required when there is paid disposal **or acquisition costs** of virtual currency, that acquisition expenses are shown even if there is no disposal revenue, that surplus costs carry forward to the next year, and that where costs exceed revenue there is no tax. It also states that crypto exchanges/businesses are not legally required to prepare PIT-8C/PIT-11 for users or tax authorities. ([Podatki.gov.pl][7])

Official e-US guidance lists **Twój e-PIT**, **Historia deklaracji**, and **Czynny żal / zawiadomienie o popełnieniu czynu zabronionego** among e-US services. It also says a logged-in e-US user does not need a qualified signature; letters sent by a logged-in user are affixed with the qualified seal of the Head of KAS, including the user’s identifier and name. ([Podatki.gov.pl][4])

### Case law / commentary support

A useful SAOS-published appellate judgment rejected the argument that a tax office had clearly documented knowledge of a late PIT offence merely because, the day after the deadline, the system lacked a filed return. The court treated “clearly documented knowledge” as more than the mere absence of a filing entry, and also did not treat ordinary help preparing a tax return as automatically criminal cooperation. This is secondary support, not a guarantee for a crypto case. ([Saos][8])

### Inference for this case

The mere fact that KAS systems do not show a 2024 PIT-38 should **not by itself** automatically defeat czynny żal, especially where KAS may not know the taxpayer had crypto acquisition/disposal obligations. But third-party reports, exchange-of-information data, PIT-8C data, or an internal analysis could matter if they gave the authority a clearly documented, specific notice of this taxpayer’s offence or triggered official checking/audit actions. DAC8 increases future crypto data visibility: the European Commission states that DAC8 covers automatic exchange of information on crypto-assets, applies from 1 January 2026, and first reporting/exchanges for the 2026 reporting year occur in 2027. ([ISAP][1])

## 5. Key answers to the disputed legal questions

### What art. 16 protects against

A valid art. 16 active-regret notice eliminates penal-fiscal punishability for the disclosed fiscal offence/petty offence. It does **not** eliminate the underlying tax obligation, interest, the correctness of the PIT-38 cost basis, the possibility of tax checking/audit, or later civil/procedural consequences. Its effect depends on truthful and sufficient disclosure, timing, and payment of any reduced public-law receivable. ([ISAP][1])

### What art. 16a protects against

Art. 16a is narrower but powerful: it applies to offences concerning the filing of a declaration or sending a book, if a legally effective correction is filed. It should be sufficient for the 2023 wrong PIT-38 and for 2024 if an auto-accepted/submitted PIT-38 exists. It may not cover separate conduct outside the declaration/correction context, such as unreliable evidence, obstruction, false documents, or a later separate wrong return not corrected. ([ISAP][1])

### Does art. 16a apply where tax remains 0 PLN?

Yes, on the statutory wording, art. 16a is not limited to corrections producing tax due. Its §2 adds a payment condition **if** a public-law receivable was reduced; where the correction still shows 0 PLN PIT-38 tax, there may simply be no payment condition. The official crypto guidance makes clear that crypto costs and carry-forward amounts must be shown even where there is no tax or no disposal revenue, so those fields are not legally irrelevant. ([ISAP][1])

### Which KKS provision matters for a late zero-tax 2024 PIT-38?

For a late first 2024 PIT-38 with 0 PLN tax, **KKS art. 56 §4** is the more relevant provision because it punishes late filing of a declaration and does not use the art. 54 “naraża podatek na uszczuplenie” language. **KKS art. 54** is less likely if the correct tax is genuinely zero, because art. 54 requires failure to disclose/failure to file thereby exposing tax to understatement. If the cost basis is later challenged and tax becomes positive, art. 54 or art. 56 §1 risk becomes more arguable. ([ISAP][1])

For a 2024 late PIT-38 offence occurring after the 30 April 2025 deadline, the likely petty-offence fine range is best stated as the statutory formula: from **1/10 to 20 times the minimum wage**, with a fiscal mandate capped at 5 times and a penal order capped at 10 times. Using the 2025 minimum wage of 4,666 PLN gives 466.60 PLN to 93,320 PLN as the court fine range; however, in practice a valid active regret should avoid punishment, and more-favourable-law questions can matter if the applicable law changes between offence and adjudication. ([ISAP][1])

### Limitation period

If the late 2024 zero-tax filing is treated as a fiscal petty offence, KKS art. 51 gives a one-year limitation period; if proceedings against the offender are initiated within that year, punishability ends two years from the end of that original period. Because the 2024 PIT-38 deadline was 30 April 2025, 27 April 2026 is still inside the one-year window. ([ISAP][1])

### Tax company explanation

Mentioning professional help is useful, but it should be neutral. KKS art. 16 requires disclosure of material circumstances, especially cooperating persons, and KKS art. 9 §3 can extend perpetrator-type liability to a person who, under law/decision/contract/factual performance, deals with another person’s economic, especially financial, affairs. That means blaming a preparer can create a “cooperating person” issue. The safer formulation is: the taxpayer used external professional help, gave general crypto-related information, but the full PIT-38 crypto transaction/cost reconstruction was not identified/reconstructed then; the taxpayer has no knowledge that anyone intentionally cooperated in a prohibited act. ([ISAP][1])

KKS art. 10 mistake/lack of awareness may be relevant as a defence or culpability argument, especially for a foreign taxpayer who recently moved to Poland, but the filing should not rely on art. 10 as the main mechanism. Use art. 16 / 16a as the operative protection, and include lack of understanding only as factual context. ([ISAP][1])

## 6. Critique of the draft wording

### What is good

The draft is factual, short enough, and avoids saying the taxpayer intentionally evaded tax. It correctly distinguishes 2024 Variant A and Variant B and states that tax due is 0 PLN. That is useful because the payment condition under art. 16 / 16a matters only if there is a public-law receivable reduced by the act. ([ISAP][1])

### What I would change

1. **Add art. 16a language for corrections.** For 2023, and for 2024 if auto-accepted/submitted, the legally relevant mechanism is correction under art. 16a. The notice can still be under art. 16, but it should say the 2023/2024 corrections are being filed as legally effective corrections.

2. **Avoid “W popełnieniu czynu nie współdziałały inne osoby” as an absolute statement.** Because a tax company was involved, say “według mojej wiedzy” and clarify that professional assistance is given as factual context, not as an allegation of criminal cooperation.

3. **Avoid over-specific blame.** Do not write that the tax company “forgot,” “failed,” or “was busy.” That is not needed and could trigger preparer-liability questions. Use passive/neutral wording.

4. **Use “według składanych zeznań/korekt” before the 0 PLN statement.** This avoids making the active-regret notice look false if the authority later disputes the cost basis.

5. **Do not rely on ePUAP in 2026.** Use e-Urząd Skarbowy or e-Doręczenia, not ordinary ePUAP, because current guidance says ordinary ePUAP filings by individuals/private entities to tax/KAS authorities are generally ineffective from 1 January 2026. ([Podatki.gov.pl][5])

6. **Consider omitting 2025 entirely.** If mentioned, state only that the notice concerns 2023–2024 and 2025 will be filed within the statutory deadline.

I indicate the use of external help in preparing the returns as a factual circumstance; I have no knowledge that any person acted with intent to breach tax obligations or cooperated in committing a prohibited act. If requested, I will provide additional explanations and documents concerning preparation of the filings.

This notice concerns settlements for 2023–2024. The PIT-38 return for 2025 will be filed within the statutory deadline.

Please accept this notice as a notice referred to in Article 16 §1 of the Fiscal Penal Code. With respect to the corrections filed, I also indicate that the corrections are filed as legally effective corrections of declarations concerning the obligations whose incorrect performance has been described above.

## 8. Practical filing sequence and evidence checklist

1. **Before any call or visit to the tax office**, log into e-Urząd Skarbowy and check **Historia deklaracji** and **Twój e-PIT** for 2024 PIT-38 status. Avoid describing the issue informally to the office before the formal notice is filed, because art. 16 timing can be defeated by clearly documented authority knowledge or official revealing actions. ([ISAP][1])

2. **Prepare all filings first:** 2023 PIT-38 correction, 2024 late PIT-38 or correction depending on status, and 2025 ordinary PIT-38 if ready. Check that the 2023 carry-forward is 825,656.90 PLN, 2024 carry-forward is 528,067.97 PLN, and 2025 carry-forward is 472,061.65 PLN under the selected filing posture.

3. **Submit the active-regret notice through e-Urząd Skarbowy**, using the service for “Zawiadomienie o popełnieniu czynu zabronionego / Czynny żal,” or through the competent office’s e-Delivery address. Do not use ordinary ePUAP in 2026 unless a Polish adviser has confirmed a specific legal exception. ([Podatki.gov.pl][4])

4. **Immediately file the PIT-38 correction/late return electronically.** If the system allows, do it in the same e-US session after the active-regret notice. The safest timestamp pattern is: active regret first, then the correction/late filing minutes later. If a correction is filed first, art. 16a may still protect correction cases, but for a true missing 2024 first return art. 16 is cleaner if the notice precedes the late filing.

5. **Download and retain proof:** active-regret submission confirmation, PIT-38 UPOs, status 200 confirmations, reference numbers, screenshots of Historia deklaracji showing 2024 status before remediation, copies/PDFs of all filed PIT-38 forms, the crypto cost-basis workpaper, exchange exports, FX conversion support, and any correspondence with the tax company. UPO matters because a reference number alone is not proof of filing; official guidance says the declaration is correctly filed only after status 200, and UPO is the only confirmation. ([Podatki.gov.pl][9])

6. **Pay immediately if the result changes.** If the authority or a later correction produces any PIT due, pay the tax and interest promptly and no later than any authority deadline. The active-regret/correction protections do not remove the tax debt or interest. ([ISAP][1])

---

## 9. If the zero-tax position is later challenged

A valid active regret or art. 16a correction should still protect against punishment for the **disclosed** late/wrong filing if the disclosure was truthful, timely, and complete enough. It does not freeze the tax merits. If KAS later disagrees with the cost basis and assesses tax, the taxpayer must pay the public-law receivable and interest; if the issue involves facts not disclosed or a materially different later correction, an additional correction and possibly an additional active-regret notice may be prudent. ([ISAP][1])

The highest risk is not the 0 PLN number itself; it is whether the cost-basis reconstruction is defensible, whether imported/pre-residency costs are legally positioned correctly, whether any 2024 return was already auto-accepted, and whether KAS had already started documented action before the notice.

## 10. Red flags requiring a Polish tax adviser or penal-fiscal lawyer before filing

Consult a **doradca podatkowy** plus, if penal-fiscal exposure is material, an **adwokat/radca prawny handling KKS** before submission if any of these apply:

| Red flag                                                                                                                                                                                       | Why it matters                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any letter, phone call, e-US message, czynności sprawdzające, audit notice, summons, or question from KAS about PIT-38, crypto, PIT-8C, foreign assets, or capital gains                       | Could make art. 16 ineffective if the authority already has documented knowledge or official revealing actions started. ([ISAP][1]) |
| The 0 PLN position is fragile and a realistic adjustment would create positive PIT for 2023/2024/2025                                                                                          | Payment conditions under art. 16 / 16a and underpayment offences become more important.                                             |
| Large undocumented exchange history, missing wallet records, unverifiable acquisition costs, or aggressive cost import from pre-Polish-residency periods                                       | The correction may be challenged on merits; art. 16a does not validate the tax computation.                                         |
| The taxpayer may have operated a professional crypto business, exchange, brokerage, custody, OTC, payment, or AML-registered virtual-currency activity rather than ordinary private investment | Official PIT crypto guidance distinguishes ordinary PIT-38 treatment from professional/business activity. ([Podatki.gov.pl][7])     |
| The tax company had a formal mandate to handle tax affairs and knew the specific PIT-38 crypto facts                                                                                           | KKS art. 9 §3 / cooperating-person issues may need careful handling. ([ISAP][1])                                                    |
| The taxpayer wants to accuse the tax company, name staff, or seek compensation                                                                                                                 | That is a separate professional-liability strategy and should not be improvised inside active regret.                               |
| 2024 e-US status is unclear or there are multiple filings/auto-acceptance artifacts                                                                                                            | Filing “late first return” when a return already exists, or filing a correction when none exists, can create procedural problems.   |
| Any preparatory proceedings or fiscal-penal proceedings may already be pending                                                                                                                 | Art. 16a and art. 16 timing rules become decisive. ([ISAP][1])                                                                      |

The proposed approach is reasonable if there has been **no prior KAS contact/action**, the corrected PIT-38 calculations are defensible, and the notice is filed before the remedial returns/corrections or essentially simultaneously through e-US.
