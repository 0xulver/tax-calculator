## Bottom-line recommendation

Use the **split-year / imported-cost model**, not the synthetic full-history Polish pool.

For the 2023 PIT-38, assuming the factual centre-of-life move was **2023-04-12**, Polish PIT-38 should start from disposals on or after **2023-04-12**. Crypto disposals from **2020-2022** and **2023-01-01 through 2023-04-11** should be excluded from Polish PIT-38 revenue, unless a separate Poland-source rule applied while the taxpayer was nonresident. On the facts provided, exchange accessibility after the move and later remittance to Poland should not convert pre-move disposals into Polish PIT-38 revenue. Polish residency rules and MF guidance support a prospective split in the year of residence change: resident period = worldwide taxation; nonresident period = only Polish-source income. 

The current all-history Polish-style pool is therefore **methodologically wrong** if it includes 2020-2022 and pre-2023-04-12 disposals as if the taxpayer had been in the Polish PIT-38 regime. The 2022 stablecoin disposals should not be in a Polish crypto revenue pool at all if the taxpayer was Swedish resident and not Polish resident in 2022. The 2023 pre-move MATIC revenue of **147,030.09 PLN** should likewise be excluded if 2023-04-12 is the correct Polish residence start date.

The opening imported cost should be rebuilt from a **move-date inventory and foreign-consumption analysis**, not from a synthetic Polish annual pool. High-confidence imported costs are documented pre-residency fiat-to-crypto acquisition costs that were not already consumed/deducted in Sweden and are traceable to assets still held at the Polish residence start. KIS has accepted pre-residency virtual-currency acquisition costs in the first Polish PIT-38 period after residence change, where documented and not deducted before the change. ([Inforlex][1])

For **pre-residency USDC salary / compensation**, the current “exclude because Poland did not tax the receipt” policy is too conservative if stated as law. KIS accepts, in Polish-taxed cases, that crypto received for services or employment remuneration is an **odpłatne nabycie** and that the value of the settled wage/service receivable is a PIT-38 acquisition cost on later disposal. Swedish official guidance similarly treats crypto salary/business receipts as income at receipt and gives later disposal basis equal to the value reported as income. The cross-border extension to **Sweden-taxed pre-residency remuneration still held at the move date** is supportable, but not directly ruled on by KIS; it is the main issue for an individual interpretation. ([Interpretacje][2])

Practical filing stance: if documented pre-residency fiat purchase costs alone exceed the **134,579.93 PLN** threshold, use the conservative/supportable split-year model and keep salary-USDC out pending KIS. If the zero-tax 2024/2025 outcome depends on salary-USDC basis, include it only if Swedish income inclusion and move-date provenance are strong, or file conservatively now and amend after a favourable KIS interpretation.

## Answers to the core questions

### A. Split-year PIT-38 revenue

**1. January 1 to April 11, 2023 disposals:** exclude from Polish PIT-38, assuming the taxpayer was not Polish resident and the disposals were not Poland-source. The Polish tax system does not create a retroactive full-year PIT-38 pool merely because the taxpayer became resident later in 2023. 

**2. 2020-2022 disposals:** exclude entirely from Polish PIT-38 revenue. They also should not be run through a fictional Polish annual revenue/cost pool. They matter only because they may have consumed acquisition costs under Swedish tax calculations before the asset entered the Polish tax base.

**3. Same exchange account / later remittance to Poland:** no change. A Binance/Kraken account accessible after the move does not make pre-move disposals Polish revenue. Bringing fiat or crypto proceeds to Poland later is not the Polish PIT-38 revenue event; the relevant event is the disposal, and the taxpayer was nonresident at that time.

**4. DTT answer:** pre-move native crypto gains should fall under the Poland–Sweden treaty’s residual capital-gains rule for “other property,” taxable in the state of residence of the transferor. For pre-move gains, that points to Sweden if the taxpayer was Swedish treaty resident before 2023-04-12. 

Treaty caveat: the Poland–Sweden treaty has a 10-year emigrant-gains clause. KIS has applied it to Swedish-taxed securities/fund gains after a move to Poland and treated those gains as exempt from Polish PIT-38. But Skatteverket’s English nonresident guidance specifically flags Swedish filing for “shares etc.” after departure, not native crypto, and Swedish “close ties” can also affect whether Sweden considers someone fully taxable. This should be checked with Swedish advice if Sweden has taxed any **post-move** crypto gains. ([Inforlex][5])

## Calculation-method recommendation

Use this order of operations:

1. **Set the Polish start date.** Use **2023-04-12** if evidence supports that the centre of personal/economic interests moved then. The PESEL / registration date of **2023-05-04** is evidence, but not necessarily the tax-residence start date.

2. **Cut the Polish PIT-38 universe at the move date.** Exclude all disposals before **2023-04-12**. Include Polish PIT-38 revenue only from disposals on/after **2023-04-12**, subject to the Sweden treaty caveat above.

3. **Build a move-date inventory.** For every asset held at the move date, identify quantity, wallet/exchange, acquisition path, and whether the acquisition path is fiat purchase, salary/compensation USDC, or crypto-to-crypto successor asset.

4. **Use Swedish K4-style records to determine consumption before the move.** Sweden taxes crypto-to-crypto exchanges and applies average cost. Therefore, pre-move Swedish K4 records are the most relevant evidence of which costs were consumed before Poland became relevant. A cost is “consumed” if it was used, or should have been used, in Swedish capital-gains calculations before the move—even if a Swedish loss was only partly deductible. ([Skatteverket][4])

5. **Import only unconsumed acquisition costs into the first Polish PIT-38 period.** For pre-residency fiat purchases, this is directly supported by KIS. For pre-residency salary-USDC, use a separate schedule labelled “KIS-dependent remuneration basis” unless an interpretation has been obtained. ([Inforlex][1])

6. **Do not run 2020-2022 through Polish annual pooling.** Polish annual pooling applies once the taxpayer is within Polish PIT-38 jurisdiction. It is not a method for inventing carryforward from foreign-residence years.

7. **From 2023-04-12 onward, apply normal Polish PIT-38 pooling.** Once valid imported costs enter the Polish PIT-38 computation, they are combined with post-residency Polish PIT-38 costs and carried forward under Art. 22(16) if they exceed Polish crypto revenue. 

### PLN valuation for salary-USDC

For salary/service USDC, the defensible cost is the **gross value of the remuneration or receivable settled by the USDC**, not the Swedish tax paid and not the later exchange-deposit value. KIS remuneration cases focus on the value of the settled receivable/wage. ([Interpretacje][2])

Use this hierarchy:

| Situation                                                 | Recommended PLN value                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Contract/invoice states USD remuneration settled in USDC  | USD receivable amount converted to PLN using NBP average USD rate from the last working day before receipt / cost-incurrence date.            |
| Swedish employment income reported in SEK                 | SEK amount reported/taxed as employment income, converted to PLN using NBP SEK rate from the last working day before the income/receipt date. |
| Contract states fixed USDC amount with no fiat receivable | Market value at receipt, reconciled to Swedish reported income value; convert to PLN using NBP rate for the currency used in the valuation.   |
| Later exchange deposit or sale value                      | Do **not** use as acquisition cost unless it is the same as the receipt-date income value by coincidence.                                     |

KIS’s pre-residency cost interpretation used NBP average exchange rate from the last working day before the acquisition cost was incurred for foreign-currency costs. ([Inforlex][1])

   Conservative schedule = pre-residency fiat purchases + post-residency Polish-taxed remuneration.
   Supportable schedule = conservative schedule + Sweden-taxed salary-USDC still held at move.
