# Pre-Residency USDC Basis and Split-Year PIT-38 After a Sweden-to-Poland Move

## Bottom line

The legally stronger filing method is the **split-year / imported-cost model**, not the synthetic full-history Polish pool. On the facts you gave, Polish PIT-38 for 2023 should begin only when Polish tax residence began in 2023, and pre-residency disposals from 2020-2022 and from 2023 before the move date should be kept **out of Polish PIT-38 revenue altogether**. The Polish PIT Act taxes residents on worldwide income, but non-residents only on Polish-source income; the Ministry’s residency guidance expressly accepts that tax residence can change during the year, with consequences applying prospectively from the change. The Poland-Sweden treaty then points ordinary capital gains to the taxpayer’s state of residence, using treaty tie-breaker rules if both states could claim residence. citeturn14view0turn14view1turn38view1turn6view0turn34search0turn10search0

That means the current policy is **wrong if it runs 2020-2022 and January through April 11, 2023 through a Polish PIT-38 pool**. That approach treats years with no Polish PIT-38 filing duty as if they had been Polish crypto years, and it lets non-Polish disposals artificially consume or generate a Polish carry-forward. The right opening figure for 2023 is not a hypothetical Polish carry-forward from prior foreign-residency years. It is the **documented acquisition cost that remained unrelieved when Polish residence began**, measured by what was still economically and tax-wise “alive” at the move date. citeturn14view1turn38view1turn6view0turn20view1turn21view0

For imported costs, the best method is to use **Swedish tax consumption**, not synthetic Polish annual pooling. Sweden taxes crypto sales, crypto-for-crypto swaps, and payments with crypto, and it uses a per-asset average-cost method; Sweden also treats salary paid in crypto as employment income at receipt with the later capital-gains cost equal to the value already taxed as employment income. So the practical question is not “what would a Polish annual pool have looked like in Swedish years?” The real question is: **what acquisition cost remained attached to assets still held on 2023-04-12 after applying Swedish rules to all pre-move sales and swaps?** citeturn32view0turn29view0turn31view0turn31view2

On the most disputed issue, the current “safe default” policy appears **too categorical** if it says pre-residency USDC salary can never become Polish PIT-38 cost merely because Poland did not tax the receipt. Polish published practice clearly accepts two propositions: first, documented pre-residency crypto acquisition costs can be imported into the first Polish PIT-38 year if they were not deducted before the residency change; second, crypto received as remuneration can be an “odpłatne nabycie,” with the settled receivable or already taxed remuneration value functioning as the direct acquisition expense. What remains missing is a published source squarely combining those two ideas for **foreign-taxed, pre-residency** remuneration in crypto. That makes the position **arguable and materially stronger than a “never” rule**, but still less certain than imported fiat-purchase costs. citeturn21view0turn24view3turn24view4turn24view2turn31view0

My recommendation for 2023-2025 filings is therefore:

1. **Use the split-year / imported-cost model.**
2. **Exclude all pre-residency disposals from Polish PIT-38 revenue.**
3. **Import only pre-residency costs that survived to the move date under Swedish rules.**
4. **Treat post-residency Poland-taxed USDC remuneration as Polish PIT-38 cost when later disposed.**
5. **Treat the pre-residency salary-USDC bucket as supportable but disputed**: conservative filing excludes it pending an individual interpretation; a stronger but more contentious filing includes it only where Swedish taxation and the surviving post-move holding can be demonstrated asset by asset. citeturn21view0turn24view3turn24view2turn31view0turn31view2

## Black-letter law

Under Article 3 of the Polish PIT Act, a person with a place of residence in Poland is subject to unlimited tax liability, while a person without a place of residence in Poland is subject only to limited tax liability on income earned in Poland. Article 3(2b) gives examples of Polish-source income for non-residents, such as work performed in Poland, business carried on in Poland, Polish real estate, and certain Polish securities or rights. Native crypto disposals by a Swedish resident on foreign exchanges do not fit the normal Polish-source categories shown in Article 3(2b) on the facts you described. citeturn14view0turn14view1turn38view1

The Ministry’s residency guidance expressly gives split-year examples. In one example, a person moves to Poland on August 1, becomes a Polish resident from that arrival date, and income earned before arrival is not taxed in Poland. That guidance is important here because it directly rejects the idea that a mid-year move retroactively drags pre-move foreign income into Poland merely because the taxpayer later becomes Polish resident in the same calendar year. On that logic, a PESEL date or later administrative registration date is evidentiary background, not the legal start date of residence if the factual move and centre-of-life shift occurred earlier. citeturn6view0

For crypto, the Polish statute defines “odpłatne zbycie waluty wirtualnej” as exchange of crypto for legal tender, goods, services, non-crypto property rights, or the use of crypto to settle other obligations. It does **not** define a mere same-owner transfer between wallets or exchange accounts as a taxable disposal. It also says resident taxpayers pay 19% on crypto income, with the crypto income base equal to revenue minus costs determined under Article 22(14)-(16). Domestic law also provides a foreign-tax credit mechanism where a Polish resident has foreign crypto income, but that is only a backstop if foreign tax exists; the treaty is still the main allocation rule. citeturn16view2turn17view1turn17view0

Article 22(14)-(16) is the core Polish cost rule. It allows only **documented expenses directly incurred to acquire virtual currency** and disposal-related costs, and it carries forward any excess of those costs over the year’s disposal revenue into the next year. The Ministry’s crypto guidance states the same rule in plain language and confirms that excess costs roll forward. Neither the statute nor the Ministry’s guidance creates a special immigration “step-up” to fair market value at the date of arrival. The statute works from actual acquisition expense, not from a deemed migration value. citeturn15view1turn15view2turn3view1turn39search3

For foreign-currency values, Article 11a(2) says costs incurred in foreign currencies are translated into PLN using the average rate published by **entity["organization","Narodowy Bank Polski","polish central bank"]** for the last business day before the cost was incurred. The 2025 KIS interpretation on imported pre-residency crypto purchase costs applies that rule to foreign-currency acquisition costs. citeturn40view0turn21view0

Under the Poland-Sweden treaty, the residence article uses the standard tie-breaker sequence of permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality when both states could claim residence. The capital-gains article then allocates gains from property other than the specifically listed categories to the state of residence of the alienator. On the usual reading, privately held native crypto falls into that residual capital-gains rule, not the immovable-property or permanent-establishment rules. So for pre-move periods when treaty residence remained in Sweden, ordinary crypto gains belong to Sweden; after the move, they belong to Poland. citeturn34search0turn10search0

## Recommended method and bucket treatment

The recommended calculation method is:

- **Step one:** determine the residence cutover date for Polish PIT purposes based on the actual move and treaty residence facts.
- **Step two:** exclude all disposals before that date from Polish PIT-38 revenue.
- **Step three:** calculate a move-date opening basis by asset using **Swedish** pre-move consumption rules.
- **Step four:** import only the cost that remained attached to assets still held at the move date.
- **Step five:** run Polish PIT-38 only on post-move events, adding post-move Polish acquisition costs normally. citeturn6view0turn21view0turn32view0turn29view0

That is why the synthetic Polish annual pool is not the right legal method for 2020-2022. Polish annual carry-forward is a mechanism for years in which the taxpayer is actually in the Polish PIT-38 system. The 2025 imported-cost interpretation did **not** tell the taxpayer to reconstruct hypothetical Polish annual PIT-38 carry-forwards for years spent abroad. It focused on costs “poniesione i nieodliczone” before the residency change and allowed them to be shown in the first Polish year. citeturn20view1turn21view0

A further reason not to use a synthetic Polish pool is that Sweden treats swaps differently from Poland. Poland generally ignores crypto-to-crypto swaps for disposal purposes, while Sweden treats a swap as a sale of the old crypto and an acquisition of the new crypto at the market value of what was received. A Polish synthetic pool built over Swedish years therefore risks mismeasuring surviving basis in both directions. It can be too low or too high. It is not a legally reliable measure of imported cost. citeturn32view0turn29view0turn21view0

If the disputed salary-USDC bucket is not needed to achieve an acceptable result, I would file on the **conservative** version now and seek an interpretation for later amendment. If the disputed bucket is essential and the evidence is unusually strong, a **supportable** filing is possible, but it should be accompanied by a detailed contemporaneous memo and an immediate KIS request. ### Practical next steps

1. **Lock the residence cutover date.** On your present facts, April 12, 2023 is the working date, because Polish residence is a factual issue, not a PESEL-registration issue. If there is evidence that the treaty centre of vital interests moved a few days earlier or later, document it once and use it consistently. citeturn14view0turn6view0  
2. **Cut the ledger in two.** One bucket is all disposals before the cutover; the other is all disposals on and after the cutover. The first bucket should not flow into Polish PIT-38 revenue. citeturn6view0turn10search0turn34search0  
3. **Obtain a Swedish proof package.** The most important pieces are Swedish returns, K4 schedules or equivalent working papers, tax assessments, per-asset average-cost computations through 2023-04-11, and records showing which assets were still held on 2023-04-12. Without this, you cannot prove whether pre-move cost was consumed or survived. citeturn32view0turn29view0  
4. **Build a move-date opening basis schedule by asset.** For each coin held at the move date, show quantity, Swedish surviving acquisition cost, source documents, and PLN conversion. 5. **Separate safe costs from disputed costs.** Safe costs are pre-residency fiat purchases still alive at the move date and post-residency Polish-taxed remuneration. Disputed costs are pre-residency salary-USDC or replacement-asset basis derived from such remuneration. citeturn21view0turn24view2turn24view3  
6. **File the 2025 return by April 30, 2026 and align 2023-2024 corrections to the same method.** The official deadline for the current PIT season is April 30, 2026. citeturn39search11  

### Evidence that matters

For exchange and card-funded fiat purchases, keep exchange trade exports, bank or card statements, payment confirmations, and proof that the wallet or exchange account was yours. That is exactly the kind of evidence both Polish and Swedish guidance expect. citeturn31view2turn29view3

For salary / compensation USDC, keep the service or employment contract, invoices or payroll documents, on-chain transfer history, wallet-address ownership proof, exchange deposit history, and the Swedish tax record showing that the remuneration value was actually recognized in Sweden. For the disputed bucket, Swedish taxation evidence is not just corroborative; it is the core anti-double-taxation proof. citeturn24view3turn24view2turn31view0

For Swedish “not already used” proof, annual totals are not enough. What you really need is a per-asset running record showing how Swedish average-cost calculations were affected by each pre-move sale and swap, plus the move-date holdings inventory. In other words, the decisive evidence is the **Swedish computation of surviving basis**, not merely proof that money once went into crypto at some point in the past. citeturn29view0turn32view0

## Draft KIS interpretation questions

The KIS request should be narrow, should not ask KIS to audit your arithmetic, and should present the Swedish-tax evidence as a core fact. I would frame it around the following questions.

### Question on split-year revenue

Whether, in a situation where the taxpayer was a Swedish tax resident until April 11, 2023 and became a Polish tax resident from April 12, 2023, revenues from the disposal of virtual currencies realized before April 12, 2023 are **not** subject to inclusion in the taxpayer’s Polish PIT-38 for 2023, and whether only revenues from disposals realized from April 12, 2023 onward are reportable in that return. citeturn6view0turn14view1turn10search0turn34search0

### Question on pre-residency salary-USDC held at the move

Whether, if before April 12, 2023 the taxpayer received USDC as remuneration for work or services while being resident in Sweden, the value of that remuneration having been recognized and taxed in Sweden, and the USDC remained in the taxpayer’s assets on April 12, 2023, the value of the settled remuneration receivable / remuneration already taxed in Sweden constitutes a **documented expense directly incurred to acquire virtual currency** within the meaning of Article 22(14) of the PIT Act and may be recognized as an acquisition cost upon the later disposal of that USDC after the taxpayer became Polish resident. citeturn24view3turn24view2turn31view0

### Question on pre-move swaps of that USDC

Whether, if the USDC described above was exchanged before April 12, 2023 for another virtual currency, and that replacement virtual currency remained in the taxpayer’s assets on April 12, 2023 and was disposed of after the taxpayer became Polish resident, the acquisition cost that may be recognized in Poland is the acquisition value of the **replacement** virtual currency determined under the pre-move exchange, provided that this cost was not deducted before the residence change. citeturn32view0turn29view0

### Question on valuation and PLN conversion

Whether, for the purposes above, the correct amount of the cost is the value of the remuneration receivable / employment income recognized for Swedish tax purposes at the date of receipt of the USDC, or, in the case of the replacement virtual currency after a pre-move swap, the acquisition value of that replacement currency under the pre-move exchange, and whether such cost should then be translated into PLN under Article 11a(2) using the average NBP rate from the last business day preceding the date on which the cost was incurred. citeturn40view0turn24view4turn31view0

### Question on proof of non-consumption before the move

Whether, for a taxpayer changing residence from Sweden to Poland during a tax year, the condition that pre-change costs were not deducted before the residence change may be demonstrated by Swedish tax returns, K4 schedules or equivalent computation files, records of Swedish taxation of remuneration in crypto, and a move-date inventory of assets still held by the taxpayer. citeturn21view0turn32view0turn31view0

The key drafting point is to ask KIS about the **legal characterization** and the **valuation principle**, not for approval of a spreadsheet. If the salary-USDC bucket matters materially, that request should be filed immediately, because it is the one area where the current published authorities are close but not fully on top of your exact facts.
