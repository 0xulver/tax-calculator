# Pre-Residency USDC Salary Basis and Split-Year PIT-38
## Polish Tax Analysis for a Swedish-to-Poland Residency Change in 2023

***

## Executive Summary

**Bottom line:** The split-year / imported-cost model (Model 2) is legally correct. Running pre-residency years 2020–2022 and January–April 2023 through a synthetic Polish annual PIT-38 pool is methodologically wrong under Polish tax law. Pre-residency crypto-to-fiat disposals should be excluded from Polish PIT-38 revenue entirely. The Poland-Sweden DTT allocates all pre-move gains exclusively to Sweden. For costs: documented pre-residency fiat-to-crypto acquisition costs not already deducted in Sweden are importable into the first Polish PIT-38 year. Pre-residency USDC salary taxed in Sweden occupies a legally contested but defensible intermediate position—stronger than nothing, weaker than Polish-taxed remuneration.

***

## Part I: Black-Letter Law Foundation

### 1.1 Polish Tax Residency (Art. 3 PIT Act)

Article 3(1a) of the Polish Personal Income Tax Act (Ustawa o PIT) defines a Polish tax resident as any individual who:
1. has the centre of personal or economic interests (ośrodek interesów życiowych) in Poland, **or**
2. stays in Polish territory for more than 183 days in a tax year.

The Ministry of Finance 2021 Tax Explanations (Objaśnienia Rezydencja) confirm that residency change during a tax year operates **prospectively, not retroactively**. From the date of arrival and establishment of centre of vital interests, the taxpayer becomes a Polish tax resident; the period before that date carries no retroactive unlimited tax liability. A 2025 KIS interpretation (ref. 0114-KDIP3-2.4011.944.2024.2.JM) explicitly states: "From the day of arrival in Poland, the taxpayer becomes a Polish tax resident," confirming the prospective effect.[^1]

The MF residency explanations illustrate (Example 9, departure scenario) that income earned *before* the residency change is subject to unlimited tax liability in the former state; income earned *after* is taxed in the new state of residence. The inverse applies to arrivals: pre-arrival income is not subject to Polish unlimited tax liability.[^2]

**For this taxpayer:** Polish unlimited tax liability begins on **2023-04-12** (rental agreement, centre-of-life transfer to Poland). Pre-arrival periods—2020, 2021, 2022, and 1 January–11 April 2023—are outside the scope of Polish unlimited tax liability.

### 1.2 Limited Tax Obligation Before Residency (Art. 3(2a) PIT Act)

Before becoming a Polish resident, the taxpayer was subject only to *limited tax liability* in Poland (ograniczony obowiązek podatkowy): tax applies only to Polish-source income. Virtual currency traded on foreign exchanges (Binance, Kraken, etc.) with no Polish-source connection does not constitute Polish-source income for a non-resident. There is no mechanism under Art. 3(2a) or any other provision by which pre-residency crypto-to-fiat disposals on foreign platforms become Polish-taxable.[^2]

### 1.3 PIT-38 Acquisition Costs (Art. 22(14)–(16) PIT Act)

The statutory formula for PIT-38 crypto costs is narrow: **documented expenditures directly incurred to acquire virtual currency** plus disposal-related costs. The Ministry of Finance confirms on podatki.gov.pl that:[^3][^4]
- costs must be *directly incurred* (bezpośrednio poniesione),
- they are matched in the year incurred,
- excess over revenues carries forward to the next year—indefinitely.[^4][^3]

The carry-forward mechanism operates within the **Polish PIT-38 regime only**—it does not operate retroactively for years in which the taxpayer had no Polish unlimited tax liability.

## Part II: Research Questions Resolved

### A. Split-Year PIT-38 Revenue (Questions 1–4)

**Q1: Should Polish PIT-38 for 2023 include disposals from January through April 11, 2023?**

**Answer: No.** The taxpayer was a Swedish tax resident during that period. Polish unlimited tax liability began on 2023-04-12. Pre-residency disposals (MATIC sales January–April 11) are outside Polish taxing jurisdiction for an individual who was a non-resident at the time of disposal. The MF residency explanations and the KIS 2025 interpretation (0114-KDIP3-2.4011.944.2024.2.JM) both confirm prospective-only effect.[^1][^2]

**Confidence: High (black-letter law, confirmed by KIS and MF objaśnienia).**

The pre-move 2023 revenue of 147,030.09 PLN (all MATIC disposals) should be excluded from Polish PIT-38 revenue. This is not a conservative or aggressive position—it is the legally required position.

**Q2: Should Polish PIT-38 include revenue from 2020–2022 disposals when the taxpayer was Swedish resident?**

**Answer: No.** The taxpayer had no Polish unlimited tax liability in those years. Running 2020–2022 through a Polish-style annual pool is legally unfounded. There is no provision in the PIT Act that imposes retroactive unlimited tax liability on income earned while a non-resident. The synthetic Polish pool for 2020–2022 should be discarded entirely as a revenue inclusion.[^2]

**Confidence: High (black-letter law).**

**Q3: Does the answer change if assets were held on exchanges also accessible after the move, or if proceeds were brought to Poland?**

**Answer: No.** The taxable event under Art. 17(1f) PIT Act is the disposal (exchange for fiat, goods, or services). The disposal occurred pre-residency. Post-disposal deposit of proceeds to a Polish account or continued holding of assets on the same exchange does not retroactively create Polish PIT revenue for pre-residency disposals. Poland taxes disposal events, not asset custody events.[^4][^2]

**Q4: Under the Poland-Sweden DTT, are pre-move native crypto gains taxable only in Sweden, only in Poland, or both?**

**Answer: Taxable only in Sweden for pre-move disposals; taxable only in Poland for post-move disposals.** The Poland-Sweden Convention of 2004 provides:

- **Article 13(4):** "Gains from the alienation of any property other than that referred to in paragraphs (1), (2) and (3), shall be taxable only in the Contracting State of which the alienator is a resident." Virtual currencies are not immovable property (Art. 6), not business PE property (Art. 13(2)), and not ships/aircraft (Art. 13(3)). They fall squarely under Art. 13(4): taxable **only in the state of residence** at the time of disposal.[^5]

- **Article 21(1) (Other Income):** "Items of income of a resident of a Contracting State, wherever arising, not dealt with in the foregoing Articles of this Convention shall be taxable only in that State." To the extent any crypto income falls outside Art. 13, Art. 21 also allocates it exclusively to the state of residence.[^5]

**Key residency-timing consequence:** For disposals made when the taxpayer was resident in Sweden, Sweden has exclusive taxing rights under Art. 13(4) or Art. 21(1). For disposals made after 2023-04-12 (Polish residency), Poland has exclusive taxing rights. There is no dual-taxation overlap on the same disposal event.

**Art. 13(5) note:** The Poland-Sweden DTT includes a 10-year "former resident" rule: gains from property derived by an individual who *was* resident in Sweden and *became* resident in Poland may still be taxed in Sweden for 10 years after ceasing Swedish residency. This provision runs in the direction of Sweden potentially taxing post-move gains—it does not create Polish taxing rights over pre-move disposals. It is primarily relevant for documentation purposes (confirming Swedish taxing right over certain assets).[^5]

**Confidence: High (primary source: full DTT text verified).**

### B. Method for Importing Pre-Residency Costs (Questions 5–9)

**Q5: How should "not deducted" or "not consumed" be measured?**

The WSA Warsaw judgment of 29 August 2024 (III SA/Wa 1290/24, final/non-appealable) is the controlling judicial authority. The court held: costs incurred before obtaining Polish residency **may be included** in PIT-38 **if not previously accounted for as tax expenses in another country**. This is a binary test applied at the cost-item level: was this specific expenditure used as a deduction in a Swedish K4 calculation?[^6][^7]

KIS practice in late 2025 (Holandia case, confirmed by kryptokancelaria.pl review) mirrors the WSA holding: "in the year of residency change, the taxpayer may recognise unused costs in the Polish return and carry them forward until fully utilised."[^8]

"Not deducted" therefore means: not included in a Swedish K4 as cost basis for a disposal that was reported as a gain or loss on Swedish income tax. Costs corresponding to assets **still held at the move date** have never been used in a K4 disposal calculation.

**Q6: Is the right method transaction-level tracing to assets still held at the move date?**

**Answer: Yes, this is the only legally defensible method.** The WSA decision rejected pooling pre-residency disposals into a Polish cost mechanism: costs of crypto-to-crypto swaps that were taxable in the UK were rejected as lacking Polish legal basis. The court accepted only **fiat-to-crypto purchase costs** for assets not already disposed of (and thus not K4-deducted) in the UK.[^7][^6]

By analogy for the Swedish case: the importable pool = sum of fiat-to-crypto purchase costs for tokens still held at 2023-04-12, verified against Swedish K4 records showing those tokens were not disposed of before the move.

**Q7: Is it acceptable to run a Polish-style annual aggregate pool for 2020–2022 and import the remainder?**

**Answer: No.** This approach has two cascading errors:
1. It incorrectly treats pre-residency disposals as generating Polish revenue (they don't).
2. It allows pre-residency costs corresponding to those disposed assets to flow into a carry-forward figure, potentially inflating the importable basis.

The synthetic Polish pool method mixes pre-residency disposal revenues (which should not appear) with costs (some of which may be importable). The resulting carry-forward number cannot be verified as representing only costs of assets *still held* at the move date. It is methodologically incompatible with the WSA/KIS framework.

**Q8: Should pre-residency sales reduce the importable pool only if costs were actually deducted in Sweden?**

**Answer: Yes.** This is the precise consequence of the "not deducted abroad" test from WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24. Swedish K4 records are the authoritative source for determining which acquisition costs were matched against Swedish-taxed disposals. If a pre-residency disposal was reported on K4 and a cost was claimed, that cost is consumed and cannot be re-imported into Polish PIT-38.[^6][^7]

However, if pre-residency tokens were acquired but never disposed of before the move (or were disposed of but with no taxable gain/loss reported in Sweden and thus no cost deducted), those acquisition costs remain importable.

**Q9: Sweden taxes crypto-to-crypto swaps unlike Poland. Does this affect the importable pool?**

**Answer: Yes—and this is a critical asymmetry.** Sweden taxes every crypto-to-crypto exchange as a disposal at the average cost basis (genomsnittlig anskaffningsutgift). Poland does not tax crypto-to-crypto swaps at all. The WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24 decision (applying to a UK-to-Poland emigrant) explicitly refused to allow costs associated with crypto-to-crypto swaps as Polish PIT-38 costs, even though those swaps were taxed in the UK. The court's reasoning was: **Polish PIT Act does not recognise crypto-to-crypto swap costs as deductible expenditures**, regardless of whether a foreign jurisdiction taxed them.[^9][^10][^11][^3][^7][^4][^6]

**Consequence for USDC:** If pre-residency USDC was received by *swapping another crypto* into USDC on-chain (rather than purchasing with fiat), the acquisition cost of that USDC may be the value of the crypto surrendered in the swap—but this cost cannot be imported into Polish PIT-38 under WSA logic, even if Sweden taxed the swap. Only **fiat-to-crypto** acquisition costs (or equivalently, settled-receivable-for-service costs—see Part III below) can pass the Polish legal basis test.

Swedish K4 records showing which cryptos were disposed of (and their Swedish cost basis consumed) provide the controlling evidence for what costs are available for Polish import.

### C. Pre-Residency Salary-Paid USDC (Questions 10–15)

This is the most contested bucket and requires careful disaggregation.

**The Legal Framework for Post-Residency USDC Salary:**

KIS 0115-KDIT1.4011.22.2025.1.MR (March 4, 2025) confirmed that a crypto bonus included in PIT-36 (Polish employment income) constitutes an "odpłatne nabycie" (paid acquisition) of virtual currency. The cost basis is the value of the settled receivable (wynagrodzenie), i.e., the amount already included as Polish taxable income. The settled labour receivable is the "directly incurred expenditure" under Art. 22(14).[^12]

KIS 0112-KDIL2-2.4011.651.2023.1.WS (November 9, 2023) accepted the same principle for service fees paid in crypto: the invoice value of the service = cost basis of acquired virtual currency. This is the *barter characterisation*: providing the service settles the acquisition cost of the crypto received.[^13][^14]

**The Gap: Pre-Residency / Foreign-Taxed Salary USDC**

Neither KIS interpretation cited above involves foreign-taxed or pre-residency salary. The KIS holding in both cases depends on the crypto remuneration being *included in Polish PIT (PIT-36 or similar)* as income first. That Polish income recognition creates the "settled receivable" that becomes the cost basis.[^12]

**Q10: Can USDC received as pre-residency salary/compensation become Polish PIT-38 acquisition cost?**

**Analysis (three legal theories, ranked by strength):**

*Theory A (Strongest — Foreign-Taxed Fiat-Like Settlement):* The USDC compensation was received pursuant to a contract/invoice specifying a USD amount; USDC was the payment vehicle. The acquisition cost of the USDC is the settled USD/SEK receivable, documented by the invoice/contract. This parallels the KIS barter framework but for a foreign-taxed payment. **The strongest argument is that the "directly incurred expenditure" (wydatek bezpośrednio poniesiony) in Art. 22(14) is the value of the service provided, regardless of jurisdiction**—the taxpayer genuinely expended economic value (labour/services) to acquire the USDC. The WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24 ruling accepted pre-residency fiat purchase costs; the analogous principle would extend to labour-for-crypto arrangements where the economic cost is documented.[^7][^6]

*Theory B (Medium — "Not Deducted" Extension):* The WSA/KIS framework accepts pre-residency acquisition costs "not previously accounted for as tax expenses abroad". If Sweden taxed the USDC salary as income (not as capital gain), the capital gain cost basis for the USDC was never formally "deducted" in a Swedish K4. The salary was taxed as employment income; the USDC acquisition cost was never separately claimed. Under a strict reading, the cost was not "deducted" in Sweden.[^6][^7]

*Theory C (Weakest — Direct KIS Analogy):* The KIS interpretations on crypto remuneration (0115-KDIT1.4011.22.2025.1.MR; 0112-KDIL2-2.4011.651.2023.1.WS) all involve Polish-income-recognised remuneration. These cannot be directly applied. The missing step is the Polish income recognition event that both KIS and WSA treat as the cost trigger. Without Polish income recognition, the analogy fails at its core premise.

**Q11: Does it matter that the USDC compensation was reported and taxed in Sweden?**

**Answer: Yes, in two respects:**

1. **It proves the acquisition was arm's-length and bona fide.** Swedish tax records (Swedish income tax return or Skatteverket assessment showing the USDC salary as employment income) corroborate the invoice value, prevent double-dipping, and demonstrate the payment was compensation for services rather than a gratuitous transfer.

2. **It creates an ambiguity:** The Swedish tax system taxed the *income* from services at receipt; it did not allow a capital gains cost deduction at the same time (because Sweden didn't tax the receipt of USDC as a capital gain event—it taxed it as employment income). So the "cost" of the USDC (its SEK/USD employment value) was *consumed as Swedish employment income*, not as a capital gains cost deduction. This arguably means the economic cost was "used" in Sweden, even if not in the K4 capital gains sense.

**Q12: Does foreign tax recognition play the same anti-double-taxation role as Polish PIT-36 recognition in KIS interpretations?**

**Answer: Partially, but not equivalently.** The KIS rationale in 0115-KDIT1.4011.22.2025.1.MR is that recognising the crypto bonus as a PIT-38 cost prevents *Polish* double taxation (taxing the same value twice under Polish law). For pre-residency Swedish-taxed salary, the anti-double-taxation argument is between *two different tax systems*. Polish law has no explicit mechanism analogous to the DTT credit that applies to PIT-38 costs. The DTT's elimination of double taxation provisions (Art. 22) govern income, not cost basis allocation. There is no KIS interpretation or Polish court case that has directly accepted the proposition that *Swedish-taxed employment income creates Polish PIT-38 cost basis*—this remains an inference gap.[^12]

**Q14: PLN valuation of importable pre-residency salary USDC cost:**

If the Theory A / Theory B argument succeeds, the cost should be:

- **Invoice/contract USD value × NBP USD/PLN rate from the day before the on-chain payment** (consistent with Art. 22(14) documentation requirement and general NBP rate methodology applied to foreign-currency crypto transactions).[^15]
- USDC is pegged to USD (1:1 under normal conditions); the applicable rate is the NBP USD rate from the table published the day before receipt.
- Swedish taxed SEK value converted to PLN is an alternative but less direct—SEK/PLN conversion introduces an additional conversion step with no explicit legal basis, while USD/PLN directly reflects the USD-denominated invoice amount.
- The invoice/contract amount expressed in USD is the best anchor, as it represents the settled receivable (the economic cost of acquiring the USDC).

**Q15: Is this a defensible aggressive position for an individual interpretation request?**

**Answer: Yes, with appropriate framing.** There is no directly adverse KIS interpretation on this exact fact pattern. The WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24 decision supports importation of pre-residency costs not previously deducted abroad; the KIS barter interpretations (0112-KDIL2-2.4011.651.2023.1.WS; 0115-KDIT1.4011.22.2025.1.MR) support treating settled service receivables as cost basis. An interpretation request combining both lines of authority is viable. The threshold question KIS will focus on is whether the employment/service income recognition in Sweden is equivalent to Polish PIT recognition for purposes of Art. 22(14). This is genuinely open.[^13][^7][^12][^6]

## Part V: Calculation Method Recommendation

### Use: Transaction-Level Tracing / Swedish K4 Consumption Method

**Reject:** Synthetic Polish annual pooling for 2020–2022. This method is legally incorrect and numerically unreliable for two independent reasons:

1. **Revenue side:** It includes pre-residency disposals in Polish PIT-38 revenue, which is wrong under Art. 3 PIT Act and DTT Art. 13(4). There is no Polish jurisdiction over those disposal events.[^2][^5]
2. **Cost side:** It comingles costs of disposed assets (whose acquisition costs were K4-consumed in Sweden) with costs of assets still held at the move date (which are importable). The synthetic carry-forward of 274,399.39 PLN from 2020 into 2021, and so on, builds a number that cannot be verified as representing only importable costs.

**Recommended method:**

**Step 1 — Inventory as at 2023-04-11:** Compile a complete token-level inventory of all crypto assets held at end of 2023-04-11 (the day before Polish residency), with acquisition dates and fiat-purchase prices (or USDC salary value per Theory A, where applicable), verified against Swedish K4 records.

**Step 2 — Swedish K4 reconciliation:** For each asset in inventory, confirm via Swedish K4 returns (2020–2022 and Jan–Mar 2023) that the specific acquisition costs have *not* been matched against a Swedish-reported disposal.

**Step 3 — Import eligible costs:** Sum the acquisition costs of assets still held at 2023-04-12 that were not K4-consumed in Sweden. This is the opening importable cost for first Polish PIT-38.

**Step 4 — 2023 Polish PIT-38:** Revenue = post-move disposals only (from 2023-04-12 through 31 Dec 2023 = 264,113.82 PLN per the data provided). Costs in current year = costs incurred post-move in 2023 (500,922.55 PLN) + imported pre-residency basis (Step 3 amount). Carry-forward = excess costs.

**Step 5 — Note on 2022 salary costs (503,901.67 PLN):** The current safe-default policy correctly excludes these from the Polish cost pool. The analysis above confirms this exclusion is *legally required*, not merely conservative. The 2022 salary payments were received when the taxpayer was Swedish resident; the stablecoin disposals in 2022 are excluded from Polish revenue entirely; therefore there is no Polish pooling mechanism in 2022 that these costs could enter.

**The 294,073.39 PLN opening imported cost figure (current safe default):** This number was generated by the synthetic pool method and thus has no clean methodological basis. It may under- or over-estimate the true importable amount. The correct number must come from transaction-level tracing + Swedish K4 reconciliation. If that number exceeds 134,579.93 PLN (the threshold for zero 2024–2025 tax in the simplified model), the 2024–2025 PIT-38 tax falls to zero.

**Q17: What does the taxpayer need from Swedish filings to prove costs were not already used?**

The critical document is the **Swedish K4 form** (Kapitalinkomst section of Inkomstdeklaration) for each year 2020, 2021, 2022, and the 2023 partial year. The K4 lists each crypto disposal with cost basis claimed. A professional extraction of the K4 data showing the average cost basis applied to each disposal allows mathematical proof that specific token lots (identified by acquisition date and price) were or were not included as cost offsets in Swedish disposals. Additionally, a Skatteverket tax assessment (slutskattebesked) confirming the reported income and capital gains for each year provides corroboration.

## Part VIII: Filing Posture Recommendation

**Q18: Should the taxpayer file now with a conservative position and amend if an individual interpretation confirms the aggressive basis?**

**Yes—for the deadline reason.** The Polish PIT-38 deadline is 30 April of the following year. Filing conservatively now (excluding pre-residency salary USDC from the imported cost pool) preserves the filing obligation and avoids penalties. An individual interpretation (ORD-IN) typically takes 3 months but can extend to 6 months. Polish Ordynacja Podatkowa permits amendment of a return (korekta zeznania) after an interpretation is received, provided no control/audit has been commenced. The amendment window is effectively open until the statute of limitations (5 years from year end). Filing conservatively now + amendment after KIS interpretation confirmation is the standard professional approach for disputed cost items of material size.

## Part IX: Draft KIS Interpretation Questions (ORD-IN)

### Fact Pattern for ORD-IN Application

The applicant is a Swedish citizen and tax resident who transferred their centre of vital interests to Poland on [date]. Before the move, the applicant performed IT/blockchain services for foreign entities. Compensation was paid in USDC (a USD-pegged stablecoin, a virtual currency in the meaning of Art. 2(2)(26) of the AML Act). The compensation was documented by invoices specifying amounts in USD; USDC was delivered on-chain at a contractually agreed value equal to the invoice USD amount. The USDC compensation was reported as employment/service income in Sweden and subjected to Swedish income tax in years [YYYY], [YYYY]. The USDC was received by the applicant's self-custody wallet. At the date of transfer of Polish residency, a portion of the USDC received pre-residency remained in the applicant's wallet (not sold or exchanged). The USDC was never declared in a Swedish K4 as a capital gain event at receipt; it was declared as employment/service income only.

After becoming a Polish tax resident, the applicant sold (exchanged for PLN/EUR) some of the pre-residency salary USDC, realising revenue in the Polish tax year.

### Question 1 (Core Question)

*Whether the value of USDC received by the applicant as compensation for services rendered before becoming a Polish tax resident, documented by invoice and equal to the USD invoice amount, constitutes an "udokumentowany wydatek bezpośrednio poniesiony na nabycie waluty wirtualnej" (documented expense directly incurred to acquire virtual currency) within the meaning of Art. 22(14) of the PIT Act, which may be treated as a deductible cost in the PIT-38 return for the year in which the applicant (as a Polish tax resident) disposes of that USDC for fiat currency?*

### Question 2 (Not Consumed Condition)

*Whether the condition that pre-residency acquisition costs must not have been "deducted or accounted for as tax costs abroad" (within the meaning applied in WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24) is satisfied where the USDC compensation was taxed as employment income in Sweden and the USDC was not separately reported on a Swedish K4 capital gains form as a disposal event at receipt, such that no K4 cost deduction was claimed in Sweden for the acquisition cost of the USDC?*

### Question 3 (PLN Valuation)

*Whether the PLN value of the above acquisition cost should be determined using the NBP mid-rate for USD/PLN published on the business day preceding the date of on-chain transfer of USDC to the applicant's wallet, applied to the USD invoice amount?*

### Question 4 (Carry-Forward)

*Whether, if the acquisition cost referred to in Question 1 is recognised in the first Polish PIT-38 year and exceeds the revenue from disposal of USDC in that year, the excess may be carried forward to subsequent tax years pursuant to Art. 22(16) of the PIT Act?*

### Strategic Framing Notes for ORD-IN

- Cite WSA III SA/Wa 1290/24 as the controlling authority on pre-residency cost importation.[^7][^6]
- Cite KIS 0115-KDIT1.4011.22.2025.1.MR for the proposition that settled employment receivable = Art. 22(14) acquisition cost.[^12]
- Cite KIS 0112-KDIL2-2.4011.651.2023.1.WS for the barter/service-payment framework.[^14][^13]
- Emphasise the "not deducted abroad" condition is satisfied: Sweden taxed *income*, not *capital gain at receipt*; no K4 cost deduction was available for or claimed against the USDC receipt.
- Do not request confirmation of the excluded-revenue position (pre-residency disposals outside Polish PIT-38) in the same ORD-IN—this creates unnecessary scope and the black-letter law on residency is sufficiently clear.
